Article 39 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says that an entry in the Turkish civil status register (kişisel durum sicili, nüfus kaydı) can only be corrected by a court decision. The civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) cannot change an entry on its own judgment or simply because you ask; the only exception is a clerical error made when the event was recorded. Anyone whose date of birth, name, parents' names or place of birth is wrong in the Turkish register, in Türkiye or abroad, needs to start with this distinction.

## Article 39 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **IV. Correction**
>
> **1. In general**
>
> Article 39 – No entry in the civil status register may be corrected without a court decision.

Original Turkish text:

> **IV. Düzeltme**
>
> **1. Genel olarak**
>
> Madde 39- Mahkeme kararı olmadıkça, kişisel durum sicilinin hiçbir kaydında düzeltme yapılamaz.

As of November 2026, Article 39 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 38 of the former Civil Code No. 743; the wording was simplified and the rule itself was not changed (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### The rule: a final court decision

TMK Article 39 sets the principle; the details are in the Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). Under its Article 35(1), no entry may be corrected, and no annotation changing the meaning or content of an entry may be added, without a final court judgment. The Civil Code speaks of a "court decision"; Law No. 5490 requires it to be final.

### The exception: clerical errors (maddî hata)

Under the second sentence of the same paragraph, the registry office itself corrects errors made when events were entered in the family register (aile kütüğü), on the basis of the underlying document. Article 38 of Law No. 5490 defines these errors: information that appears in the underlying document but was entered wrongly, incompletely or not at all, and duplicate entries.

So the key question is: does the correct information appear in the document on which the entry is based? If it does and the official copied it wrongly, that is a clerical error and no court case is needed. If the information in the underlying document is itself wrong, only a court decision can correct it. Religion entries follow their own rule: they are recorded, changed, left blank or deleted on the person's written statement (Law No. 5490, Article 35(2)).

### The correction case

Under Article 36(1) of Law No. 5490:

- The case is brought by the person seeking the correction, or by the public prosecutor at the request of the relevant authority.
- The competent court is the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) at the place of the person's residence address. The Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) lists the correction of a civil status entry as non-contentious jurisdiction (HMK Article 382(2)(a)(5)), for which the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) is normally competent, but only "unless otherwise provided" (HMK Article 383). Article 36 of Law No. 5490 is such a provision.
- The case is heard in the presence of the head of the registry office or an official he or she appoints. The requirement that a public prosecutor attend was abolished by Law No. 6217 in 2011.
- Where there is good reason, the same item may be corrected again (Article 36(1)(b)).
- Declaratory judgments create a presumption for later cases seeking cancellation or correction of an entry (Article 36(1)(c)).

Registry offices may appeal court decisions and are exempt from fees and court costs (Law No. 5490, Article 37). The court registry notifies the registry office of decisions requiring an entry within ten days after they become final (Article 55(1)).

### How correction differs from similar procedures

If the entry is right and you want a different name, that is not a correction but a change of name for good reason (TMK Article 27). Entries after gender reassignment are governed by the special conditions of TMK Article 40. Recognition, a paternity judgment or adoption actually change a person's status and are entered as new facts (TMK Article 42). An entry based on an improperly issued document is cancelled by court decision at the request of the authorities (Law No. 5490, Article 40).

## Practical points for Turkish citizens living abroad

A common situation is that a detail in a German or Dutch document does not match the Turkish entry. If the foreign document was the basis of the Turkish entry and the correct detail was simply copied wrongly, the registry office route comes first. If the foreign document was not the basis of the entry, or the Turkish entry itself has to change, a court case is needed; the foreign document can be used there as evidence.

Article 36(1)(a) of Law No. 5490 points to the court of the residence address. The law does not expressly say which Turkish court has territorial jurisdiction if your residence is only abroad. I do not cite a verified court decision on this; it should be assessed for the specific case before filing. For dates of birth see [changing your date of birth in Turkey](/en/publications/change-date-of-birth-turkey-court-case).

You can have the case filed by a lawyer in Türkiye. A representative may not bring cases concerning strictly personal rights unless the power of attorney expressly covers them (HMK Article 74). In the author's assessment, because it can be argued whether a registry correction falls into this category, it is prudent to name this case expressly in the power of attorney.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an error in the Turkish register be corrected by application?

Only a clerical error: if the correct information is in the underlying document, the registry office corrects it (Law No. 5490, Articles 35(1) and 38). Everything else needs a final court decision (TMK Article 39).

### Which court hears a civil registry correction case?

The civil court of first instance at the place of the residence address (Law No. 5490, Article 36(1)(a)).

### Who is the opposing party?

The law names no defendant; the case is heard in the presence of the head of the registry office or an appointed official (Article 36(1)(a)). The registry office may appeal (Article 37).

### Can the same entry be corrected a second time?

Yes, if there is good reason (Law No. 5490, Article 36(1)(b)).

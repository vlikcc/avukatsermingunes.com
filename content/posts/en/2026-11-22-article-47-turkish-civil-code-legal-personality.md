Article 47 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) opens Part Two, on legal persons (tüzel kişiler). Organised groups of persons and independent bodies of property dedicated to a purpose acquire legal personality under the special rules that apply to them; groups whose purpose is unlawful or immoral cannot. The article concerns anyone setting up an association, foundation or company in Türkiye, and foreign associations and foundations that want to act there.

## Article 47 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **A. Legal personality**
>
> Article 47 – Groups of persons organised so as to have an existence of their own, and independent bodies of property dedicated to a particular purpose, acquire legal personality in accordance with the special provisions that apply to them.
>
> Groups of persons or property whose purpose is contrary to law or morality cannot acquire legal personality.

Original Turkish text:

> **A. Tüzel kişilik**
>
> Madde 47- Başlıbaşına bir varlığı olmak üzere örgütlenmiş kişi toplulukları ve belli bir amaca özgülenmiş olan bağımsız mal toplulukları, kendileri ile ilgili özel hükümler uyarınca tüzel kişilik kazanırlar.
>
> Amacı hukuka veya ahlâka aykırı olan kişi ve mal toplulukları tüzel kişilik kazanamaz.

As of November 2026, Article 47 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 45 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The drafters deliberately avoided a general rule on when legal personality arises: the old text, following the Swiss model, linked it to registration, but that was true only for foundations, commercial companies and cooperatives, not for associations, which can be formed freely. Rather than listing types, the definition names the common elements: a group of persons or property, a particular purpose, organisation as a separate entity, and recognition by the legal order. Paragraph 2 says "contrary to law" instead of "contrary to statute", and by using "or" instead of "and" makes either breach sufficient on its own (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Two types: groups of persons and bodies of property

A legal person is an entity that is not a human being but that the legal order recognises as holder of rights and obligations. The Code's two basic examples are the association and the foundation:

- An association (dernek) is a group of persons: at least seven natural or legal persons continuously combine their knowledge and work for a specific common purpose other than sharing profits (Article 56(1) TMK).
- A foundation (vakıf) is a body of property: natural or legal persons dedicate sufficient assets and rights to a specific and permanent purpose (Article 101(1) TMK).

The laws on public legal persons and commercial companies are reserved (Article 55 TMK).

### When is legal personality acquired?

Article 47 does not say; it leaves this to the special rules. The choice described in the reasoning leads to this difference:

- Associations acquire legal personality at the moment they submit the notice of formation, the statutes and the required documents to the chief administrative officer of their seat (Article 59(1) TMK). No permission or registration is required; the documents are reviewed within sixty days (Article 60).
- The will to set up a foundation is declared by official deed or by testamentary disposition; the foundation acquires legal personality on registration in the register kept at the court of its seat (Article 102(1) TMK).

### Paragraph 2: unlawful or immoral purpose

A group whose purpose is unlawful or immoral from the start does not become a legal person. Article 56(2) repeats this for associations and the last paragraph of Article 101 for foundations. If the purpose only later becomes unlawful, the route differs: for an association, the court dissolves it on application of the public prosecutor or an interested party (Article 89); a foundation whose purpose is later prohibited is dissolved if its purpose cannot be changed (Article 116(2)). The assets of a legal person ended by a court because of an unlawful or immoral purpose pass in every case to the relevant public body (Article 54(3)).

The limits of legal capacity (Article 48) and the organs (Articles 49–50) follow in the next articles.

## Practical points for people living abroad

When an association or foundation registered in Germany or the Netherlands wants to buy property, make a donation or sue in Türkiye, the first question is which law governs its capacity. Under Article 9(4) MÖHUK (the Turkish private international law act), the legal capacity and capacity to act of legal persons are governed by the law of the administrative seat stated in their statutes; if the actual seat of administration is in Türkiye, Turkish law may be applied ([Article 9 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-9-mohuk-legal-capacity)). Whether a German or Dutch association is a legal person is therefore, as a rule, a question of German or Dutch law, on which you should consult a lawyer admitted in that country.

Activity in Türkiye needs a separate permit. Under Article 5(2) of the Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu), foreign associations may operate or cooperate in Türkiye, open offices or branches, set up associations or join existing ones with the permission of the Ministry of the Interior after obtaining the opinion of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. As of November 2026 this paragraph is in its original wording (latest amendment: Law No. 7533, in force since 30/11/2024, not affecting Article 5). Acquisition of real estate by foreign legal persons follows separate rules and is not covered here.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a legal person under Turkish law?

An entity that is not a human being but is recognised as holder of rights and obligations; examples are the association as a group of persons and the foundation as a body of property (Articles 47, 56, 101 TMK).

### When does an association in Türkiye acquire legal personality?

When the formation documents are submitted to the administrative officer (Article 59 TMK); a foundation only on registration with the court register (Article 102 TMK).

### Can a foreign association operate in Türkiye?

With permission of the Ministry of the Interior, after the Ministry of Foreign Affairs has given its opinion (Law No. 5253, Article 5(2)).

A will made abroad is formally valid in Turkey if it meets the form rules of the place where it was made, of a state whose nationality the testator held, or, for property in Turkey, of Turkish law (Articles 20(4) and 7 MÖHUK; Article 1 of the 1961 Hague Convention). Its content is a separate question: forced shares (saklı pay) in Turkish property follow Turkish law, and using the will in Turkey requires opening, an apostille and a translation.

How a will is made under Turkish law is covered in [making a will in Turkey](/en/publications/making-a-will-in-turkey); which law governs the estate is covered in the post on [Article 20 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-20-mohuk-inheritance-turkey).

## Which form rules make a foreign will valid in Turkey?

Under Article 20(4) of MÖHUK (Turkish Act No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure), the form of a disposition on death is governed by Article 7: a legal act may follow the form of the place where it is made or the form of the law that governs its substance. A disposition that follows the form of the testator's national law is also valid.

Turkey is also a party to the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 on the Conflicts of Laws Relating to the Form of Testamentary Dispositions. Article 1(2) MÖHUK keeps international treaties to which Turkey is a party in force, so the Convention is applied first. According to the official status table of the Hague Conference (HCCH), the Convention entered into force for Turkey on 22 October 1983, for Germany on 1 January 1966 and for the Netherlands on 1 August 1982 (as of November 2026).

Under Article 1 of the Convention, a will is valid as to form if it complies with the internal law of **any one** of these:

- the place where the testator made it,
- a state whose nationality the testator had when making the will or at death,
- the place of the testator's domicile at either of those times,
- the place of habitual residence at either of those times,
- for immovable property, the place where the property is.

One of these laws may be refused only when it is manifestly contrary to public policy (Article 7). As of November 2026 there are no new amendments to Articles 7 and 20 MÖHUK or to the Turkish Civil Code (TMK) articles cited here.

Turkey made three reservations on accession (Articles 9, 10 and 12). It determines the testator's domicile under Turkish law. It reserves the right not to recognise oral wills made by its own nationals with no other nationality, except in exceptional circumstances. It also excludes from the Convention any clauses that, under Turkish law, do not concern succession.

## Wills made in Germany and the Netherlands

A will made in Germany under German form rules, handwritten or before a notary, is formally valid in Turkey through the place where it was made. If the testator is a Turkish national, the form of Article 538 TMK is also enough: the whole text must be handwritten, show the year, month and day, and be signed. A will that does not meet the Turkish form only needs to satisfy one of the other links in the Convention.

The Netherlands is different. According to the Dutch government's official website, a will can only be made through a notary there, and a will you write yourself is not legally valid in the Netherlands. If a Turkish national living in the Netherlands writes a will under Article 538 TMK, a Turkish court would still treat it as formally valid through nationality (Article 1 of the Convention; Article 20(4) MÖHUK). The Netherlands is also a party to the Convention, but how Dutch authorities treat such a will for assets in the Netherlands is a matter of Dutch law. Ask a notary there.

The Convention accepts any nationality the testator holds, so a dual national can use the form of either state. For Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holders who gave up Turkish citizenship with permission, Turkish form through nationality is not available; for property in Turkey, it still works through the place where the property is.

Abroad, Turkish consuls carry out notarial work (Article 191, Turkish Notaries Act). Article 193 also applies Article 69 on the safekeeping of wills to consuls. Whether a formal will can be made at a Turkish consulate is discussed in the post on making a will in Turkey; ask your consulate for the practical details.

## Property in Turkey: valid form, Turkish law on content

Turkish law applies to immovable property in Turkey in every case (Article 20(1) MÖHUK). Who inherits, the size of the forced shares and whether a reduction claim (tenkis) can be brought are decided under Turkish law for that property. Capacity to make a will follows the testator's national law at the time the will was made (Article 20(5) MÖHUK).

The wording also matters. A testator may appoint heirs to the whole estate or a share of it (Article 516 TMK) or leave a specific asset to someone without making them an heir (a legacy, Article 517 TMK). A legatee does not become owner; they have a personal claim against the executor or the heirs and may sue for delivery (Article 600 TMK). An appointed heir receives a certificate from the civil court of peace (sulh mahkemesi) showing that status if no objection is raised within one month of notice of the disposition (Article 598(2) TMK).

Two countries may apply different laws to the same estate, since German and Dutch authorities apply their own private international law. Turkey and Germany are also bound by the 1929 Estates Agreement annexed to their Consular Treaty, which has its own succession rules. I could not check its official text on the form of wills for this post, so I do not cover it here.

## Joint wills (the German Berliner Testament)

In Germany, spouses often make a joint will in one document (gemeinschaftliches Testament, commonly called a Berliner Testament). The Hague Convention also applies to the form of wills made by two or more persons in one document (Article 4), so its formal validity is judged by the links above.

The Turkish Civil Code does not regulate joint wills. It recognises formal, handwritten and oral wills (Article 531 TMK); for a binding arrangement between two people it provides the inheritance contract made in the form of a formal will (Article 545 TMK).

This point is disputed. The two possibilities and the assessment below are the author's view; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on it. How a court would decide in a given case is uncertain.

If the absence of the joint will in Turkish law is treated as a matter of form, a joint will that is valid in form under German law is valid in Turkey too under Article 4 of the Convention. If it is treated as a matter of content, Turkish law governs Turkish property. The TMK also says nothing on whether the surviving spouse remains bound after the first death. My view: spouses with property in Turkey reduce the risk of later disputes if they make separate wills, or an inheritance contract under Turkish law, for that property.

## Does a will opened abroad have to be opened again in Turkey?

In Germany, the probate court (Nachlassgericht) opens a will in its custody once it learns of the death, records the opening and informs the parties in writing of the parts that concern them (§ 348 FamFG). Under Turkish law, a will found after death must be handed to the civil judge of peace (sulh hâkimi), whether valid or not (Article 595 TMK). The judge of the testator's place of domicile opens it within one month and reads it to those concerned (Article 596 TMK), and each beneficiary receives a certified copy of the part that concerns them (Article 597 TMK).

The law has no rule for a will already opened abroad, or for a testator whose domicile was abroad.

This point is disputed. The assessment below is the author's view; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on it. How a court would decide in a given case is uncertain.

My view: if the will is to be relied on in Turkey, for example to obtain the certificate under Article 598(2) TMK, asking a Turkish civil court of peace to open it is the more predictable route. The original stays abroad, so a certified copy, the opening record, an apostille and a Turkish translation are filed. For jurisdiction, the test in Article 43 MÖHUK may help (last domicile in Turkey, failing that the place of the estate's assets). It is also unclear whether a German opening record is a non-contentious decision that must be recognised under Article 58(2) MÖHUK.

Watch the deadlines. The ten- and twenty-year outer limits for an action to annul a will run from the will's "opening" (Article 559(1) TMK). Since it is unclear which opening counts, counting from the earlier opening abroad is the cautious approach. How statutory heirs obtain a Turkish certificate of inheritance is explained in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey).

The apostille comes from the Hague Apostille Convention of 5 October 1961, to which Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are parties (HCCH status table: in force for Turkey since 29 September 1985). It covers documents issued by courts and notarial acts (Article 1). A handwritten will is not itself a public document; the apostille goes on the certified copy issued by the court or notary and, where there is one, on the opening record. Documents issued by a Turkish consulate fall outside the Convention (Article 1).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a German notarial will valid in Turkey?

As to form, yes, because it complies with the law of the place where it was made (Article 1, 1961 Hague Convention). For property in Turkey, its content, especially the forced shares, follows Turkish law (Article 20(1) MÖHUK).

### Is a handwritten will made in the Netherlands valid in Turkey?

If you are a Turkish national and the will meets Article 538 TMK, a Turkish court would treat it as formally valid. In the Netherlands a self-written will is not legally valid; ask a Dutch notary about the effect there.

### Does a foreign will need to be enforced (tenfiz) in Turkey?

No. Enforcement under Article 50 MÖHUK applies to final judgments of foreign courts in civil cases, and a will is not a judgment. In Turkey it is used through opening and, where needed, the certificate under Article 598(2) TMK.

### Which Turkish court opens the will?

By law, the civil judge of peace at the testator's domicile (Article 596 TMK). If the domicile was abroad there is no express rule; in the view set out above, the test in Article 43 MÖHUK can be used.

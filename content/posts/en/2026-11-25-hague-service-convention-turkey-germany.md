When a Turkish court or enforcement office has to serve documents on someone living in Germany or the Netherlands, Turkish law offers two routes: through the competent authority of the receiving country, or, if the addressee is a Turkish citizen, through the Turkish embassy or consulate there (Articles 25 and 25/a of the Notification Law No. 7201, Tebligat Kanunu). With Germany and the Netherlands the authority route runs under the 1965 Hague Service Convention. The documents are translated, and as a rule the court cannot decide the case until service has been made.

I checked the provisions against the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr in November 2026. The last law listed in the amendment table of the Notification Law is Law No. 7517 (in force since 28 June 2024), which only added Article 26/A on service from military units in Northern Cyprus. Articles 25, 25/a, 28 and 31, which govern service abroad, have not changed in the last two years.

If you have already received a Turkish payment order, the objection deadline is covered in [Turkish payment order: objection deadline from abroad](/en/publications/turkey-payment-order-objection-deadline).

## Service from Turkey: the two statutory routes

**Through the receiving state's authority.** Service abroad is made through the competent authority of that country (Article 25(1) Tebligat Kanunu). The documents go via the ministry responsible for the issuing body and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to the Turkish embassy or consulate general (Article 38 of the Regulation on the Implementation of the Notification Law). If the addressee is not a Turkish citizen, this is the only route (Article 42 of the Regulation).

**Through the consulate (Article 25/a).** A Turkish citizen abroad may also be served through the Turkish embassy or consulate, and courts may send the papers there directly. The consulate sends the addressee a notice warning that service will be deemed made if he or she does not come forward within 30 days. Once delivery of that notice is documented and the addressee stays silent, service is deemed made at the end of the 30th day. If the addressee comes to the consulate but refuses the documents, the date of the refusal record is the date of service (Article 25/a; Article 43(3)–(4) of the Regulation). The court decides which route to use (Article 43(8) of the Regulation).

## The Hague Service Convention: Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands

According to the status table of the Hague Conference (HCCH), Turkey (in force 28 April 1972), Germany (26 June 1979) and the Netherlands (2 January 1976) are parties to the 1965 Convention on the Service Abroad of Judicial and Extrajudicial Documents in Civil or Commercial Matters. Requests go to the Central Authority each state designates.

Turkey has declared that diplomatic or consular agents may serve under Article 8 only on their own nationals, and it objects to the methods in Article 10, such as postal service. It has also declared that its courts may give judgment under the conditions of Article 15(2), and that applications under Article 16 will not be entertained if filed more than one year after the date of the judgment.

### Germany

Under Germany's declaration, formal service (Article 5(1)) is permissible only if the document is written in or translated into German. Without a translation it can only be handed to an addressee who accepts it voluntarily, and the addressee may refuse without giving reasons (Article 5(2)). Germany objects to Articles 8 and 10: consular service is allowed only on a national of the sending state. Each German federal state (Land) has its own Central Authority.

Guidance from the Turkish Ministry of Justice's Directorate General for Foreign Relations and EU Affairs (updated 6 January 2025) says documents for Germany are attached to Form No. 184 and prepared in two sets, in Turkish and German. The Ministry also warns enforcement offices (icra müdürlüğü) that Germany refuses requests where enforcement or payment orders contain wording that threatens criminal penalties, because it regards this as contrary to its sovereignty. Such wording has to be removed before the papers are sent. A 1929 bilateral agreement on legal relations in civil and commercial matters also exists between the two countries.

### The Netherlands

According to the same guidance, Turkey has no bilateral agreement with the Netherlands. Documents are attached to Form No. 184 and prepared in two sets, in Turkish and Dutch. Because the Netherlands has not objected to postal service, the documents, in Turkish with a Dutch translation, may also be sent to the addressee by registered mail with return receipt. The Dutch Central Authority is the public prosecutor in The Hague (Officier van Justitie, Arrondissementsparket Den Haag).

### Blue Card holders and dual citizens

A Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holder keeps the rights listed in Article 28 of the Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901 but is not a Turkish citizen. Article 25/a expressly refers to Turkish citizens, and Germany's declaration limits consular service to nationals of the sending state. In my view service on a Blue Card holder should therefore go through the Central Authority; no provision deals with this case expressly. A Turkish-German dual citizen is a Turkish citizen for Article 25/a. How Germany treats consular service on such a person is a question of German law.

## Translation and costs

The document and the sealed envelope with the proof-of-service form are translated into the language of the receiving country or the language set by treaty. If a party supplies the documents, the translation is done before a notary (Article 40 of the Regulation). At the start of each calendar year the Ministry of Justice publishes in the Official Gazette how much has to be advanced for service in each country (Article 39(3) of the Regulation). The court or enforcement office handling the file can tell you the current amount.

Not paying has consequences. Paying the cost advance is a procedural requirement (Article 114(1)(g) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). If the advance runs short, the court sets a final two-week deadline (Article 120(2) HMK), and if it is not met the claim is dismissed on procedural grounds (Article 115(2) HMK). For documents other than the statement of claim, failure to pay means the request is treated as withdrawn (Article 34(2) Tebligat Kanunu; Article 8(2) of the Regulation).

## How long does service from Turkey take?

The law sets no time frame. The only rule concerns hearing dates: documents that name a specific day must reach the ministry at least three months before that day (Article 41 of the Regulation). In the practical information Germany has given the HCCH, service is usually completed within three months, including shipping; the Netherlands states an average of two to three months. These figures cover only the receiving country. Preparation, translation and correspondence in Turkey come on top.

Since 31 July 2026, Article 147(3) HMK, added by Law No. 7589 of 16 July 2026, provides that no more than three months may pass between two hearings. In compelling cases, such as a lengthy expert examination or taking evidence by letter rogatory, the judge may set a longer interval with reasons. Service abroad is not among the examples. My view is that it can also count as a compelling case; I have not found a binding court decision on this.

## What if service does not come back?

Under Article 15(1) of the Convention, where a writ of summons had to be sent abroad and the defendant has not appeared, judgment may not be given until it is established that the document was properly served in sufficient time to allow a defence. Because of Turkey's declaration, a Turkish court may give judgment without a certificate of service if all three conditions of Article 15(2) are met:

1. The document was sent by one of the methods provided for in the Convention.
2. At least six months, considered adequate by the judge in that case, have passed since it was sent.
3. No certificate has been received despite every reasonable effort through the competent authorities.

A defendant against whom such a judgment is given may be relieved from the expiry of the time for appeal if, without fault, he or she did not learn of the document in time and has a prima facie defence on the merits (Article 16). Under Turkey's declaration, the application is barred one year after the date of the judgment. Turkish procedural law does not say how this is to be done. In my view the obvious route is restoration of a missed time limit (eski hâle getirme, Articles 95–96 HMK).

**Unknown address: service by publication.** If service fails and no residence, home or workplace can be found, the address is treated as unknown. The court first asks public and private bodies and, if needed, the police (Article 28 Tebligat Kanunu; Article 48 of the Regulation). Service by publication (ilanen tebligat) is the last resort (Article 48(4) of the Regulation). The notice is published in a newspaper, on a news website and on the Press Advertising Agency's (Basın İlân Kurumu) portal; a second notice may also appear in a foreign newspaper (Article 29). For someone living abroad, the court also sends the documents by registered mail with return receipt to the last known foreign address (Article 28, last paragraph). Service is deemed made seven days after the last notice; the court can extend this to no more than 15 days (Article 31).

## Electronic service and an address in Turkey

Individuals outside the mandatory groups, such as lawyers and companies, receive an electronic service address if they ask for one, and from then on service must be electronic (Article 7/a(2) Tebligat Kanunu). The law does not require residence in Turkey. You can apply through e-Devlet (Article 7(3) of the Regulation on Electronic Service). If you have such an address, documents are served there even while you live abroad and are deemed served at the end of the fifth day after they arrive (Article 7/a).

As of November 2026, no amendment to the Notification Law requiring every citizen to have an electronic address has been published in the Official Gazette. The change made by Law No. 7587 on 1 July 2026 concerns only Article 107/A of the Tax Procedure Law (Vergi Usul Kanunu), that is, tax notifications.

If you defend the case, your statement of defence must give an address in Turkey for the proceedings (Article 129(1)(b) HMK). If a Turkish citizen was once served at a foreign address and moves without reporting the new one, service is deemed made 30 days after documented delivery of the consular notice to the old address (Article 35, last paragraph, Tebligat Kanunu).

## If the Turkish judgment is to be used in Germany

Under section 328(1) no. 2 of the German Code of Civil Procedure (ZPO), a foreign judgment is not recognised if the defendant did not enter an appearance, relies on this, and was not served with the document instituting the proceedings properly or in time to defend. Turkish law applies the same idea to the enforcement of foreign judgments ([Article 54 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-54-mohuk-enforcement-conditions-turkey)). Whether a Turkish judgment is recognised in Germany or the Netherlands is a question of that country's law, and you should ask a lawyer qualified there. For Turkish enforcement matters, see [enforcement and insolvency law](/en/practice-areas/enforcement-insolvency).

## Frequently asked questions

### How is service made from Turkey to Germany?

Through the competent authority under the Hague Service Convention (Article 25 Tebligat Kanunu) or, for Turkish citizens, through the embassy or consulate (Article 25/a). For formal service Germany requires a German translation.

### How long does service from Turkey take?

The law sets no period. Germany tells the HCCH that its part usually takes up to three months; the time in Turkey comes on top. Documents with a hearing date go to the ministry at least three months in advance (Article 41 of the Regulation).

### Can a Turkish court decide without service?

Only under Article 15(2) of the Convention: transmission by a Convention method, at least six months' wait and reasonable efforts without result. If the address is unknown, service is by publication (Articles 28–31 Tebligat Kanunu).

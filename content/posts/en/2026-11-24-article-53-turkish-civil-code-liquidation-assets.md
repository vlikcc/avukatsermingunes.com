Article 53 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) decides which rules apply when the assets of a legal person that has come to an end are liquidated. A special provision of law comes first, then the founding document; if both are silent, the rules on the official liquidation of an estate (terekenin resmî tasfiyesi) apply. This concerns board members of a dissolved association or foundation and anyone with a claim against it.

## Article 53 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Liquidation of assets**
>
> Article 53 – Unless the law and the founding document provide otherwise, the assets of a legal person are liquidated in accordance with the provisions on the official liquidation of an estate.

Turkish original:

> **II. Malvarlığının tasfiyesi**
>
> Madde 53- Tüzel kişinin malvarlığının tasfiyesi, kanunda ve kuruluş belgesinde aksine hüküm bulunmadıkça, terekenin resmî tasfiyesine ilişkin hükümlere göre yapılır.

As of November 2026, Article 53 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The article replaces Article 51 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The old text referred to the rules for cooperatives; the new one follows the 1984 preliminary draft and uses the official liquidation of an estate instead. The reasoning makes two points explicit: the reference is not to Articles 632 and 633 TMK, which say when official liquidation may be requested, but to the procedure in Articles 634–636; and the judge applies these rules by analogy, not directly. Because allocating the assets can only come after liquidation, this article was placed before the one on allocation (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Special law and founding document first

For associations, the special rule is Article 15 of Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu): the money, property and rights of an association dissolved by its general assembly, or found to have ended automatically, are liquidated according to the principles in its statutes. The statutes must also state how the assets will be liquidated on dissolution (Art. 4(l) Law No. 5253). Where the statutes deal with liquidation adequately, the fallback rule in Article 53 TMK is not needed.

For foundations, the founding document is the foundation deed (vakıf senedi). Where the assets left after paying the debts go is also governed by Article 27 of Foundations Law No. 5737.

Commercial companies fall outside this article. Article 55 TMK reserves the laws that apply to them; the liquidation of a joint-stock company, for example, has its own rules in Articles 533 et seq. of the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 (TTK).

### Fallback: official liquidation of an estate

If the law and the founding document are silent, Articles 634–636 TMK apply by analogy:

- Liquidation is carried out by the civil court of peace (sulh mahkemesi) or by one or more liquidators it appoints; a liquidator works under the court's supervision (Art. 634(1) and (4)).
- It starts with an inventory; at the same time, a public notice asks creditors and debtors to declare their claims and debts within the period set (Art. 634(2)).
- Acts done or planned by the liquidator can be challenged by a written complaint to the court within seven days of learning of them (Art. 634(4)).
- Ordinary liquidation covers finishing pending business, paying debts, collecting receivables and, where necessary, selling assets (Art. 635).
- If the assets do not cover the debts, the civil court of peace liquidates under the bankruptcy rules (Art. 636).

These rules were written for heirs and estates. The law does not spell out how they are adapted to an association; for example, the wording does not say who, in an association, holds the right of complaint that Article 634(4) gives to heirs. There is no express statutory rule on this and no binding court decision verified for this post; the reasoning's word "by analogy" leaves the adaptation to the judge. This is the author's assessment, and how a court will adapt the rules in a given case is not certain.

For the limited continuation of personality during liquidation, see the post on [Article 52 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-52-turkish-civil-code-dissolved-association-liquidation); for who receives the remaining assets, see the post on [Article 54 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-54-turkish-civil-code-assets-after-dissolution).

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you have a claim against a dissolved Turkish association or foundation, find out whether liquidation follows the statutes or the official liquidation rules. In the second case, creditors are called by public notice and declare their claims within the period stated in it (Art. 634(2) TMK). In inheritance law, official liquidation is an option an heir can choose instead of renouncing the estate or accepting it under a public inventory (Art. 632(1) TMK); on renunciation, see [this post](/en/publications/renouncing-inheritance-in-turkey) and the [inheritance law page](/en/practice-areas/inheritance-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which rules govern the liquidation of a dissolved association in Türkiye?

First Article 15 of Law No. 5253 and the statutes. If they are silent, the rules on the official liquidation of an estate (Articles 634–636 TMK) apply by analogy (Art. 53 TMK).

### What happens if the association's debts exceed its assets?

Under the official liquidation rules, the civil court of peace then liquidates under the bankruptcy rules (Art. 636 TMK).

Under Turkish law, once the divorce becomes final a woman takes back her pre-marriage surname by operation of law; no application is needed (Article 173(1) of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). If she wants to keep her ex-husband's surname, she needs the court's permission and must prove that she has an interest in it and that it will not harm him (Article 173(2) TMK). This post explains when the surname changes in the Turkish registry, how the request to keep the name works, and what applies if you divorced in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere abroad. Current as of November 2026.

## Does the surname change automatically after divorce?

Yes. Under Article 173(1) TMK, a divorced woman keeps the personal status acquired through marriage but takes back her former surname. This is a legal consequence of the divorce.

For the change to appear in the Turkish civil registry (nüfus kaydı), the judgment must be final. The court registry must notify the registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) of decisions requiring registration within ten days of the finality record (Article 55 of the Civil Registry Services Law No. 5490). The registry office registers documents from other authorities within seven days of receipt (Article 54). When the new surname shows up in e-Devlet therefore depends on the date of finality and that notification. ID cards and passports are not renewed automatically; you apply at the registry office or, abroad, at the consulate.

If the woman was widowed before the marriage and her previous surname came from that earlier marriage, she may ask the court for permission to use her maiden name (Article 173(1), last sentence).

## Keeping your ex-husband's surname: the conditions

Article 173(2) TMK requires two things:

1. The woman has an **interest** in using her former husband's surname.
2. It is proven that this will **not harm** him.

Both are required, and the wording ("ispatlanırsa", "if it is proven") puts the burden of proof on the woman. If both are proven, the court grants permission.

The law does not define "interest"; the court decides case by case. We do not give examples from Court of Cassation case law here, because the current case law could not be checked in an official database for this post.

### Which court?

Divorce and its consequences are in Book Two of the TMK (family law), and family courts (aile mahkemesi) hear those cases (Article 4(1)(1) of Law No. 4787). Where no family court exists, the civil court of first instance designated by the Council of Judges and Prosecutors hears them (Article 2(2) of Law No. 4787). Article 173 does not expressly say whether the request must be made within the divorce case or in a separate case, nor does it set a time limit.

### Can the permission be withdrawn?

Yes. If circumstances change, the former husband may ask for the permission to be withdrawn (Article 173(3) TMK).

## Returning to your former surname later

A woman who used her ex-husband's surname with permission and later wants her pre-marriage surname back does not need a court. A written request is enough; the registry office makes the change (Additional Article 3(1)(a) of Law No. 5490).

Article 187 TMK, on the surname of a married woman, was annulled by the Constitutional Court's decision of 22 February 2023 (E. 2022/155, K. 2023/38), effective 28 January 2024. As of November 2026, the TMK amendment table on mevzuat.gov.tr shows no new law on that article. Surnames during marriage are outside this post; Article 173 on the effects of divorce was not affected by the annulment.

## Do the children's surnames change?

Article 173 only covers the woman's surname; it does not provide for any change to the children's surname because of the divorce. Changing a first name or surname is a non-contentious court matter and requires a court decision (Article 382(2)(a)(2) HMK). More: [How to change your name in Turkey](/en/publications/how-to-change-your-name-in-turkey).

## Divorced abroad: how does the surname reach the Turkish registry?

A foreign divorce decision can reach the Turkish registry in two ways:

- **Administrative registration.** Decisions of foreign courts or authorities are registered directly if both spouses apply together (or the Turkish spouse alone where the other has died or is a foreign national), the decision was issued by the competent authority under that state's law and is formally final, and it is not manifestly contrary to Turkish public policy (Article 27/A(1) of Law No. 5490). Abroad, the consulate carries out the registration (Article 27/A(2)).
- **Recognition case.** If the application is refused because these conditions are not met, recognition takes place in Turkey under the Private International Law Act No. 5718 (MÖHUK) (Article 27/A(3)). See also: [Uncontested divorce in Turkey when living abroad](/en/publications/uncontested-divorce-turkey-living-abroad).

Until the divorce is registered, you still appear as married in the Turkish registry, and your surname there does not change either.

**A disputed point.** Under German law, for example, a divorced spouse keeps the married name (§ 1355(5) BGB). Whether the surname of a Turkish citizen in the Turkish registry follows that foreign-law name or Article 173(1) TMK when the foreign divorce is registered is not expressly regulated, and no binding court decision could be verified for this post. Which interpretation the authority and the court will take in a given case is uncertain. In the author's assessment, if you want to keep your ex-husband's surname in the Turkish registry too, you should look at this question separately before applying for registration or recognition. For your surname under German or Dutch law, ask the local registry office (Standesamt, gemeente) or a lawyer admitted in that country.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to change my surname after divorce in Turkey?

The woman takes back her pre-marriage surname by law (Article 173(1) TMK). Keeping her ex-husband's surname requires court permission (Article 173(2)).

### Which court decides on keeping the surname?

The family court (Article 4(1)(1) of Law No. 4787); where there is none, the designated civil court of first instance (Article 2(2)).

### When does the new surname appear in e-Devlet?

After finality, the court notifies the registry office within ten days (Article 55 of Law No. 5490), which registers it within seven days (Article 54).

### Can I stop using my ex-husband's surname later?

Yes, by written request to the registry office (Additional Article 3(1)(a) of Law No. 5490).

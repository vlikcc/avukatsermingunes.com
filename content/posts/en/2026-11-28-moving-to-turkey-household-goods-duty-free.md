If you have lived abroad for at least 24 months and are moving your residence to Turkey for good, you can bring your used household goods in without paying Turkish customs duties (Article 167(5)(b) of Customs Law No. 4458). The goods have to be declared before you arrive, together with you, or within six months after your arrival, and you hand customs a residence transfer certificate (ikamet nakil belgesi) issued by the Turkish consulate.

The rules for moving household goods to Turkey are spread over three texts: the Customs Law itself, the Decision on the Application of Certain Articles of Customs Law No. 4458 (Council of Ministers Decision No. 2009/15481, “the Decision” below) and the Customs Exemption Communiqué Serial No. 2 (Gümrük Muafiyeti Tebliği, “the Communiqué” below). This overview reflects these texts as of November 2026 and covers the Turkish side only.

## Who can bring household goods to Turkey duty-free

Article 10(1) of the Communiqué lists four groups:

- people who move their residence permanently to Turkey after living outside the Turkish customs territory for at least 24 months,
- public servants returning from a permanent posting abroad,
- people who move to Turkey by acquiring Turkish citizenship,
- heirs living in Turkey of a person who was resident abroad and has died.

If you worked in Germany or the Netherlands and are retiring to Turkey, you are in the first group. “Moving your residence” means cutting your ties to your home and work abroad and coming to Turkey to settle there (Article 3(1)(aa) of the Decision). Someone who comes on holiday, or spends part of the year in Turkey, cannot use this exemption.

The exemption belongs to the family unit (Article 49(1) of the Decision), meaning husband and wife and their children under 18 (Article 3(1)(a)). Customs establishes the family unit from the civil registry extract with events (vukuatlı nüfus kayıt örneği) (Article 11(1)(a) of the Communiqué) and checks in its BİLGE system whether you or another member of the family unit has already used the right (Article 16(1)).

## 24 months abroad and days spent in Turkey

Article 48(1)(a) of the Decision requires at least 24 months of “continuous” residence abroad. Article 11(1)(b) of the Communiqué spells this out:

- you must not have stayed in Turkey for more than six months in any calendar year;
- stays in Turkey of up to 45 days in a calendar year count as time abroad;
- if you lived abroad for at least five of the last ten years, “this time requirement relating to residence” does not apply.

Customs sees your entries and exits in its own electronic records (Article 11(1)(c)). If you have spent long periods in Turkey in recent years, check your entry and exit dates beforehand, for example through e-Devlet.

The text does not make clear how far the five-year exception goes. The sentence comes straight after the six-months-per-calendar-year rule. In our view it removes at least that rule; whether it also removes the 24-month requirement is open to argument. This is our reading, not settled law, so in a borderline case ask the customs office where you plan to declare the goods before you move.

Once you have used the exemption, you need another 24 months of residence abroad before you can use it again (Article 11(2)).

### Deadline for the goods: before, with you, or within six months

The customs declaration for the household goods is made before your arrival, together with you, or within six months after your arrival (Article 49(3) of the Decision, Article 13(1)(a) of the Communiqué). Within that period you may bring the goods in several consignments; this does not count as using the right twice (Article 16(2)).

In cases of force majeure or unforeseen circumstances (for example an accident, illness or unexpected changes in transport conditions, Article 111 of the Decision), the regional directorate of customs and trade can extend the deadline once by up to half its length (Article 40(1) of the Communiqué). For a six-month period that means three months. The extension is not automatic; you have to apply and support your request with documents.

## Dual nationals and Blue Card holders

For Turks living in Europe this distinction matters:

- **Dual nationals** can use the household goods exemption (second sentence of Article 12(2) of the Communiqué).
- People who gave up Turkish citizenship **with a release permit (çıkma izni)** and took another nationality, which covers most [Blue Card](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights) holders, cannot use it (first sentence of Article 12(2)).

Article 48 of the Decision speaks of “natural persons” and has no such restriction; it comes from the Communiqué. Customs offices apply the Communiqué. Whether this rule is consistent with the Decision can be questioned, but there is no express statutory provision and no binding court decision on the point that we know of. If you are returning as a Blue Card holder, expect that the exemption may be refused. If you have [regained Turkish citizenship](/en/publications/regain-turkish-citizenship), have your position checked before you apply to customs; the Communiqué does not deal with this case separately.

## Which goods are covered and which are not

Household goods are items, furniture and similar things intended for personal use in the home or for household needs (Article 3(1)(ğ) of the Decision). The Law and the Decision refer to “used” household goods; new items are not covered by this exemption.

You must use the goods for the same purpose in your home in Turkey (Article 49(2)). Equipment for a trade or profession is excluded, apart from portable instruments and devices (Article 49(4)). Workshop machinery or shop stock cannot come in this way.

A car falls under a separate exemption with different conditions: it must have been registered in your name abroad at least six months before your entry, and must not be more than three years old, counted from the year it was registered in your name and including the model year (Article 47(1) of the Decision). Since 11 April 2023, Article 6(7) of the Communiqué excludes dual nationals from the vehicle exemption. Other taxes on vehicles are outside the scope of this article.

## Documents for Turkish customs

You, your spouse, or a person authorised to represent you under Article 225 of the Customs Law can file the application (Article 14(1) of the Communiqué). For a change of residence you need:

1. the residence transfer certificate (ikamet nakil belgesi),
2. the civil registry extract with events (vukuatlı nüfus kayıt örneği),
3. a list of the goods, attached to the oral declaration form.

The residence transfer certificate is issued by the Turkish mission in the country where you live; Article 8 of the Communiqué says so expressly for vehicles, and for household goods the same certificate is obtained from the consulate. For appointments and any additional documents the consulate asks for, see your consulate's page via [konsolosluk.gov.tr](https://www.konsolosluk.gov.tr). The oral declaration form is completed electronically (Article 39), and household goods can be cleared at any authorised customs office (Article 38(5)).

A list drawn up room by room and item by item makes clearance easier. Customs decides on the basis of the list and the inspection whether the goods are used household goods.

## One-year ban on selling or transferring

Household goods imported duty-free may not, without permission from customs, be sold, transferred, rented out, lent, given as security to anyone who is not entitled to the exemption, or used for another purpose (Article 41(1) of the Communiqué). For household goods this ban lasts **one year** (Article 107(4)(a) of the Decision). If it is breached, the customs duties are collected; if documents submitted turn out to be false, the customs office files a criminal complaint (Article 41(2) and (7)).

## Inherited household goods

If a relative who lived abroad has died and you live in Turkey, you can bring in their personal effects and household goods without customs duties (Article 53(1) of the Decision). The deadline is 24 months from the date of the Turkish certificate of inheritance (veraset ilamı) (Article 13(1)(b) of the Communiqué). You need the certificate of inheritance, the civil registry extract, the list of goods and, if there are several heirs, notarised waivers from the other heirs in Turkey (Article 14(1)(ç)). How to obtain the certificate from abroad is explained in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey); for inheritance disputes see [inheritance law](/en/practice-areas/inheritance-law).

Export from Germany or the Netherlands is also subject to that country's own customs and export rules; the competent authorities there can advise you on them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I pay customs duty on household goods when moving to Turkey?

No, if you have lived abroad for at least 24 months, are moving your residence to Turkey for good, and declare your used household goods before, with, or within six months after your arrival (Article 167(5)(b) of the Customs Law, Articles 48–49 of the Decision).

### Where do I get the residence transfer certificate (ikamet nakil belgesi)?

From the Turkish consulate in the country where you live. Appointments and the document list are on konsolosluk.gov.tr.

### Can dual nationals bring household goods to Turkey duty-free?

Yes (Article 12(2) of the Communiqué). Blue Card holders who left Turkish citizenship with a release permit are excluded under the Communiqué.

### Can I ship my household goods in several loads?

Yes, in several consignments within the six-month period; this does not count as a second use (Article 16(2) of the Communiqué).

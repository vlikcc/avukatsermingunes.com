Under Turkish law, an action to challenge paternity (soybağının reddi davası) can now be brought by the husband, the mother or the child (Article 286 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). The husband has one year from the date he learns of the birth and that he is not the father. For the mother the one-year limit runs from the birth, for the child from the day they come of age (Article 289 TMK).

The mother's right is new. It comes from Law No. 7531, in force since 14 November 2024. I checked the provisions against the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr in November 2026. The last law in the TMK's amendment list is Law No. 7589 (31 October 2026); the last amendment to Articles 285–291 was Law No. 7531.

## What a paternity challenge removes

The father of a child born during the marriage, or within three hundred days after it ends, is the husband (Article 285(1) TMK). This is a presumption (babalık karinesi). The Turkish civil register shows the husband as the father, with no acknowledgement or judgment needed. If he is not the biological father, that legal tie ends only through a paternity challenge. An agreement between the spouses, or the husband saying the child is not his, does not change the register entry.

If the parents were not married and the father acknowledged the child, a different route applies: annulment of the acknowledgement (tanımanın iptali) under Articles 297–300 TMK, with its own time limits.

If paternity is to be established with the biological father, the tie to the husband has to go first; only then can acknowledgement or a paternity suit follow. Where the child already has a legal father, the one-year limit for a paternity suit starts only when that tie ends (Article 303(3) TMK). The paternity suit itself is covered in [Paternity suit in Turkey: who can file, time limit, court](/en/publications/paternity-suit-turkey-time-limit-court).

## Who can challenge paternity? Can the mother?

Unofficial translation of Article 286 TMK as it stands today:

> The husband, the mother or the child may rebut the presumption of paternity by bringing an action to deny paternity. The action is brought against the other persons entitled to bring it.

Turkish text:

> Koca, ana veya çocuk soybağının reddi davasını açarak babalık karinesini çürütebilir. Bu dava, dava açma hakkına sahip diğer kişilere karşı açılır.

**Can the mother sue?** Yes. The original text gave the right only to the husband and the child. The Ankara 18th Family Court referred the rule to the Constitutional Court in a case brought by a mother. The Court struck down Article 286(1) as contrary to Articles 20 and 40 of the Constitution (AYM, E. 2023/37, K. 2023/140, decision of 26 July 2023; Official Gazette of 20 October 2023, No. 32345). Its reasoning: a claim brought by a guardian ad litem (kayyım) for the child does not protect the mother's private life, because the guardian acts in the child's name and in the child's interest. The annulment was set to take effect nine months after publication. The new rule came only afterwards: Law No. 7531, which gives the mother an express right to sue, was published on 14 November 2024 and took effect the same day.

The defendants are the other persons entitled to sue. A husband's claim is brought against the mother and the child; a mother's claim against the husband and the child; a child's claim against the mother and the husband.

If the husband dies before his time limit runs out, is declared missing, or permanently loses mental capacity, the man who claims to be the father, the husband's descendants, and the husband's mother or father may also bring the claim (Article 291(1) TMK, as amended by Law No. 7531). Outside these cases the biological father has no right to sue.

For a minor child, the claim is brought by a guardian ad litem appointed for the child (Article 291(2) TMK). The Constitutional Court noted in the same decision that an application can be made to have one appointed.

## Time limits: a deadline that extinguishes the right

Article 289 TMK, headed "time limits that extinguish the right" (hak düşürücü süreler), reads in unofficial translation:

> The husband must bring the action within one year, (…) from the date he learns of the birth and that he is not the father, or that the mother had sexual relations with another man at the time she conceived.
>
> The mother must bring the action within one year from the birth at the latest, and the child within one year from coming of age at the latest.
>
> If the delay is based on a justified reason, the one-year period starts on the date that reason ceases.

Turkish text:

> Koca, davayı, doğumu ve baba olmadığını veya ananın gebe kaldığı sırada başka bir erkek ile cinsel ilişkide bulunduğunu öğrendiği tarihten başlayarak bir yıl, (…) içinde açmak zorundadır.
>
> Ana doğumdan, çocuk ise ergin olduğu tarihten başlayarak en geç bir yıl içinde dava açmak zorundadır.
>
> Gecikme haklı bir sebebe dayanıyorsa, bir yıllık süre bu sebebin ortadan kalktığı tarihte işlemeye başlar.

Where the "(…)" now stands, the text used to say "and in any case five years from the birth". The Constitutional Court struck those words down on 25 June 2009 (E. 2008/30, K. 2009/96). For the husband, only the date of knowledge counts; there is no outer limit running from the birth.

The limits at a glance:

- **Husband:** one year from learning of the birth and that he is not the father (or of the mother's relations with another man when she conceived).
- **Mother:** one year from the birth.
- **Child:** one year from coming of age, which is the eighteenth birthday (Article 11 TMK).
- **Guardian ad litem of a minor child:** one year from service of the appointment decision (Article 291(2) TMK). The five-year cap that stood there was struck down by the Constitutional Court on 10 October 2013 (E. 2013/62, K. 2013/115).
- **Others after the husband's death:** one year from learning of the birth and of the death, permanent incapacity or declaration of absence (Article 291(1) TMK).

People often search for a "limitation period" (zamanaşımı), but that label is not accurate: the legislative reasoning calls these deadlines that extinguish the right, and once they pass the right to sue is gone. If the delay rests on a justified reason, the year starts when that reason ends. The law does not list what counts as a justified reason; according to the reasoning, the court decides.

### Children born before 14 November 2024

The transitional provision of Law No. 7531 deals only with the husband's time limit. If the child was born more than a year before 14 November 2024, the mother's one-year limit seems to have expired before she was even allowed to sue. This point is disputed. The law has no express rule on it, and I have not found a binding court decision in the official databases that settles it. What follows is my own assessment, and it is not certain which approach a court would take in a given case. In my view the mother can argue that being unable to sue until the law gave her the right is a justified reason under Article 289(3) TMK. If the court holds that her time limit runs from the birth in every case, her claim will be dismissed. For that reason a claim in the child's name through a guardian ad litem should also be considered.

## Proof and DNA testing

If the child was conceived during the marriage, the claimant must prove that the husband is not the father (Article 287(1) TMK). A child born at least 180 days after the wedding and at most 300 days after the marriage ends is treated as conceived during the marriage (Article 287(2)). If the child was conceived before the marriage or while the spouses lived apart, the claimant needs no further evidence. If there is convincing evidence that the spouses had sexual relations during the conception period, though, the presumption stands (Article 288).

The judge investigates the facts of their own motion. Parties and third persons must consent to examinations that are necessary and pose no risk to their health (Article 284 TMK). Under Article 292 of the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK), everyone must, on these conditions, allow blood or tissue to be taken to establish parentage; without a justified reason the judge orders the test to be carried out by force. Parentage cases are heard during the judicial recess too (Article 103(1)(b) HMK).

## Competent court and the statement of claim

The family court (aile mahkemesi) has subject-matter jurisdiction (Article 4(1)(1) of Law No. 4787); where none exists, a civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) designated under the law hears the case (Article 2(2)). Territorial jurisdiction follows Article 283 TMK: the court of the domicile of either party at the time of the claim or of the birth. Even if you live abroad, a party living in Turkey or a Turkish domicile at the time of the birth can establish jurisdiction.

The statement of claim must contain what Article 119 HMK requires: the court, the parties' names and addresses, a numbered summary of the facts, the evidence for each fact, the legal grounds and a clear request. In a paternity challenge it should also show when the time limit began (knowledge, birth, coming of age or service on the guardian).

## Living abroad: applicable law, jurisdiction, recognition

**Applicable law.** The challenge to parentage is governed by the law under which the parentage was established (Article 16(2) of the Turkish Private International Law Act No. 5718, MÖHUK). If the child's national law at birth was Turkish law, the parentage arose under Turkish law and the challenge follows Articles 286–291 TMK. More on this in the post on [Article 16 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-16-mohuk-parentage-paternity-turkey).

**International jurisdiction of Turkish courts.** It follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 MÖHUK), here Article 283 TMK. If no party is domiciled in Turkey, Article 41 MÖHUK provides that personal status cases of Turkish citizens, if not or not able to be brought abroad, are heard at the place of residence, at the last domicile in Turkey, or in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir. Treating a paternity challenge as a personal status case in this sense is my own assessment; the law does not list these cases expressly.

**A judgment from Germany or the Netherlands.** A paternity challenge decided there is not entered in the Turkish civil register automatically. The direct registration route under Article 27/A of the Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490 covers only divorce, nullity and annulment of marriage and findings on whether a marriage exists. A parentage judgment needs recognition (tanıma) by a Turkish court (Article 58 MÖHUK), and the family court hears that case too (Article 4(1)(2) of Law No. 4787). The conditions are explained in the post on [Article 58 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-mohuk-recognition-foreign-judgments-turkey). A Turkish judgment is reported to the civil registry by the court registry within ten days after it becomes final (Article 55(1) of Law No. 5490).

In German law, challenging paternity (Vaterschaftsanfechtung) is governed by sections 1600 et seq. BGB; in Dutch law, denial of paternity (ontkenning van het vaderschap) is in Book 1 of the Burgerlijk Wetboek. Who may sue there, which time limits apply and which court is competent are questions of that country's law; for those, please contact a lawyer qualified in that country. Other family law topics are on the [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can challenge paternity under Turkish law?

The husband, the mother and the child (Article 286 TMK). If the husband died, was declared missing or permanently lost capacity before his time limit ran out, also the man claiming to be the father and the husband's descendants, mother or father (Article 291(1)).

### Can the mother challenge paternity in Turkey?

Yes, since 14 November 2024 (Law No. 7531). Her time limit is one year from the birth (Article 289(2) TMK).

### What is the time limit to challenge paternity in Turkey?

One year: for the husband from knowledge, for the mother from the birth, for the child from coming of age, for the guardian from service of the appointment. With a justified reason, the year starts when the reason ends (Article 289(3) TMK).

### Which Turkish court hears the case?

The family court at the domicile of either party at the time of the claim or of the birth (Article 283 TMK). If nobody lives in Turkey, Article 41 MÖHUK applies.

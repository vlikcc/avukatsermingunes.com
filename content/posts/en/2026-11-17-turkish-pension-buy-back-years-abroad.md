Foreign service borçlanma (yurt dışı borçlanması) lets you count the time you spent abroad as a Turkish citizen, working, unemployed or as a housewife, towards your Turkish social security record by paying an amount to the Social Security Institution (Sosyal Güvenlik Kurumu, SGK). The legal basis is Law No. 3201 on the evaluation of periods spent abroad by Turkish citizens for social security purposes. You apply to the SGK, you pay within three months of being notified of the amount, and a pension is granted only once your work abroad has ended.

This article covers the Turkish side: who can buy back time, which periods count, how the amount is calculated and what the law says about the link to a German or Dutch pension. It reflects the law as of November 2026. The last amendment to the Law was made by Law No. 7247 (in force since 26 June 2020); no amendment to Law No. 3201 was found for 2024 to 2026.

## Who can use foreign service borçlanma?

Article 1 of Law No. 3201 names two groups: Turkish citizens, and people who were Turkish by birth and gave up Turkish citizenship with a release permit (çıkma izni). Dual citizens are covered because they are Turkish citizens. If the insured person died while working abroad or after returning for good, surviving dependants who are Turkish citizens can also apply (Art. 3).

You cannot use it if you or your dependants already receive a pension under the social security laws, or if you applied for a pension and are already entitled to it (Art. 8). People who receive a partial Turkish pension under a social security agreement have a separate rule (see below).

### Can Blue Card holders buy back time?

Yes, but only for periods when they were still Turkish citizens. The Law includes people released from citizenship, but it covers only periods spent abroad "as a Turkish citizen" (Art. 1). The implementing regulation (Yurtdışında Geçen Sürelerin Borçlandırılması ve Değerlendirilmesine İlişkin Yönetmelik, Art. 6) also excludes periods after Turkish citizenship was lost. If you took German or Dutch citizenship with a release permit, the work after your release from Turkish citizenship cannot be bought back.

According to the SGK information page, a Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holder can buy back his or her own periods. A dependant who holds a Blue Card cannot, because that person is not a Turkish citizen on the date of application. For the other rights of the card: [Blue Card (Mavi Kart): rights in Turkey](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights)

## Which periods can be bought back?

Under Article 1, the following periods can be bought back if they were spent abroad as a Turkish citizen after turning 18 and can be documented:

- insurance periods,
- periods of unemployment between or after those periods, up to one year each,
- periods abroad as a housewife (ev kadını).

You do not have to buy back all of them. Since the 2020 amendment, the regulation requires anyone buying back only part of the time to state the number of days. Periods before age 18, before acquiring or after losing Turkish citizenship, and days that overlap with contributions already paid in Turkey are excluded.

Days abroad are counted backwards from the last date in the documents; a year counts as 360 days and a month as 30 (Art. 5(1)).

## Where do you apply and which documents are needed?

You apply to the SGK (Art. 3). According to the SGK page, you file a signed application with the SGK provincial directorate or social security centre for your address; from abroad you can send it by post. Applications by email or fax are not accepted. Online through e-Devlet is possible only if your documents are already in the SGK's system or if you want to convert a partial pension into a full one. An attorney-in-fact needs a power of attorney that expressly authorises borçlanma.

Turkey has social security agreements with Germany and with the Netherlands. For agreement countries the SGK accepts a service record from the foreign insurance institution (with a sworn Turkish translation) or a service certificate from a Turkish consulate or embassy. For housewife periods it asks for a residence certificate and the entry and exit pages of your passport, or an entry-exit record from the Turkish police.

## How much does borçlanma cost in 2026? Amount and deadline

Under Article 4, the amount for each day is **45%** of a daily earnings figure that you choose between the lower and upper limits of daily earnings subject to contributions under Article 82 of Law No. 5510 on the date of application. The calculation is: chosen daily earnings × 45% = cost per day; cost per day × number of days = total debt.

According to the figures published by the SGK for 2026, the daily earnings limits are 1,101.00 TL and 9,909.00 TL. For applications made in 2026, one day therefore costs at least 495.45 TL and at most 4,459.05 TL. What counts is the date of application; a later application uses the limits in force at that time.

You must pay **within three months** of notification. If you do not, you have to apply again (Art. 4(1)). According to the SGK page, you can pay in instalments within that period; the days credited correspond to the amount paid. From abroad, the amount stated in the notice can be paid in foreign currency through correspondent banks.

If you later withdraw, or you cannot meet the conditions for a pension, the amount you paid is refunded without interest on request (Art. 4(2)). Once a pension has been granted on the basis of the bought-back periods, withdrawal and refund are no longer possible.

## How does borçlanma affect your retirement date?

The days you buy back are added to your Turkish contribution days (Art. 5(2)). Your insurance start date is set as follows:

- If you have Turkish insurance and buy back periods before its start, the start date moves back by the number of days bought (Art. 5(2)).
- If you have no Turkish insurance, the start date is the date you paid in full, moved back by the number of days bought (Art. 5(3)).

Bought-back periods count as past insurance under the self-employed status in Article 4(1)(b) of Law No. 5510 (Art. 5(4), since 1 August 2019 under Law No. 7186). For people who applied before that date and paid on time, the earlier rules apply (provisional Art. 9).

The date you first worked in an agreement country does not count as your first entry into Turkish insurance, unless the relevant social security agreement contains a special provision to that effect (Art. 5(5)). Whether your agreement has such a provision is assessed by the SGK in your file.

Law No. 3201 itself does not require a minimum number of days. How many days you need for a Turkish pension, together with the age and insurance-period conditions, is set by Law No. 5510. Figures such as "3,600 days" refer to one of those conditions; ask the SGK to calculate your own number.

## When does the pension start? Do I have to live in Turkey?

A pension based on bought-back periods requires three things (Art. 6(A)): a final return (yurda kesin dönüş), full payment of the debt and a written request made after payment. The pension starts on the first day of the month after the written request.

The regulation defines the final return in Article 4 as the end of your work abroad. Neither the Law nor the regulation requires you to live in Turkey; what is required is that your work abroad has ended.

If, after the pension is granted, you start working abroad again under foreign law, or begin receiving a residence-based social insurance or social assistance benefit, the Turkish pension is stopped from that date (Art. 6(B)). When the work or benefit ends, it is paid again from the first day of the month after a written request. Short-term work as defined in the regulation does not stop the pension, even if it is compulsorily insured abroad (Art. 6(C), since 26 June 2020 under Law No. 7247). Whether a specific German or Dutch benefit is "residence-based" in this sense is decided by the SGK.

## Link to a German or Dutch pension

Law No. 3201 deals only with your Turkish insurance. The periods you buy back are not removed from your German or Dutch insurance record; the conditions and amount of a pension there follow the law of that country. The Law itself says two things on the subject:

- If you receive a partial Turkish pension under a social security agreement, you can have it converted into a full pension by paying the debt in full. The debt is based on the days in the foreign institution's service record that are enough for a full pension; on request you can buy back all the days (Art. 9).
- If contributions paid abroad are transferred to Turkey under an international agreement, the share relating to the bought-back periods is refunded in Turkish lira to the insured person, the dependants or the heirs (Art. 10).

How a Turkish pension affects your German or Dutch pension, tax or health insurance is a matter of that country's law. Ask the Deutsche Rentenversicherung in Germany or the SVB in the Netherlands, or a lawyer licensed there. Other topics: [Turkey and Germany](/en/turkiye-germany)

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does Turkish pension borçlanma cost per day in 2026?

For applications in 2026, at least 495.45 TL and at most 4,459.05 TL per day, which is 45% of the daily earnings you choose (Art. 4, Law No. 3201).

### Can a Blue Card holder buy back years abroad for a Turkish pension?

Yes, but only for periods when you were a Turkish citizen. Periods after your release from citizenship cannot be bought back.

### Do I have to move back to Turkey to get the pension?

The Law requires a final return; the regulation defines it as the end of your work abroad. Living in Turkey is not required. If you work abroad again, the pension is stopped (Art. 6(B)).

### Can housewife periods abroad be bought back?

Yes. Periods abroad as a housewife after age 18, as a Turkish citizen, can be bought back with a residence certificate and entry-exit records.

### What happens if I do not pay within three months?

You have to apply again (Art. 4(1)). The new application uses the limits in force on its date.

Article 6 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets the burden of proof (ispat yükü): unless the law provides otherwise, each party must prove the facts on which it bases its right. The rule decides who loses when a fact cannot be proven. It applies in every civil case before a Turkish court, whether the dispute concerns divorce, inheritance, land registry or a money claim. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 6 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **D. Rules of proof**
>
> **I. Burden of proof**
>
> Article 6 – Unless the law provides otherwise, each party must prove the existence of the facts on which it bases its right.

Turkish original:

> **D. İspat kuralları**
>
> **I. İspat yükü**
>
> Madde 6 - Kanunda aksine bir hüküm bulunmadıkça, taraflardan her biri, hakkını dayandırdığı olguların varlığını ispatla yükümlüdür.

The article has not changed since it entered into force. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in UYAP Mevzuat (recorded 31 July 2026) does not list Article 6; the last law in the table is Law No. 7589, which amended Articles 440 and 444 with effect from 31 October 2026.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 6 of the former Civil Code No. 743. It was taken, with partial changes, from Article 6 of the 1984 preliminary draft, and its headings were brought into modern Turkish (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Article 6 TMK and Article 190 HMK

How evidence is taken is governed today by the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (Hukuk Muhakemeleri Kanunu, HMK). Article 190(1) HMK states the same rule as Article 6 TMK in procedural terms: unless the law provides otherwise, the burden of proof lies with the party that derives a right in its favour from the legal consequence of the alleged fact.

The HMK also sets the practical limits. Only disputed facts need to be proven; facts that are common knowledge or admitted in court (ikrar) do not (Articles 187 and 188 HMK). The parties must state their facts concretely enough to be proven and say which evidence supports which fact (Article 194 HMK). A party that submits evidence to rebut the other side's claim (counter-proof) does not take on the burden of proof by doing so (Article 191 HMK).

### "Unless the law provides otherwise": presumptions

Some provisions set the burden themselves. Under the statutory regime of participation in acquired property, a person who claims that an asset belongs to one spouse must prove it; assets that cannot be shown to belong to either spouse are held in co-ownership; and all assets of a spouse are treated as acquired property until the contrary is proven (Article 222 TMK). The last rule is a statutory presumption (karine). Under Article 190(2) HMK, the party relying on it only has to prove the fact on which the presumption rests; the other side may, except where the law excludes this, prove the contrary.

### Documentary proof and the value limit

Who bears the burden and how a fact may be proven are separate questions. Under Article 200 HMK, legal transactions above a value limit must be proven by a written instrument (senet), and witness evidence is not allowed against such an instrument (Article 201 HMK). The text of the Code says TRY 2,500, but under Additional Article 1 HMK the limit is raised every year by the revaluation rate. Since 4 June 2025 (Law No. 7550), the limit in force on the date of the transaction applies. Please check the current figure in an official source.

Article 203 HMK lists the exceptions. Transactions within the family come first: between ascendants and descendants, siblings, spouses, parents-in-law and children-in-law, witnesses may be heard (Article 203(1)(a) HMK). Witnesses may also be heard where there is a beginning of written proof (delil başlangıcı) (Article 202 HMK).

As of November 2026, the HMK text in UYAP Mevzuat (recorded 31 July 2026) includes Law No. 7589. That law amended Articles 107, 109, 147, 149, 166, 168 and 362 HMK; the evidence provisions in Articles 187–224 were not touched.

## Practical points if you live abroad

**Living abroad does not change the burden of proof.** Article 6 TMK and Article 190 HMK make no distinction based on where a party lives. If you follow a case in Turkey from abroad, gather the documents your claim rests on early.

**Foreign public documents need certification.** Public documents issued by foreign authorities only have that status in Turkey if certified by the competent authority of the issuing state or by the Turkish consulate; international treaties to which Turkey is a party take precedence (Article 224 HMK). One of them is the Hague Apostille Convention of 5 October 1961. According to the Hague Conference status table, it has applied in Turkey since 29 September 1985, in Germany since 13 February 1966 and in the Netherlands since 8 October 1965. An apostille certifies the authenticity of the signature, the capacity of the signatory and, where relevant, the seal or stamp (Article 5 of the Convention); it does not certify that the content is correct. Article 224 HMK covers only public documents; the court assesses private documents, such as a statement from a private bank, under the general rules (Article 198 HMK).

**Translation is required.** A party relying on a document in a foreign language must also submit a translation; the court may ask for an official translation (Article 223 HMK). Under Article 199 HMK, e-mails and electronic data are documents (belge) too, so correspondence in English, German or Dutch also has to be translated.

**Money sent to family in Turkey.** Transfers to parents or siblings can later become an issue in inheritance and land registry cases. These transactions fall under the exception to documentary proof (Article 203(1)(a) HMK), so witnesses can be heard. The author's view: keeping bank transfer records is usually safer than relying on witnesses. The decision below also concerned money sent from Germany. More on this: [Inheritance law in Turkey: sham sale of property](/en/publications/inheritance-law-turkey-sham-sale-property).

**Witnesses abroad.** The court may order a witness to be heard from where they are by simultaneous audio and video transmission (Article 149(2) HMK); the details are set by regulation. Under Article 149(4) HMK, added by Law No. 7589 and in force since 31 October 2026, the rules on handwritten signatures do not apply to people who take part in a hearing this way, except for admission (ikrar), taking an oath, consent to withdrawal of the action, waiver of the claim, acceptance of the claim and settlement.

**If foreign law applies.** This point is disputed. Where a case with a foreign element is decided on the merits under foreign law, the Turkish Private International Law Act No. 5718 (MÖHUK) has no general rule on whether the burden of proof follows that foreign law or Turkish law, and no binding court decision on the question was found for this article. It is not certain which approach a court will take in a given case. The documents needed to use a foreign judgment in Turkey are governed separately: [Article 53 MÖHUK – documents and apostille](/en/publications/legislation/article-53-mohuk-documents-apostille-turkey).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 1. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2024/1842, K. 2025/2351, T. 30.04.2025**

In a claim to cancel a land registry transfer as a sham sale meant to bypass heirs (muris muvazaası), the defendants argued that they had worked in Germany, had sent money from there to buy a flat for the claimant, and that the deceased had transferred his share in the property to them in return. The first-instance court allowed the claim because the defendants had not proven that the transfer was made for consideration, and the regional court of appeal followed that view. The Chamber cited Article 6 TMK and Article 190(1) HMK, held that the burden of proving an intent to deprive the heirs lay with the claimant, and found that the claimant had not met it. The judgment was reversed unanimously.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who bears the burden of proof in Turkish civil proceedings?

Unless the law provides otherwise, the party that derives a right in its favour from a fact (Article 6 TMK, Article 190(1) HMK). A person claiming a debt proves that it arose; a person claiming to have paid proves the payment.

### Where is the burden of proof regulated in the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure?

In Article 190 HMK. Paragraph 2 deals with statutory presumptions and Article 191 HMK with counter-proof. The rules on admissible evidence start at Article 187 HMK.

### Is a document from Germany or the Netherlands accepted as evidence in Turkey?

A public document needs an apostille (Article 224 HMK, Hague Apostille Convention), and every document in a foreign language must be submitted with a translation (Article 223 HMK). The court assesses its weight.

### Can I prove money I sent to my parents with witnesses?

Yes. Between ascendants and descendants, witnesses may be heard even above the value limit (Article 203(1)(a) HMK). The court assesses the testimony freely (Article 198 HMK).

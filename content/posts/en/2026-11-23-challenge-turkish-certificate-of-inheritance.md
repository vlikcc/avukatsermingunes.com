There is no statutory deadline for challenging a Turkish certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi, commonly called veraset ilamı): under Article 598(3) of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK), its invalidity can be raised at any time. The action is brought by someone with a legal interest, such as an heir who is missing from the certificate or shown with the wrong share. The competent court follows from Article 11(3) of the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) and, if the deceased last lived abroad, from Article 43 MÖHUK.

Whether to obtain the certificate from a notary or a court is covered in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey). This article is about what you can do once a certificate has been issued and turns out to be wrong.

## Why a Turkish certificate of inheritance can be challenged at any time

The relevant paragraphs of Article 598 TMK read (unofficial translation):

> Persons who, upon their application, are determined to be statutory heirs shall be given a document showing their status as heirs by the civil court of peace or by a notary.
>
> The invalidity of the certificate of inheritance may be asserted at any time.

Turkish original:

> Başvurusu üzerine yasal mirasçı oldukları belirlenenlere, sulh mahkemesince veya noterlikçe mirasçılık sıfatlarını gösteren bir belge verilir.
>
> Mirasçılık belgesinin geçersizliği her zaman ileri sürülebilir.

Issuing the certificate is a non-contentious proceeding (çekişmesiz yargı, HMK Article 382(2)(c)(6)). Unless the law provides otherwise, such decisions do not have substantive res judicata effect (HMK Article 388). The certificate shows who the heirs are; it does not settle the question for good. It can be annulled even years later.

As of November 2026, Articles 598 and 637–639 TMK and Articles 11 and 382–388 HMK have not been amended in recent years, according to the amendment tables of the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr. The words "or by a notary" in Article 598(1) were added by Law No. 6217, in force since 1 October 2011.

## Why heirs living in Germany or the Netherlands are often missing

A notary identifies the heirs from the Turkish civil register (nüfus kaydı). If the register is not sufficient, the notary may not issue a certificate (Article 71/B of Notaries Law No. 1512). A court-issued certificate can also miss a marriage or birth that was never entered in the Turkish register. Typical situations:

- A second marriage in Germany was never reported to the Turkish register. The second spouse and any children of that marriage not registered in Turkey will be missing.
- A German divorce was not recognised and registered in Turkey. The former spouse still appears as spouse in the register and may be listed as an heir.
- A child who only holds German or Dutch nationality may not appear in the Turkish register at all. The TMK does not require any particular nationality for statutory heirs (Articles 495 et seq.); such a child is still an heir.

Being left out of the certificate does not take away anyone's inheritance rights. The way to correct the certificate is an action for annulment (iptal davası). In the author's assessment, completing the missing register entries before or during the proceedings makes proof easier. On birth registration, see [registering a child born abroad in Turkey](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad).

If an heir's name is merely misspelt, first check whether the register itself is correct. Whether a pure spelling error is fixed through an annulment action or in another way depends on the certificate in question; this article covers cases where an heir is missing entirely or a share is wrong.

## Who sues, and against whom?

Article 598(3) TMK does not say who may sue. The general rule applies: the claimant must have a legal interest in bringing the action (HMK Article 114(1)(h)). An heir who was left out, an heir shown with too small a share, and the heirs alongside whom a non-heir is listed all have that interest.

The law does not separately state who the defendants are. In the author's assessment, all heirs whose position would change under a new certificate should be party to the case. A new certificate can be requested together with the annulment; HMK Article 11(3) mentions both requests together.

## Which court has jurisdiction?

### Territorial jurisdiction: HMK Article 11(3) and MÖHUK Article 43

HMK Article 11(3) reads (unofficial translation): "In actions for the annulment of a certificate of inheritance and the issue of a new certificate of inheritance, the court of the place where each of the heirs resides is also competent." Turkish original:

> Mirasçılık belgesinin iptali ve yeni mirasçılık belgesi verilmesine ilişkin davalarda, mirasçıların her birinin oturduğu yer mahkemesi de yetkilidir.

The word "also" (de) indicates that, besides the court of the deceased's last domicile (HMK Article 11(1)), the courts where the heirs live may be chosen. Because the annulment action is not expressly listed in paragraph 1, this reading is the author's interpretation.

If the deceased last lived in Germany or the Netherlands and none of the heirs lives in Turkey, the domestic rules point to no Turkish court. Under MÖHUK Article 43 the case then goes to the court where assets of the estate are located in Turkey. More on this: [Article 43 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-43-mohuk-inheritance-dispute-turkey-court).

### Subject-matter jurisdiction: no express rule

No specific provision says which type of court hears the annulment action. Under HMK Article 2(1), the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) hears property-related actions unless otherwise provided. The civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) only hears actions assigned to it by law (HMK Article 4(1)(ç)). The fact that the civil court of peace issued the certificate does not change this: issuing it is non-contentious (HMK Article 383), while the annulment action is contentious.

That the action is heard by the civil court of first instance reflects court practice. No statutory provision says so expressly, and no Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) decision on this point was reviewed for this article. How a court will rule on its jurisdiction in a given case is not certain.

## Certificate issued by a notary: objection to the civil court of peace

The Notaries Law provides a separate route for notarial certificates (Article 71/C of Law No. 1512, added by Law No. 6217, in force since 1 October 2011; unofficial translation): "Those whose interests are infringed may object to the civil court of peace regarding certificates of inheritance issued by notaries. The civil court of peace notifies the notary concerned and the Union of Turkish Notaries of a copy of its decision on the objection." Turkish original:

> Noterlerin verdikleri mirasçılık belgesi hakkında, menfaati ihlal edilenler tarafından sulh hukuk mahkemesine itirazda bulunulabilir. Sulh hukuk mahkemesi, itiraz üzerinde verdiği kararın bir örneğini ilgili notere ve Türkiye Noterler Birliğine bildirir.

The law sets no deadline for this objection. Nor does it say expressly whether the objection rules out an annulment action against a notarial certificate; which route fits depends on the case.

Against a certificate issued by the civil court of peace, the parties may file an appeal (istinaf) within two weeks of learning of the decision (HMK Article 387). If that deadline has passed, an annulment action under Article 598(3) TMK is still available.

## Time limits: "at any time" and the action for recovery of the estate

The annulment action has no time limit. If, because of the wrong certificate, someone else has taken estate assets, getting them back is a separate action: the action for recovery of the estate (miras sebebiyle istihkak davası). Article 637(1)–(2) TMK (unofficial translation):

> A statutory or appointed heir may bring an action for recovery of the estate against a person holding the estate or certain estate assets, asserting their superior right as heir.
>
> In this action the judge also resolves disputes concerning the status of heir.

The time limits are in Article 639 TMK (unofficial translation):

> The action for recovery of the estate becomes time-barred one year from the date on which the claimant learned that they are an heir and that the good-faith defendant holds the estate or the estate asset, and in any event ten years after the death of the deceased or the opening of the will.
>
> Against those not acting in good faith, the limitation period is twenty years.

Turkish original of Article 639:

> Miras sebebiyle istihkak davası, davacının kendisinin mirasçı olduğunu ve iyiniyetli davalının terekeyi veya tereke malını elinde bulundurduğunu öğrendiği tarihten başlayarak bir yıl ve her hâlde mirasbırakanın ölümünün veya vasiyetnamenin açılmasının üzerinden on yıl geçmekle zamanaşımına uğrar.
>
> İyiniyetli olmayanlara karşı zamanaşımı süresi yirmi yıldır.

The defendant cannot claim to have acquired the estate asset by prescription (Article 638(2) TMK). Even if the certificate is annulled, the Article 639 time limits apply to recovering the assets.

This point is disputed. No express statutory provision governs what effect an annulment obtained after the Article 639 periods have expired has on the estate assets, and no binding court decision on it was confirmed for this article. How a court will decide in a specific dispute is not certain.

The law does not fix how long the proceedings take; it depends on factors such as service abroad and missing register entries. On shares, see [inheritance shares in Turkey](/en/publications/inheritance-shares-turkey-spouse-children).

## A German certificate of inheritance (Erbschein) in Turkey

A Turkish court cannot annul a German Erbschein; its withdrawal is a matter of German law, for which you should consult a lawyer admitted in Germany.

On the Turkish side, MÖHUK Article 58(2) makes the recognition (tanıma) of non-contentious decisions (ihtilafsız kaza) subject to the same rule as foreign judgments (the conditions for enforcement must be met), and Article 58(3) requires the same procedure where an administrative act in Turkey is to be based on a foreign decision. For foreign nationals, Article 37 of the Land Registry Law (Tapu Kanunu) provides that certificates of inheritance issued by foreign authorities must be confirmed by Turkish courts. Whether an Erbschein counts as a "court judgment" within the meaning of MÖHUK is not expressly regulated and is disputed. In the author's assessment, a Turkish certificate is the more predictable route for transactions in Turkey. On recognition: [Article 58 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-mohuk-recognition-foreign-judgments-turkey). For general information see [inheritance law](/en/practice-areas/inheritance-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which court hears a challenge to a Turkish certificate of inheritance?

The court of the deceased's last domicile and the court where any of the heirs lives (HMK Article 11(3)). If the deceased and the heirs all lived abroad, the court where estate assets are located in Turkey (MÖHUK Article 43).

### Is there a deadline to challenge it?

No. Invalidity may be asserted at any time (Article 598(3) TMK). Recovering estate assets is subject to the one-, ten- and twenty-year periods in Article 639 TMK.

### How do I challenge a certificate issued by a Turkish notary?

By objecting to the civil court of peace (Article 71/C Notaries Law). The law sets no deadline.

### Is a German Erbschein valid in Turkey?

Not automatically. Whether and how it is recognised is disputed (MÖHUK Article 58, Tapu Kanunu Article 37). In the author's assessment, a Turkish certificate is more predictable for transactions in Turkey.

## Questions about your case

If your name or a relative's name is missing from a Turkish certificate of inheritance, or a share is wrong, you may want to clarify which court the annulment action belongs in and which register entries should be completed first. You can choose a meeting date on the [appointment page](/en/appointment), online by video, by phone or in person in Düsseldorf, or send a written question through the [contact form](/en/contact). Meetings are held in Turkish and cover only the Turkish law side of the matter (§ 206 BRAO); questions about a German Erbschein should go to a lawyer admitted in Germany. It helps to have ready:

- the certificate you want to challenge, and whether a notary or a court issued it,
- Turkish civil register extracts (nüfus kayıt örneği) for the deceased and the heirs, and their nationalities,
- German marriage, divorce and birth certificates, and whether they have been entered in the Turkish register,
- a list of estate assets and who currently holds them.

When a Turkish citizen dies in Germany, the Netherlands or any other country, the death is entered in the Turkish civil register (nüfus kütüğü) once the death certificate issued by the local authority is handed to the Turkish embassy or consulate (Article 36(1) of the Implementing Regulation on Civil Registration Services, Nüfus Hizmetleri Uygulama Yönetmeliği). The law sets no deadline for relatives. Until the death is registered, however, the deceased still appears alive in the Turkish register, and that is where the certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi), the transfer of title deeds (tapu) and a surviving spouse's new marriage get stuck.

As of November 2026, according to the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr, the last change to the death provisions (Articles 31–34) of Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu) is the second paragraph added to Article 33 by Law No. 7039 (in force since 3 November 2017). The most recent amendment of the Law as a whole, Law No. 7394 (in force since 15 April 2022), does not touch the rules on death. The Implementing Regulation took effect on publication in the Official Gazette of 9 May 2020 (Presidential Decision No. 2505); the mevzuat.gov.tr text shows no later amendments.

## How is a death abroad registered with the Turkish consulate?

Under the Regulation, the death of a Turkish citizen abroad is notified by handing the local authority's document to the Turkish mission, which registers it through the central system (Article 36(1)). If that is not possible, the mission prepares two copies of a death notification form, attaches the deceased's identity card (nüfus cüzdanı or kimlik kartı) and sends them to the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) where the person is registered, within ten days of receiving the death certificate (Article 36(2)).

The ten-day period in the Law is also addressed to the mission: diplomatic and consular missions must report a death to the General Directorate or the registry office within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1) of Law No. 5490). Relatives have no statutory deadline. In practice, the process starts once the death certificate reaches the consulate.

Heirs living in Turkey can also go straight to a Turkish registry office. Article 33(1), first sentence, of Law No. 5490:

> Ölmüş olduğu halde aile kütüklerinde sağ görülenlere ait ölüm tutanakları, ölüm olayını gösterir belge ile başvurulması halinde nüfus müdürlüklerince düzenlenir ve gerekli işlem yapılır.

Unofficial translation: for persons who have died but still appear alive in the family register, the registry offices draw up the death record and make the necessary entry when an application is made with a document showing the death.

If no document is available, the registry office checks the statement and the record is entered on the order of the provincial or district governor (mülkî idare amiri) (Article 33(1)). There is no fine for relatives who report a death late: in its current text, Article 68 imposes fines for missed reporting deadlines only on public officials.

Appointments, postal applications and extra documents vary from one consulate to another. Check your consulate general's website or [konsolosluk.gov.tr](https://www.konsolosluk.gov.tr).

## Multilingual death certificate or apostille?

Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are parties to the Vienna Convention of 8 September 1976 of the International Commission on Civil Status (CIEC) on the issue of multilingual extracts from civil-status records (No. 16). According to the CIEC's official website, it entered into force for Turkey on 30 June 1985, for the Netherlands on 26 April 1987 and for Germany on 18 July 1997. On request, extracts of birth, marriage and death records are issued in multilingual form (Article 1), and they are accepted in the contracting states "without legalisation or equivalent formality" (Article 8). You can ask for this multilingual death extract at the German Standesamt or the Dutch gemeente.

How the Turkish authorities accept foreign documents is set out in Article 59 of the Regulation:

- Multilingual documents issued under the CIEC conventions are processed directly, without any certification (Article 59(1)(a)); this provision does not require a translation.
- Other documents from states party to the Hague Apostille Convention are processed with an apostille on the original and a Turkish translation certified by a notary (Article 59(1)(b)). All three states are parties; according to the Hague Conference status table, the Convention entered into force for Turkey on 29 September 1985.
- Documents issued by honorary consulates are not accepted as a basis for registration (Article 59(3)).

So with the multilingual death certificate you do not need an apostille. If you only have a single-language German or Dutch certificate, you need an apostille and a certified Turkish translation. Also check that the first name, surname and date of birth are written exactly as in the Turkish register; if they differ, ask the consulate before applying.

## No Turkish record, or a missing person

If the deceased was never entered in the Turkish register and Turkish citizenship and family are established, the birth is entered first and then the death (Article 31(4) of Law No. 5490). This happens with people born abroad who were never registered in Turkey; on birth registration see [birth registration in Turkey for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad). Death records that arrive after the statutory period are accepted if they are based on records of public health institutions or other official documents (Article 31(2)).

If a person disappeared in circumstances that make death certain, for example an accident in which the body was not found, he or she is deemed to have actually died (Article 31 of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK), and the death is entered on the order of the highest administrative officer of the place (Article 44(1) TMK). The application is made by a descendant, an ascendant or a sibling, failing them by the heirs (Article 32(2) of Law No. 5490). Any interested person may also ask the court to determine whether the person is alive or dead (Article 44(2) TMK).

## What an unregistered death means for an inheritance in Turkey

Legal personality ends with death (Article 28 TMK), the succession opens on death (Article 575 TMK), and the heirs acquire the estate at the moment of death by operation of law (Article 599(1) TMK). What counts is the date of death, not the date of registration. The difficulty is proof. Article 30 TMK:

> Doğum ve ölüm, nüfus sicilindeki kayıtlarla ispat olunur.
>
> Nüfus sicilinde bir kayıt yoksa veya bulunan kaydın doğru olmadığı anlaşılırsa, gerçek durum her türlü kanıtla ispat edilebilir.

Unofficial translation: birth and death are proved by the entries in the civil register. If there is no entry, or the entry turns out to be incorrect, the true facts may be proved by any means of evidence.

The second paragraph therefore allows the death to be proved with the foreign certificate. Turkish public bodies and notaries, however, learn of deaths through the register; they check deaths electronically (Additional Article 4 of Law No. 5490). Without the entry the person appears alive there, and a notary cannot issue a certificate of inheritance. The choice between notary and court is covered in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey). Transferring a property to the heirs also requires that certificate first: [inheriting property in Turkey](/en/publications/inheriting-property-in-turkey-title-deed).

Late registration does not stop the time limit for renouncing the inheritance. For statutory heirs the three months run from the date they learned of the death, unless they prove they only learned later that they were heirs (Article 606 TMK). The Code does not mention registration as a starting point. If the estate may be in debt, calculate the deadline without waiting for the consulate: [renouncing an inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/renouncing-inheritance-in-turkey).

## The surviving spouse: register entry and remarriage

Until the death is registered, the surviving spouse appears as married in the Turkish register. Anyone who wants to remarry must prove that the previous marriage has ended (Article 130 TMK). If you intend to marry in Turkey or at a consulate, in the author's view the simplest route is to have the death registered first.

For women there is a waiting period of three hundred days from the end of the marriage (Article 132(1) TMK). Under the Regulation, a widow's waiting period runs from the date of her husband's death (Article 23(1)), so late registration does not extend it. The period ends on giving birth, and the court lifts it if it is established that the woman is not pregnant from the previous marriage (Article 132(2) and (3) TMK).

If the surviving spouse remarries abroad, that marriage must also be notified to the Turkish register (Article 24(1) of Law No. 5490). If the former spouse's death is missing from the register, registering the new marriage may stall as well. This is an assessment; the Law does not expressly set such a sequence.

## Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holders

People who were Turkish by birth and left Turkish citizenship with permission, and their descendants, are recorded in the Blue Card Holders Register (Mavi Kartlılar Kütüğü); they must report all civil-status events to the Turkish missions when abroad and to the registry offices in Turkey (Article 8/A of Law No. 5490). Death is one of the civil-status events listed in the Law (Article 3(1)(y)). The Law does not say who reports the death of a Blue Card holder. In the author's view, the heirs should take the death certificate to the consulate; if there is property in Turkey, the entry is needed for the transfer. Which law governs a Blue Card holder's estate is explained in [Blue Card Turkey (Mavi Kart)](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I get a death certificate for Turkey?

From the civil registry of the place of death: the Standesamt in Germany, the gemeente in the Netherlands. The Turkish authorities process the multilingual version without any certification (Article 59(1)(a) of the Regulation). Once the death is registered, you can obtain a Turkish register extract (nüfus kayıt örneği) through e-Devlet; the Regulation treats extracts obtained there as valid for public bodies (Article 50(3)).

### How many days do I have to report a death to the Turkish consulate?

Relatives have no statutory deadline. The ten days apply to the mission, counted from when it learns of the death (Article 31(1) of Law No. 5490).

### Is there a fine if the death is not reported?

Not under the current text of Article 68 of Law No. 5490. The effect shows up in the inheritance: without the entry, a notary will not issue a certificate of inheritance.

### Can I get a certificate of inheritance before the death is registered?

Not from a notary, because the notary checks deaths through the register (Additional Article 4 of Law No. 5490). Before the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) the death can be proved with other documents (Article 30(2) TMK); in the author's view, registering first still makes that case easier.

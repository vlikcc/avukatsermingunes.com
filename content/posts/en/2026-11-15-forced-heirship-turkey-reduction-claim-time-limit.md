Turkish law protects certain heirs with a reserved share (saklı pay), and the tool for enforcing it is the reduction claim (tenkis davası): an heir who does not receive the value of their reserved share asks for the deceased's wills and gifts exceeding the freely disposable portion to be reduced (Article 560 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). The claim must be brought within one year of learning that the reserved share was infringed and in any case within ten years (Article 571 TMK). This post covers the reserved shares, which gifts are affected, how the time limits run and which court hears the case if you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere abroad. Current as of November 2026.

## Who has a reserved share?

A deceased who leaves descendants, parents or a spouse as heirs may dispose by will only of the part of the estate outside the reserved shares (Article 505(1) TMK). If there are no such heirs, the whole estate may be disposed of (Article 505(2)).

The reserved shares under Article 506 TMK:

- descendants: half of their statutory share,
- father and mother: one quarter of their statutory share each,
- surviving spouse: the whole statutory share when inheriting with descendants or with the parents' line, otherwise three quarters.

Siblings have no reserved share; that provision was repealed by Law No. 5650 in 2007 (Article 506(3), repealed). On the statutory shares themselves: [Inheritance shares in Turkey](/en/publications/inheritance-shares-turkey-spouse-children).

### Example

The deceased leaves a wife and two children. The wife's statutory share is one quarter (Article 499(1) TMK), each child's is three eighths. Reserved shares: wife one quarter (her whole statutory share), each child three sixteenths (half). Together the reserved shares make five eighths; three eighths are freely disposable. If the net estate is TRY 1,200,000 (an example figure) and the deceased left everything by will to one child, the wife can claim TRY 300,000 and the other child TRY 225,000 by way of reduction.

## How is the disposable portion calculated?

The calculation is based on the estate as it stood on the day of death (Article 507(1) TMK). The deceased's debts, funeral costs, the costs of sealing and inventorying the estate, and three months' living costs for people who lived with and were supported by the deceased are deducted (Article 507(2)). Gratuitous lifetime gifts are added back to the extent they are subject to reduction (Article 508). For life insurance in favour of a third party, the surrender value at the time of death is added (Article 509).

## Which dispositions can be reduced?

Testamentary dispositions such as wills and inheritance contracts, if they exceed the disposable portion (Article 560 TMK). The following lifetime gifts are also subject to reduction like testamentary dispositions (Article 565):

1. Gifts on account of the inheritance share to a statutory heir who loses heir status; transfers of assets or debt releases to descendants without a duty to bring them into account; trousseau and start-up capital beyond the usual,
2. Gifts made to settle inheritance rights before death,
3. Gifts other than those made with a reserved right of free revocation and customary presents in the last year before death,
4. Gifts clearly made to defeat the reserved-share rules.

Reduction starts with testamentary dispositions; if that is not enough, it moves to lifetime gifts, starting with the most recent and going back (Article 570(1)). Gifts to public legal entities and public-benefit associations and foundations are reduced last (Article 570(2)).

### Reduction or sham sale?

If the deceased openly gifted a property in the land registry, reduction is the route. If the registry shows a "sale" that was really a gift, the action for annulment of title and registration on grounds of simulation (muris muvazaası) may apply; according to the Court of Cassation (1st Civil Chamber, E. 2010/2775, K. 2010/5274), the time limits in Article 571 do not apply to it. More: [Sham sale of property in Turkish inheritance law](/en/publications/inheritance-law-turkey-sham-sale-property).

## Time limits: one year and ten years

Under Article 571 TMK the right to bring the claim lapses:

- **one year** after the heir learns that their reserved share was infringed, and
- in any case **ten years** after the will was opened or, for other dispositions, after the estate opened (death).

These are preclusive periods (hak düşürücü süre): the law says the right "lapses" (düşer). If annulling one disposition revives an earlier one, the periods start when the annulment becomes final (Article 571(2)). Reduction can always be raised as a defence (Article 571(3)).

The one-year period does not start on learning of the death, but on learning that your reserved share was infringed. If you live abroad, you may learn about a land registry transfer in Turkey late; keep proof of when you found out (the date you obtained the title deed extract, service of the will on you), because the year runs from that date.

## Who sues whom, and in which court?

The heir whose reserved share was infringed brings the claim. If that heir is bankrupt, or fails to sue despite a demand from creditors holding a certificate of insufficient assets, the bankruptcy administration or those creditors may sue within the same period (Article 562). The defendant is the person who received the gift or bequest.

For claims relating to property, the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) generally has jurisdiction (Article 2(1) HMK). For claims to reduce testamentary dispositions, the court of the deceased's last domicile has exclusive jurisdiction (Article 11(1)(a) HMK). As of November 2026, the reduction claim is not among the disputes for which prior mediation is a precondition under Article 18/B of Mediation Law No. 6325.

## If you live abroad

**Applicable law from the Turkish perspective.** Under the Turkish Private International Law Act (MÖHUK), the estate of a Turkish citizen is governed by Turkish law, and Turkish law always applies to immovable property in Turkey (Article 20(1) MÖHUK). If the deceased held another citizenship alongside Turkish citizenship, Turkish law applies (Article 4(1)(b) MÖHUK). A Turkish court will therefore apply the reserved shares and time limits above even if the Turkish deceased died in Germany or the Netherlands.

**Competent court.** If the deceased's last domicile was not in Turkey, inheritance disputes are heard by the court where estate assets are located (Article 43 MÖHUK). For a flat in Izmir, the claim is brought in Izmir. More: [Article 43 MÖHUK – inheritance disputes](/en/publications/legislation/article-43-mohuk-inheritance-dispute-turkey-court).

The German Pflichtteil and the Dutch legitieme portie follow their own rules, and courts in those countries decide the applicable inheritance law under their own conflict rules. Please consult a lawyer admitted in the relevant country on those points.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the time limit for a reduction claim in Turkey?

One year from learning of the infringement of the reserved share and, in any case, ten years from the opening of the will or the death (Article 571(1) TMK).

### Who has a reserved share in Turkey?

Descendants, parents and the surviving spouse (Article 506 TMK).

### Do siblings have a reserved share?

No. It was abolished in 2007 (Article 506(3), repealed).

### Is mediation required before suing?

As of November 2026, the reduction claim is not on the list in Article 18/B of Law No. 6325.

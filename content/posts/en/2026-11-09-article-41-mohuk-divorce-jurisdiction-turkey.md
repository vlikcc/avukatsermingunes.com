Article 41 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which court in Turkey hears cases on the personal status (kişi hâlleri) of Turkish citizens, such as divorce, parentage or guardianship. You can sue in Turkey even if you have no domicile there: the article lists the competent courts in order, ending with the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir. If you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere abroad and want to file for divorce in Turkey, this is the rule that tells you where.

## Article 41 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Cases concerning the personal status of Turks**
>
> Article 41 – (1) Cases of Turkish citizens concerning personal status, where they have not been or cannot be brought before the courts of a foreign country, are heard by the court in Turkey that has territorial jurisdiction; failing that, by the court of the place where the person concerned is staying; if he or she is not staying in Turkey, by the court of the last domicile in Turkey; and failing that, by one of the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir.

Turkish original:

> **Türklerin kişi hâllerine ilişkin davalar**
>
> MADDE 41 – (1) Türk vatandaşlarının kişi hâllerine ilişkin davaları, yabancı ülke mahkemelerinde açılmadığı veya açılamadığı takdirde Türkiye’de yer itibariyle yetkili mahkemede, bulunmaması hâlinde ilgilinin sâkin olduğu yer, Türkiye’de sâkin değilse Türkiye’deki son yerleşim yeri mahkemesinde, o da bulunmadığı takdirde Ankara, İstanbul veya İzmir mahkemelerinden birinde görülür.

The article has not changed since the law came into force on 12 December 2007; as of November 2026 the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note for it.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning of the government bill, the article partly corresponds to Article 28 of the former Law No. 2675. That provision allowed an objection of lis pendens (derdestlik) based on a case pending abroad only if the Turkish citizen was domiciled abroad and the case was pending in that country. Because these two conditions stood in the way of procedural economy and legal certainty, the words “without domicile in Turkey” and “the country where they reside” were removed, and “ikametgâh” was replaced by “yerleşim yeri” (domicile). (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 40 of the bill.)

## Explanation

### The order of courts

The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts generally follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 MÖHUK). Article 41 adds a sequence for personal status cases of Turkish citizens:

1. First, the court with territorial jurisdiction under Turkish law. For divorce and judicial separation, that is the court of either spouse’s domicile or of the place where the spouses last lived together for six months before the case (Article 168 of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK). Parentage cases are brought at the domicile of one of the parties at the time of the case or of the birth (Article 283 TMK).
2. If there is none, the court of the place where the person concerned is actually staying in Turkey (sâkin).
3. If he or she is not staying in Turkey, the court of the last domicile in Turkey.
4. Failing that, one of the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir.

Domicile is the place where a person lives with the intention of staying permanently (Article 19 TMK). If both spouses live in Germany, their domicile in this sense is usually there, so the first step points to no Turkish court and you move down the list. The law does not define “personal status”; the Turkish Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has applied the article to divorce and also to guardianship and restriction of capacity cases concerning Turks living abroad (e.g. Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2011/3669, K. 2011/16395, T. 20.10.2011, concerning a person registered as domiciled in Nuremberg).

Article 41 only decides the court. Which law governs the divorce is a separate question, answered by Article 14 MÖHUK ([Article 14 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-14-mohuk-divorce-separation)).

### A case already filed abroad: an open question

The article gives Turkish courts jurisdiction “where” the case “has not been or cannot be brought” abroad. What this means is disputed. The law contains no express rule on how a case pending abroad affects a case in Turkey, and the lis pendens requirement in Article 114(1)(ı) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (HMK) does not mention foreign proceedings. What follows is my own assessment based on the wording and the decisions I could find; how a court will decide a particular case is uncertain.

- Read literally, if the same case has been filed abroad, Article 41 does not give a Turkish court jurisdiction.
- The legislative reasoning, however, treats the issue as an objection of lis pendens. The Court of Cassation, in decisions given before the HMK, also examined foreign proceedings as a lis pendens question and set strict conditions (see below).
- In my view, “cannot be brought” refers to cases where the foreign court considers itself not competent or its law does not allow the claim.

If the foreign case has ended in a final judgment, the position is different. The judgment has res judicata effect in Turkey only once it is recognised (tanıma) (Article 58 MÖHUK), and recognition takes effect from the date it became final abroad (Article 59 MÖHUK). The recognition case is governed by Article 51 MÖHUK, not Article 41.

## What this means if you live abroad

**You have never lived in Turkey.** Two Turkish citizens who grew up in Germany and never had a domicile in Turkey can file for divorce in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir, provided they have not filed in Germany. If the defendant properly names one of these cities, the court has to treat that court as competent (2014 decision, below).

**One spouse has moved back to Turkey.** The case can then be brought at that spouse’s domicile under Article 168 TMK; if he or she merely lives there in fact, the “place of stay” step applies. In one case, a divorce filed in Ankara was sent to the court in Didim, where the husband actually lived (2023 decision).

**Turkey or your country of residence?** Article 41 does not make the Turkish court the only option; whether a German or Dutch court has jurisdiction depends on that country’s law. A German divorce takes effect in Turkey only after recognition there. Whether a Turkish judgment is recognised in Germany or the Netherlands is a matter of that country’s law, on which a lawyer admitted there should advise.

**Running the case from abroad.** The case can be filed through a lawyer in Turkey; the power of attorney must expressly cover the divorce case. See also the posts on [uncontested divorce in Turkey while living abroad](/en/publications/uncontested-divorce-turkey-living-abroad) and [contested divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/contested-divorce-turkey-grounds).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2014/9210, K. 2014/19748, T. 15.10.2014**

The parties had no domicile, no place of stay and no last domicile in Turkey. The first-instance court declared the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir competent. The 2nd Civil Chamber reversed: since the defendant had properly named the Izmir Family Court, that court should have been declared competent; naming several courts was wrong.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2015/24523, K. 2016/7966, T. 19.04.2016**

The Chamber held that, in view of Article 168 TMK and Articles 14 and 41 MÖHUK, a Turkish court was wrong to decline jurisdiction in favour of the German courts. It also stated that parties who were Turkish by birth and gave up citizenship with permission could not be treated as foreigners in the divorce case, under Article 28 of the Citizenship Law No. 5901 as then in force.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/2958, K. 2023/6102, T. 07.12.2023**

The spouses had lived in Germany; the husband returned to Turkey and lived in Didim. The regional court of appeal read “the place where the person concerned is staying in Turkey” as the place where he actually lives in Turkey and held the Didim Family Court competent. The Court of Cassation upheld that decision.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2010/6713, K. 2011/8635, T. 17.05.2011**

The Turkish case had been dismissed because the wife had earlier filed for divorce in Germany. The Chamber held that a foreign case can ground lis pendens only if the parties, subject matter and cause of action are the same, the foreign judgment is enforceable in Turkey, and there is a treaty and a rule in Turkish private international law. As the causes of action and the parties’ roles differed, it reversed. It applied the same test to a case in Canada (E. 2009/13541, K. 2010/11899, T. 15.06.2010). Both decisions predate the HMK.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Turkish court hears my divorce if I live abroad?

If one spouse has a domicile in Turkey, the court there or at the place of the last six months of living together (Article 168 TMK). Otherwise, in turn: the place of stay in Turkey, the last domicile in Turkey, and finally Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir (Article 41 MÖHUK).

### Does a divorce filed abroad block a divorce case in Turkey?

The law does not say so expressly and the point is disputed. Before the HMK, the Court of Cassation allowed a lis pendens objection only under strict conditions, including identical cases, enforceability and a treaty.

### Can Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holders file for divorce in Turkey?

Article 41 refers to “Turkish citizens”. The 2016 decision says former Turks who gave up citizenship with permission may not be treated as foreigners in divorce proceedings; I could not confirm that this is settled case law.

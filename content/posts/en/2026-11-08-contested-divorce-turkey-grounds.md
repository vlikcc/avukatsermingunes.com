In a contested divorce (çekişmeli boşanma) in Turkey, the court grants a divorce only if one of the grounds listed in the Turkish Civil Code (Türk Medeni Kanunu, TMK) is proven: adultery, an attempt on the spouse’s life or seriously degrading treatment, a degrading crime or dishonourable way of life, desertion, mental illness, or the irretrievable breakdown of the marriage (Art. 161–166 TMK). If the spouses cannot agree on the divorce or its consequences, the uncontested route in Art. 166(3) TMK is closed, and the judge may not treat the facts relied on as proven unless genuinely convinced of them (Art. 184(1) TMK).

“Contested divorce” is not a statutory term; in practice it covers every divorce case that is not an uncontested one. This article explains the grounds, the time limits and how the case runs if you live in Germany or the Netherlands. The provisions cited were checked in November 2026 against the current text in the official UYAP Mevzuat system. Uncontested divorce is covered separately: [Uncontested divorce in Turkey while living abroad](/en/publications/uncontested-divorce-turkey-living-abroad).

## Grounds for divorce in Turkey: specific grounds and breakdown

The TMK has two kinds of ground. A specific ground describes a particular event; once it is proven, you do not also have to show that married life has become unbearable. The general ground is the breakdown of the marriage (Art. 166 TMK).

### Adultery and attempt on life: six months and five years

If one spouse commits adultery (zina), the other may sue for divorce (Art. 161 TMK). A separate ground exists where a spouse makes an attempt on the other’s life, treats them very badly or behaves towards them in a seriously degrading way (Art. 162 TMK).

The time limits for both grounds are strict. The right to sue lapses **six months** after learning of the ground and in any event **five years** after the event. A spouse who has forgiven has no right to sue (Art. 161(2)–(3), Art. 162(2)–(3) TMK). Whether the same events can still be relied on after the deadline in a breakdown case under Art. 166(1) TMK is not expressly regulated. That this is possible is the author’s assessment; no verified Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) decision on the point is cited here.

### Crime, dishonourable life and mental illness

If a spouse commits a degrading crime or leads a dishonourable life and the other spouse therefore cannot be expected to live with them, that spouse may sue at any time (Art. 163 TMK). Divorce on grounds of mental illness requires that common life has become unbearable for the other spouse and that an official medical board report confirms that the illness cannot be cured (Art. 165 TMK).

### Desertion: six months, a formal warning, two months

Desertion (terk) has the most tightly defined timetable (Art. 164 TMK):

1. One spouse has left the other in order to avoid their marital obligations, or does not return to the family home without good reason. A spouse who forces the other to leave the home, or prevents their return without good reason, is also treated as having deserted.
2. The separation has lasted at least six months and is continuing.
3. Once the fourth month has ended, the deserted spouse asks a judge or a notary to issue a warning (ihtar). The warning tells the other spouse to return to the home within two months and explains the consequences of not doing so. Where necessary it is served by public notice.
4. The divorce case cannot be filed until two months after the warning.

The law requires the warning to have had no effect; if the spouse returns within the time limit, a divorce for desertion is not available.

## Irretrievable breakdown of the marriage (Art. 166 TMK)

This is the ground used most often. If the marriage has broken down so fundamentally that the spouses cannot be expected to continue their life together, either spouse may sue (Art. 166(1) TMK). The law does not list which events lead to this; the judge assesses the evidence freely (Art. 184(4) TMK).

If the claimant bears the greater share of fault, the respondent may object to the divorce. If the objection is an abuse of rights and the marriage no longer offers the respondent and the children any interest worth protecting, the court may still grant the divorce (Art. 166(2) TMK).

Fault matters most for the financial consequences. Only the spouse who is not at fault or less at fault may claim material compensation, and non-material compensation is claimed from the spouse at fault (Art. 174 TMK); a spouse claiming poverty maintenance (yoksulluk nafakası) must not bear the greater fault (Art. 175 TMK). That is why contested cases involve detailed argument about who is responsible for which events. The Constitutional Court ruling on the duration of maintenance is discussed in [Alimony in Turkey: the ruling on indefinite maintenance](/en/publications/alimony-turkey-indefinite-maintenance-ruling).

## If your case was dismissed: the one-year rule (Art. 166(4) TMK)

If a divorce case brought on any ground is dismissed, and **one year** passes after that dismissal becomes final without the spouses resuming their life together, the marriage is deemed to have broken down; the court grants a divorce at the request of either spouse (Art. 166(4) TMK).

This paragraph was amended by Article 13 of Law No. 7532 and has applied since 27/11/2024. The previous text required three years; the Constitutional Court (Anayasa Mahkemesi) had annulled that rule by its decision of 22/2/2024, E. 2023/116, K. 2024/56. A referral challenging the new one-year period was rejected by majority in the decision of 12/2/2026, E. 2025/203, K. 2026/39 (Official Gazette 17/4/2026, No. 33227). As of November 2026 the one-year period applies.

The claimant has to prove that married life was not resumed after the dismissal became final. The 2nd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has held that resumption requires the spouses to have come together for that purpose and lived together, and that this must be proven by evidence. In the case before it, the chamber reversed the dismissal because residence records, a tenancy agreement and witness statements showed that the spouses lived in different districts (E. 2024/506, K. 2025/3618, judgment of 14.04.2025). Given the dates of that case, the judgment quotes the earlier three-year wording.

## How the case runs: court, evidence, duration

**Which court.** Divorce cases are heard by the family court (aile mahkemesi) (Law No. 4787, Art. 4). The competent court is that of either spouse’s domicile or of the place where the spouses lived together for the last six months before the case (Art. 168 TMK). For Turkish citizens with no domicile in Turkey, Art. 41 MÖHUK applies: in turn, the court where the person is resident in Turkey, the court of their last domicile in Turkey and, failing both, a court in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir. This jurisdiction depends on the case not having been filed, or not being capable of being filed, before a foreign court.

**Special procedural rules.** The case follows the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK), subject to Art. 184 TMK: the judge may not put the parties on oath about the divorce facts, admissions do not bind the judge, the evidence is assessed freely, and the hearing may be held in private at a spouse’s request. Agreements on the consequences of divorce are valid only once the judge approves them.

**While the case is pending.** The judge orders interim measures on housing, maintenance, management of assets and the care of the children on their own motion (Art. 169 TMK). For custody see [Child custody in a Turkish divorce](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey); for matrimonial property see [Division of property on divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/division-of-property-divorce-turkey).

**How long does it take?** The law sets no overall duration. Under Art. 147(3) HMK, added by Law No. 7589 and in force since 31/7/2026, no more than three months may pass between two hearings; in unavoidable cases, such as a lengthy expert examination or taking evidence through another court (istinabe), the judge may set a longer interval with reasons. How long the case takes overall depends on the number of witnesses, service of documents abroad and whether appeal (istinaf) and further appeal (temyiz) follow.

**Separation instead of divorce.** The claimant may ask for judicial separation (ayrılık) instead of divorce (Art. 167 TMK). If the claim is for divorce, the court may order separation only if there is a prospect of the spouses resuming their life together; separation lasts one to three years (Art. 170–171 TMK).

## If you live in Germany or the Netherlands

If both spouses are Turkish citizens, the Turkish court applies Turkish law to the grounds and consequences of divorce (Art. 14(1) MÖHUK). See [Article 14 MÖHUK – divorce and separation](/en/publications/legislation/article-14-mohuk-divorce-separation).

You can bring and pursue the case through a lawyer in Turkey. Unless expressly authorised, a representative may not bring cases concerning strictly personal rights (Art. 74 HMK), so the power of attorney must expressly cover the divorce case. The court may allow a party, at their request, and witnesses to take part by simultaneous audio and video link from where they are (Art. 149(1)–(2) HMK). How participation from abroad is arranged in practice is for the court to decide.

If divorce proceedings are already pending in Germany, this may affect the Turkish court’s jurisdiction under Art. 41 MÖHUK. A German divorce judgment has effect in Turkey only once recognised. For questions of German or Dutch law, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. General information is on the [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a contested divorce take in Turkey?

The law sets no overall time. As of November 2026, no more than three months may as a rule pass between two hearings (Art. 147(3) HMK); the total depends on the evidence, service abroad and any appeals.

### How long must I wait to file for divorce for desertion?

The separation must have lasted at least six months. The warning can only be requested once the fourth month has ended, and the case can only be filed two months after the warning (Art. 164 TMK).

### What is the time limit for divorce on grounds of adultery?

Six months from learning of it, and in any event five years from the act. A spouse who has forgiven cannot sue (Art. 161 TMK).

### My divorce case was dismissed. Can I file again?

If one year has passed since the dismissal became final and married life has not been resumed, the court grants a divorce at the request of either spouse (Art. 166(4) TMK).

Article 38 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which law governs claims arising from restraints of competition (rekabetin engellenmesi). The rule is the law of the country where the directly affected market is located. Where a foreign law is applied to a restraint of competition in Turkey, damages may not exceed what Turkish law would award. The article matters mainly to businesses harmed by a cartel or by abuse of a dominant position.

## Article 38 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Restraint of competition**
>
> Article 38 – (1) Claims arising from a restraint of competition are governed by the law of the country in which the market directly affected by the restraint is located.
>
> (2) Where a foreign law is applied to a restraint of competition in Turkey, no more damages may be awarded than would be awarded if Turkish law were applied.

Turkish original:

> **Rekabetin engellenmesi**
>
> MADDE 38 – (1) Rekabetin engellenmesinden doğan talepler, bu engellemeden doğrudan etkilenen piyasanın bulunduğu ülkenin hukukuna tâbidir.
>
> (2) Türkiye’de rekabetin engellenmesine yabancı hukuk uygulanan hâllerde, Türk hukuku uygulansaydı verilecek tazminattan daha fazla tazminata hükmedilemez.

The article has not changed since the law came into force on 12 December 2007; as of November 2026 the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning of the government bill, the article is new; the former Law No. 2675 had no equivalent. As with unfair competition, the “directly affected market” is treated as the closest connection. The cap in paragraph 2 is meant to prevent excessive damages claims where a foreign law applies, following similar provisions in other countries’ private international law statutes. (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 37 of the bill.)

## Explanation

Where a price-fixing agreement was made and where the companies are based does not matter. The market where the loss arises is decisive. If an agreement made abroad affects the Turkish market, Turkish law governs the losses suffered there.

Under Turkish law, the relevant rules are in Law No. 4054 on the Protection of Competition. It covers restrictive agreements and practices of undertakings active in Turkey or affecting those markets (Article 2, Law No. 4054). Anyone who restricts competition or abuses a dominant position must compensate all losses of those harmed; where several are responsible, they are jointly and severally liable (Article 57). Those harmed can claim the difference between the price they paid and the price they would have paid without the restriction. If the loss results from an agreement, a decision or gross negligence, the court may on request award three times the loss suffered or the profits obtained or likely to be obtained (Article 58). As of November 2026, Articles 57–59 carry no amendment note; the latest amendment to the law, by Law No. 7549, concerned Articles 22 and 23 (in force since 4 June 2025).

The cap in paragraph 2 applies only where a foreign law governs a restraint of competition in Turkey. Because Turkish law itself allows treble damages in some cases, it is my assessment that the cap includes that multiple; the law does not say so expressly.

Unfair competition is covered by Article 37 MÖHUK ([Article 37 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-37-mohuk-unfair-competition)). Both are special cases of the general tort rule ([Article 34 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-34-mohuk-tort-accident-turkey)).

## Points to note if you live abroad

The article concerns businesses more than consumers. If a company in Germany or the Netherlands buys goods in Turkey and believes it was harmed by price-fixing among Turkish suppliers, which market counts as “directly affected” depends on the facts. Proceedings before the Turkish Competition Authority (Rekabet Kurumu) and a civil damages claim are separate routes. For German or Dutch competition law, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. More on related topics: [commercial and contract law](/en/practice-areas/commercial-and-contract-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which law applies to cartel damages with a Turkish link?

Before a Turkish court, the law of the country where the directly affected market is located (Article 38(1) MÖHUK).

### Does Turkish law allow treble damages for cartels?

Yes. If the loss results from an agreement, a decision or gross negligence, the court may on request award three times the loss (Article 58, Law No. 4054).

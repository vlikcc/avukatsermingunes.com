Article 24 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) allows anyone whose personality rights (kişilik hakkı) are unlawfully infringed to ask the court for protection against those responsible. The weight of the provision lies in paragraph 2: every infringement of personality rights is unlawful unless it is justified by the consent of the person affected, by an overriding private or public interest, or by the exercise of a power conferred by law. Every claim based on a personality rights infringement starts here; the available actions are set out in Article 25 TMK.

## Article 24 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Against infringement**
>
> **1. Principle**
>
> Article 24 – A person whose personality rights are unlawfully infringed may ask the court for protection against those who commit the infringement.
>
> Every infringement of personality rights is unlawful unless it is justified by the consent of the person whose personality rights are harmed, by an overriding private or public interest, or by the exercise of a power conferred by law.

Turkish original:

> **II. Saldırıya karşı**
>
> **1. İlke**
>
> Madde 24- Hukuka aykırı olarak kişilik hakkına saldırılan kimse, hâkimden, saldırıda bulunanlara karşı korunmasını isteyebilir.
>
> Kişilik hakkı zedelenen kimsenin rızası, daha üstün nitelikte özel veya kamusal yarar ya da kanunun verdiği yetkinin kullanılması sebeplerinden biriyle haklı kılınmadıkça, kişilik haklarına yapılan her saldırı hukuka aykırıdır.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 24; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 24 of the former Code. Because Articles 24 and 25 deal with protection "against infringement", the old heading "In case of violation" was changed; Article 24 received the side heading "Principle" and Article 25 "Actions". The rule introduced into the old Code by Law No. 3444 was rewritten in clearer terms (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### What do personality rights cover?

The Code does not list them. Life, physical and mental integrity, honour and reputation, name, image and voice, private life and personal data are counted among the protected values. The name has its own provision (Article 26 TMK).

### Why "every infringement is unlawful"?

Paragraph 2 sets a clear starting point for the burden of proof. Once it is shown that personality rights were infringed, the infringement is treated as unlawful. A party claiming justification must rely on one of the three statutory grounds and prove it (Article 6 TMK).

### The three grounds of justification

- **Consent:** the person may consent to the interference, for example to the publication of a photo or to medical treatment. Consent covers only what it was given for. Agreeing to a wedding photo for the family album is not agreement to its use in a company's advertising. Consent itself cannot go beyond the limit in Article 23 TMK.
- **Overriding private or public interest:** the court weighs the interest of the person affected against that of the actor or the public. Accurate and proportionate reporting on a matter of public interest is the typical case. The balance is struck case by case; the Code does not say which interest prevails when.
- **Exercise of a legal power:** a lawful police search, or truthful testimony in court. If the power is exceeded, the justification falls away.

### What does protection include?

Paragraph 1 only states the principle. Actions to prevent, stop or declare an infringement, publication of a correction, damages, surrender of profits and jurisdiction are in Article 25 TMK. The conditions for non-pecuniary damages (manevi tazminat) are also set out in the Turkish Code of Obligations (Article 58 TBK).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

When someone in Germany or the Netherlands posts defamatory content about a relative in Türkiye, or the other way round, two separate questions arise. The first is the applicable law. For infringements through the media, the internet or other mass communication, the injured person may choose between the connecting factors in the law; for example, if the infringer could foresee that the harm would occur there, the law of the injured person's habitual residence may apply (Article 35 of the Turkish private international law act No. 5718, MÖHUK). If Turkish law applies, unlawfulness is assessed under Article 24 TMK. The second question is whether a Turkish court has jurisdiction; see Article 25(5) TMK and Article 40 MÖHUK. Whether German or Dutch courts have jurisdiction, and which law they apply, depends on their own rules; please ask a lawyer admitted in that country.

Whether a post stays within reporting or criticism is weighed separately in each case. This article does not assess any particular post or person.

## Frequently asked questions

### What personality rights does Turkish law protect?

The Code gives no list. Life, physical and mental integrity, honour, name, image, voice and private life are among the protected values. The name is protected separately in Article 26 TMK.

### When is an infringement of personality rights lawful?

Only with the consent of the person affected, an overriding private or public interest, or the exercise of a legal power (Article 24(2) TMK). The party relying on it must prove it.

### Does Turkish law apply to a post made from abroad?

Not automatically. For infringements through media and the internet, the injured person chooses among the options in Article 35 MÖHUK. If Turkish law can be chosen, Articles 24 and 25 TMK apply.

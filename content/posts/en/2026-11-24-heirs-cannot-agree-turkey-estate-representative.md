If the heirs cannot agree on how to manage an estate in Turkey, any one of them can ask the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) to appoint a representative for the community of heirs until the estate is divided (Article 640(3) of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK). The law does not require the other heirs to consent; the court decides.

This usually comes up when some heirs live in Germany or the Netherlands and nobody can get unanimous agreement on the rent from the inherited flat, a bank account, or a pending court or enforcement case in Turkey. Below are the rules as of November 2026, the court procedure, and the points the law leaves open. This article covers Turkish law only.

## The community of heirs under Turkish law and why unanimity is needed

Where there are several heirs, a community covering all rights and debts of the estate arises on death and lasts until division (Article 640(1) TMK). In Turkish it is often called “miras şirketi”; the statutory name is “miras ortaklığı”. It is not a commercial company.

The heirs hold the estate in joint ownership (elbirliği mülkiyeti) and act together on all estate rights, unless a contractual or statutory power of representation or management exists (Article 640(2) TMK). There are no fixed shares; each heir's right extends to the whole (Article 701(2) TMK). Unless the law or a contract says otherwise, management and disposal require unanimity (Article 702(2) TMK).

In practice this means renewing a lease, collecting rent, enforcing a debt owed to the estate or selling the house needs all heirs acting together. If one heir objects, does not reply, or cannot be found, everything stops. Meanwhile the heirs remain jointly and severally liable for the estate's debts (Article 641(1) TMK).

## Appointing a representative: who applies, which court

The legal basis is one sentence: at the request of one heir, the civil court of peace may appoint a representative for the community of heirs until division (Article 640(3) TMK). It follows directly that:

- Any heir can apply alone.
- The civil court of peace has subject-matter jurisdiction. Article 640(3) TMK says so, and it is also the general rule for non-contentious matters (Article 383 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK).
- The law says “may”. The court is not bound to grant every application, so the applicant should explain why joint action is not possible.
- The appointment lasts “until division”.

Appointing a representative is not listed among the non-contentious matters (çekişmesiz yargı) in Article 382(2) HMK. In the author's assessment it is still non-contentious, because the court does not decide a dispute over rights but takes a measure for the community. On that view the simplified procedure applies and the court investigates of its own motion (Article 385(1) and (2) HMK); anyone with a legal interest can appeal (istinaf) within two weeks of learning of the decision (Article 387(1) HMK).

### What to put in the application

The law does not set out what the application must contain. Instead of a template, here is the information the court will need, in the author's view:

1. The Turkish certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi, Article 598 TMK) and the addresses of all heirs, including those abroad.
2. What the estate consists of: land registry details, bank accounts, leases, file numbers of pending cases.
3. Why joint action fails: an heir objecting, not responding, or unreachable, with correspondence.
4. The tasks for which a representative is sought, such as collecting rent or conducting a particular case.
5. A proposed person, if any.

## Which local court: no specific rule

Article 640(3) TMK does not say which place's civil court of peace has jurisdiction.

This point is disputed. What follows are arguable views; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision verified for this article. Which view a court will follow in a given case is uncertain.

- **General rule for non-contentious matters:** unless the law provides otherwise, the court where the applicant or one of the interested parties lives has jurisdiction (Article 384(1) HMK).
- **Place where succession opens:** succession opens at the deceased's last domicile, and partition and similar actions are heard there (Article 576 TMK). The appointment of a representative is not mentioned, but linking it to that place is arguable.
- **Last domicile abroad:** inheritance actions are heard at the deceased's last domicile in Turkey or, if there was none, where estate assets are located (Article 43(1) of the Turkish Private International Law Act, MÖHUK). The provision concerns lawsuits; whether it applies here is unclear.

In the author's assessment, if the deceased's last domicile in Turkey is also where one of the heirs lives, the civil court of peace there has jurisdiction on either reading. If all heirs live abroad, the question is less certain and should be checked before applying.

## What can the representative do?

The law does not regulate the representative's powers, who may be appointed, remuneration or supervision. Article 640(3) TMK only provides for the appointment and its duration until division.

This point is disputed. What follows are views; there is no express statutory rule on the scope of the powers and no binding court decision verified for this article. Which view a court will follow in a given case is uncertain.

- **Management and protection.** The core of the role is managing and preserving the estate: collecting rent, pursuing claims of the estate, conducting cases to which the estate is a party. In the author's assessment, that is the very purpose of the appointment.
- **Sale, mortgage and other dispositions.** On one reading the representative may do this too, since he or she steps into the heirs' place. On the other, the role is to preserve the estate; selling assets goes beyond that and needs express court permission or the heirs' consent.
- **Partition action.** Every heir can demand division alone anyway (Article 642(1) TMK). In the author's assessment, a representative is appointed to run the community until division, not to end it; whether the representative can sue for partition in the community's name is disputed.

A bank, land registry or opposing party will read the representative's authority from the court decision. In the author's view it helps to list the tasks specifically in the application and ask for the decision to state them in the same way.

## Other routes without a representative

- **Protection by one heir.** Each heir can seek protection of the estate's rights alone, and all heirs benefit (Article 640(4) and Article 702(4) TMK).
- **Conversion into shared ownership.** An heir can ask the judge of the civil court of peace to convert joint ownership into ownership in shares (paylı mülkiyet) (Article 644 TMK). After that, each co-owner may carry out ordinary management acts (Article 690(1) TMK); important management acts such as concluding or terminating a lease need a majority of shares and co-owners (Article 691(1) TMK). If shares and co-owners are evenly split, the judge may appoint a curator (kayyım) from among the co-owners or an outsider (Article 691(3) TMK). Disposing of the whole property still requires all co-owners as a rule (Article 692(1) TMK).
- **Division.** An heir who wants out of the community altogether can demand division (Article 642 TMK); see [Inherited property in Turkey: partition and sale](/en/publications/inherited-property-turkey-partition-lawsuit).
- **Heirs not yet known.** If the heirs' rights are not yet established, the guardianship authority appoints an administrator (Article 427(3) TMK). That is a different situation from known heirs who disagree.

If the heirs agree and distance is the only problem, a representative is often unnecessary: with a power of attorney given at the Turkish consulate, everyone can act together. For bank balances, see [Deceased person's bank account in Turkey](/en/publications/deceased-bank-account-turkey-heirs).

## Notes for heirs living in Germany or the Netherlands

If the deceased was a Turkish national, Turkish law governs the succession; even for a foreign national, Turkish law applies to immovable property in Turkey. Acquisition and division of the estate are governed by the law of the country where the estate is located (Article 20(1) and (2) MÖHUK). Management of the estate in Turkey therefore follows the TMK.

You can have the application filed by a lawyer in Turkey under a power of attorney without travelling. If the court wants to hear the other heirs, those abroad must be formally served, which takes time. If the estate also includes assets in Germany or the Netherlands, their management is governed by the law there; for that part, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. General information is on the [inheritance law](/en/practice-areas/inheritance-law) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all heirs have to agree to appoint a representative?

No. One heir's application is enough; the civil court of peace decides (Article 640(3) TMK).

### Which court in Turkey handles the application?

The civil court of peace (Article 640(3) TMK, Article 383 HMK). Local jurisdiction is not specifically regulated; Article 384 HMK, Article 576 TMK and Article 43 MÖHUK may point to different places.

### Can the representative sell the inherited house?

The law does not say so expressly, and the point is disputed. One view holds that dispositions need express court permission or the heirs' consent.

### When does the representative's role end?

The representative is appointed until division (Article 640(3) TMK). Once the estate is divided or joint ownership ends in another way (Article 703(1) TMK), the basis for the role falls away.

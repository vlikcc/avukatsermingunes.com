Under Turkish law, a parent who does not live with the child is entitled to reasonable personal contact with the child (kişisel ilişki, Article 323 of the Turkish Civil Code, Türk Medeni Kanunu, TMK). Contact is set in the divorce judgment or requested later in a separate case before the family court. If the other parent does not comply, the order is no longer enforced by the enforcement office: since the 2021 reform, the Ministry of Justice’s Directorate of Judicial Support and Victim Services (adli destek ve mağdur hizmetleri müdürlüğü) carries it out (Article 41/A of the Child Protection Act No. 5395).

Who gets custody, and which law applies to it, is covered in [child custody after divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey). This article reflects the law as of November 2026, checked against the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr.

## What visitation covers and who can ask for it

The Code does not set the length or form of contact; it only speaks of “reasonable” contact (TMK Art. 323). The court decides the days, hours, holidays and overnight stays. The test is the child’s welfare, in particular health, education and moral development (TMK Art. 182(3)).

Each parent must avoid harming the child’s relationship with the other parent and must not obstruct the child’s upbringing (TMK Art. 324(1)). In exceptional circumstances, and where it serves the child, other people, especially relatives such as grandparents, may also be granted contact (TMK Art. 325).

Until a court has made an arrangement, no contact may take place without the consent of the parent with custody or the person the child has been entrusted to (TMK Art. 326(3)). If the parents cannot agree, you need a court order.

## Filing for visitation (kişisel ilişki kurulması davası)

In a divorce, the court sets contact in the judgment on its own initiative (TMK Art. 182(1)). While the case is pending, contact is arranged through the interim measures the judge also orders on its own initiative (TMK Art. 169).

A separate case is needed where the parents were never married, the divorce judgment left contact out, or the parents live apart without divorcing. The family court hears these cases (Act No. 4787, Art. 4), and the court where the child lives also has jurisdiction for any contact arrangement (TMK Art. 326(1)). The court draws on psychologists, educational specialists and social workers (Act No. 4787, Art. 5).

Ask for something precise. The Ministry of Justice department responsible (Adli Destek ve Mağdur Hizmetleri Dairesi Başkanlığı) stated in an opinion of 17 March 2023 that a contact order must set its start and end times and the place clearly enough to leave no doubt at enforcement.

Where the child’s welfare requires it, the court can order supervised contact in the presence of a third person or a specialist; the handover then takes place at the venue chosen by the directorate (Regulation, Art. 38). Contact can be refused or withdrawn if it endangers the child’s well-being, if the parent uses it in breach of their duties, does not take a genuine interest in the child, or for other serious reasons (TMK Art. 324(2)).

## Changing the arrangement

If new facts such as a parent remarrying, moving away or dying make it necessary, the judge takes the required measures on its own initiative or at the request of either parent (TMK Art. 183). This is the basis for a case to change the contact arrangement (kişisel ilişkinin yeniden düzenlenmesi davası). Contact can be extended, reduced or rescheduled.

Act No. 7343 added two rules in 2021. If the parent with custody does not comply with the contact arrangement, custody may be changed, provided this is not against the child’s interests (TMK Art. 324(3)). The court warns the parents of this in the divorce judgment and in the contact order (TMK Art. 182(2), 324(3)).

## Does the contact order have to be final?

Under the Code of Civil Procedure, an appeal does not as a rule stop enforcement, but decisions on the law of persons, family law and rights in rem over immovable property cannot be enforced until they are final (HMK Art. 350(2), 367(2)). Contact is regulated in the family law book of the TMK (Art. 323 et seq.). On the wording of the statute, a judgment setting or changing contact is therefore enforced by the directorate only once it is final.

Interim orders are different. The Child Protection Act lists “judgments or interim orders” (ilam veya tedbir kararları) as the decisions the directorate enforces (Art. 41/A, Act No. 5395). Contact ordered while the case is pending is therefore enforced without waiting for finality.

The statute does not expressly say what applies between the judgment and finality. This point is disputed: there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision verified for this article, and what follows is the author’s own view. To avoid a gap, it is sensible to ask the court to state expressly in the judgment that the interim contact continues until the judgment becomes final. How the court rules depends on the case.

## Enforcement through the Directorate of Judicial Support and Victim Services

Act No. 7343 (Official Gazette of 30 November 2021, No. 31675) repealed Articles 25, 25/a, 25/b and 341 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Act (İİK) on the handover of children and moved the subject to Part Four of the Child Protection Act No. 5395. The details are in the Regulation on the enforcement of judgments and interim orders on child handover and contact (Çocuk Teslimi ve Çocukla Kişisel İlişki Kurulmasına İlişkin İlam ve Tedbir Kararlarının Yerine Getirilmesine Dair Yönetmelik), published in the Official Gazette of 4 August 2022, No. 31913. The Act provided for the new system to apply nationwide no later than one year after the Regulation entered into force (Act No. 5395, provisional Art. 2(2)).

The procedure works like this:

1. If the other parent does not comply voluntarily, the parent entitled to contact applies to the directorate where the child is domiciled (Art. 41/A(4), 41/C(1)). Where there is no directorate, a civil court registry designated by the Ministry does this work (Art. 41/A(3)).
2. The directorate contacts the obligated parent and asks them to bring the child to a set place. If they cannot be reached or do not bring the child, a handover order (teslim emri) is served on them; it also covers later contact dates (Art. 41/C(2), (3)).
3. The handover takes place at a venue chosen by the directorate, with a specialist or teacher present (Art. 41/A(5), 41/D).
4. After the order is served, the entitled parent must give at least 48 hours’ notice each time that they will collect the child (Art. 41/C(7)). If, having given notice, they fail to appear without good reason twice in a row or three times in a year, the file is closed and the order lapses (Art. 41/C(12)).

These proceedings are exempt from court fees, and costs other than lawyers’ fees are paid by the Ministry of Justice (Art. 41/H).

A person who breaches the handover order or prevents it being carried out faces **disciplinary detention (disiplin hapsi) of three to ten days**, on a complaint filed within one month (Art. 41/F(2)). The entitled parent who does not bring the child back at the end of the contact period faces disciplinary detention of up to three months (Art. 41/F(3)). The family court where the directorate sits hears these complaints (Art. 41/F(4)). Once final, detention is enforced by the chief public prosecutor’s office, and it can no longer be enforced two years after becoming final (Art. 41/F(6)).

Steps taken by the directorate can be challenged before the family court within one week of learning of them or being served. The court’s decision can be objected to within two weeks, a period extended by Act No. 7499; the decision on the objection is final (Art. 41/E).

If the child refuses contact, the specialist or teacher first speaks with the child and each parent separately, records the reasons and draws up a plan to restore contact (Regulation, Art. 39).

## If a parent lives in Germany or the Netherlands

**Enforcement in Turkey.** The directorate where the child is domiciled is responsible. If the child has no domicile in Turkey, the directorate of the place where the child is in Turkey is responsible (Regulation, Art. 11). This matters, for example, for a child who is in Turkey on holiday and has no domicile there. If the child moves, the file goes automatically to the new directorate (Act No. 5395, Art. 41/C(10)). A parent travelling from abroad should plan around the 48-hour notice and the consequences of not turning up (Art. 41/C(7), (12)).

**Video calls.** Some orders set contact by video call. According to the Ministry of Justice opinion of 17 March 2023, such contact cannot be enforced by the directorate if the obligated parent does not cooperate voluntarily, because neither the Act nor the Regulation provides a procedure or the sanction of disciplinary detention for it. The opinion is an administrative view and does not bind the courts, but a parent abroad should know about it before accepting a video-only arrangement.

**Child taken abroad.** The 1980 Hague Child Abduction Convention and Turkey’s implementing Act No. 5717 cover not only return but also the exercise of rights of access (Act No. 5717, Art. 1, 11). Such orders are also enforced under Part Four of Act No. 5395, without serving a handover order (Act No. 5717, Art. 18(1)). See [Hague Convention child abduction: Turkey and return](/en/publications/hague-convention-child-abduction-turkey).

**Cases in Germany or the Netherlands.** If the child lives in Germany and the case is decided there, German law applies; § 1684 BGB gives the child a right to contact with each parent and makes contact both a right and a duty of each parent. In the Netherlands, contact is governed by Book 1, Title 15 of the Burgerlijk Wetboek (Omgang en informatie, Art. 1:377a et seq. BW). Whether a Turkish court has jurisdiction and whether a Turkish order is recognised there are separate questions. For proceedings and enforcement in Germany or the Netherlands, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country or the competent authority, such as the Jugendamt in Germany.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Turkish contact order have to be final before it is enforced?

A judgment does; family law decisions cannot be enforced until final (HMK Art. 350(2), 367(2)). An interim order made during the case is enforced by the directorate without waiting for finality (Act No. 5395, Art. 41/A).

### What happens if the child does not want to go?

The specialist or teacher talks with the child and each parent separately, records the reasons and prepares a plan (Regulation, Art. 39). If the obstacle continues, changing the contact arrangement may be considered (TMK Art. 183).

### What happens to a parent who blocks contact?

Despite a handover order, they face three to ten days of disciplinary detention on a complaint filed within one month (Act No. 5395, Art. 41/F(2)). Persistent obstruction can also lead to a change of custody (TMK Art. 324(3)).

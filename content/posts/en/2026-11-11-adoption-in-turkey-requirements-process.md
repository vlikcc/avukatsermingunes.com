To adopt a minor in Turkey you must have cared for and raised the child for one year, be at least 18 years older than the child, and the adoption must be in the child's interest (Articles 305 and 308 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). Married couples adopt only jointly and only after five years of marriage or once they are 30; a single person can adopt from the age of 30. The adoption (evlat edinme) is created by a decision of the family court (aile mahkemesi) (Articles 306, 307 and 315 TMK).

I checked the provisions below in November 2026 against the current text in UYAP Mevzuat, the legislation database of the Turkish Ministry of Justice (TMK recorded on 31 July 2026; the last statute in its amendment list is Law No. 7589). The latest change to the adoption rules is Law No. 7531, which rewrote the paragraph of Article 314 on the civil register entry (in force since 14 November 2024).

## Adoption requirements in Turkey for a minor

A minor can be adopted only if the adopter has cared for and raised the child for one year (Article 305(1) TMK). The adoption must in every case serve the child's interest and must not unfairly harm the interests of the adopter's other children (Article 305(2)).

### Married couples, single people and stepchild adoption

- **Married couples:** Spouses can adopt only jointly, and unmarried people cannot adopt jointly. The spouses must have been married for at least five years or have reached the age of 30 (Article 306(1)–(2) TMK). The Turkish Ministry of Family and Social Services explains this on its application page as both spouses being 30.
- **Single people:** An unmarried person who is 30 or older can adopt alone (Article 307(1) TMK).
- **Married but adopting alone:** A spouse aged 30 or older can adopt alone if he or she proves that a joint adoption is impossible because the other spouse permanently lacks capacity, has been missing for more than two years, or the spouses have lived apart under a court decision for more than two years (Article 307(2)).
- **Stepchild:** One spouse can adopt the other's child if they have been married for at least two years or the adopting spouse is 30 or older (Article 306(3) TMK).

### Age gap and age limit

The child must be at least 18 years younger than the adopter (Article 308(1) TMK). The TMK sets no upper age limit and no maximum age gap; when deciding which family a child is placed with, the Ministry also applies its own criteria.

## Consent: the child, the parents and the guardianship authority

A child capable of judgement cannot be adopted without his or her consent. A child under guardianship can be adopted with the permission of the guardianship authorities, whether or not capable of judgement (Article 308(2)–(3) TMK).

Both parents must consent. Consent is declared orally or in writing at the court of the place where the child or the parents live and is recorded in minutes; it is valid even if the adopters are not named or not yet chosen (Article 309). It cannot be given before six weeks have passed since the birth, and it can be withdrawn the same way within six weeks of being recorded. Consent given again after a withdrawal is final (Article 310).

A parent's consent is not needed if it is unknown who the parent is or where he or she has lived for a long time, if the parent permanently lacks capacity, or if the parent has not properly cared for the child (Article 311). The court decides this question (Article 312).

## Adult adoption in Turkey

With the express consent of the adopter's descendants, an adult or a person under guardianship can be adopted only in three cases (Article 313(1) TMK):

1. the person permanently needs help because of a physical or mental disability and the adopter has cared for him or her for at least five years,
2. the adopter cared for and raised the person for at least five years as a child,
3. there are other good reasons and the person has lived with the adopter as a family for at least five years.

A married person can be adopted only with the spouse's consent (Article 313(2)). Otherwise the rules for minors apply by analogy (Article 313(3)).

## Adoption process in Turkey

The adoption decision is made by the court of the adopter's place of residence or, in a joint adoption, of one spouse's residence; the adoption takes effect with that decision (Article 315(1) TMK). The family court is competent (Law No. 4787, Article 4(1)). Before deciding, the court examines all essential circumstances, hears the adopter and the child and, if needed, obtains expert opinion; the views of the adopter's descendants are also considered (Article 316).

Only institutions authorised by the President may act as intermediaries in the adoption of minors (Article 320 TMK). According to the Ministry's page, Turkish citizens resident in Turkey apply to the Ministry's provincial directorate (il müdürlüğü) where they live.

## Effects of adoption: surname, civil register, inheritance

- **Parental rights:** The parents' rights and duties pass to the adopter (Article 314(1) TMK).
- **Surname and first name:** An adopted minor takes the adopter's surname, and the adopter may give the child a new first name. An adult may choose at the time of adoption whether to take the adopter's surname (Article 314(3)).
- **Civil register:** For a child not capable of judgement, the adopting spouses are entered as mother and father, or the single adopter as mother or father; for other adoptees this applies on request (Article 314(4), as amended by Law No. 7531). The court notifies the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) within ten days, and the child's record is moved to the adopter's family register (Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490, Article 29). Adoption records may not be disclosed without a court order or the adoptee's request (Article 314, last paragraph, TMK).
- **Inheritance:** The adopted child and his or her descendants inherit from the adopter like blood relatives, and the child keeps inheritance rights in the birth family. The adopter and the adopter's relatives do not inherit from the adopted child (Article 500 TMK). On the shares: [inheritance shares in Turkey](/en/publications/inheritance-shares-turkey-spouse-children).
- **Citizenship:** A foreign minor adopted by a Turkish citizen can acquire Turkish citizenship from the date of the decision, unless national security or public order stands in the way (Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901, Article 17).

Can an adoption be reversed? The Code provides for annulment of the adoption: a person whose consent was not obtained without a legal reason (Article 317), or, for other substantive defects, the public prosecutor or any interested person (Article 318), may sue. The right to sue lapses one year after learning of the ground (Article 319). The former five-year outer limit was annulled by the Constitutional Court (27 December 2012, E. 2012/35, K. 2012/203).

## Living abroad: applicable law and recognition

### Applicable law

Where there is a foreign element, the requirements are governed for each party by his or her national law at the time of adoption, the other spouse's consent by the national laws of both spouses together, and the effects by the adopter's national law (Article 18 of the Private International Law Act No. 5718, MÖHUK). More on this: [Article 18 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-18-mohuk-adoption-turkey).

### Adopting a child from Turkey: the Hague Convention

Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are parties to the Hague Convention of 29 May 1993 on Protection of Children and Co-operation in Respect of Intercountry Adoption (in force for Turkey since 1 September 2004, Germany since 1 March 2002, the Netherlands since 1 October 1998; HCCH status table). It applies where a child habitually resident in one Contracting State is moved to another Contracting State after adoption or for the purpose of adoption (Article 2). If the child already lives with you in Germany, a stepchild adoption there will therefore normally fall outside the Convention. Turkey's Central Authority is the Directorate General of Child Services of the Ministry of Family and Social Services. According to the Ministry, Turkish citizens living abroad apply to the central authority of the receiving state, that is, the country where they live.

### Recognition of a foreign adoption in Turkey

An adoption carried out before foreign authorities is valid in Turkey if it complies with Turkish law in its essential requirements; however, foreign decisions and documents on adoption can be acted on in Turkey only after a Turkish court has recognised (tanıma) or enforced (tenfiz) them (Law No. 5490, Article 30). This also applies to non-contentious decisions (Article 58(1)–(2) MÖHUK); the family court hears these cases (Law No. 4787, Article 4(2)). Three decisions of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay):

- **Yargıtay, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2021/5783, K. 2022/2231, 9 March 2022.** That the foreign decision did not require the one-year care period of Article 305(1) TMK was, in view of the child's best interests, not a manifest breach of Turkish public policy; the dismissal on that ground was reversed.
- **Yargıtay, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2022/4373, K. 2022/5899, 15 June 2022.** In an adult adoption, the missing five-year period of Article 313 was not a manifest breach either; the parties had lived together as a family since 2013.
- **Yargıtay, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5584, K. 2025/11094, 11 December 2025.** Recognition cannot be granted without a document certified by the authorities of the issuing state showing that the decision is final, with a certified translation.

Under the Hague Convention, an adoption certified by the competent authority as made in accordance with the Convention is recognised by operation of law in the other Contracting States (Article 23(1)) and can be refused only if it is manifestly contrary to public policy (Article 24). I could not find any official statement on whether the Turkish civil register still requires recognition under Article 30(2) of Law No. 5490 for such an adoption. There is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on this point; in my view a recognition action is the safer route, but that is my assessment.

In Germany the family court (Familiengericht) pronounces the adoption on the adopter's application (§ 1752 BGB); in the Netherlands adoption is governed by Title 12 of Book 1 of the Burgerlijk Wetboek (Articles 1:227–1:232). For the requirements and procedure there, please contact the Jugendamt, the competent authority or a lawyer qualified in that country. More on related topics: [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the age limit for adoption in Turkey?

Thirty for a single person; for married couples five years of marriage or age 30 (Articles 306(2) and 307(1) TMK). The age gap must be at least 18 years (Article 308(1)); the TMK sets no upper limit.

### Can a single person adopt in Turkey?

Yes, from the age of 30 (Article 307(1) TMK).

### What are the requirements for stepchild adoption?

At least two years of marriage or the adopting spouse being 30 (Article 306(3) TMK). The child's parents must consent (Article 309); exceptions are in Article 311.

### Does an adopted child inherit?

Yes, and the child keeps inheritance rights in the birth family (Article 500 TMK).

### Can an adopted child keep his or her own surname?

A minor takes the adopter's surname. An adult chooses at the time of adoption whether to take it (Article 314(3) TMK).

Article 51 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets the domicile (yerleşim yeri) of a legal person: unless the founding document says otherwise, it is the place where the legal person's affairs are managed. It matters to anyone in a dispute with a Turkish association or foundation, to its members, and to people abroad who sit on the board of an association in Türkiye.

## Article 51 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **D. Domicile**
>
> Article 51 – Unless the founding document provides otherwise, the domicile of a legal person is the place where its affairs are managed.

Turkish original:

> **D. Yerleşim yeri**
>
> Madde 51- Tüzel kişinin yerleşim yeri, kuruluş belgesinde başka bir hüküm bulunmadıkça işlerinin yönetildiği yerdir.

As of November 2026, Article 51 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 49 of the former Civil Code No. 743 and does not change the rule. The heading "İkametgâh" became "Yerleşim yeri", and "founding document" (kuruluş belgesi) replaced "statutes" (nizamname) so that every kind of legal person is covered. The place-of-management rule applies where the founding document names no domicile, until that gap is filled, and so extends to legal persons the principle that every person must have a domicile (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Where is the domicile recorded for associations and foundations?

For associations, the details are in the Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu). It defines the association's domicile as the place where its management activities are carried out, and the association's seat (dernek merkezi) as the province or district in which that domicile lies (Art. 2(b) and (c)). The statutes must state the association's name and seat (Art. 4(a)). Law No. 5253 removed "domicile" from the mandatory contents of the statutes listed in Article 58 TMK. A change of domicile is reported to the associations office (dernekler birimi) in the same way as a change in the association's bodies (Art. 23 Law No. 5253).

Foundations work differently. The foundation deed states the foundation's domicile (Art. 106 TMK), and the foundation is entered in the register kept at the court of its domicile (Art. 104 TMK). An action to remove foundation managers is also heard by the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) of the foundation's domicile (Art. 10 Foundations Law No. 5737).

One difference from individuals: no one can have more than one domicile at a time, but that rule does not apply to commercial and industrial establishments (Art. 19(2) and (3) TMK). The rules for individuals are covered in the post on [Article 19 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-19-turkish-civil-code-domicile).

### Where do you sue a legal person?

The court of general jurisdiction is the court of the domicile of the defendant, whether an individual or a legal person, on the date the action is filed; domicile is determined under the TMK (Art. 6 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). For a legal person, that leads to Article 51. The HMK adds two rules. For claims arising from the dealings of a branch, the court where the branch is located also has jurisdiction (Art. 14(1)). For claims arising from the partnership or membership relationship, brought against a partner or member or by a partner or member in that capacity against the others, the court of the legal person's seat has exclusive jurisdiction (Art. 14(2)). Articles 6 and 14 HMK are unchanged as of November 2026 (last entry in the table: Constitutional Court decision E. 2025/124, K. 2025/203 on Art. 362, effective 20 October 2026).

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you live in Germany or the Netherlands and want to sue your association in Türkiye over a membership matter, you must go to the court at the association's seat. That jurisdiction is exclusive: the court examines it of its own motion until the end of the proceedings, and either party may raise lack of jurisdiction at any time (Art. 19(1) HMK). The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts is likewise determined by the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction ([Article 40 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-40-mohuk-international-jurisdiction-turkey)).

Whether a foreign association has legal capacity is a separate question: under Article 9(4) MÖHUK (Turkish private international law act) it is governed by the law of the administrative seat named in its statutes. Article 51 TMK does not answer that question; it sets the domicile of a legal person under Turkish law. For the general framework, see the post on [Article 47 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-47-turkish-civil-code-legal-personality).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is a legal person domiciled under Turkish law?

At the place named in its founding document (association statutes, foundation deed); if the document is silent, where its affairs are managed (Art. 51 TMK).

### Which court hears a claim against an association in Türkiye?

As a rule, the court of the association's domicile (Art. 6 HMK). For disputes arising from membership, the court at the association's seat has exclusive jurisdiction (Art. 14(2) HMK).

### What must an association do when it moves?

Report the change of domicile to the associations office (Art. 23 Law No. 5253).

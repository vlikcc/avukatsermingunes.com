If you gave up Turkish citizenship with a renunciation permit (çıkma izni), you can regain it without living in Turkey. The only condition is that there is no national security obstacle, and the decision is taken by the Turkish Ministry of Interior (art. 13(1)(a) of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901). From abroad, you apply at a Turkish consulate.

This article is for Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holders who want to become Turkish citizens again. What the Mavi Kart is and which rights it gives is covered in [Blue Card Turkey (Mavi Kart)](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights). Here I look at the conditions, the procedure and the consequences of regaining citizenship. As of November 2026, the last amendment to Law No. 5901 concerned art. 27 (Law No. 7196, in force since 24/12/2019); I found no later change to the provisions on regaining citizenship (arts. 13 and 14).

## Who can regain Turkish citizenship?

The law distinguishes by how you lost your citizenship. One route requires no residence in Turkey; the other requires three years.

Unofficial translation of art. 13:

> (1) Provided that there is no situation constituting an obstacle in terms of national security, the persons listed below may regain Turkish citizenship by decision of the Ministry, regardless of how long they have resided in Turkey:
>
> a) Persons who lost Turkish citizenship by obtaining permission to renounce it.
>
> b) Persons who lost Turkish citizenship through their mother or father and did not exercise their right of option within the period set out in Article 21.

Original Turkish text:

> (1) Millî güvenlik bakımından engel teşkil edecek bir hali bulunmamak şartıyla aşağıda belirtilen kişiler Türkiye'de ikamet etme süresine bakılmaksızın, Türk vatandaşlığını Bakanlık kararıyla yeniden kazanabilirler.
>
> a) Çıkma izni almak suretiyle Türk vatandaşlığını kaybedenler.
>
> b) Ana veya babalarına bağlı olarak Türk vatandaşlığını kaybedenlerden 21 inci maddede öngörülen süre içerisinde seçme hakkını kullanmayanlar.

If you obtained a renunciation permit in order to naturalise in Germany or the Netherlands, you fall under (a). That covers most Mavi Kart holders.

Point (b) is for people who lost citizenship as children together with a parent. One spouse's renunciation does not affect the other; children lose citizenship with the renouncing parent if that parent asks for it and the other parent consents (art. 27(2)). These children can become Turkish again by exercising a right of option (seçme hakkı) within **three years** of reaching majority (art. 21). If they missed that window, art. 13(1)(b) is open to them without any residence requirement.

Three years' residence in Turkey is required under art. 14, for people whose citizenship was withdrawn by presidential decision under art. 29 and for people who left by option under art. 34. I do not go into that route here.

People who lost citizenship under art. 25(a), (ç), (d) or (e) of the former Law No. 403 can also be readmitted without residence, by Ministry decision (art. 43(1)).

## Applying at the consulate: which documents?

From abroad you apply at the Turkish mission, in person or through a power of attorney that expressly covers this (art. 37). Article 23 of the Regulation on the Implementation of the Turkish Citizenship Law lists the documents for the file sent to the Ministry:

- the application form,
- your Turkish civil registry extract, which the authority retrieves from the system,
- the passport or similar document of the state whose national you now are,
- a marital status certificate and documents on any change in marital status since you lost Turkish citizenship,
- if married, a document showing your family link to your spouse and children,
- a document showing any change in your personal details (for example your surname) since the loss,
- the receipt for the service fee (hizmet bedeli).

The fee is set jointly by the Ministry of Interior and the Ministry of Finance (art. 45(1)). People with no income under the Turkish Income Tax Law, and their minor children processed with them, pay nothing (art. 45(2)). I could not confirm the 2026 amount from an official source, so ask the consulate. The consulate will also tell you which foreign documents need an apostille and translation.

The Ministry checks for a national security obstacle by requesting an archive search from the Directorate General of Security (Regulation art. 35(3)). Neither the law nor the regulation sets a processing time.

## The decision, the Mavi Kart and the civil registry

Regaining citizenship takes effect from the date of the Ministry's decision (art. 20(1)). From then on you are a Turkish citizen again and can obtain Turkish identity documents.

You no longer need the Mavi Kart. Under art. 55(2) of the Regulation, the authority issuing your Turkish identity card takes it back and destroys it. Your other citizenship is not a problem under Turkish law; multiple citizenship is noted in your registry entry (art. 44).

As a citizen you get back the right to vote and stand for election in Turkey. At the same time, the exemption from military service in art. 28(2) ends (see below).

## What about your spouse and children?

Regaining citizenship does not affect your spouse's nationality (art. 20(2)). Children in your custody (velayet) acquire Turkish citizenship with you if the other parent consents; without consent, a court in the country of the parent's habitual residence decides (art. 20(2)). Children who are not processed with you and apply later, after reaching majority, fall under art. 11, the general naturalisation rule for foreigners (art. 20(3)). Art. 11 requires, among other things, five years' uninterrupted residence in Turkey before the application.

So think beforehand about whether to include minor children in your application. Children born within marriage after you regain citizenship are Turkish by birth (art. 7(1)).

## Military service and inheritance: what changes

**Military service.** Mavi Kart holders have no military service obligation (art. 28(2)); for men this ends when they regain citizenship. Under art. 43(2) of Military Service Law No. 7179, the military service procedure for people who regain Turkish citizenship after losing it is carried out under the provisions that fit their situation, taking their previous military service record into account. The rule in paragraph 1 that people who are 22 or older in the year they are naturalised are treated as having served applies, by its wording, to people who acquire citizenship later in life; paragraph 2 does not refer to it for people who regain citizenship. Whether it applies to them is not clear from the text, and I found no binding court decision on the point; it is a question of interpretation. Check your military status with the consulate or the military recruitment office (askerlik şubesi) before you apply. If you live abroad, the rules in [Turkish military service paid exemption](/en/publications/turkish-military-service-paid-exemption) may become relevant again.

**Inheritance and family law.** For Turkish courts, your national law changes. Under art. 4(1)(b) MÖHUK, Turkish law applies to people with several nationalities who are also Turkish citizens. Under Turkish conflict rules, your estate will in principle be governed by Turkish law (art. 20(1) MÖHUK); for a German Mavi Kart holder it was German law. See [Article 4 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-4-mohuk-dual-nationality-turkey). Which law German or Dutch authorities apply is a matter for their own law.

## Effect on German and Dutch citizenship

Whether regaining Turkish citizenship affects your German or Dutch citizenship depends on German or Dutch law, not on Turkish law. I only give the general framework from official sources here.

**Germany.** According to the Federal Ministry of the Interior, the reformed nationality law entered into force on 27 June 2024. In the current Nationality Act (Staatsangehörigkeitsgesetz, StAG), § 25, which provided for loss of German citizenship on acquiring a foreign one, is shown as “weggefallen” (repealed), and § 17 no longer lists this ground for loss. In general terms, a German who regains Turkish citizenship today does not lose German citizenship for that reason. § 28 StAG on voluntarily joining foreign armed forces is a separate provision. If you regained Turkish citizenship before 27 June 2024, the earlier law applies to you. For your own case, contact the German nationality authority (Staatsangehörigkeitsbehörde) or a lawyer admitted in Germany.

**Netherlands.** According to the Dutch government, a Dutch national who voluntarily acquires another nationality in principle loses Dutch nationality automatically. There are three exceptions: being born in that country and living there when you acquire its nationality; having lived there for at least five uninterrupted years before reaching majority; or acquiring the nationality of your spouse or registered partner. If you are Dutch, speak to the IND or a lawyer admitted in the Netherlands before taking this step, or you may lose Dutch nationality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to live in Turkey to regain Turkish citizenship?

Not if you renounced with a permit. If there is no national security obstacle, the Ministry decides without any residence requirement (Law 5901 art. 13(1)(a)).

### Which documents do I need?

The application form, your foreign passport, a marital status certificate, documents on any changes in marital status or personal details, proof of your family link to spouse and children, and the fee receipt (Regulation art. 23). The consulate may ask for more.

### Do I have to do military service after regaining citizenship?

The Mavi Kart exemption ends. The procedure depends on your previous military service record (Law 7179 art. 43(2)). Check with the consulate first.

### Will I lose German citizenship?

Under the current StAG, acquiring a foreign nationality is no longer a ground for loss (§ 25 repealed). The competent German authority can confirm this for your case.

### Do I have to return my Mavi Kart?

Yes. It is taken back by the authority that issues your identity card (Regulation art. 55(2)).

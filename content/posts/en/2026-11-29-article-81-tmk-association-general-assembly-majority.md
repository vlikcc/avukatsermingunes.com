Article 81 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets the majority with which the general assembly (genel kurul) of an association (dernek) takes decisions. Decisions need an absolute majority (salt çoğunluk) of the members attending; amending the statute (tüzük değişikliği) and dissolving the association (fesih) need two thirds of those attending. The article matters to the meeting's presiding board (divan heyeti), which runs the vote, and to members who want to know whether a resolution was actually passed.

## Article 81 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **7. General assembly decisions**
>
> **a. Majority required for decisions**
>
> Article 81 – General assembly decisions are taken by an absolute majority of the members attending the meeting. However, decisions to amend the statute and to dissolve the association may only be taken by a two-thirds majority of the members attending the meeting.

Original Turkish text:

> **7. Genel kurul kararları**
>
> **a. Karar yeter sayısı**
>
> Madde 81- Genel kurul kararları, toplantıya katılan üyelerin salt çoğunluğuyla alınır. Şu kadar ki, tüzük değişikliği ve derneğin feshi kararları, ancak toplantıya katılan üyelerin üçte iki çoğunluğuyla alınabilir.

As of November 2026, Article 81 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The reasoning states that the general assembly decides by an absolute majority of the members attending, and that a two-thirds majority of those attending is required for amending the statute and for dissolution (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Commentary

### Quorum and voting majority

The two numbers are measured against different bases. [Article 78 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-78-tmk-association-general-assembly-quorum) says how many of the members entitled to attend must be present for the meeting to open (the quorum, toplantı yeter sayısı). Article 81 then sets how many votes a decision needs, counted against the members who actually attend (karar yeter sayısı).

Take an amendment of the statute in an association with 100 members entitled to attend:

1. At least 67 members must be present for the meeting to open (Art. 78(2)).
2. If 70 members attend, the amendment needs at least 47 votes (Art. 81).
3. If the first meeting lacked a quorum and 20 members come to the second one (provided the minimum in Art. 78(2) is met), 14 votes are enough. No majority is needed to open the second meeting, but the two-thirds share in Article 81 stays the same.

All other decisions need an absolute majority, meaning more than half of those attending: with 51 present, at least 26 votes.

### Minutes, filing and sanctions

The matters discussed and the decisions taken are recorded in minutes (tutanak), signed by the chair and the secretaries of the meeting (Associations Regulation, Dernekler Yönetmeliği, Art. 15). If the statute was amended, the minutes, the old and new wording of the amended articles and the final text of the statute must be filed with the local administrative authority (mülki idare amirliği) within 45 days of the meeting (Regulation Art. 17(1)). Rigging votes or the vote count is a criminal offence under Article 32(1)(e) of the Associations Act No. 5253. Challenges to decisions that breach the law or the statute are governed by Article 83 TMK.

### Disputed points

On the following questions the law has no express rule, and no binding court decision was found for this article. What follows are opinions; which one a court would adopt in a particular dispute is uncertain.

- **Abstentions.** The law requires a majority of the members “attending”. In the author's view, members who abstain or do not vote therefore still count, and in effect their position works like a no vote.
- **A different majority in the statute.** The statute must set out how votes are cast and decisions taken (Associations Act Art. 4(1)(e)). Whether it may set majorities other than those in Article 81 is not expressly regulated.
- **Decisions without a meeting.** Whether the Article 81 majority is enough for decisions taken without a meeting or without notice, with all members taking part, is covered in the article on [Article 76 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-76-tmk-association-decisions-without-meeting).
- **Members barred from voting.** Whether a member who may not vote under Article 82 is counted is covered in the article on [Article 82 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-82-tmk-association-voting-exclusion).

## Points for members living abroad

Voting by proxy is not allowed; each member votes in person (Art. 69(1) TMK). A member living abroad only counts if they attend. If the association has decided to hold the general assembly electronically, members log in with a secure electronic signature or two-factor authentication, and all rules for in-person meetings apply there as well (Regulation Art. 15/A).

## Frequently asked questions

### What majority does a Turkish association's general assembly need?

An absolute majority of the members attending; for amending the statute or dissolution, two thirds of those attending (Art. 81 TMK).

### What majority is needed to amend an association's statute?

Two thirds of the members entitled to attend must be present for the meeting to open, a requirement that falls away at the second meeting (Art. 78(2)). The decision needs two thirds of those attending (Art. 81).

### Are the quorum and the voting majority the same thing?

No. The quorum is counted against the members entitled to attend, the voting majority against those actually attending.

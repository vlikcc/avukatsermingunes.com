Under Turkish law, the relationship by marriage (affinity, kayın hısımlığı) does not end with divorce or with the death of a spouse. Article 18 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says how this in-law relationship arises and that it outlasts the marriage that created it. It matters to anyone asking whether the legal tie to a former spouse’s family disappears after the divorce.

## Article 18 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Relationship by marriage**
>
> Article 18 – A spouse is related by marriage to the blood relatives of the other spouse in the same line and to the same degree.
>
> The relationship by marriage does not end when the marriage that created it ends.

Turkish original:

> **2. Kayın hısımlığı**
>
> Madde 18- Eşlerden biri ile diğer eşin kan hısımları, aynı tür ve dereceden kayın hısımları olur.
>
> Kayın hısımlığı, kendisini meydana getiren evliliğin sona ermesiyle ortadan kalkmaz.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 18; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the provision corresponds to Article 18 of the former Code. The old term “sıhrî hısımlık” was replaced by “kayın hısımlığı”, the expression in common use. The former second paragraph (“Evlenmenin zevaliyle, sıhrî hısımlık zail olmaz.”) was rewritten to state its purpose more clearly (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

The line and degree of affinity are taken over unchanged from the spouse’s blood relationship. How the degree is counted is explained in our post on [Article 17 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-17-turkish-civil-code-blood-relationship-degree). So a mother-in-law or father-in-law is related to a daughter-in-law or son-in-law in the first degree of the direct line, and a brother-in-law or sister-in-law in the second degree of the collateral line. A stepmother from your father’s later marriage is also related to you by marriage in the first degree, because you are a descendant of her spouse.

Which rules continue after a divorce? The statutes say so expressly:

- **Marriage ban:** Even after the marriage that created the affinity has ended, a spouse may not marry the other spouse’s ascendants or descendants (Article 129(2) TMK). Such a marriage is absolutely void (Article 145(4) TMK).
- **Exclusion of judges:** A judge may not hear the case of a blood relative or relative by marriage up to and including the third degree, and for affinity the statute adds “even if the marriage that created it has ended” (Article 34(1)(d) of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK).
- **Refusal to testify:** Relatives by marriage up to and including the third degree may refuse to testify even after the marriage has ended (Article 248(1)(d) HMK).

The marriage ban covers only the direct line. Article 129 does not mention collateral in-laws, so the Turkish Civil Code does not prohibit a divorced or widowed person from marrying the former spouse’s brother or sister.

Affinity does not carry every consequence of blood relationship. The duty to support relatives applies only between ascendants, descendants and siblings (Article 364 TMK); in-laws are not listed. They are not each other’s statutory heirs either.

## Practical points if you live abroad

Capacity to marry and the conditions of marriage are governed by each party’s national law at the time of the marriage (Article 13(1) MÖHUK). For a Turkish citizen, the ban in Article 129(2) TMK therefore applies from the Turkish point of view even if the new marriage takes place in Germany or the Netherlands. A divorce granted abroad makes no difference here, since the affinity survives the marriage anyway.

If a former mother-in-law, brother-in-law or sister-in-law is named as a witness in divorce or inheritance proceedings in Turkey, they keep the right to refuse to testify after the divorce (Article 248(1)(d) HMK).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the in-law relationship end with divorce under Turkish law?

No. Under Article 18(2) TMK it continues when the marriage ends by divorce or death.

### Can you marry your former spouse’s sibling in Turkey?

Article 129(2) TMK prohibits marriage only with the former spouse’s ascendants and descendants. Siblings of the former spouse are collateral in-laws and fall outside this ban.

### What degree of affinity is a mother-in-law?

First degree of the direct line, just as your spouse is related to their mother in the first degree (Articles 17 and 18 TMK).

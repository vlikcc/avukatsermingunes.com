In Türkiye you can apply for a declaration of absence (gaiplik kararı) only once at least one year has passed since the mortal danger, or at least five years since the last news. The court then calls on anyone with information about the person by public notice and waits at least six months from the first notice. Both rules are in Article 33 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK). The general conditions, who may apply and which court is competent are covered in [Article 32 TMK – Declaration of absence in Turkey](/en/publications/legislation/article-32-tmk-declaration-of-absence-turkey); this post deals only with the time limits and how the proceedings run.

## Article 33 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Procedure**
>
> Article 33 – A declaration of absence may be applied for only if at least one year has passed since the mortal danger or at least five years since the date of the last news.
>
> The court shall, by duly made public notice, call on those who have information about the person to be declared absent to provide it within a set period.
>
> This period is at least six months, starting from the day of the first notice.

Turkish original:

> **2. Yargılama usulü**
>
> Madde 33- Gaiplik kararının istenebilmesi için, ölüm tehlikesinin üzerinden en az bir yıl veya son haber tarihinin üzerinden en az beş yıl geçmiş olması gerekir.
>
> Mahkeme, gaipliğine karar verilecek kişi hakkında bilgisi bulunan kimseleri, belirli bir sürede bilgi vermeleri için usulüne göre yapılan ilânla çağırır.
>
> Bu süre, ilk ilânın yapıldığı günden başlayarak en az altı aydır.

As of November 2026 the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr carries no amendment note for this article. The last law in the amendment table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026), which does not touch Article 33.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 32 of the former Civil Code and was carried over unchanged apart from simpler wording. The one change replaces "gaip hakkında" (about the absent person) with "gaipliğine karar verilecek kişi hakkında" (about the person to be declared absent). The reasoning explains that "gaiplik" is the name of the legal institution and the declaration is the decision given about that person. (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly.)

## Explanation

### One year or five years?

The two periods match the two cases in Article 32. If the person disappeared in mortal danger, one year runs from the event. If nothing has been heard from them for a long time, five years run from the date of the last news. Because the law says the declaration may only be "applied for" after that, the period must have passed when you file.

Which case applies matters beyond the waiting time. The declaration takes effect from the day of the mortal danger or the last news (Article 35(2)). The period for which heirs must give security also depends on it: five years for mortal danger, fifteen years for long absence of news (Article 584(2)). The application should therefore prove the date of the danger or the last news with documents.

If death is practically certain, because the person disappeared in circumstances that make death certain, none of these periods applies. The presumption of death is used instead and the death is registered on the order of the administrative official ([Article 31 TMK – Presumption of death](/en/publications/legislation/article-31-turkish-civil-code-presumption-of-death)).

### Public notice and the six-month period

The court does not decide straight away. It calls on anyone who knows something about the person, by public notice (ilan), to come forward within a set period. That period starts with the first notice and cannot be shorter than six months; since the law says "at least", the court may set a longer one.

The TMK does not say by what means, in which publication or how many times the notice is given. It only refers to a "duly made" notice. The provisions on non-contentious proceedings in the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (HMK, Articles 382–388) have no specific rule on it either. I therefore give no details on the form here. Follow in the court file when and how notice was given, since the six-month period runs from the first notice.

Put together, the earliest timeline is one year plus at least six months for mortal danger, and five years plus at least six months for long absence of news, in each case plus the time the proceedings themselves take. If the person reappears, sends news, or the date of death is established during the notice period, the application lapses ([Article 34 TMK – When an absence application lapses](/en/publications/legislation/article-34-tmk-absence-application-lapses)). If the notice brings no result, the court declares the person absent (Article 35(1)).

### Non-contentious proceedings under the HMK

A declaration of absence is a non-contentious matter (çekişmesiz yargı, Article 382(2)(a)(4) HMK). The simple procedure applies as far as suits the nature of the matter, and unless otherwise provided the court investigates of its own motion (Article 385(1) and (2) HMK). It is not limited to what the applicant puts forward. Anyone with a legal interest can appeal (istinaf) within two weeks of learning of the decision (Article 387(1) HMK). Unless the law says otherwise, decisions in non-contentious matters do not become res judicata in the substantive sense (Article 388(1) HMK).

As of November 2026, the latest entries in the HMK amendment table on mevzuat.gov.tr are Law No. 7589 (Article 149, in force since 31 October 2026) and a Constitutional Court decision on Article 362; Articles 382–388 are unchanged.

### Registration after the decision

The declaration is entered in the death register on notification by the judge (Article 45 TMK). Under the Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu), courts notify declarations of absence to the local civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) within ten days, and they are entered in the family register (Article 34). A declaration of absence is one of the grounds on which a registry entry is closed (Article 14(1)).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

If a Turkish citizen went missing abroad, the same periods apply, because the declaration of absence follows the person's national law ([Article 11 MÖHUK – Declaration of Absence or Death](/en/publications/legislation/article-11-mohuk-declaration-of-absence)). In calculating the period, the question is usually the date of the last news. Before applying, collect documents showing the date of the last correspondence, the last conversation, or exchanges with authorities where the person was living; they make it easier to show that the five years have passed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a declaration of absence take in Turkey?

The application is possible at the earliest one year after the mortal danger or five years after the last news. After that, a period of at least six months runs from the first public notice (Article 33 TMK). The time the proceedings take comes on top.

### How many times and where is the notice published?

The TMK only speaks of a "duly made" notice and names neither the number of notices nor the publication. The HMK provisions on non-contentious matters do not regulate this either. How notice was given can be seen from the court file.

### Can I apply before the one-year or five-year period ends?

No. The law requires the period to have passed for a declaration to be "applied for"; it must have ended on the date of the application.

There are two deadlines for challenging a cadastral determination (kadastro tespiti) in Turkey. While the cadastral team is still working in the village, you can object at the cadastral office. Once the results are publicly posted, you have **30 days** to file a claim at the cadastral court (kadastro mahkemesi) (Cadastre Law No. 3402, Arts. 9 and 11). After that, the record becomes final. From that point you have **ten years** to bring a claim in the ordinary courts based on a legal ground that existed before the cadastre; after ten years that route closes too (Art. 12).

A field or house in the village that you inherited from a grandparent may have been recorded during the cadastre in the name of a relative or neighbour who lives there. Heirs living in Germany or the Netherlands often find out years later, when they go to the land registry (tapu) to sell or to register the inheritance.

As of November 2026, according to the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr, the latest amendment to the Cadastre Law was made by Law No. 7579 (in force since 22 May 2026). It only concerns Art. 41 on correcting measurement and calculation errors. The rules on posting, the cadastral lawsuit and the ten-year period (Arts. 11, 12 and 24–34) were not changed.

## How the cadastre is carried out

A cadastral region is announced at least one month in advance in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete), on radio or television, and in a local newspaper and on an internet news site in the region (Art. 2(3); form of announcement amended by Law No. 7418, in force since 1 April 2023). The cadastral office announces the start of work in the village or neighbourhood at least 15 days in advance (Art. 4(2)).

Cadastral technicians make the determination. They examine title and tax records, consult at least three local experts and the village head (muhtar), and draw up a record for each parcel (Art. 7). The law decides in whose name a parcel is recorded:

- **Parcel registered in the land registry:** if the registered owner or the heirs are in possession, it is recorded in the owner's name or, if the owner has died, in the heirs' names. Recording it in another possessor's name is possible only in narrow cases, for example with the consent of the owner or heirs given before the technician, or where the possessor proves that he or she acquired the parcel outside the land registry from the owner or heirs and has possessed it as owner, undisputed and without interruption, for at least ten years (Art. 13).
- **Unregistered parcel:** it is recorded in the name of the person who proves undisputed, uninterrupted possession as owner for at least twenty years, subject to the area limits and documentary requirements in the law (Art. 14).

So an unregistered field that a relative has farmed for decades can end up in that relative's name. For a registered parcel, recording it in the name of someone other than the owner and the heirs requires one of the conditions in Art. 13(B).

## Objecting during the survey

Once the record is drawn up, an objection can be filed until the team finishes its work in the area, either with the cadastral technicians or the cadastral office; it is passed to the cadastral commission (Art. 9(1)–(2)). The objection may only concern the validity of the documents applied, and an objection not based on a document is not examined (Art. 9(3)). The commission decides, draws up a new record if needed and announces the result by posting (Art. 10).

Objecting does not take away your right to sue within the posting period (Art. 9(4)).

## Public posting and the 30-day deadline

When the work is complete, the cadastral office posts the results. Art. 11(1) of Law No. 3402:

> Kadastro müdürü, kadastro tutanaklarına göre yapılan tespitlere dayanarak, askı cetvellerini düzenler; bu cetvelleri ve pafta örneklerini, müdüriyette ve ayrıca muhtarın çalışma yerinde 30 gün süre ile ilan ettirir; itirazı olanların ilan süresi içinde kadastro mahkemesinde dava açabileceklerini belirtir. Bu ilanda kadastro harçları da gösterilir.

Unofficial translation: the head of the cadastral office prepares posting lists based on the determinations, has them displayed with map extracts for 30 days at the office and at the village head's office, and states that anyone who disagrees may file a claim at the cadastral court within the posting period. The cadastral fees are also shown in the notice.

For heirs abroad, the last paragraph matters most:

> Bu Kanun gereğince yapılan ilanlar, ilgili gerçek kişilere, kamu ve özel hukuk tüzelkişilerine şahsen tebliğ edilmiş sayılır.

Unofficial translation: notices made under this Law are deemed to have been served personally on the natural persons and the public and private legal entities concerned.

You will not receive a letter; the deadline runs while the list is posted. If no claim is filed within 30 days, the boundaries and determinations become final (Art. 12(1)). The record is entered in the land register with the date of finality shown as the registration date (Art. 12(2)).

## The cadastral lawsuit: court and procedure

The cadastral court hears cases sent by the commission, claims filed within the posting period and cases transferred to it from local courts (Art. 26(1)). Your statement of claim must give the grounds and evidence. If you have not provided them by the first hearing, the claim is deemed not filed and the parcel is registered as determined; you may then sue again in the local civil court within the cut-off period (Art. 28(2) and last paragraph). You are not bound by the grounds and evidence of an earlier objection (Art. 28(3)).

Two points matter for heirs:

- One heir can sue without the consent of the others and pursue the case alone (Art. 29(2)).
- The court hears the case even if a party does not appear, and cadastral courts do not observe the judicial recess (Art. 29(1) and last paragraph).

Appeals follow the rules for civil courts of first instance (Art. 32(1)); judgments of the cadastral court can be appealed (istinaf) and taken to the Court of Cassation (temyiz) regardless of the value in dispute (Additional Art. 6). A final judgment binds the parties and anyone who joined as intervener (Art. 34).

Where the cadastral court in a region has been abolished, pending cases go to the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) where the parcel is located and continue there under the rules of Law No. 3402 (Art. 33(1)).

## The ten-year cut-off period

If you missed the posting period, there is a second and last deadline. Art. 12(3) of Law No. 3402:

> Bu tutanaklarda belirtilen haklara, sınırlandırma ve tespitlere ait tutanakların kesinleştiği tarihten itibaren on yıl geçtikten sonra, kadastrodan önceki hukuki sebeplere dayanarak itiraz olunamaz ve dava açılamaz. (Ek cümle: 25/2/2009-5841/2 md.; İptal: Anayasa Mahkemesi’nin 12/5/2011 tarihli ve E.: 2009/31, K.: 2011/77 sayılı Kararı ile.)

Unofficial translation: once ten years have passed from the date on which the records became final, no objection may be raised and no claim may be brought against the rights, boundaries and determinations in them on legal grounds predating the cadastre. (A sentence added in 2009 was annulled by the Constitutional Court on 12 May 2011.)

What follows from this:

- The period starts when the **record becomes final**, not when it was drawn up or when you found out. That date appears in the land register as the registration date (Art. 12(2)).
- Claims brought after the posting period go to the ordinary courts, not the cadastral court (Additional Art. 6 refers to them as claims "based on pre-cadastre grounds filed after the thirty-day posting period"). In practice this is an action to cancel and re-register the title (tapu iptali ve tescil davası).
- The period is a **cut-off period** (hak düşürücü süre). After ten years you can no longer rely on pre-cadastre grounds, such as the deceased's old title record.
- Grounds that arise after the record becomes final fall outside this rule; the provision only covers legal grounds "before the cadastre".

The law contains two exceptions. Art. 12(3) does not apply to parcels whose title carries a foundation annotation for icareteyn or mukataa (Additional Art. 1(2)). The ten-year period also does not apply to corrections of measurement, boundary and calculation errors under Art. 41 (Art. 41, last paragraph). Under Art. 41(1) as amended by Law No. 7579, such a correction is served on the owners and becomes final unless a claim is filed at the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) within thirty days.

Whether the State can be held liable after the ten years is a separate question. Art. 1007 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (TMK) makes the State liable for all damage arising from keeping the land register; the law does not expressly say when a loss caused by a cadastral determination falls under this rule. This question is disputed, and this article does not rely on a verified court decision on it. In the author's assessment it has to be examined case by case; how a court will decide is uncertain.

## Article 713 TMK: land outside the cadastre

Within the cadastre, the twenty-year rule in Art. 14 applies to unregistered land. Outside the cadastre, the same idea appears in Art. 713 TMK (extraordinary acquisitive prescription, olağanüstü zamanaşımı). Art. 713(1) TMK:

> Tapu kütüğünde kayıtlı olmayan bir taşınmazı davasız ve aralıksız olarak yirmi yıl süreyle ve malik sıfatıyla zilyetliğinde bulunduran kişi, o taşınmazın tamamı, bir parçası veya bir payı üzerindeki mülkiyet hakkının tapu kütüğüne tesciline karar verilmesini isteyebilir.

Unofficial translation: a person who has possessed an unregistered immovable as owner for twenty years, undisputed and without interruption, may ask the court to order registration of his or her ownership of the whole immovable, a part of it or a share in it.

For unregistered land, this claim is brought against the Treasury (Hazine) and the public bodies concerned. The court announces the claim at least three times, in a newspaper, on an internet news site and at the location of the land (Art. 713(3)–(4) TMK; form of announcement amended by Law No. 7418, in force since 1 April 2023). If no well-founded objection is made within three months of the last announcement and the claim is proven, the judge orders registration (Art. 713(5)). Here too, you will usually learn of such a case only through these announcements.

## What heirs living abroad can do

- **Find out whether a cadastre is under way.** The announcements under Arts. 2 and 4 are hard to follow from abroad. The village head and the district cadastral office can tell you.
- **Watch the posting period.** The list is displayed for 30 days at the office and at the village head's office and is deemed served on you (Art. 11). If you cannot be in Turkey during that time, a claim can be filed under a power of attorney given at a Turkish consulate; on powers of attorney see [selling property in Turkey from abroad](/en/publications/selling-property-in-turkey-from-abroad).
- **Check the land register.** The General Directorate of Land Registry and Cadastre (TKGM) offers the "Tapu Bilgileri Sorgulama" service on the e-Devlet portal; you log in with an e-Devlet password, mobile or electronic signature, Turkish ID card or online banking. I could not confirm on the official page whether it also shows parcels still registered to the deceased; ask the land registry office. The registration date tells you when the ten-year period ends.
- **If the parcel was recorded in the heirs' names,** the next steps are transfer and possibly partition: [inheriting property in Turkey](/en/publications/inheriting-property-in-turkey-title-deed) and [partition lawsuit for inherited property](/en/publications/inherited-property-turkey-partition-lawsuit).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I have to object to a cadastral determination in Turkey?

During the survey you can object at the cadastral office (Art. 9). After posting, you have 30 days to sue at the cadastral court (Arts. 11, 12(1)).

### What if I missed the 30-day posting period?

The record becomes final. Within ten years from finality you can still sue in the ordinary courts on pre-cadastre grounds (Art. 12(3)).

### When does the ten-year cadastre deadline start?

When the record becomes final; that date is entered in the land register as the registration date (Art. 12(2)).

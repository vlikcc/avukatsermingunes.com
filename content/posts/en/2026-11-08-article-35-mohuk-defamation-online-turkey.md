Article 35 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which country’s law applies to claims arising from a violation of personality rights (kişilik hakları) through the press, radio, television, the internet or other mass media. Unlike the general tort rule in Article 34, here the injured person chooses the law. You will come across this provision if you live in Germany or the Netherlands and a Turkish news site or a social media post has published something about you.

## Article 35 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Liability for violation of personality rights**
>
> Article 35 – (1) Claims arising from the violation of personality rights through media such as the press, radio and television, through the internet or through other means of mass communication shall be governed, at the choice of the injured person, by
>
> a) the law of the injured person’s habitual residence, if the person causing the damage was in a position to know that the damage would occur in that country,
>
> b) the law of the country where the person causing the damage has their place of business or habitual residence, or
>
> c) the law of the country where the damage occurred, if the person causing the damage was in a position to know that the damage would occur in that country.
>
> (2) The right of reply in the event of a violation of personality rights is, for periodical publications, governed exclusively by the law of the country where the publication was printed or the programme was broadcast.
>
> (3) Paragraph 1 also applies to claims arising from a violation of personality through the processing of personal data or through the restriction of the right to obtain information about personal data.

Turkish original:

> **Kişilik haklarının ihlâlinde sorumluluk**
>
> MADDE 35 – (1) Kişilik haklarının, basın, radyo, televizyon gibi medya yoluyla, internet veya diğer kitle iletişim araçları ile ihlâlinden doğan taleplere, zarar görenin seçimine göre;
>
> a) Zarar veren, zararın bu ülkede meydana geleceğini bilecek durumda ise zarar görenin mutad meskeni hukuku,
>
> b) Zarar verenin işyeri veya mutad meskeninin bulunduğu ülke hukuku veya
>
> c) Zarar veren, zararın bu ülkede meydana geleceğini bilecek durumda ise zararın meydana geldiği ülke hukuku,
>
> uygulanır.
>
> (2) Kişilik haklarının ihlâlinde cevap hakkı, süreli yayınlarda, münhasıran baskının yapıldığı ya da programın yayınlandığı ülke hukukuna tâbidir.
>
> (3) Maddenin birinci fıkrası, kişisel verilerin işlenmesi veya kişisel veriler hakkında bilgi alma hakkının sınırlandırılması yolu ile kişiliğin ihlâl edilmesinden doğan taleplere de uygulanır.

The provision has not changed since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the current text in the Ministry of Justice’s UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note for this article.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the explanatory memorandum to the bill, this was a new provision with no equivalent in the former Law No. 2675. The general tort rule was considered inadequate for publications reaching an indefinite number of people through technical means, so the person whose rights were violated was given a choice between the listed laws. For the right of reply in periodicals, the law of the place of printing or broadcasting was chosen so that a media company is subject to a law it knows rather than to many foreign ones. Paragraph 3 extends the same protection to violations through data processing. (Source: explanatory memorandum to the bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly, Article 34 of the bill.)

## Explanation

The provision covers only violations through mass media. An insult between neighbours or a private letter falls under the general rule in Article 34.

The injured person may choose one of three laws. The law of their habitual residence (point a) and the law of the place where the damage occurred (point c) are available only if the publisher could know that the damage would occur there. The law of the publisher’s place of business or habitual residence (point b) has no such condition. The substantive rules of the chosen law apply, not its conflict-of-laws rules (Article 2(4) MÖHUK).

The law does not say until when, or in what form, the choice has to be made. This point is disputed: there is no express provision in the law and I have not found a verified, binding decision of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) on it. In my view the safest course is to state the choice expressly in the statement of claim; this is an opinion, not a rule of law. The law is also silent on what happens if the injured person makes no choice at all.

Paragraph 2 is a separate rule. For newspapers, magazines and regularly broadcast programmes, the right of reply is governed only by the law of the place of printing or broadcasting, with no choice.

Paragraph 3 grants the same choice where personality is violated by processing personal data or by restricting the right of access to one’s data.

## Practical points for people living abroad

### A Turkish website or social media post about you

If you live in Düsseldorf and a Turkish news site reported on you while knowing, or being in a position to know, that you live there, you may choose German law (point a) or Turkish law as the law of the site’s place of business (point b) in proceedings before a Turkish court. If you choose Turkish law, actions to protect personality rights are governed by Articles 24–25 of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK), and damages for non-pecuniary harm (manevi tazminat) by Article 58 of the Turkish Code of Obligations (TBK). Under Turkish law, tort claims become time-barred two years after the injured person learns of the damage and the person liable, and in any event ten years after the act; if the act is a crime with a longer limitation period under criminal law, that period applies (Article 72(1) TBK). Limitation follows the law governing the claim itself ([Article 8 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-8-mohuk-limitation-periods)).

### Where to sue

The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on venue (Article 40 MÖHUK). Under Article 25(5) TMK, an action to protect personality rights may be brought at the claimant’s or the defendant’s domicile. In tort cases the court of the place where the act was committed, where the damage occurred or is likely to occur, also has jurisdiction (Article 16 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK). If you sue in Germany or the Netherlands, that court applies its own rules; Article 35 MÖHUK binds only Turkish courts. For German or Dutch law, consult a lawyer qualified in that country.

### Right of reply and correction

For a newspaper printed in Turkey or a news website operating in Turkey, the right of reply is governed by Turkish law (Article 35(2)). Under Article 14 of Press Law No. 5187, the correction and reply (düzeltme ve cevap) must be sent within two months of publication; for internet news sites, the responsible editor must publish it within one day of receiving it at the latest. If it is not published, you may ask the criminal judgeship of peace (sulh ceza hâkimi) to order publication within fifteen days after the publication deadline has passed. Removal of content and blocking of access online are outside the scope of this article.

### If your personal data was processed in Turkey

If you believe a Turkish company has processed your data unlawfully, you may apply to the data controller under Article 11 of Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 (KVKK): you can find out whether your data is processed, ask for correction or erasure, and claim compensation for your damage. The law governing the compensation claim again depends on your choice (Article 35(3)).

For other damages claims see [compensation law](/en/practice-areas/compensation-law); for the general rule see [Article 34 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-34-mohuk-tort-accident-turkey).

## Frequently asked questions

### A Turkish website published false claims about me and I live in Germany. Which law applies?

Before a Turkish court you choose: German law if the site could know you live in Germany, and in any case the law of the country where the site has its place of business (Article 35(1) MÖHUK).

### What if the newspaper does not publish my reply?

Turkish law applies to periodicals published in Turkey. If the reply is not published in time, you may apply to the criminal judgeship of peace within fifteen days after the deadline (Article 14 of Law No. 5187).

### Do social media posts fall under Article 35?

The provision expressly mentions the internet and “other means of mass communication”. Whether a specific post is covered is for the court to assess, for example depending on who could see it.

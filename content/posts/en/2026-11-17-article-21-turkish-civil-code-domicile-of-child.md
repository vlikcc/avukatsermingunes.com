Article 21 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) fixes by law the domicile of a child under parental custody (velayet) and of a person under guardianship (vesayet), whatever they themselves intend. A child is domiciled at the parents' common domicile; if there is none, at the domicile of the parent to whom the child has been entrusted. A person under guardianship is domiciled where the guardianship authority is located. When a case concerns a child or a relative under guardianship in Türkiye, the question of which court has jurisdiction often starts with this article.

## Article 21 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **3. Legal domicile**
>
> Article 21 – The domicile of a child under parental custody is that of the mother and father; where the mother and father have no common domicile, it is the domicile of the parent to whom the child has been entrusted. In other cases, the child's place of residence is deemed to be its domicile.
>
> The domicile of persons under guardianship is the place where the guardianship authority to which they are subject is located.

Turkish original:

> **3. Yasal yerleşim yeri**
>
> Madde 21- Velâyet altında bulunan çocuğun yerleşim yeri, ana ve babasının; ana ve babanın ortak yerleşim yeri yoksa, çocuğun kendisine bırakıldığı ana veya babanın yerleşim yeridir. Diğer hâllerde çocuğun oturma yeri, onun yerleşim yeri sayılır.
>
> Vesayet altındaki kişilerin yerleşim yeri, bağlı oldukları vesayet makamının bulunduğu yerdir.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 21; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 21 of the former Code, and the heading "Legal domicile" was chosen to describe the domicile of persons under parental custody and guardianship. Where the parents have no common domicile, for instance because they live apart or are divorced, the child is domiciled with the parent to whom it has been entrusted. The reasoning states expressly that "entrusted" does not mean the parent who actually keeps the child, but an entrustment that is lawful and based on a right. If neither parent has custody and the child has not been placed with either of them, the child's place of residence is its domicile (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Child under parental custody

The article works in three steps:

1. If the mother and father share a domicile, that is the child's domicile.
2. If they do not, the domicile of the parent to whom the child has lawfully been entrusted applies. On divorce, custody belongs to the parent to whom the child is entrusted (Article 336(3) TMK); if the parents are not married, custody belongs to the mother (Article 337(1) TMK).
3. If neither step gives an answer, the child's place of residence counts as its domicile.

The child's domicile has a direct effect on jurisdiction. Parentage claims are filed at the domicile of one of the parties at the time of the claim or of the birth (Article 283 TMK); since the child is a party, its domicile is one of the options.

### Person under guardianship

Paragraph 2 covers minors under guardianship and adults whose legal capacity has been restricted (kısıtlı). The guardianship authority is the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi, Article 397(2) TMK). Guardianship matters fall to the guardianship authorities at the person's domicile (Article 411 TMK). The court that appointed the guardian therefore remains the centre of the guardianship even if the person is moved elsewhere. Changing the domicile of a person under guardianship requires the authority's consent; afterwards jurisdiction passes to the authorities of the new domicile and the restriction is announced there (Articles 412 and 462(14) TMK). Placement in an educational, care or health institution also requires consent (Article 462(13) TMK).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

### The parent with custody lives in Germany

If custody was given after divorce to the mother living in Germany, the child's domicile is her domicile in Germany (Article 21(1) TMK), and the child has no domicile in Türkiye. The father living in Türkiye does not change this, but in claims tied to the domicile of either party, such as a parentage claim, the court at his Turkish domicile may have jurisdiction (Article 283 TMK).

The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 of the Turkish Private International Law Act No. 5718, MÖHUK). Personal status cases of Turkish citizens can be heard in Türkiye if they are not or cannot be brought before foreign courts; where no court has territorial jurisdiction, the court of the person's residence in Türkiye applies, then the last Turkish domicile, and finally the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir (Article 41 MÖHUK).

### Domicile, habitual residence and the Hague Convention

In cross-border cases involving children, the rules often look not at domicile but at the child's habitual residence (mutad mesken), for example for the law that applies where parentage cannot be established under the national law (Article 16(1) MÖHUK). International treaties to which Türkiye is a party take precedence (Article 1(2) MÖHUK). One of them is the 1980 Hague Child Abduction Convention; according to the status table of the Hague Conference it entered into force for Türkiye on 1 August 2000, for Germany on 1 December 1990 and for the Netherlands on 1 September 1990. Under Türkiye's implementing Law No. 5717, applications for return or for contact are heard by the court of the place where the child is living, or has been placed under protection, at the time of the application (Article 6(2) Law No. 5717). In those cases the child's actual residence counts, not the legal domicile under Article 21 TMK.

The reasoning has a consequence here too: a parent who keeps the child in Türkiye without the right to do so does not, according to the reasoning, meet the "entrusted" condition, so the child's legal domicile does not automatically move to that parent's domicile. The return procedure is explained in [Hague Convention child abduction cases in Türkiye](/en/publications/hague-convention-child-abduction-turkey).

### A parent under guardianship in Türkiye

Even if the guardian of a parent placed under guardianship in Türkiye lives in Germany or the Netherlands, the parent's domicile is where the guardianship authority is (Article 21(2) TMK), and the guardianship is run from there (Article 411 TMK). If you are thinking of moving the parent elsewhere for care, including abroad, it makes sense to clarify with the guardianship authority beforehand whether consent under Article 462(13) or (14) TMK is required; this is my assessment, not an express rule of the Code. For the applicable law, see [Article 10 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-10-mohuk-guardianship-turkey).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the domicile of a child of divorced parents?

If the parents have no common domicile, it is the domicile of the parent to whom the child has lawfully been entrusted, usually the parent with custody (Articles 21(1) and 336(3) TMK).

### Where is the domicile of a child born outside marriage?

If the parents live at a common domicile, that is the child's domicile. If not, custody belongs to the mother by law (Article 337(1) TMK); read together with Article 21(1), the child is then as a rule domiciled with the mother.

### Can the domicile of a person under guardianship be changed?

Yes, but only with the consent of the guardianship authority (Articles 412(1) and 462(14) TMK). After the change, the authorities of the new domicile have jurisdiction.

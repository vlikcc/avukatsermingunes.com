Article 37 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says who keeps the Turkish civil status register (kişisel durum sicili, the nüfus record): officials appointed by the State. Keeping the entries and issuing extracts from them is their job. The second paragraph allows Türkiye's missions abroad to be given the powers of a registrar (nüfus memurluğu yetkisi). This is why Turkish citizens in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere can have births, marriages and deaths entered in the Turkish register through the consulate.

## Article 37 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Officials**
>
> Article 37 – The civil status register is kept by officials appointed by the State. Keeping the register entries and issuing extracts are the duties of these officials.
>
> Representatives of Türkiye in foreign countries may be granted the powers of a civil registrar with the approval of the Presidency (Cumhurbaşkanlığı).²

Turkish original:

> **II. Görevliler**
>
> Madde 37- Kişisel durum sicili, Devletçe atanan memurlar tarafından tutulur. Sicil kayıtlarını tutmak ve örnek vermek bu memurların görevidir.
>
> Yabancı memleketlerdeki Türkiye temsilcilerine, Cumhurbaşkanlığının onayı ile nüfus memurluğu yetkisi verilebilir.²

Footnote in the official text:

> ² 2/7/2018 tarihli ve 700 sayılı KHK’nin 139 uncu maddesiyle, bu fıkrada yer alan “Dışişleri Bakanlığının önerisi, İçişleri Bakanlığının katılması ve Başbakanlığın” ibaresi “Cumhurbaşkanlığının” şeklinde değiştirilmiştir.

The footnote means: Article 139 of Decree-Law (KHK) No. 700 of 2 July 2018 replaced the words "on the proposal of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, with the participation of the Ministry of the Interior and the approval of the Prime Ministry" with "of the Presidency". As of November 2026 the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr shows no later change to Article 37; the latest law in the amendment table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 36 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The phrase "officials appointed by the State" comes from the Swiss model, where it separates state officials from church officials. The reasoning notes that in Türkiye everyone who keeps the civil status register is a state official anyway (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly). The reasoning belongs to the original text and deals only with the first paragraph.

## Explanation

### Who keeps the register

Civil registration services are carried out by the central and provincial organisation of the Ministry of the Interior and by the missions abroad (Article 4(1) Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490). The law's definitions name the General Directorate of Civil Registration and Citizenship Affairs, the provincial directorate and the district registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) (Article 3(1)(i), (j), (ü)). Officials who record an event sign the supporting documents after entering them in the family register (Article 12(2)). Registry records are confidential: apart from the competent officials, those with inspection powers and the courts, nobody may see them (Article 9(1)).

The marriage officer (evlendirme memuru) is a separate role. Where there is a municipality it is the mayor or an official he or she appoints, in villages the village headman; the Ministry may also give marriage powers to registry offices, missions abroad and mufti offices (Article 22(2)).

### The duty to issue extracts

Article 44 of Law No. 5490 lists who may obtain a registry extract (nüfus kayıt örneği) directly: the person registered, their spouse, parents, guardian, ancestors and descendants, anyone holding a power of attorney from them, judicial authorities, missions abroad and the other bodies listed. Other third parties cannot obtain extracts or information about a person's record beyond marital status (Article 44(4)). You can also obtain extracts for yourself and your ancestors and descendants through e-Devlet (Article 44(5)). The evidential weight of an extract is covered in [Article 36 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-36-turkish-civil-code-civil-status-register). State liability for damage caused in keeping the register is governed by Article 38 TMK.

### Missions abroad

Article 37(2) TMK says missions "may" be given registrar powers. Law No. 5490 lists the missions abroad directly among the bodies that carry out civil registration services (Article 4(1)) and assigns them the notification and registration tasks listed below. The Ministry may authorise missions to send supporting documents electronically or to register them themselves (Additional Article 2(1)). At the request of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, civil registration staff may also be posted to the consular sections of embassies and to consulates-general (Additional Article 2(2)).

The author's assessment: today the detailed legal basis for civil registration work at a consulate is Law No. 5490; Article 37(2) TMK provides the general framework in the Civil Code. Ask the mission itself which services it handles before you apply.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

Turkish citizens abroad must have themselves entered in the civil registry through the mission (Article 11(1) Law No. 5490). For events abroad the law provides the following routes:

- Birth: report it to the mission within sixty days with the foreign authority's certificate; if you cannot go in person, the certificate and a written request can be sent (Article 15(1) and (6)). More: [registering a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad).
- Marriage before a foreign authority: the marriage certificate is handed in or sent to the local mission within thirty days at the latest; the mission draws up the marriage notification and sends it to the registry office (Article 24(1)). If there is no mission or notification to it was not possible, a certified Turkish translation can be filed with a registry office in Türkiye (Article 24(2)). More: [registering a marriage abroad in Türkiye](/en/publications/register-marriage-abroad-in-turkey).
- Marriage at the consulate: the mission reports marriages it performs to the registry office within thirty days (Article 23(3)). If one partner is a foreign national, only municipal marriage offices and registry office heads may perform the marriage (Article 22(2)).
- Death: the mission reports the death to the General Directorate or the registry office within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1)). It only learns of the death if someone tells it. More: [registering a death abroad](/en/publications/death-certificate-turkey-register-death-abroad).
- Foreign divorce judgment: decisions that meet the statutory conditions are registered abroad by the missions in the country where the decision was given (Article 27/A(2)). Otherwise recognition under the Turkish private international law act (MÖHUK) is the route (Article 27/A(3)).
- Acknowledgement of paternity and address: notifications of an acknowledgement made abroad may be filed with the mission (Article 28(5)), as may address notifications (Article 50(2)).

Holders of the Turkish Blue Card (Mavi Kart) must also report every civil status event abroad to the mission (Article 8/A). The effect of an event under German or Dutch law is a question for the local registry office or a lawyer admitted in that country.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a Turkish consulate act as a registry office?

For the services the law provides, yes. Law No. 5490 lists missions abroad among the bodies that carry out civil registration services (Article 4(1)), and Article 37(2) TMK allows them to be given registrar powers.

### Where do I report a birth, marriage or death abroad?

To the competent Turkish mission: a birth within sixty days (Article 15(1)), a marriage before a foreign authority within thirty days (Article 24(1)). The mission reports a death to Türkiye within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1)).

### Who can get a nüfus kayıt örneği?

The person registered, their spouse, parents, guardian, ancestors and descendants, anyone holding their power of attorney, and the authorities named in the law (Article 44(1) Law No. 5490). For yourself and your ancestors and descendants, also through e-Devlet (Article 44(5)).

Article 55 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) is the last of the general provisions on legal persons. It reserves the laws on public-law legal persons and on commercial companies (saklı hükümler), so for these two groups their own laws come first. This concerns shareholders in a Turkish company and anyone deciding whether to organise a group as an association or as a company.

## Article 55 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **F. Reserved provisions**
>
> Article 55 – The statutory provisions on public-law legal persons and on commercial companies are reserved.

Turkish original:

> **F. Saklı hükümler**
>
> Madde 55- Kamu tüzel kişileri ile ticaret şirketleri hakkındaki kanun hükümleri saklıdır.

As of November 2026, Article 55 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The article corresponds to Article 52 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The marginal heading was shortened to “Saklı hükümler” in line with drafting practice, and the article was reduced to one paragraph. The old sentence “Associations pursuing an economic aim are subject to the provisions on companies” was dropped as unnecessary. According to the reasoning, associations in Turkish law cannot pursue an economic aim in the narrow sense and cannot be formed to give their members economic benefits or to share profits; the only bodies of persons with legal personality that can be formed for those aims are commercial companies (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Commercial companies: references both ways

Commercial companies are the general partnership, limited partnership, joint-stock company, limited liability company and cooperative (kollektif, komandit, anonim, limited, kooperatif; Art. 124(1) Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102, TTK). Article 55 TMK reserves the laws that govern them, and the TTK in turn refers back to the Civil Code:

- Commercial companies have legal personality and, within the framework of Article 48 TMK, can enjoy all rights and assume obligations, subject to statutory exceptions (Art. 125 TTK).
- Subject to the rules specific to each type of company, the general TMK provisions on legal persons also apply to commercial companies, to the extent they suit the company's nature (Art. 126(1) TTK).

So the TTK rule for the company type comes first, and otherwise the general TMK rules on legal persons. Liquidation shows this clearly. A joint-stock company that has come to an end enters liquidation, keeps its legal personality until liquidation ends, uses its trade name with the words “tasfiye hâlinde” (in liquidation), and its organs' powers are limited to the purpose of liquidation (Art. 533 TTK). How liquidation is carried out is also set out in Articles 533 et seq. TTK, so Article 53 TMK's reference to the official liquidation of an estate does not come into play for a joint-stock company.

### Public-law legal persons

The state, municipalities, universities and public professional bodies such as bar associations are created under public law by their own statutes. Their formation, powers and end follow those statutes, not the association and foundation rules of the Civil Code.

### Association or company?

An association is formed for a specific common purpose other than sharing profits (Art. 56 TMK). A group that wants to distribute profits to its members and have legal personality must choose one of the forms of commercial company. That is why the old sentence was dropped. For legal capacity under Article 48 TMK, to which Article 125 TTK refers, see the post on [Article 48 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-48-tmk-legal-capacity-legal-persons); for the remaining assets of a dissolved association, see the post on [Article 54 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-54-turkish-civil-code-assets-after-dissolution).

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you are a shareholder in a Turkish limited liability company (limited şirket) or joint-stock company (anonim şirket) that is being wound up, liquidation and the remaining assets follow the TTK; the Civil Code rules for associations do not apply. See also the page on [commercial and contract law](/en/practice-areas/commercial-and-contract-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the Turkish Civil Code apply to a Turkish limited company?

The TTK rules specific to limited companies apply first. Where they are silent, the general TMK provisions on legal persons apply to the extent they suit the company's nature (Art. 126(1) TTK).

### Can a Turkish association distribute profits to its members?

No. An association is formed for a purpose other than sharing profits (Art. 56 TMK); to distribute profits, you need a commercial company.

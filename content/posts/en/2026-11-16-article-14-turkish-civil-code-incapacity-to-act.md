Article 14 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) lists who lacks capacity to act (fiil ehliyeti): persons without capacity of discernment, minors and persons under legal interdiction. It is a short list; what these persons can and cannot do is set out in the next two articles, 15 and 16. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 14 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Lack of capacity to act**
>
> **1. In general**
>
> Article 14 – Persons without capacity of discernment, minors and persons under interdiction have no capacity to act.

Original Turkish text:

> **III. Fiil ehliyetsizliği**
>
> **1. Genel olarak**
>
> Madde 14- Ayırt etme gücü bulunmayanların, küçüklerin ve kısıtlıların fiil ehliyeti yoktur.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 14; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 14 of the former Code. The heading "medenî hakları kullanmağa ehliyetsizlik" (incapacity to exercise civil rights) was replaced by "fiil ehliyetsizliği" to keep the terminology consistent with the preceding articles; the rule itself was not changed (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

Article 14 is the reverse side of Article 10 TMK. Full capacity to act requires capacity of discernment, majority and not being under interdiction; if one is missing, the person does not have full capacity. The three groups are not treated alike:

- **Persons without capacity of discernment** have no capacity at all, whatever their age and whether or not they are under interdiction. Their acts have no legal effect, except where the law provides otherwise (Article 15 TMK).
- **Minors and persons under interdiction who have capacity of discernment** have limited capacity. They can take on obligations with the consent of their legal representative, and can accept gratuitous benefits and exercise strictly personal rights on their own. They are liable for their own torts (Article 16 TMK).

Lacking capacity to act does not stop a person from holding rights. Every human being has legal capacity (Article 8 TMK). A baby can own land or inherit; the legal representative exercises those rights. A child under parental custody is represented by the mother and father (Article 342 TMK). A person under interdiction is, as a rule, represented by the guardian; an adult child placed under interdiction remains under parental custody if the court does not consider a guardian necessary (Article 335(2) TMK).

## Practical points if you live abroad

If a minor or a person under interdiction is one of the heirs in a division of an estate or a land registry transaction in Turkey, their legal representative acts for them. A parent living abroad can handle this for a child under their custody in person or through a power of attorney. If the person is under guardianship, transactions such as buying or selling land also need the permission of the guardianship authority (Article 462(1) TMK).

The capacity to act of a Turkish citizen is governed by Turkish law (Article 9(1) MÖHUK). Whether a protective measure ordered for a relative in Germany or the Netherlands has effect in Turkey is a separate question of recognition and is not covered here.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who lacks capacity to act under Turkish law?

Persons without capacity of discernment, minors and persons under interdiction (Article 14 TMK).

### Can a person without capacity to act inherit?

Yes. Inheriting depends on legal capacity, which every human being has (Article 8 TMK). The legal representative carries out the steps relating to the estate.

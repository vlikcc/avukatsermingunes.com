Under Turkish law, tağşiş (adulteration) means unlawfully removing or changing the amount of the components that give a food its essential character, or adding a cheaper substance as if it were the real one. Taklit (imitation) means presenting a product as having qualities it does not have, or as if it were another product (Article 3 of Law No. 5996). Both are prohibited, and the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (Tarım ve Orman Bakanlığı) may publish the products it identifies in official controls (Articles 24(4) and 31(6)).

“Tağşiş” is a frequent search in Turkey at the moment, including among Turkish readers living in Europe. This article sets out the Turkish legal position as of November 2026. It does not assess any particular company, product or case.

## What taklit and tağşiş mean in Turkish law

The governing statute is Law No. 5996 on Veterinary Services, Plant Health, Food and Feed (Veteriner Hizmetleri, Bitki Sağlığı, Gıda ve Yem Kanunu). The definitions are in Article 3, points 63 and 64.

Unofficial translation:

> 63) Tağşiş: the removal, contrary to the legislation, of all or part of the components and nutritional values that give the products covered by this Law their essential character, or changing their quantity, or adding another substance that does not have the same value in place of that substance as if it were the same substance,

> 64) Taklit: presenting the products covered by this Law, in terms of form, composition and properties, as if they had characteristics not present in their structure or as if they were identical to another product,

Turkish original:

> 63) Tağşiş: Bu Kanun kapsamındaki ürünlere temel özelliğini veren öğelerin ve besin değerlerinin tamamının veya bir bölümünün mevzuata aykırı olarak çıkarılmasını veya miktarının değiştirilmesini veya aynı değeri taşımayan başka bir maddenin, o madde yerine aynı maddeymiş gibi katılmasını,

> 64) Taklit: Bu Kanun kapsamındaki ürünlerin, şekil, bileşim ve nitelikleri itibarıyla yapısında bulunmayan özelliklere sahip gibi veya başka bir ürünün aynısıymış gibi göstermeyi,

Tağşiş is about what is inside the product; taklit is about how the product is presented. Neither definition requires a health risk. A harmless food whose content differs from what is declared can still be tağşiş.

The prohibition itself is in Article 24(4): taklit and tağşiş may not be carried out in food or feed. Since Law No. 7255 (28 October 2020) the paragraph adds that such products may not be processed or placed on the market. Under Article 24(3), labelling, advertising and presentation must not mislead consumers either.

## What is the legal basis for the ministry's list?

The general basis in the Law is Article 31(6). The ministry may, where it sees fit, make information obtained from its controls available to the public. It may not publish, among other things, information on confidential investigations, ongoing judicial and administrative proceedings, personal data or trade secrets. The provision is discretionary (“sunabilir”, may) and sets its own limits.

Article 26(4) adds a duty for health risks: where the ministry suspects that a food may pose a risk to human health, it decides on measures and informs the public about the nature of the risk and the measures taken.

The announcements appear on the ministry's Güvenilir Gıda portal under “Gıda Kamuoyu Duyurusu”. There are two separate lists, “Taklit veya Tağşiş” and “Sağlığı Tehlikeye Düşürecek” (endangering health). The split mirrors the sanctions in the Law.

## Sanctions under Law No. 5996

The sanctions are in Article 40(1)(l), as amended by Law No. 7255 on 28 October 2020. Adulterated or imitated food is seized and recalled at the expense of the person responsible. Products that cannot be used for any other purpose are destroyed under ministry supervision; usable products are confiscated.

The administrative fine (idari para cezası) depends on the business's role. It applies to producers, importers and businesses selling under their own name or trade name, and to retailers who place the product on the market without ensuring traceability. In both cases the fine is one per cent of the business's annual gross revenue for the previous financial year, within a statutory minimum and maximum. I could not confirm from an official source which amounts apply to those limits in 2026, so I give no figures. Article 17(7) of the Misdemeanours Law (Kabahatler Kanunu, No. 5326) raises fixed fines each year by the revaluation rate, but expressly excludes proportional (nispi) fines.

Repetition makes it heavier. If the act is repeated within three years, the limits are doubled for producers, importers and own-brand sellers. On a second repetition within the same period, producers or importers receive a **judicial fine (adli para cezası) of 1,000 to 3,000 days** and are barred from the sector for five to ten years.

Where the food endangers human life and health, Article 40(1)(a) applies: recall and destruction, and for producers, importers and own-brand sellers **imprisonment of one to five years and a judicial fine of 1,000 to 5,000 days**, plus a five-to-ten-year sector ban if repeated within three years. A mismatch between label declarations and the product's content and analytical values carries a separate administrative fine under Article 40(1)(k).

In the official text on mevzuat.gov.tr, the latest amendment to Law No. 5996 is Law No. 7584 (in force since 20 June 2026). It concerns Articles 5 and 36, not Articles 3, 24, 26, 31 or 40 discussed here.

## Turkish Criminal Code: Articles 186 and 185

Article 186 of the Turkish Criminal Code (Türk Ceza Kanunu, No. 5237, TCK) punishes anyone who sells, supplies or possesses food, drink or medicine that has been spoiled or altered in a way that endangers human life and health: **imprisonment of one to five years and a judicial fine of up to 1,500 days**. If the offence is committed in the course of a profession or trade requiring official authorisation, the penalty is increased by one third (Article 186(2)).

The key element is danger to life and health. On the wording, an altered but harmless product falls outside Article 186; the administrative sanctions of Law No. 5996 then remain. Whether a danger exists in a given case is for the court to decide, usually on the basis of an expert report.

Article 185 covers a more serious act: poisoning drinking water or any food, or spoiling it in another way, and thereby endangering human life and health. The penalty is **imprisonment of two to fifteen years**, or **three months to one year** if committed through negligence.

In the text on mevzuat.gov.tr, the latest amendment to the Criminal Code is Law No. 7593 (in force since 18 August 2026). In that current text, Articles 185 and 186 carry no amendment note.

## Bought adulterated food in Turkey? Your consumer rights

If you bought an adulterated or imitated product in Turkey as a consumer, the defective goods (ayıplı mal) rules of Consumer Protection Law No. 6502 apply. Under Article 8(2), goods are defective if they lack a feature stated on the packaging, label or in advertising, or do not meet the quality set by their technical regulation.

Article 11 lets you choose one of these remedies:

1. withdraw from the contract and return the goods,
2. keep the goods and ask for a price reduction,
3. free repair, unless it would involve excessive cost,
4. replacement with a non-defective item, where possible.

For food, withdrawal, price reduction or replacement are the realistic options. The price or the reduction must be refunded immediately (Article 11(5)). You may also claim damages under the Turkish Code of Obligations alongside your chosen remedy (Article 11(6)).

The limitation period is two years from delivery, but it does not apply if the defect was concealed through gross negligence or fraud (Article 12(1) and (3)). Below a statutory value threshold you must first go to the consumer arbitration committee (tüketici hakem heyeti), either where you live or where the purchase took place (Article 68(1) and (3)). The threshold rises each year by the revaluation rate (Article 68(4)); check the 2026 amount on the website of the Turkish Ministry of Trade. Keep the receipt and the packaging.

## Where to complain: the ALO 174 food line

The ministry runs the ALO 174 food line (Gıda Hattı) for food reports and complaints. According to the ministry's website, 174 can be called from anywhere in Turkey, and reports can also be made through the form on alo174.gov.tr and the “Güvenilir Gıda” app. The site asks for a Turkish identity number (T.C. kimlik no), the full address of the business, and a mobile number and email address. It does not say whether the line can be reached from abroad.

A report to ALO 174 starts an official inspection; it does not get your money back. For a refund you need to use your rights under Law No. 6502 against the seller. If your health was harmed and you suspect an offence, you can file a criminal complaint with the Turkish chief public prosecutor's office (Cumhuriyet başsavcılığı).

## If you live outside Turkey

The ministry's lists are based on controls in Turkey. You can check on the portal whether a product you bought in Turkey or brought back from there is listed. Products bought in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe are checked by that country's authorities, and the Turkish list does not tell you what rights you have against a seller there. Which foods you may take from Turkey into another country is a matter for that country's and EU rules, not Turkish law; ask that country's customs authority.

Where a consumer can sue in Turkey is covered in my article on [Article 45 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-45-mohuk-consumer-claims-turkey).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does tağşiş mean?

Adulteration: unlawfully removing or changing the amount of a food's essential components, or replacing them with a cheaper substance presented as the same (Article 3, point 63, Law No. 5996).

### Where can I find the taklit tağşiş list?

On the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry's Güvenilir Gıda portal under “Gıda Kamuoyu Duyurusu”, split into “Taklit veya Tağşiş” and “Sağlığı Tehlikeye Düşürecek”.

### What is the fine for taklit and tağşiş in 2026?

One per cent of annual gross revenue within statutory limits (Article 40(1)(l)); the limits rise on repetition, and a second repetition brings a judicial fine and a sector ban. I could not confirm the 2026 limit amounts from an official source.

### Can I get a refund for an adulterated product?

Yes, if you bought it in Turkey as a consumer: goods lacking the declared features are defective (Article 8(2), Law No. 6502), and you can withdraw from the contract (Article 11).

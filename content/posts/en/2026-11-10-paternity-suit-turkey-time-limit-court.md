Under Turkish law only the mother and the child can bring a paternity suit (babalık davası). It is brought against the father or, if he has died, against his heirs (Article 301 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). The mother's right lapses one year after the birth; for the child's action, after two rulings of the Turkish Constitutional Court, the Code no longer sets any time limit (Article 303 TMK).

I checked the provisions below in November 2026 against the current texts in UYAP Mevzuat, the legislation database of the Turkish Ministry of Justice (TMK and the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK, both recorded on 31 July 2026; the last statute in the TMK amendment list is Law No. 7589). The most recent changes to Articles 301–304 TMK are the Constitutional Court annulments of 2011 and 2012.

## Paternity suit or acknowledgement?

Legal parentage (soybağı) between a child and the father arises through marriage to the mother, through acknowledgement (tanıma) or through a court judgment (Article 282(2) TMK). If the parents are not married, the father can acknowledge the child by a written application to the registrar (nüfus memuru) or to a court, in an official deed or in his will (Article 295(1) TMK). If he does not, the mother or the child can sue. The Grand Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu) puts the difference this way: acknowledgement depends on the father's will, a judgment does not need his consent. The paternity suit creates a new legal status and a judgment in favour of the claimant takes effect retroactively (Yargıtay HGK, E. 2023/383, K. 2024/294, 29.05.2024).

If the child already has legal parentage with another man, for example the mother's husband (Article 285 TMK), the official reasoning of the provision says no paternity suit can be brought while that tie exists; the one-year period starts only when it ends (Article 303(3) TMK). How an acknowledgement reaches the Turkish register is explained in [birth registration in Turkey for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad).

## Who can file a paternity suit? Can the father?

Article 301(1) TMK is clear: the mother and the child may ask the court to establish parentage between the child and the father. According to the Grand Civil Chamber, the two rights are independent; if one of them loses the case or withdraws it, the other is not affected (same ruling).

If the parents are not married, parental custody (velayet) belongs to the mother (Article 337(1) TMK), so she often files the child's claim as the child's legal representative. If the mother sues, the case must be notified to the child's guardian ad litem (kayyım); if the guardian sues, to the mother. In every case the public prosecutor and the State Treasury (Hazine) are notified (Article 301(3) TMK).

**Can the father file a paternity suit?** No. Article 301 TMK does not give him that right. His route is acknowledgement (Article 295 TMK), but he cannot acknowledge a child who has legal parentage with another man until that tie is removed (Article 295(3) TMK). An action to contest paternity (soybağının reddi) belongs to the husband, the mother and the child (Article 286 TMK). A man who claims to be the father may bring it only if, before the husband's time limit ran out, the husband died, was declared missing or permanently lost capacity of judgement, and only within one year of learning of this (Article 291(1) TMK as amended by Law No. 7531).

## Time limit for a paternity suit

The current text reads (unofficial translation):

> A paternity suit may be brought before or after the birth of the child. The mother's right of action lapses one year after the birth.
>
> (Second paragraph annulled by Constitutional Court ruling of 27/10/2011, E. 2010/71, K. 2011/143.)
>
> If the child has legal parentage with another man, the one-year period starts on the date on which that relationship ends.
>
> If, after the one-year period has expired, there are reasons that justify the delay, the action may be brought within one month after the reason ceases.

Original Turkish text:

> Babalık davası, çocuğun doğumundan önce veya sonra açılabilir. Ananın dava hakkı, doğumdan başlayarak bir yıl geçmekle düşer.
>
> (İptal ikinci fıkra: Anayasa Mahkemesi’nin 27/10/2011 tarihli ve E.: 2010/71, K.: 2011/143 sayılı Kararı ile.)
>
> Çocuk ile başka bir erkek arasında soybağı ilişkisi varsa, bir yıllık süre bu ilişkinin ortadan kalktığı tarihte işlemeye başlar.
>
> Bir yıllık süre geçtikten sonra gecikmeyi haklı kılan sebepler varsa, sebebin ortadan kalkmasından başlayarak bir ay içinde dava açılabilir.

### The mother: one year from the birth

The mother can sue even before the child is born. One year after the birth her own right lapses; as the heading of the article says, this is a limitation period that extinguishes the right (hak düşürücü süre). If there is a reason that justifies the delay (haklı sebep), she has **one month** from the moment it ceases (last paragraph of Article 303 TMK).

The Code does not say what counts as such a reason. The Grand Civil Chamber accepted one where the defendant kept the mother waiting with promises of marriage and assurances that everything would be sorted out once they married, and kept putting off acknowledgement while the couple lived together before and after the birth. The child was born on 24.07.2017 and the case was filed on 17.08.2020. The Chamber also relied on the rule that manifest abuse of a right is not protected (Article 2(2) TMK) (Yargıtay HGK, E. 2023/383, K. 2024/294, 29.05.2024). The ruling concerns one set of facts; what decided it was that the defendant's deceptive conduct had been proven beyond doubt.

### The child: no time limit in the Code

The Constitutional Court (Anayasa Mahkemesi) annulled the paragraph setting the child's time limit by its ruling of 27.10.2011, E. 2010/71, K. 2011/143 (Official Gazette of 7.2.2012, No. 28197). It annulled the last paragraph, the one-month rule, "with regard to the child" by its ruling of 15.03.2012, E. 2011/116, K. 2012/39; that annulment took effect one year after publication on 21.07.2012 (No. 28360).

Since then the Court of Cassation has held that no time limit restricts the child's paternity suit, whether or not a guardian was appointed (Yargıtay HGK, E. 2023/383, K. 2024/294). Two examples:

- **Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2346, K. 2024/8355, 06.11.2024.** The mother had sued and the regional appeal court dismissed the case as out of time. The Chamber noted that a guardian had been appointed for the child and had continued the proceedings, so the case had to be treated as brought on the child's behalf, where no time limit applies. The judgment was reversed.
- **Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6540, K. 2023/6465, 25.12.2023.** Adult children asked the court to establish that a deceased man, whose estate had passed to the State Treasury, was their father; the Treasury was the defendant and argued that no claim could be brought "21 years later". The first-instance judgment in their favour was upheld on appeal and in cassation.

So a mother missing her own deadline does not affect the child's right.

## Competent court and parties living abroad

The family court (aile mahkemesi) has jurisdiction over cases arising from the Second Book of the TMK on family law (Article 4(1) of Law No. 4787). Where no family court exists, a designated civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) hears these cases (Article 2(2) of Law No. 4787).

Venue is governed by Article 283 TMK: cases on parentage are brought at the court of the domicile of either party at the time of filing or at the time of the birth. These rules also determine the international jurisdiction of Turkish courts (Article 40 of the Private International Law Act No. 5718, MÖHUK). If the father is domiciled in Turkey, the case can be filed there.

If no party is domiciled in Turkey, Article 41 MÖHUK applies: personal status cases of Turkish citizens that are not or cannot be brought abroad are heard by the court with local jurisdiction in Turkey; failing that, at the place where the person concerned resides, if they do not reside in Turkey, at their last domicile in Turkey, and failing that in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir. Treating a paternity suit as a personal status case in this sense is my own assessment; see [Article 41 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-41-mohuk-divorce-jurisdiction-turkey) for details.

Which law applies is a separate question. The establishment of parentage is governed first by the child's national law at the time of birth (Article 16(1) MÖHUK); for a Turkish child that is Turkish law. The rest of the sequence is explained under [Article 16 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-16-mohuk-parentage-paternity-turkey).

A paternity judgment given in Germany or the Netherlands does not automatically have the force of a final judgment in Turkey; it needs recognition or enforcement (tanıma, tenfiz) under Article 58 MÖHUK, also before the family court (Article 4(2) of Law No. 4787). The court can hear witnesses and parties by video link from where they are (Article 149(2)–(3) HMK).

## DNA testing: can you refuse?

The judge investigates the facts on his or her own motion (Article 284(1) TMK). If the defendant refuses a test that is necessary to establish parentage and poses no risk to health, the judge may treat the expected result as established against him (Article 284(2) TMK). The HMK goes further: on those conditions everyone must allow blood or tissue samples to be taken to establish parentage; if someone refuses without good reason, the judge orders the test to be carried out by force (Article 292(1) HMK).

A test is possible even after the father's death. In the 2023 case mentioned above, the first-instance court had blood and bone/tissue samples from the claimants and the deceased examined at the Forensic Medicine Institute (Adli Tıp Kurumu). The Code also sets a presumption: if the defendant had sexual intercourse with the mother between the 300th and the 180th day before the birth, he is presumed to be the father (Article 302(1) TMK).

## Effects of a paternity judgment

Once final, the child is transferred to the father's family record in the civil register with the father's name and surname (Article 28(4) of the Civil Registry Services Law No. 5490). Other effects:

- **Inheritance:** a person born outside marriage whose parentage has been established by judgment inherits on the father's side like relatives born in wedlock (Article 498 TMK).
- **Child maintenance:** maintenance can be claimed together with the paternity suit; if the judge finds paternity highly likely, he or she can order suitable maintenance for the child before the judgment (Article 333 TMK).
- **The mother's claims:** with the suit or separately, the mother can claim from the father or his heirs the costs of the birth, her living costs for six weeks before and six weeks after the birth, and other costs caused by pregnancy and birth (Article 304(1) TMK), even if the child was stillborn. The article sets no separate time limit for these claims.
- **Custody:** the judgment does not give the father custody; if the parents are not married, custody belongs to the mother (Article 337(1) TMK). The father can ask for appropriate personal contact with the child (Article 323 TMK). See [child custody after divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey).

## Acknowledgement and court-established paternity in Germany

Under German law the father of a child is the man who was married to the mother at the time of birth, who has acknowledged paternity (Vaterschaftsanerkennung), or whose paternity has been established by a court (section 1592 BGB; for court establishment, section 1600d BGB and section 169 FamFG). Dutch law also provides for acknowledgement (erkenning) and court establishment (gerechtelijke vaststelling van het ouderschap) in Book 1 of the Burgerlijk Wetboek. Which court is competent there, which law it applies and which time limits run are questions of German or Dutch law; for these please contact the Jugendamt, your gemeente or a lawyer qualified in that country.

From the Turkish side, a judgment given there needs recognition under Article 58 MÖHUK to take effect in Turkey; how an acknowledgement made there is entered in the Turkish register is a separate matter (Article 28(5) of Law No. 5490). For related topics see [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can file a paternity suit in Turkey?

The mother and the child (Article 301(1) TMK). The defendant is the father or, after his death, his heirs.

### Can the father file a paternity suit in Turkey?

No. Article 301 TMK does not give him that right; his route is acknowledgement (Article 295 TMK).

### What is the time limit for a paternity suit in Turkey?

For the mother, one year from the birth; if there is a justified reason for the delay, one month after it ceases (Article 303 TMK). For the child the Code sets no time limit.

### Which Turkish court is competent?

The family court at the domicile of either party at the time of filing or of the birth (Article 283 TMK); if nobody is domiciled in Turkey, Article 41 MÖHUK applies. Maintenance and the mother's claims can be made in the same proceedings.

### How long does a paternity suit take in Turkey?

The law does not fix a duration; it depends on the DNA test, on parties abroad and on appeals. Since 31.07.2026 the interval between hearings may as a rule not exceed three months; where an expert examination takes longer or evidence is taken by letter of request, the judge may set a longer interval with reasons (Article 147(3) HMK). Parentage cases continue during the judicial recess (Article 103(1)(b) HMK).

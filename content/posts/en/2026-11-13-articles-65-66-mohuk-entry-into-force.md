The last two articles of Turkey's Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) say when the law took effect and who executes it. MÖHUK was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 12 December 2007, No. 26728, and under Article 65 entered into force the same day. Both provisions are short and belong together, so they are covered in one post. This post completes the series on every article of MÖHUK.

## Articles 65 and 66 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Entry into force**
>
> Article 65 – (1) This Law enters into force on the date of its publication.

> **Execution**
>
> Article 66 – (1) The provisions of this Law are executed by the Council of Ministers.

Original Turkish text:

> **Yürürlük**
>
> MADDE 65 – (1) Bu Kanun yayımı tarihinde yürürlüğe girer.

> **Yürütme**
>
> MADDE 66 – (1) Bu Kanun hükümlerini Bakanlar Kurulu yürütür.

As of November 2026, neither article has been amended. The amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27, amended by Law No. 7550 (in force since 4 June 2025).

## Legislative reasoning

The reasoning of the government bill (Articles 64 and 65 of the bill) only states that these are the provisions on entry into force and execution (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Date of entry into force

The law was adopted on 27 November 2007 and published on 12 December 2007. Because entry into force is tied to publication, MÖHUK has applied since 12 December 2007. On the same day, under Article 64, the former Law No. 2675 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure was repealed. MÖHUK contains no transitional provisions (geçici madde).

### How to read "Council of Ministers" today

Article 66 still refers to the Council of Ministers (Bakanlar Kurulu). In 2018 this wording was replaced in some laws by decree-laws (KHK); for example, Article 139 of Decree-Law No. 700 changed "Bakanlar Kurulunca" (by the Council of Ministers) to "Cumhurbaşkanınca" (by the President) in one paragraph of the Turkish Civil Code. No decree-law appears in MÖHUK's amendment table.

The Council of Ministers itself was abolished by the 2017 constitutional amendment (Law No. 6771). Under Article 8 of the Constitution, executive power and function belong to the President. Provisional Article 21(G) of the Constitution provides that powers given to the Council of Ministers by laws and other legislation are exercised by the President until the legislation concerned is amended. Under Article 18 of Law No. 6771, this provision entered into force on the day the President took office after the 2018 elections (9 July 2018). Article 66 MÖHUK is read together with this constitutional rule; the wording of MÖHUK itself has not been changed.

## Practical points if you live abroad

These two articles have no separate practical consequences for people living abroad. Questions on the applicable law, the jurisdiction of Turkish courts, and recognition and enforcement are answered by the earlier articles; a good starting point is [Article 1 MÖHUK – Scope of the Turkish PIL Act](/en/publications/legislation/article-1-mohuk-scope).

## Frequently asked questions

### When did Turkey's private international law act enter into force?

On 12 December 2007, the day it was published in Official Gazette No. 26728 (Article 65 MÖHUK).

### Has MÖHUK been amended since then?

As of November 2026, only one amendment has been incorporated into the current text: Article 27, amended by Law No. 7550 (in force since 4 June 2025).

Under Article 28 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK), legal personality (kişilik) begins at the moment a child is completely born alive and ends at death. An unborn child (cenin) has legal capacity from conception, on condition that it is born alive. In practice the rule matters most in inheritance cases: when a child is on the way at the time of a death, or when a newborn dies shortly after birth.

## Article 28 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **C. Beginning and end of personality**
>
> **I. Birth and death**
>
> Article 28 – Personality begins at the moment the child is completely born alive and ends with death.
>
> The child acquires legal capacity from the moment of conception, on condition that it is born alive.

Original Turkish text:

> **C. Kişiliğin başlangıcı ve sonu**
>
> **I. Doğum ve ölüm**
>
> Madde 28- Kişilik, çocuğun sağ olarak tamamıyla doğduğu anda başlar ve ölümle sona erer.
>
> Çocuk hak ehliyetini, sağ doğmak koşuluyla, ana rahmine düştüğü andan başlayarak elde eder.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026 it does not appear in the amendment list of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr; the latest law in that list is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026). The general rule on legal capacity is covered in [Article 8 TMK – Legal capacity](/en/publications/legislation/article-8-turkish-civil-code-legal-capacity).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 27 of the former Civil Code No. 743 and was carried over unchanged, with its headings simplified in language (Source: reasoning of the draft law submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Born alive or stillborn: why it changes the inheritance

To inherit, a person must be alive and capable of inheriting when the deceased dies (Article 580(1) TMK). An unborn child is not yet born at that moment. It still inherits because Article 28(2) dates its legal capacity back to conception, and Article 582 TMK says the same for inheritance: an unborn child inherits if it is born alive; a stillborn child cannot be an heir.

Take a father who dies while his wife is pregnant:

- If the child is stillborn, it was never an heir. The father's estate is divided as if the child had never existed.
- If the child is born alive and dies a few hours later, it has inherited from its father (Articles 495 and 582 TMK). That share is now part of the child's own estate and passes to the child's own statutory heirs.

Who ends up with what is different in the two cases, so whether the child was born alive is a real question of rights between the heirs. Anyone who claims that a person was alive at a given time must prove it (Article 29(1) TMK), and birth and death are proved, as a rule, by the entries in the civil registry (nüfus sicili) (Article 30(1) TMK). The law sets no minimum period of survival; it is enough that the child is completely born alive.

### No division before the birth

As a rule, every heir may ask for the estate to be divided at any time (Article 642(1) TMK). If there is an unborn child who may become an heir when the succession opens, division is postponed until the birth (Article 643(1) TMK). If the mother is in need, she can ask for her living costs until the birth to be paid from the estate (Article 643(2)).

Where the interests of the unborn child require it, the guardianship authority, which is the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi), appoints an administrator for the estate (yönetim kayyımı) (Article 427(1)(3) and Article 397(2) TMK). If someone asks for an inherited house or plot in Türkiye to be divided before the birth, the division is postponed until the child is born.

### The end of personality

Personality ends with death, and the succession opens at death (Article 575(1) TMK); the heirs acquire the estate as a whole by operation of law (Article 599(1) TMK). If a death is missing from the civil registry or the entry is wrong, the true facts may be proved by any evidence (Article 30(2) TMK). The presumption of death (Article 31) and the declaration of absence (Articles 32–35) deal with people whose bodies are not found.

## What this means if you live abroad

**Which law applies?** Legal capacity and capacity to act are governed by the person's national law (Article 9(1) of the Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure, MÖHUK). Succession is governed by the national law of the deceased, and Turkish law applies to immovable property in Türkiye (Article 20(1) MÖHUK). The official reasoning of Article 20 MÖHUK says that the questions of who becomes an heir and who is capable of inheriting fall under this main rule, and it cites the TMK provisions on the unborn child's right to inherit as an example (the reasoning refers to the article by its draft number, 19). On that basis, the unborn child's right to inherit from a Turkish father, or in respect of property in Türkiye, is decided under Article 582 TMK. This is the author's assessment based on the reasoning: the text of the law has no separate conflict rule for the unborn child's right to inherit, and this article does not rely on a verified court decision. How a court would decide in a specific dispute is not certain. More on the law of succession: [Article 20 MÖHUK – Inheritance](/en/publications/legislation/article-20-mohuk-inheritance-turkey).

**Registration.** The birth of every child born alive must be reported, if it happens abroad, to the Turkish consulate or embassy within sixty days (Article 15(1) of the Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490, Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). The law makes no distinction according to how long the child lived. Stillborn children are not entered in the family register (Article 15(7)). If a baby died shortly after birth in Germany or the Netherlands and has rights in an estate in Türkiye, keep the local birth and death certificates; if there is no Turkish entry, the person relying on the live birth has to prove it (Articles 29(1) and 30(2) TMK). How a birth is reported: [Birth registration in Turkey for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad).

When a Turkish citizen dies abroad, the consulate or embassy must report the death to the General Directorate or the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1) Law No. 5490). Because the period runs from when the mission learns of the death, it helps to inform it yourself and hand in the foreign death certificate.

Whether and when a child has legal capacity or can inherit under German or Dutch law is a matter for that country's law; please consult a lawyer admitted there.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does legal personality begin under Turkish law?

At the moment the child is completely born alive; it ends with death (Article 28(1) TMK).

### Does a baby who dies shortly after birth inherit?

Yes. If it was born alive, it is an heir however briefly it lived, and its share passes to its own heirs (Articles 28 and 582 TMK).

### My husband died while I was pregnant. Can the estate be divided?

If there is an unborn child who may become an heir, division is postponed until the birth. A mother in need can ask for her living costs until the birth to be paid from the estate (Article 643 TMK).

### Is a stillborn child entered in the Turkish register?

No. Stillborn children are not entered in the family register (Article 15(7) Law No. 5490).

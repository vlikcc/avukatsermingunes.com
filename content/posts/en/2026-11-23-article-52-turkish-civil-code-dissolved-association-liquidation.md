Article 52 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) opens the section on the end of a legal person's personality. A legal person that has come to an end keeps its personality during liquidation (tasfiye), but only for the purpose of liquidation. This concerns members and board members of a dissolved association or ended foundation, and anyone who has a claim against it or owes it money.

## Article 52 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **E. End of personality**
>
> **I. Limited continuation**
>
> Article 52 – The personality of a legal person that has come to an end continues during liquidation, its capacity (ehliyet) being limited to the purpose of liquidation.

Turkish original:

> **E. Kişiliğin sona ermesi**
>
> **I. Sınırlı devam etme**
>
> Madde 52- Sona eren tüzel kişinin kişiliği, ehliyeti tasfiye amacıyla sınırlı olmak üzere tasfiye sırasında da devam eder.

As of November 2026, Article 52 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article is new: neither the former Civil Code No. 743, nor the Swiss source code, nor the 1984 preliminary draft contained it. A principle found in the Turkish Commercial Code for commercial companies, and accepted in legal writing for all private-law legal persons, was made a general rule of the Civil Code: when a ground for termination arises, personality does not disappear at once; until liquidation is complete, the purpose of liquidation replaces the founding purpose. Only then can it be determined to whom acts done during liquidation are attributed. The purpose of liquidation is to turn the assets into money, pay the debts and collect the receivables (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Between the ground for termination and the end of personality

An association ends automatically (Art. 87 TMK; any interested party may ask the justice of the peace to confirm this), by a dissolution resolution of the general assembly (Art. 88), or by court order where its purpose becomes contrary to law or morality (Art. 89). A foundation ends automatically when its purpose can no longer be achieved and cannot be changed, and is struck off the register by court decision; where its purpose or activities are prohibited, it is dissolved (Art. 116). Article 52 applies after any of these events: the legal person no longer exists for its founding purpose, only for liquidation.

For associations, the law also fixes the end of this phase: once liquidation and transfer are complete, the association's entry in the associations register (dernekler kütüğü) is deleted (Art. 15(2) Associations Law No. 5253).

### Which rules govern liquidation?

The general rule is Article 53 TMK: unless the law or the founding document provides otherwise, the rules on official liquidation of an estate apply. For associations, Article 15 of Law No. 5253 sets a special rule. Liquidation follows the statutes. If the statutes leave it to the general assembly and no resolution is passed, or if the association was dissolved by court, its money, property and rights pass by court decision to the association whose purpose is closest and which had the most members on the date of closure. If an investigation or court case for dissolution is pending, no transfer takes place until it is concluded (Art. 15(3)).

For foundations established under the Civil Code (yeni vakıf), what remains after the debts are settled passes according to the foundation deed or, if the deed is silent, by court decision after consulting the Directorate General of Foundations, to a foundation with a similar purpose; for dissolved foundations, it passes to the Directorate General (Art. 27 Foundations Law No. 5737).

How the general allocation rule in Article 54 TMK (nearest public body, and in every case the competent public body where the purpose was unlawful or immoral) relates to the rule in Article 15 of Law No. 5253 for associations dissolved by court is not clear from the wording; this post takes no position on it.

### Standing as a party in court

Anyone with legal capacity also has capacity to be a party (Art. 50 Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). Since Article 52 TMK preserves legal capacity during liquidation, an association or foundation in liquidation can be a party to proceedings, within the limits of the liquidation purpose. This is the author's assessment, drawn from reading the two provisions together.

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you are a member of, or donor to, a Turkish association that is being dissolved, the remaining assets follow the statutes and the rules of Article 15 of Law No. 5253 described above. If you have a claim against the association, it is directed against the association in liquidation, because its personality continues during liquidation. For domicile and the competent court, see the post on [Article 51 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-51-turkish-civil-code-domicile-legal-person); for the link with Article 47, see the post on [Article 47 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-47-turkish-civil-code-legal-personality).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a dissolved association in Türkiye cease to exist at once?

No. Its personality continues during liquidation, limited to the liquidation purpose (Art. 52 TMK); its registry entry is deleted once liquidation and transfer are complete (Art. 15(2) Law No. 5253).

### Who gets the assets of a dissolved association?

The statutes decide first. If no resolution is passed or the association was dissolved by court, they pass by court decision to the association with the closest purpose and the most members (Art. 15(1) Law No. 5253).

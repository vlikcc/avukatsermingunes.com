Article 4 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sits in the introductory part of the Code and sets the standard a Turkish court uses when the law leaves a question to its discretion (takdir yetkisi): law and equity (hukuka ve hakkaniyete göre). It is in the background of every claim whose amount the Code does not fix, such as compensation and maintenance after divorce or claims under the matrimonial property regime. If you live abroad and are a party to such a case in Turkey, it helps to know what the court's discretion rests on and where it stops.

## Article 4 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Judicial discretion**
>
> Article 4 – In matters where the law grants the judge discretion or requires him to take into account the circumstances of the case or good cause, the judge decides according to law and equity.

Original Turkish text:

> **III. Hâkimin takdir yetkisi**
>
> Madde 4 - Kanunun takdir yetkisi tanıdığı veya durumun gereklerini ya da haklı sebepleri göz önünde tutmayı emrettiği konularda hâkim, hukuka ve hakkaniyete göre karar verir.

The article has not changed since the Code entered into force on 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system (recorded 31 July 2026) does not list Article 4; the last law in the table is Law No. 7589, which amended Articles 440 and 444 (those changes in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 4 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The old wording "hak ve nısfetle hükmeder" suggested that the judge could set the law aside and look only at equity, so it was replaced with "hukuka ve hakkaniyete göre karar verir". When using his discretion, the judge must decide first according to the law and then according to equity. The heading was taken from the 1984 preliminary draft (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Where the article comes in

Article 4 does not create a claim of its own. It applies when another provision leaves the outcome to the court. Its three phrases recur throughout the Civil Code:

- **Good cause (haklı sebep):** A change of first name can be requested from the court only on good cause (Article 27(1)). If a legal representative refuses consent to marriage without good cause, the court may, after hearing him, allow the minor or the person under guardianship to marry (Article 128). One spouse represents the marital union for the family's other needs where, among other cases, the court has authorised him or her to do so for good cause (Article 188).
- **Circumstances of the case (durumun gerekleri):** Material compensation and maintenance for the needy spouse (yoksulluk nafakası) may be awarded as a lump sum or, as the circumstances require, as periodic payments (Article 176(1)). If the child's staying in the family disrupts its peace to an unbearable degree and the circumstances leave no other option, the court may, on request, place the child with a family or an institution (Article 347(2)).
- **Equity (hakkaniyet):** Periodic maintenance or compensation can be increased or reduced where the parties' finances change or equity requires it (Article 176(4)). In a divorce for adultery or attempt on life, the court may reduce or cancel, on equitable grounds, the guilty spouse's share in the surplus (artık değer) (Article 236(2)). Where co-owners are split equally on major management matters, the court decides equitably (Article 691).

Sometimes the Code uses none of these words and still sets no figure, as with "appropriate" material and non-material compensation after divorce (Article 174). In one of the decisions below, the regional appeal court had set that compensation expressly by reference to the equity principle in Article 4.

### How it relates to other provisions

Article 1(2) TMK deals with the case where the law contains no applicable provision. Article 4 deals with the case where a provision exists but leaves the outcome to the court. More on Article 1: [Article 1 Turkish Civil Code – Sources of Law](/en/publications/legislation/article-1-turkish-civil-code-sources-of-law). The "circumstances of the case" standard also appears in Article 3(2): a person who fails to take the care the circumstances require of him cannot claim good faith.

Discretion rests on facts, and as a rule the party relying on a fact must prove it (Article 6). The Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 (TBK) has similar rules: where the amount of damage cannot be fully proven, the court sets it equitably (Article 50 TBK), and it determines the extent of compensation in view of the circumstances and the degree of fault (Article 51 TBK). In one of the decisions below, the Court of Cassation applied Article 4 TMK together with Article 50 TBK.

Discretion also has a procedural limit. The court is bound by the parties' requests and cannot award more than was asked for (Article 26(1) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). Equity is no ground for going beyond the request.

## Practical points if you live abroad

If Turkish law applies in a Turkish divorce, maintenance or matrimonial property case (where there is a foreign element, the applicable law is determined under the private international law act No. 5718, MÖHUK), the amount of compensation and maintenance, the contribution share or the existence of good cause is often for the court to assess. That assessment rests on the facts in the file. If you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere abroad, you need to document your income, your expenses, and when and with what money an asset was acquired, in a form the court can evaluate. Except where the law provides otherwise, the court cannot take into account of its own motion facts that neither party has put forward (Article 25(1) HMK).

When setting the amount you claim, keep the limit in Article 26 HMK in mind. Law No. 7589 repealed Article 107 HMK (action for an unquantified claim, belirsiz alacak davası) and added a fourth paragraph to Article 109 HMK (in force since 31 July 2026). Under it, where only part of a claim is sued for, the amount can be increased once in the same proceedings until the end of the evidence stage, without the bar on extending the claim.

If you moved abroad after the divorce or your income changed significantly: under Article 176(4), periodic maintenance or compensation can be increased or reduced where the parties' finances change or equity requires it. On division of property and the value-increase share: [Division of property on divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/division-of-property-divorce-turkey).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2025/6312, K. 2026/8280, 17 September 2026**

The case concerned a value-increase share (değer artış payı) and a participation claim (katılma alacağı). According to witness statements, the wife had earned income by sewing quilts and had contributed, if only slightly, to acquiring a share bought before 2002, but the evidence did not show the rate of her contribution. In one of its grounds for reversal, the Chamber held that the claim should not be dismissed; instead, under Article 4 TMK and Article 50 TBK, a "small contribution rate in line with law and equity" had to be set. The judgment was reversed (unanimous).

**Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2026/1953, K. 2026/8024, 14 September 2026**

The regional appeal court (Bölge Adliye Mahkemesi), considering the parties' economic and social position, their degree of fault, the purchasing power of money and the equity principle in Article 4 TMK, had awarded the wife TL 75,000 each in material and non-material compensation. In her appeal, however, she had limited her request to TL 75,000 in total. Stating that the court is bound by the request under Article 26 HMK, the Chamber reversed the judgment as to the compensation amounts (unanimous).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does Article 4 of the Turkish Civil Code regulate?

That the judge decides according to law and equity where the law gives him discretion or tells him to consider the circumstances or good cause.

### What are the limits of judicial discretion?

The judge decides first by law, then by equity (legislative reasoning). Discretion rests on the facts in the file, and the court cannot award more than was requested (Article 26(1) HMK). In both decisions above, the Court of Cassation reviewed discretionary rulings against these standards and reversed them.

### Where does the equity principle appear in the Turkish Civil Code?

For example in Article 176(4) (changing periodic maintenance or compensation), Article 227(2) (contribution to an asset already disposed of), Article 236(2) (reducing the share in the surplus), Article 365(3) (reducing maintenance obligations between relatives) and Article 691 (management of co-owned property).

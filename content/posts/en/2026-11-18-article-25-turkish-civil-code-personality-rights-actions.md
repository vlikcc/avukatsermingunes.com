Article 25 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) lists the actions open to someone whose personality rights (kişilik hakkı) are infringed: preventing a threatened infringement, stopping an ongoing one, declaring unlawful an infringement whose effects continue, publication of a correction or the judgment, damages and non-pecuniary damages (manevi tazminat), and surrender of profits. It also limits the assignment and inheritance of non-pecuniary damages claims and sets out which court has jurisdiction. When an infringement is unlawful is governed by the preceding Article 24 TMK.

## Article 25 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Actions**
>
> Article 25 – The claimant may ask the court to prevent a threatened infringement, to stop an ongoing infringement, and to declare unlawful an infringement whose effects continue even though it has ended.
>
> Together with these, the claimant may also request that a correction or the judgment be notified to third parties or published.
>
> The claimant's right to claim pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages, and to claim that profits obtained through the unlawful infringement be handed over to them under the rules on agency without authority, is reserved.
>
> A claim for non-pecuniary damages cannot be assigned unless the other party has accepted it; it does not pass to the heirs unless the deceased had asserted it.
>
> To protect their personality rights, the claimant may bring proceedings in the court of their own domicile or of the defendant's domicile.

Turkish original:

> **2. Davalar**
>
> Madde 25- Davacı, hâkimden saldırı tehlikesinin önlenmesini, sürmekte olan saldırıya son verilmesini, sona ermiş olsa bile etkileri devam eden saldırının hukuka aykırılığının tespitini isteyebilir.
>
> Davacı bunlarla birlikte, düzeltmenin veya kararın üçüncü kişilere bildirilmesi ya da yayımlanması isteminde de bulunabilir.
>
> Davacının, maddî ve manevî tazminat istemleri ile hukuka aykırı saldırı dolayısıyla elde edilmiş olan kazancın vekâletsiz iş görme hükümlerine göre kendisine verilmesine ilişkin istemde bulunma hakkı saklıdır.
>
> Manevî tazminat istemi, karşı tarafça kabul edilmiş olmadıkça devredilemez; miras bırakan tarafından ileri sürülmüş olmadıkça mirasçılara geçmez.
>
> Davacı, kişilik haklarının korunması için kendi yerleşim yeri veya davalının yerleşim yeri mahkemesinde dava açabilir.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 25; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 24/a of the former Code, added by Law No. 3444 and modelled on Article 28a of the Swiss Civil Code, in force since 1985. The actions to prevent, stop and declare require neither damage nor fault. Under the old text a claim for non-pecuniary damages passed to the heirs even if the holder had never asserted it; the reasoning considers this incompatible with the nature of such damages and therefore changed paragraph 4. To "assert" the claim, a lawsuit is not required; a provable intention of the injured person is enough. On this point the Code departs deliberately from the Swiss model (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Prevention, removal and declaratory actions

The three actions follow the stage of the infringement. If a serious infringement is imminent, the claimant seeks prevention; if it is ongoing (for example a post that is still online), removal; if it has ended but its effects continue, a declaration of unlawfulness. As the reasoning states expressly, neither fault nor damage is required; an unlawful infringement is enough (Article 24 TMK).

The claimant may also ask for a correction or the judgment to be notified to third parties or published. The Code of Obligations likewise allows the court, instead of or in addition to a sum of money, to issue a decision condemning the infringement and order its publication (Article 58(2) TBK).

### Damages, non-pecuniary damages and surrender of profits

Pecuniary damages require fault and loss under tort law (Articles 49 and 50 TBK). For non-pecuniary harm, the injured person may claim a sum of money (Article 58(1) TBK). Surrender of profits is a separate claim: if the infringer earned money, for example by using a photo without permission, that profit is handed over under the rules on agency without authority.

Damages claims become time-barred two years after the injured person learns of the loss and of the person liable, and in any case ten years after the act; if criminal law provides a longer limitation period for the act, that period applies (Article 72(1) TBK). This rule concerns damages; the Code sets no separate time limit for the actions to prevent, stop or declare.

### Assignment and inheritance of non-pecuniary damages

A claim for non-pecuniary damages can only be assigned if the other party has accepted it. If the holder did not assert it during their lifetime, it does not pass to the heirs. According to the reasoning, a provable intention is enough, for example a formal notice (ihtarname) sent to the other side; a lawsuit is not required.

### Jurisdiction and mediation

The claimant may sue at their own domicile or at the defendant's. For tort claims, the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure also gives jurisdiction to the court where the act was committed, where the damage occurred or may occur, and where the injured person is domiciled (Article 16 HMK). Personality rights claims are not on the list in Article 18/B of the Mediation in Civil Disputes Act No. 6325 (HUAK), which makes mediation a precondition for suing (that list covers tenancy, partition of co-ownership, condominium and neighbour disputes). If the dispute falls within an area where another law requires mediation (such as commercial or employment disputes), that needs to be checked separately.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

### Can I sue in Türkiye?

The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 of the Turkish private international law act No. 5718, MÖHUK). If the claimant lives in Germany, their domicile is not in Türkiye, so jurisdiction under Article 25(5) TMK will usually rest on the defendant's domicile in Türkiye. If the defendant also lives abroad, whether the place where the damage occurred under Article 16 HMK gives a Turkish court jurisdiction depends on the facts. The Code does not expressly regulate how Article 25(5) TMK and Article 16 HMK relate to each other, so this assessment is my view, and a court may decide otherwise.

### Which law applies?

For infringements through the media and the internet, the injured person may choose among the connecting factors in Article 35 MÖHUK. If Turkish law applies, the actions and damages follow this article and the TBK. Whether a Turkish judgment is recognised and enforced in Germany or the Netherlands is governed by the law of that country; please ask a lawyer admitted there.

### A deceased relative's claim

If a relative in Türkiye whose personality rights were infringed died without asserting a claim for non-pecuniary damages, the claim does not pass to the heirs (Article 25(4) TMK). The deceased's own claim and any claims of relatives based on their own personality rights are different things.

## Frequently asked questions

### What actions are available for infringement of personality rights in Türkiye?

Prevention, removal and declaratory actions, together with publication of a correction or the judgment, as well as pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages and surrender of profits (Article 25(1)–(3) TMK).

### What is the limitation period for non-pecuniary damages?

Two years from learning of the loss and the person liable, and in any case ten years from the act; if the act is a crime with a longer limitation period, that period (Article 72(1) TBK).

### Which Turkish court has jurisdiction?

The court of the claimant's or the defendant's domicile (Article 25(5) TMK). For torts, the places listed in Article 16 HMK also have jurisdiction.

### Is mediation required before suing?

Personality rights claims are not on the HUAK Article 18/B list. If the dispute falls within an area where another law requires mediation (such as commercial or employment disputes), check that separately.

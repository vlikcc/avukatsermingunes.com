Article 56 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) opens the part on associations (dernek) and defines what an association is in Türkiye: a group of persons with legal personality, formed by at least seven natural or legal persons who permanently pool their knowledge and work for a specific common purpose other than sharing profit. It matters to you if you want to found or join an association in Türkiye, deal with a Turkish association from abroad, or want your own association to become a founder of one in Türkiye.

## Article 56 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **A. Formation**
>
> **I. Definition**
>
> Article 56 – Associations are groups of persons with legal personality, formed by at least seven natural or legal persons who permanently combine their knowledge and work in order to achieve a specific and common purpose other than sharing profit.
>
> No association may be formed for purposes contrary to law or morals.

Original Turkish text:

> **A. Kuruluşu**
>
> **I. Tanımı**
>
> Madde 56- Dernekler, gerçek veya tüzel en az yedi kişinin kazanç paylaşma dışında belirli ve ortak bir amacı gerçekleştirmek üzere, bilgi ve çalışmalarını sürekli olarak birleştirmek suretiyle oluşturdukları, tüzel kişiliğe sahip kişi topluluklarıdır.
>
> Hukuka veya ahlâka aykırı amaçlarla dernek kurulamaz.

Footnote in the official text to paragraph 1: Article 31 of Law No. 4963 of 30 July 2003 replaced “en az yedi gerçek kişinin” (at least seven natural persons) with “gerçek veya tüzel en az yedi kişinin” (at least seven natural or legal persons).

As of November 2026, the amendment table on mevzuat.gov.tr lists only this Law No. 4963 entry for Article 56 (in force since 30 July 2003); the article has not changed since (latest law in the table: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the old Civil Code regulated associations without defining them. The definition combines Article 1 of the former Associations Law No. 2908 with Article 53 of the old Code and draws on legal writing; its aim is to separate the association from the simple partnership, commercial companies, the foundation and informal volunteering. The reasoning belongs to the original wording and therefore lists “at least seven natural persons”; legal persons have only been able to act as founders since the 2003 amendment (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### The Associations Law uses the same definition

The detailed rules are now in the Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu) and the Associations Regulation (Dernekler Yönetmeliği). Article 2(a) of Law No. 5253 defines the association with the same elements and adds that the purpose must be one “not prohibited by law”. Where the Associations Law is silent, the Civil Code applies (Article 36 of Law No. 5253).

The same Law describes a structure that is easily mistaken for an association. When associations join with each other, or with foundations, trade unions and similar bodies, under a name such as “initiative” or “movement” for a common aim on a temporary basis, that is a “platform” and has no legal personality (Article 2(f)). It therefore cannot hold rights or owe obligations in its own name.

### Seven founders, including legal persons

You need at least seven founders. Some or all of them can be companies, foundations or other associations. In that case the founding notice includes the legal person's name, seat and founding document, and the decision of its governing body naming the natural person who will represent it (Associations Regulation, Article 5(c)). When a legal person is a member, its chair of the board or the person it appoints to represent it casts its vote (Article 6 of Law No. 5253).

There is one exception: legal persons cannot be founders or members of children's associations (çocuk dernekleri) (Associations Regulation, Article 5).

### No sharing of profit, no unlawful purpose

What separates an association from a company is that it does not distribute profit to its members. Article 12 of Law No. 5253 gives an example: an association may set up a fund (sandık) to meet its members' basic needs, provided the statute allows it and the profit is not distributed to members or passed on to them as income, interest or under any other name.

No association may be formed for a purpose contrary to law or morals (Article 56(2)). At formation, the chief local administrator (mülkî amir) reviews the founding notice, the documents and the statute within sixty days. If the notice, the statute or the legal position of the founders is unlawful or incomplete, the administrator asks for it to be fixed; if that does not happen within thirty days, the public prosecutor is informed so that an action for dissolution can be brought (Article 60 TMK). If the purpose becomes unlawful or immoral later, the court dissolves the association (Article 89 TMK).

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

An association registered in Germany or the Netherlands is not an association under Article 56 TMK; its formation and internal rules follow the law of that country, and you should ask a lawyer or authority qualified there about them. From the Turkish law side, three points are useful:

- A Turkish association may open a branch abroad and join associations or organisations formed abroad, for the purpose set out in its statute (Article 91 TMK; Article 5(1) of Law No. 5253).
- An association formed abroad needs permission from the Ministry of the Interior, which first asks for the opinion of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, before it can found an association or become a founder in Türkiye (Article 5(2) of Law No. 5253; Associations Regulation, Article 26). A written statement signed by the founders that this permission was granted is attached to the founding notice (Regulation, Article 5(d)).
- Since Law No. 7533 of 21 November 2024 (in force since 30 November 2024), Additional Article 2 of Law No. 5253 provides that the records of associations and other non-profit organisations founded abroad by Turkish citizens are kept by the Ministry of the Interior through the relevant authorities. The provision does not say how this is done or whether it places any duty on these organisations.

For the general rules on legal persons see the post on [Article 47 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-47-turkish-civil-code-legal-personality); for an association's organs and capacity to act see [Article 49 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-49-tmk-capacity-to-act-legal-persons).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people do you need to set up an association in Türkiye?

At least seven. Founders can be natural persons or legal persons such as companies, foundations or other associations (Article 56(1) TMK; Article 2(a) of Law No. 5253).

### Can a Turkish association earn income?

The law bans sharing profit, not earning income. Paying out profit to members, or passing it to them as income or interest, is contrary to the definition of an association (Article 56(1) TMK; Article 12 of Law No. 5253).

### Can our association in Germany or the Netherlands be a founder of an association in Türkiye?

Yes, but only with permission from the Turkish Ministry of the Interior, which asks for the opinion of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Article 5(2) of Law No. 5253).

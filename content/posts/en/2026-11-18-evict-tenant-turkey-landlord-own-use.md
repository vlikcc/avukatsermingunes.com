If you, your spouse, your children, your parents or someone you are legally obliged to support has to live in your flat in Turkey, you can end the lease by bringing an eviction claim for own use (ihtiyaç nedeniyle tahliye) under Article 350(1) of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 (TBK). For a fixed-term lease the claim must be filed **within one month** after the term ends, in the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi), and mediation is compulsory before you sue.

If you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe and need the flat because you are retiring, returning to Turkey for good (kesin dönüş) or your child is going to study there, this article explains the deadlines and how to run the case from abroad. A tenant's own written promise to leave on a set date is a different route; see [how to evict a tenant in Turkey: undertaking to vacate](/en/publications/how-to-evict-a-tenant-in-turkey-undertaking).

As of November 2026, the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr show that Articles 347–356 TBK have not been amended since they came into force. The latest amendment to the TBK, Law No. 7589 (Official Gazette of 31 July 2026), only changed Article 55, and the provisions it changed in the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) are unrelated to this topic. Mandatory mediation in tenancy disputes has applied since 1 September 2023; the last amendment to the Mediation Law (HUAK), by Law No. 7531 (in force 14 November 2024), did not touch the rule for tenancy disputes.

## When a landlord in Turkey can evict for own use

For residential and covered commercial premises, the end of the lease term is not in itself a ground for eviction. Unless the tenant gives notice at least fifteen days before the term ends, the lease is extended for one year on the same terms, and the landlord cannot rely on the expiry of the term (Art. 347(1) TBK). Own use can therefore only be enforced through a court claim. Article 350(1)(1) TBK reads:

> Kiraya veren, kira sözleşmesini;
> 1. Kiralananı kendisi, eşi, altsoyu, üstsoyu veya kanun gereği bakmakla yükümlü olduğu diğer kişiler için konut ya da işyeri gereksinimi sebebiyle kullanma zorunluluğu varsa,

Unofficial translation: the landlord may end the lease where he or she must use the premises as a home or workplace for himself or herself, a spouse, descendants, ascendants or other persons the landlord is legally obliged to support.

In practice this means:

- The claim is brought by the **landlord**.
- The need must belong to you, your spouse, your children or grandchildren, your parents, or other persons you are legally obliged to support.
- The law requires a **necessity to use** the property (kullanma zorunluluğu), not just a wish.

The law does not define "necessity". The court looks at the facts of each case to decide whether the need really exists. In the author's assessment, the real question for a landlord living abroad is whether the return is concrete when the claim is filed. A pension decision, the end of a job, notice on a rented home in Germany or the Netherlands, or a child's enrolment at a school or university in Turkey can show that. "I plan to go back in a few years" may not be enough.

These rules cannot be changed to the tenant's detriment (Art. 354 TBK). A clause saying "the tenant will leave whenever the landlord asks" does not let you skip the court claim.

## The one-month deadline

**Fixed-term lease:** the claim must be filed within one month from the end of the term (Art. 350(1) TBK, last sentence).

If the lease has been extended by a year under Article 347 TBK, the author's reading is that the end of each extension year also counts as the end of the term. This is an interpretation of the statutory text; count the extension years from the start date of your own lease.

**Lease of indefinite duration:** the one-month period runs from the date that follows from the general notice dates and periods (Art. 350(1) TBK). Absent local custom, that is the end of a six-month rental period with three months' notice (Art. 329 TBK; periods are counted from the start of the lease, Art. 328).

**Extending the deadline by a year:** if you tell the tenant in writing, at the latest within the filing period, that you will sue, the period for filing is extended by one rental year (Art. 353 TBK). A notice served through a notary (ihtarname) can be used; the law only requires writing. It is not a precondition for an Article 350 claim, but if your return date is not yet fixed, it buys you a year.

## New owner: the six-month rule

If you buy a let flat before moving back, the lease continues with you; the new owner becomes a party to the lease (Art. 310 TBK). Ownership of land is acquired by registration in the land register (Art. 705, Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). Article 351 TBK gives the new owner two options:

1. **Notice and six months.** You notify the tenant **in writing** of your need within one month of acquiring the property and may end the lease by a claim filed **six months later** (Art. 351(1)).
2. **End of term.** You may also sue within one month after the lease term ends (Art. 351(2)).

The law does not say expressly how long you have after the six months have passed. No court decision on this point was reviewed for this article. In the author's assessment, applying for mediation as soon as the six months are over is the safest course. When you buy, the one-month notice period runs from registration at the land registry; if you are not in Turkey, have the notice prepared at the same time as the transfer appointment.

## Is mediation mandatory for own-use eviction in Turkey?

Yes. Since 1 September 2023, applying to a mediator (arabulucu) before suing is a procedural requirement in disputes arising from a tenancy (Art. 18/B(1)(a), Law No. 6325 on Mediation in Civil Disputes, HUAK). The only exception is eviction through enforcement proceedings without a judgment under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law. Article 350 TBK, on the other hand, provides for ending the lease "by a claim"; for own use you need a court claim and therefore mediation first. A claim filed without mediation is dismissed on procedural grounds (Art. 18/A(2) HUAK).

How it works:

- You apply to the mediation office where the competent court sits (Art. 18/A(4)).
- The mediator concludes the matter within three weeks of appointment, extendable in compelling cases by one week at most (Art. 18/A(9)).
- From the application until the final report, limitation periods are suspended and preclusive time limits do not run (Art. 18/A(15)). In the author's view the one-month period in Article 350 TBK is such a time limit; if you applied within it, the days spent in mediation do not count against it.
- If there is no settlement, you attach the original final report, or a copy certified by the mediator, to your claim (Art. 18/A(2)).

The law does not say when to apply to mediation. In the author's assessment, applying after the right to sue has arisen, that is within the month after the term ends (for a new owner, after the six months), avoids arguments that the application was premature.

## Which court, and suing from abroad through a lawyer

All disputes arising from a tenancy go to the civil court of peace regardless of value (Art. 4(1)(a) HMK), under the simple procedure (Art. 316(1)(a) HMK). The general venue is the court where the defendant, the tenant, is domiciled (Art. 6 HMK); contractual claims may also be filed at the place of performance (Art. 10 HMK). As the tenant usually lives in the flat, this is normally where the flat is.

You do not have to travel to Turkey. Parties may attend mediation in person, through legal representatives or through lawyers (Art. 15(6) HUAK). The mediator will also inform you personally even if you have a lawyer (Art. 18/A(7)), so give your current phone number and email. The lawyer must file a power of attorney drawn up or certified by a notary (Art. 76(1) HMK). You can grant it at a Turkish consulate in the country where you live. Questions of German or Dutch law, for instance on ending your own lease there, belong with a lawyer qualified in that country.

You must also be able to show during the case that the need is yours and that your return is concrete. In the author's assessment, collecting these documents before filing saves the most time in a case run from abroad.

## After the tenant leaves: three-year letting ban

If the flat was vacated for own use, you may not let it to anyone other than the former tenant for three years without a justified reason (Art. 355(1) TBK). If you breach this, you owe the former tenant compensation of at least one year's rent, based on the rent paid in the last year of the lease (Art. 355(3)).

So if your return is postponed or your child decides to study in another city, you may not be able to let the flat again soon. The law does not list what counts as a "justified reason"; the court decides case by case.

Once the ten-year extension period has run, you can also end the lease without showing need: the landlord may then terminate without giving reasons by giving notice at least three months before the end of each extension year (Art. 347(1) TBK). The notice must be in writing (Art. 348).

## Frequently asked questions

### When must a landlord in Turkey file an own-use eviction claim?

For a fixed-term lease, within one month after the term ends (Art. 350(1) TBK). Written notice within that period extends it by one rental year (Art. 353). Mediation comes first.

### Do I need to send a notice before suing?

Not for Article 350 TBK. A new owner using Article 351(1) must give written notice within one month of acquisition. The written notice under Article 353 extends the filing period.

### When can a new owner evict a tenant in Turkey?

With written notice within one month of acquisition, by a claim six months later; otherwise by a claim within one month after the lease term ends (Art. 351 TBK).

### Can I sell the flat after an own-use eviction?

Article 355 TBK only bans letting it to others for three years; it says nothing about selling. In the author's assessment, putting the flat up for sale during the case may cast doubt on the need.

Article 57 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets out the right to form an association (dernek): anyone may form one without prior permission, but the founders must have capacity to act (fiil ehliyeti). It concerns everyone who wants to found an association in Türkiye, including Turkish citizens living in Germany or the Netherlands, foreigners and holders of the Blue Card (Mavi Kart).

## Article 57 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Right to form an association**
>
> Article 57 – Everyone has the right to form an association without prior permission.
>
> The founders of an association must have capacity to act.

Original Turkish text:

> **II. Dernek kurma hakkı**
>
> Madde 57- Herkes, önceden izin almaksızın dernek kurma hakkına sahiptir.
>
> Dernek kurucularının fiil ehliyetine sahip olması gerekir.

As of November 2026, Article 57 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, paragraph 1 was taken word for word from Article 33(1) of the Turkish Constitution, so the reasoning of that provision applies here too. “Everyone” refers to natural persons. A founder must be of age, capable of judgement and not under guardianship; being 18 is not required as such, so a person who came of age by marriage (Article 11) or was declared of age by a court (Article 12) can also be a founder (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised). That legal persons can also be founders follows only from the 2003 amendment to Article 56 TMK.

## Explanation

### Notification, not approval

“Without prior permission” means that formation is completed by a notification. The association acquires legal personality as soon as the founding notice (kuruluş bildirimi), the statute and the required documents are handed to the chief local administrator of the place where it has its seat (Article 59 TMK). Under Article 5 of the Associations Regulation (Dernekler Yönetmeliği), the founders sign the notice and every page of the statute. On filing, the applicant receives a receipt and the association is entered in the register.

Review comes afterwards. The authority examines the file within sixty days; if something is unlawful or missing it asks for it to be fixed, and if that does not happen within thirty days it informs the public prosecutor so that an action for dissolution can be brought (Article 60 TMK; Regulation, Article 6). If everything is in order, the association is told so in writing; within six months of that notice it must hold its first general meeting and set up its mandatory organs (Article 62 TMK).

Under the Associations Law No. 5253, all records and procedures concerning associations may also be handled electronically (Additional Article 2).

### Limits and exceptions in the Associations Law

Article 3(1) of Law No. 5253 restates the right for “natural or legal persons with capacity to act” and adds three points:

- Restrictions in the special laws on members of the armed forces and law enforcement, and on civil servants, remain in place (Article 3(2)).
- Persons convicted of terrorist financing offences under Law No. 6415, of manufacturing or trading narcotics or stimulants, or of laundering proceeds of crime cannot hold any office in an association other than in the general meeting, even if the periods in Article 53 of the Turkish Criminal Code have expired or an amnesty applies; this does not apply once a decision restoring the forfeited rights has been given (Article 3(3), added by Law No. 7262, in force since 31 December 2020).
- Children's associations are the exception to the capacity rule. Minors aged 15 and over who are capable of judgement may form a children's association with the written consent of their legal representatives. From 12 they may join with consent but cannot sit on the board or the audit committee, and people over 18 cannot be founders or members (Article 3(4)–(6)).

Membership is a separate right: every natural person with capacity to act, and legal persons, may become members, but no one can be forced to join and no association can be forced to admit anyone (Articles 63 and 64 TMK).

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

**You are a Turkish citizen living abroad.** Neither Article 57 TMK nor Article 3 of Law No. 5253 requires a founder to live in Türkiye, and Article 5 of the Associations Regulation asks for proof of residence status only from foreign founders. The association itself has its seat in Türkiye, and the notice goes to the authority of that place (Article 59 TMK). Your capacity is governed by Turkish law, because legal capacity and capacity to act follow a person's national law (Article 9(1) of the Private International Law Act No. 5718, MÖHUK; see the post on [Article 9 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-9-mohuk-legal-capacity)).

**You hold only German or Dutch citizenship.** Turkish law treats you as a foreigner. Foreign natural persons who have the right to settle in Türkiye (yerleşme hakkı) may form associations or join existing ones; this condition does not apply to honorary membership (Article 93 TMK). Copies of documents showing that right are attached to the founding notice (Regulation, Article 5(e)). The Regulation does not list which documents are sufficient.

**You hold the Blue Card (Mavi Kart).** People who were Turkish citizens by birth and gave up citizenship with permission, and their descendants up to the third degree, keep enjoying the rights granted to Turkish citizens, except for the exceptions listed in Article 28 of the Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901; provisions on national security and public order are reserved (Article 28(1)). Forming an association is not among those exceptions (Article 28(2)–(3)).

This point is disputed. What follows is an assessment based on the wording of the law; there is no express statutory provision on it and no binding court decision. It is not certain which view a court would take in a particular case. In the author's assessment, Article 28 of Law No. 5901 supports the view that Blue Card holders are not bound by the settlement condition in Article 93 TMK when founding an association; however, no express provision sets out which documents the authority will ask a Blue Card holder to attach to the founding notice. For general information on the Blue Card see [Blue Card in Türkiye](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights).

On the age of majority and court declarations of majority see the posts on [Article 11 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-11-turkish-civil-code-age-of-majority) and [Article 12 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-12-turkish-civil-code-emancipation).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need permission to form an association in Türkiye?

No. Everyone may form an association without prior permission (Article 57(1) TMK). The notice and documents are filed with the authority, the association has legal personality from that moment, and the review follows (Articles 59 and 60 TMK). Foreign associations, by contrast, need permission from the Ministry of the Interior to form an association in Türkiye (Article 5(2) of Law No. 5253).

### Can a Turkish citizen living in Germany be a founder of an association in Türkiye?

The law does not require residence in Türkiye; capacity to act is enough (Article 57(2) TMK; Article 3(1) of Law No. 5253). The association's seat is in Türkiye.

### Can foreigners set up an association in Türkiye?

Foreign natural persons with the right to settle in Türkiye may form associations and become members (Article 93 TMK). Proof of that right is attached to the founding notice (Associations Regulation, Article 5(e)).

Under Turkish law, a prenuptial or marital agreement is a matrimonial property contract (mal rejimi sözleşmesi). You can sign it before or after the wedding. It is made before a notary, either drawn up by the notary or with the signatures certified, and couples can also state their chosen regime in writing when they apply to marry (Art. 203 and 205 Turkish Civil Code, Türk Medeni Kanunu – TMK).

The content is limited. Spouses can only choose one of the regimes set out in the Code (Art. 202(2) and 203 TMK). Below I explain which regimes are available, the rules on form and capacity, which country's law applies to couples living in Germany or the Netherlands, and how an Ehevertrag signed before a German notary may be treated in Turkey. Agreements about maintenance or custody raise separate questions and are not covered here. The information is current as of November 2026; Art. 202–205 TMK have not been amended since 2002.

## Which regimes can a prenuptial agreement in Turkey provide for?

If there is no contract, the statutory regime of participation in acquired property (edinilmiş mallara katılma) applies (Art. 202(1) TMK). How it is wound up on divorce is explained in [division of property on divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/division-of-property-divorce-turkey). By contract you can choose instead:

- **Separation of property** (mal ayrılığı): each spouse manages, uses and disposes of their own assets (Art. 242 TMK). The Code gives no share in the other spouse's assets. For proof, liability for debts and allocation of co-owned property, the rules of separation of property with sharing apply (Art. 243 TMK).
- **Separation of property with sharing** (paylaşmalı mal ayrılığı): each spouse remains owner of their own assets (Art. 244 TMK). However, assets acquired after the regime begins and dedicated to the family's common use, and investments for the family's financial future, are split equally when the regime ends; inherited assets are excluded (Art. 250 TMK).
- **Community of property** (mal ortaklığı): it consists of common property and each spouse's personal property (Art. 256 TMK). Under general community, everything that is not personal property by law, including income, is common property (Art. 257 TMK). The spouses can also limit the community to acquired property (Art. 258 TMK).

You can also stay in the statutory regime and adjust it. Spouses may agree that assets used for a profession or business are personal property, or that income from personal property does not count as acquired property (Art. 221 TMK). They may also agree on a different basis for sharing the surplus (Art. 237 TMK). On divorce, annulment or a court-ordered switch to separation of property, such an agreement applies only if the contract expressly provides for these cases (Art. 238 TMK). It cannot infringe the reserved shares of children who are not children of both spouses (Art. 237(2) TMK).

Spouses cannot invent a regime of their own. Art. 203 TMK allows them to choose, end or change a regime only "within the limits set by law".

## Form: notary, or a statement when applying to marry

Under Art. 205 TMK the contract is made before a notary in one of two ways. According to the official reasoning, the notary can draw up the contract (düzenleme), or certify the signatures on a text the spouses have prepared themselves (onaylama).

A form required by law is, as a rule, a condition of validity, and a contract made without it has no effect (Art. 12(2) Turkish Code of Obligations, TBK; under Art. 5 TMK the general provisions of the TBK also apply to family law where appropriate). A document the spouses simply sign between themselves is therefore not a valid property contract, and the statutory regime continues.

There is a simpler route at the marriage application: the couple can state in writing which regime they choose (Art. 205(1) TMK). The wording only refers to choosing a regime. If you want additional terms under Art. 221 or 237 TMK, my view is that a notarial contract is the better route; this is my assessment, not a rule in the Code. Ask the Turkish marriage registrar (evlendirme memuru) which form is used in practice.

The spouses must sign the contract themselves (Art. 205(2) TMK). The official reasoning describes the property contract as a strictly personal right that the spouses must make in person.

## Who can sign a marital agreement?

Only people with the capacity to judge (ayırt etme gücü). Minors and adults under guardianship (kısıtlı) need the consent of their legal representative (Art. 204 TMK), who must then also sign (Art. 205(2) TMK). For a person under guardianship, the contract also requires the permission of the guardianship authority (Art. 462(9) TMK).

## Can you sign it after the wedding?

Yes. The contract can be made before the marriage or during it (Art. 203 TMK). Spouses who have lived under the statutory regime for years can switch to separation of property, or end a regime they chose earlier.

It does not shield assets from creditors. Setting up or changing a regime, or winding up the previous one, cannot remove from liability the assets from which creditors could have been paid (Art. 213 TMK).

## Turkish couples in Germany or the Netherlands: which law applies?

In a marriage with a foreign element, a Turkish court first decides which country's law governs the property regime. Under Art. 15(1) of the Turkish Act on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK), spouses can **expressly** choose the law of their habitual residence or of one of their nationalities at the time of the marriage. Without a choice, the law of their common nationality at the time of marriage applies; failing that, the law of their common habitual residence at that time; failing that, Turkish law. More detail: [Article 15 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-15-mohuk-matrimonial-property-turkey).

In practice:

- If both spouses were Turkish citizens when they married and made no choice of law, a Turkish court applies Turkish law. For a spouse who also holds German or Dutch citizenship, the Turkish citizenship counts (Art. 4(1)(b) MÖHUK). Any agreement these couples make has to stay within the limits of the TMK.
- A Turkish-German couple, for example a Turkish citizen living in Germany who marries a German citizen, can choose Turkish or German law.

Agreeing on a particular regime (such as separation of property) is not the same as choosing a country's law. Because the provision requires an "express" choice, I think it is sensible to state separately and clearly in the contract which law is chosen. Whether the choice can also be made after the wedding is not expressly addressed in Art. 15 MÖHUK.

For real estate in Turkey, Turkish law always applies when the property is divided (Art. 15(2) MÖHUK).

## Is a German Ehevertrag valid in Turkey?

Form is governed by Art. 7 MÖHUK: a legal act may be made in the form required by the substantive law of the country where it is made, or by the substantive law that governs the act itself. MÖHUK contains no special form rule for property contracts. In my assessment, a contract made before a German or Dutch notary in the form required there (Ehevertrag, huwelijkse voorwaarden) is therefore formally valid, even if Turkish law governs the property regime.

The content is a separate matter, and it is disputed. What follows is my own assessment; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on the point. It is not certain which view a court would take in a given case.

- If Turkish law governs the regime, the wording of the contract decides which TMK regime a German or Dutch arrangement (such as Gütertrennung) corresponds to. An arrangement with no equivalent in the TMK may fail under the limit in Art. 203 TMK.
- If the contract contains no express choice of law, it is unclear whether a Turkish court would treat it as a choice of law under Art. 15 MÖHUK.

To submit a foreign notarial deed to a Turkish court or land registry, you need an apostille and a certified Turkish translation. For the form, content and effects of the contract under German or Dutch law, ask a notary or lawyer in that country. German and Dutch courts apply their own conflict-of-laws rules and may reach a different result for the same marriage.

## Can you sign it at a Turkish consulate?

Under Art. 191 of the Turkish Notaries Act (Noterlik Kanunu No. 1512), notarial acts abroad are carried out by consuls, on the consulate premises and in line with that Act's rules on notarial acts (Art. 192). In my view this means that a property contract governed by Turkish law can be drawn up or certified at a Turkish consulate general in Germany or the Netherlands. Check with the consulate in advance about appointments and the documents required. For related topics see [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you sign a prenup after marriage in Turkey?

Yes. Turkish law allows the contract before or after the wedding (Art. 203 TMK). Creditors' rights are not affected (Art. 213 TMK).

### Does a prenuptial agreement in Turkey have to be notarised?

Yes, it is drawn up or certified by a notary. A couple can also state the chosen regime in writing when applying to marry (Art. 205 TMK).

### Is a German or Dutch marriage contract valid in Turkey?

As to form, yes, if it meets the form required where it was made (Art. 7 MÖHUK). How its content is treated in Turkey depends on the law governing the property regime and on the wording; this point is disputed.

### Can a prenup in Turkey cover anything the couple wants?

No. Spouses can only choose a regime provided by law and adjust it as far as the Code allows (Art. 202(2) and 203 TMK).

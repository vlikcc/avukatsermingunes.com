Under Turkish divorce law, a spouse who is not at fault, or less at fault, can claim pecuniary damages (maddi tazminat) from the spouse at fault if the divorce harms their existing or expected interests. A spouse whose personality rights were violated by the events that led to the divorce can also claim non-pecuniary damages (manevi tazminat), a sum of money as moral compensation, from the other spouse if that spouse is at fault (Article 174 of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK). Both claims are usually raised in the divorce case itself; a separate claim must be filed within one year after the divorce judgment becomes final (Art. 178 TMK).

I checked the provisions cited here against the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr in November 2026. The grounds for divorce are covered in the article on [contested divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/contested-divorce-turkey-grounds); this one deals only with compensation.

## Pecuniary damages on divorce (Art. 174(1) TMK)

There are four conditions:

1. The spouse from whom damages are claimed is **at fault**.
2. The claimant is **not at fault or less at fault**. Where both are equally at fault, no pecuniary damages are owed.
3. An **existing or expected interest** of the claimant has been harmed.
4. The harm results **from the divorce**.

An existing interest is a benefit the spouse enjoyed during the marriage, such as the support provided by the other spouse. An expected interest is something the spouse would have gained later had the marriage continued. For example, divorced spouses are no longer each other's statutory heirs (Art. 181 TMK), so the prospect of inheriting ends with the divorce.

The Code sets no amount and no formula; it speaks of "appropriate" damages. Where the law leaves a matter to the court's discretion, the judge decides according to law and equity (Art. 4 TMK). For this reason no general figure can be given for how much compensation a court will award.

## Non-pecuniary damages for violation of personality rights (Art. 174(2) TMK)

The conditions are:

- there were events that led to the divorce,
- those events violated the claimant's personality rights,
- the other spouse is at fault.

Unlike the first paragraph, the wording does not require the claimant to be free of fault or less at fault. According to the official legislative reasoning, that requirement was dropped on purpose; if the claimant is also at fault, the general rules of the law of obligations allow the award to be reduced or refused. The reasoning refers to the former Code of Obligations; today the rule allowing the judge to reduce or deny compensation where the injured party contributed to the damage is Article 52 of the Turkish Code of Obligations (TBK).

What Turkish law means by a violation of personality rights is explained in the article on [Article 24 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-24-turkish-civil-code-personality-rights-infringement). Non-pecuniary damages are paid as a lump sum; the court cannot order them to be paid as periodic payments (Art. 176(2) TMK).

## Adultery and domestic violence

**Adultery.** Adultery (zina) is a specific ground for divorce under Art. 161 TMK. It is relied on both when the court weighs fault and in a claim for non-pecuniary damages. There is no separate "adultery compensation" in the Code; the claim still rests on Art. 174 TMK. Whether the conduct violated the claimant's personality rights, and how much is awarded, is for the court to decide in the individual case.

Whether the **third party** with whom the spouse committed adultery can be sued is a different question. Art. 174 TMK only governs claims between the divorcing spouses. A claim against a third party could only rest on the general provision on violation of personality rights (Art. 58 TBK).

> This question is disputed. The law contains no express rule on the liability of the third party, and no verified, binding court decision on it is cited in this article. It is uncertain which view a court would adopt in a particular case.

**Violence.** An attempt on the spouse's life, serious ill-treatment or severely degrading conduct is a separate ground for divorce (Art. 162 TMK) and can be relied on for fault and non-pecuniary damages. Separately from any compensation, Law No. 6284 on the Protection of the Family and Prevention of Violence against Women provides protective and preventive measures (Art. 4, 5).

Wedding gold is not compensation but a question of ownership; see [Turkish wedding gold after divorce](/en/publications/turkish-wedding-gold-divorce-who-owns-it).

## How compensation differs from alimony

Both can be claimed in the same case, but they rest on different grounds:

- **Pecuniary damages** are claimed from the spouse at fault, and the claimant must be free of fault or less at fault (Art. 174(1) TMK).
- **Alimony for need** (yoksulluk nafakası) is paid to the spouse who would fall into need because of the divorce; that spouse's fault must not be heavier, while the paying spouse need not be at fault at all (Art. 175 TMK).

The Constitutional Court's decision annulling the words "indefinitely" in Art. 175(1) TMK takes effect on 30 June 2027. It concerns alimony only, not damages under Art. 174 TMK (see [alimony in Turkey](/en/publications/alimony-turkey-indefinite-maintenance-ruling)). The Turkish press reports on a new judicial package that would change alimony and divorce procedure. As of 19 November 2026, no law amending Articles 174 to 178 TMK has been published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete); drafts are not binding.

## Lump sum or periodic payments (Art. 176 TMK)

Pecuniary damages and alimony may be ordered as a lump sum or, depending on the circumstances, as periodic payments (irat). Non-pecuniary damages can only be a lump sum (Art. 176(1)–(2) TMK).

Pecuniary damages ordered as periodic payments follow the same rules as alimony. They end **automatically** if the recipient remarries or either party dies. They are cancelled **by court decision** if the recipient lives with someone as if married without marrying, is no longer in need, or leads a dishonourable life. If the parties' finances change, or fairness requires it, the payments can be increased or reduced; on request, the judge can fix in the judgment how much is to be paid in future years (Art. 176(3)–(5) TMK). The Code applies these rules only to periodic payments.

## Court and one-year limitation period (Art. 178 TMK)

Claims are heard by the family court (aile mahkemesi), which has jurisdiction over disputes arising from family law (Law No. 4787, Art. 4(1)).

If no compensation was claimed in the divorce case, a separate action is possible. Article 178 TMK, in the version in force in November 2026, reads (unofficial translation): "Rights of action arising from the termination of marriage by divorce become time-barred one year after the divorce judgment becomes final." The period therefore runs from the date the judgment becomes **final**, not from the date it was delivered.

> Evliliğin boşanma sebebiyle sona ermesinden doğan dava hakları, boşanma hükmünün kesinleşmesinin üzerinden bir yıl geçmekle zamanaşımına uğrar.

The TMK contains no special venue rule for such a separate action; the general venue is the defendant's domicile (Art. 6 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK). For alimony claims after divorce, by contrast, the court at the domicile of the person entitled to alimony has jurisdiction (Art. 177 TMK).

## Divorced abroad: can you still claim compensation in Turkey?

**Divorce before a Turkish court.** If both spouses are Turkish citizens, a Turkish court applies Turkish law to the grounds and consequences of divorce (Art. 14(1) MÖHUK, the Turkish private international law act). That applies even if you live in Germany or the Netherlands; compensation claims are then decided under Art. 174 TMK. See [Article 14 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-14-mohuk-divorce-separation).

**Divorce granted by a foreign court.** A foreign divorce judgment has no automatic effect in Turkey. It must be recognised by a court (tanıma, Art. 58 MÖHUK) or, where the conditions are met, registered in the civil registry under Art. 27/A of Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490. If the foreign court also ruled on compensation, the recognised judgment has res judicata effect from the date it became final abroad (Art. 58, 59 MÖHUK). Enforcing a foreign compensation award in Turkey requires an enforcement order (tenfiz, Art. 50, 54 MÖHUK); see [Article 58 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-mohuk-recognition-foreign-judgments-turkey).

If the foreign court made no ruling on compensation at all, the law does not say expressly whether a claim under Art. 174 TMK can still be brought in Turkey.

> This question is disputed. What follows are views put forward in legal writing and my own assessment; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on the point. It is uncertain which view a court would adopt in a particular case.

1. **Recognition first?** Since the divorce only takes effect in Turkey through recognition, in my assessment recognition should be obtained before, or together with, the compensation claim.
2. **Does a Turkish court have jurisdiction?** International jurisdiction follows the domestic rules on venue (Art. 40 MÖHUK). If the defendant spouse is not domiciled in Turkey, there may be no competent Turkish court under Art. 6 HMK. Whether the special rule in Art. 41 MÖHUK for "personal status" cases of Turkish citizens covers such a claim is disputed.
3. **When does the year start?** Art. 178 TMK runs from the date the divorce judgment becomes final. Because a recognised judgment takes effect from the date it became final abroad (Art. 59 MÖHUK), it can be argued that the period runs from that date. In my assessment, the safe course is to count the year from the date the foreign judgment became final; waiting for the recognition decision may mean missing the deadline.

German, Dutch and other foreign courts apply their own conflict-of-laws rules. Whether compensation for divorce can be claimed there depends on the law of that country; please consult a lawyer admitted there. General information is on the [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a spouse claim compensation for adultery in a Turkish divorce?

Adultery can be relied on for fault and for non-pecuniary damages under Art. 174(2) TMK. The Code sets no amount; the court decides case by case.

### Can I claim compensation after the divorce?

Yes, within one year after the divorce judgment becomes final (Art. 178 TMK). After that the claim is time-barred.

### Can divorce compensation be paid in instalments?

Pecuniary damages can be ordered as periodic payments; non-pecuniary damages cannot (Art. 176 TMK).

### I divorced abroad. Can I still claim compensation in Turkey?

The foreign judgment first has to be recognised or registered in Turkey. Whether a separate claim is possible and when the year starts are disputed; the safe course is to count the period from the date the foreign judgment became final.

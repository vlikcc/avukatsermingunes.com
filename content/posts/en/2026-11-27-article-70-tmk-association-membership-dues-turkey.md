Article 70 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) deals with members' duty to pay dues (ödenti, usually called aidat) to an association. The statute decides the amount and how it is paid; if it says nothing, members contribute equally to the dues that are necessary. A member who leaves still owes dues for the time they were a member, and honorary members pay nothing.

## Article 70 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Obligations of members**
>
> **a. Duty to pay dues**
>
> Article 70 – Members' duty to pay dues is governed by the statute. If the statute contains no provision, members contribute in equal shares to the dues necessary to achieve the association's purpose and to meet its debts. A member who leaves or is expelled must pay the dues for the period of membership.
>
> Honorary members are not obliged to pay dues.

Turkish original:

> **2. Üyelerin yükümlülükleri**
>
> **a. Ödenti verme borcu**
>
> Madde 70- Üyelerin ödenti verme borcu tüzükle düzenlenir. Tüzükte düzenleme yoksa üyeler, dernek amacının gerçekleşmesi ve borçlarının karşılanması için zorunlu ödentilere eşit olarak katılırlar. Dernekten çıkan veya çıkarılan üye, üyelikte bulunduğu sürenin ödentisini vermek zorundadır.
>
> Onursal üyeler ödenti vermek zorunda değildir.

As of November 2026 the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr shows Article 70 in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (the latest law in the table is No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the provision corresponds to Article 64 of the former Civil Code No. 743 and was redrafted to follow the French and German texts of Article 71 of the Swiss Civil Code. The reasoning makes one point explicit: where the statute does not set the dues, a member does not have to pay all the association's debts, only a certain amount towards its purpose and debts (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### The statute comes first

The Code leaves dues to the statute. The Associations Law No. 5253 goes further: the way the joining fee and annual dues are set is one of the matters every statute must contain (Article 4(1)(h)). The statute can name the amount or give the power to set it to a body such as the general assembly. So the answer to "how much are the dues?" lies in the association's statute and decisions, not in the Code.

### When the statute is silent

If the statute says nothing, the second sentence applies. Members contribute, in equal shares, only to dues needed for two things: achieving the association's purpose and meeting its debts. Equal sharing mirrors the equality principle in Article 68.

The reasoning draws the limit: a member's obligation stops at the dues, and the member is not personally liable for the association's debts. The association is a legal person; its income consists of members' dues, income from its activities or assets, and donations and grants (Article 99 TMK).

### Leaving and unpaid dues

A member who leaves (Article 66) or is expelled (Article 67) owes the dues for the period of membership. Leaving does not wipe out past debts. On the other hand, a former member has no claim to the association's assets (Article 68(3)) and cannot ask for dues to be refunded.

### Dues that are not paid

The Code does not say separately what happens when dues go unpaid. Dues are a money debt, and the association can claim them like any other debt, including through enforcement proceedings (icra takibi). Whether arrears are a ground for expulsion depends on the statute: if the statute lists grounds for expulsion, an expulsion on those grounds cannot be challenged by arguing that they are not justified; if it lists none, a member can only be expelled for just cause (Article 67).

The Associations Regulation also says who may collect dues. Apart from full board members, the board appoints collectors by decision and gives them an authorisation certificate valid for at most one year (Article 46); income is collected against a receipt (alındı belgesi, Article 42). If you pay in cash, ask for a receipt.

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you have lost touch with an association in Türkiye but never left it, your duty to pay dues continues, and dues accrued until you leave remain owed. A written notice to the association is enough to leave; see the post on [Article 66 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-66-tmk-leaving-association-turkey). Keep proof that your notice reached the association. If a claim for dues comes later, it shows which period you still owe for.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much are association dues in Türkiye, and who sets them?

The Code sets no amount. The statute must state how the joining fee and annual dues are determined (Article 4(1)(h) Associations Law; Article 70(1) TMK).

### Do I still owe dues after leaving an association?

Yes, for the period you were a member (Article 70(1) TMK).

### Do honorary members pay dues?

No, they are not obliged to (Article 70(2) TMK). They have no vote either (Article 69(2)).

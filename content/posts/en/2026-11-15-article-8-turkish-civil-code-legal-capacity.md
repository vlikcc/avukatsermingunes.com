Article 8 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) opens the law of persons and states that every human being has legal capacity (hak ehliyeti). Legal capacity means being able to hold rights and owe obligations; it does not depend on age, mental health or nationality. A newborn baby can inherit or be recorded as an owner in the land registry because of this article. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 8 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **A. In general**
>
> **I. Legal capacity**
>
> Article 8 – Every human being has legal capacity.
>
> Accordingly, all human beings are equal, within the limits of the legal order, in their capacity to have rights and obligations.

Original Turkish text:

> **A. Genel olarak**
>
> **I. Hak ehliyeti**
>
> Madde 8 - Her insanın hak ehliyeti vardır.
>
> Buna göre bütün insanlar, hukuk düzeninin sınırları içinde, haklara ve borçlara ehil olmada eşittirler.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 8; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 8 of the former Civil Code No. 743. Instead of "enjoyment of civil rights" it uses "legal capacity", the term settled in legal writing and case law, and "every human being" instead of "every person". The reasoning says this stresses that legal capacity concerns human beings and is a fundamental human right (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### When it begins and ends

Personality begins at the moment the child is born fully and alive, and ends with death. A child has legal capacity from conception, on condition that it is born alive (Article 28 TMK). In inheritance law this means that an unborn child (cenin) inherits if born alive, and a stillborn child does not (Article 582 TMK). If the father dies before the birth, the child born alive is his heir.

### Legal capacity and capacity to act

Legal capacity is about being able to hold a right; capacity to act (fiil ehliyeti) is about exercising it through your own acts. A baby becomes an heir but cannot accept or renounce the inheritance itself; the legal representative does that. The conditions for capacity to act are in Articles 9 and 10 TMK.

### "Within the limits of the legal order"

Equality under paragraph 2 does not stop the law from attaching conditions to particular rights. Marriage, for example, generally requires being at least 17 (Article 124 TMK). Conditions like this do not take away anyone's legal capacity. Nobody can waive legal capacity or capacity to act, not even in part (Article 23(1) TMK).

Legal entities also have legal capacity, except for rights tied to natural human characteristics such as sex, age or kinship (Article 48 TMK).

## Practical points if you live abroad

A child born in Germany or the Netherlands who holds Turkish citizenship can inherit an estate in Turkey; the child does not need to have been born or registered in Turkey for that. But to obtain a certificate of inheritance, transfer a title deed or renounce the inheritance, you will have to show Turkish authorities the child's status as heir and identity. That is why registering a child born abroad in the Turkish civil registry matters in practice: [Birth registration for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad). Transactions for a minor are carried out by the parent holding custody.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does legal capacity begin under Turkish law?

At the full and live birth of the child; an unborn child has legal capacity from conception, on condition of being born alive (Article 28 TMK).

### Can an unborn child inherit in Turkey?

Yes, if born alive (Article 582 TMK).

### Can legal capacity be waived?

No, not even in part (Article 23(1) TMK).

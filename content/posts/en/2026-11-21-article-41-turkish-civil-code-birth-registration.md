Article 41 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says that birth notifications and the procedure for found children of unknown identity (kimliği bilinmeyen bulunmuş çocuklar) follow "the relevant laws". The Civil Code itself gives no details; who reports a birth, when and where is answered by the Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). As of November 2026, a birth must be reported within thirty days to the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) in Türkiye, or within sixty days to the Turkish mission abroad (Law No. 5490, Article 15(1)).

## Article 41 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **B. Birth register**
>
> **I. Notification**
>
> Article 41 – Notifications of births and procedures concerning found children of unknown identity are carried out in accordance with the relevant laws.

Original Turkish text:

> **B. Doğum kütüğü**
>
> **I. Bildirme**
>
> Madde 41- Doğumlara ilişkin bildirimler ve kimliği bilinmeyen bulunmuş çocuklar hakkındaki işlemler ilgili kanun hükümlerine göre yapılır.

As of November 2026, Article 41 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 39 of the former Civil Code No. 743. Because the Population Law No. 1587 was to govern the birth register and birth notification, it was worded differently from the old article (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly). Law No. 1587 is no longer in force; Law No. 5490 has replaced it.

## Explanation

Article 41 is a referral provision. The rules that apply are in Articles 15–19 of Law No. 5490:

- **Time limit and place:** Every child born alive must be reported within thirty days to the registry office in Türkiye, or within sixty days to the mission abroad. The report may also be made to the health facility where the birth took place (Article 15(1)). Health facilities report births they handled to the registry office within five working days (Article 15(2)).
- **Who reports:** the parent with parental authority (veli), guardian or curator; failing these, the grandparents, adult siblings or whoever is looking after the child (Article 15(5)). The child's given name is recorded with no more than three names and without abbreviation.
- **Stillbirth:** Stillborn children are not entered in the family register (Article 15(7)).
- **Late reporting:** For a child under six, the date of birth is taken from the declaration; for an older child the age is determined by a state health facility, unless an official birth document is produced (Article 16(1)).
- **Foundlings:** A found child too young to speak for itself is registered by the registry office of the place where it was found, on the basis of a police or agency report or the statements of those concerned (Article 19(1)). If name, surname and parents' names are missing, the registry office assigns them; an unknown date of birth is determined by a state health facility (Article 19(3)).

Later changes to the birth entry (recognition, a paternity judgment, adoption, establishing the parentage of a foundling) are covered by TMK Article 42.

## Practical points for Turkish citizens living abroad

Abroad, a birth is reported by handing the official certificate or report issued by the foreign authority to the Turkish mission. If you cannot go in person, you may send that document to the mission with a signed request stating the child's given name and documents showing the parents' identity (Law No. 5490, Article 15(6)). If the sixty days have passed, the child is still registered; the date of birth is then set under Article 16. In the author's assessment, the foreign birth certificate counts as an "official birth document" under Article 16, so no age determination is needed. More detail, including nationality: [birth registration for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I have to register a birth?

Thirty days at the registry office in Türkiye, sixty days at the Turkish mission abroad (Law No. 5490, Article 15(1)).

### Who has to report the birth?

The parent with parental authority, guardian or curator; failing these, grandparents, adult siblings or whoever has the child (Article 15(5)).

### Can a birth still be registered late?

Yes. For a child under six the declaration is used; for older children, the age is determined unless there is an official birth document (Article 16(1)).

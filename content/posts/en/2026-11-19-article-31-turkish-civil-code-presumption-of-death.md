Article 31 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets out the presumption of death (ölüm karinesi): a person who disappears in circumstances that make their death certain is deemed to have actually died, even if the body is never found. The death is registered on the order of the local administrative chief (mülki amir), not by a court; the procedure is in Article 32 of the Civil Registry Services Act No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). It matters to families, heirs and surviving spouses after earthquakes, floods, shipwrecks and similar events.

## Article 31 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **b. Presumption of death**
>
> Article 31 – If a person disappears in circumstances that require their death to be regarded as certain, they are deemed to have actually died even if their body has not been found.

Turkish original:

> **b. Ölüm karinesi**
>
> Madde 31- Bir kimse, ölümüne kesin gözle bakılmayı gerektiren durumlar içinde kaybolursa, cesedi bulunamamış olsa bile gerçekten ölmüş sayılır.

As of November 2026, the current TMK text on mevzuat.gov.tr has no amendment note for Article 31 or Article 44. The latest law in the amendment table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 30 of the former Civil Code and was rewritten in simpler language together with its side heading (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Who registers the death, and how

Article 31 states only the result. How the death is registered is set out in two other provisions. Article 44 TMK reads (unofficial translation):

> Article 44 – If a person disappears in circumstances that require their death to be regarded as certain, a death entry is made in the register on the order of the highest administrative chief of that place, even if the body has not been found.
>
> Nevertheless, any interested person may bring an action for a court to establish whether that person is dead or alive.

The procedure is in Article 32 of Law No. 5490 (unofficial translation):

> Presumption of death
>
> Article 32 – (1) If a person disappears in circumstances that require their death to be regarded as certain, a death report is drawn up and the death is registered on the order of the administrative chief of the place where the application is made, even if the body has not been found.
>
> (2) For action under this article, a descendant or ascendant of the person presumed dead, or a sibling, or, if there are none, their heirs must apply in writing and document the event, or the competent authorities must notify the registry office of the situation by official letter.
>
> (3) If the documents attached to the application and, where necessary, an inquiry carried out by the registry office are found sufficient to prove that the event took place and that the person said to have died was there when it happened, the death is registered on the order of the administrative chief.

Articles 31 to 34 of Law No. 5490 do not appear in its amendment table; the latest law in that table is Law No. 7394 (in force since 15 April 2022).

In practice this means:

- The application is made by descendants (children, grandchildren), ascendants (parents) or siblings; if there are none, by the heirs. The spouse is not named separately, but the court action under Article 44(2) TMK is open to "any interested person".
- Instead of an application, a competent authority that knows of the event may notify the registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) by official letter (Article 32(2)).
- Two things must be proven: that the event happened and that the person was there at the time (Article 32(3)).
- The law sets no waiting period. If the documents are sufficient, the death is registered on the administrative chief's order without going to court.

The two texts describe the competent official in different words: Article 44 TMK refers to "the highest administrative chief of that place", Article 32 of Law No. 5490 to "the administrative chief of the place where the application is made". No provision resolves this difference.

### Presumption of death and declaration of absence

If the documents cannot show that the person was at the scene, the presumption of death does not apply and the remaining route is a declaration of absence (gaiplik). That is a court decision: at least one year must have passed since the danger of death, and the court also publishes a notice with a period of at least six months (Articles 32–33 TMK). The presumption of death involves no court, no waiting period and no notice.

The difference also shows in the effects. The spouse of a person declared absent cannot remarry until the court has dissolved the marriage (Article 131(1) TMK). The heirs of an absent person must provide security before the estate is handed over to them (Article 584 TMK). Both rules are written only for absence; the Code sets no comparable condition for the presumption of death.

### Effects on inheritance and marriage

Because the person is deemed to have "actually died", the legal effects of death follow:

- Personality ends with death (Article 28(1) TMK).
- Succession opens on death, and the heirs acquire the estate as a whole by operation of law (Article 575, Article 599(1) TMK). Only a person alive at the time of death can inherit (Article 580(1) TMK).
- The matrimonial property regime ends on the death of a spouse (Article 225(1) TMK).
- Since the marriage has ended, the surviving wife may not remarry until 300 days have passed from its end; the period ends on giving birth, and the court may lift it in certain cases (Article 132 TMK).

If several family members disappeared in the same event, Article 29(2) TMK applies: where it cannot be proven who died first, all are deemed to have died at the same moment. Read together with the requirement in Article 580 that an heir be alive at the time of death, people deemed to have died at the same moment do not inherit from each other; each estate passes to that person's own surviving heirs.

If the person presumed dead later turns up, any interested person may sue to establish that they are alive (Article 44(2) TMK). What happens next is disputed. The Code has no specific rule for the presumption of death on a new marriage concluded in the meantime or an estate already divided; the return rule in Article 585 TMK is written for absent persons, and the Code does not say expressly whether it also applies to the presumption of death. I cannot cite any binding court decision on this that I have been able to verify. How a court would decide in a given case is uncertain.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

### A Turkish citizen goes missing abroad

Articles 31 and 44 TMK and Article 32 of Law No. 5490 do not distinguish between events in Türkiye and events abroad. Relatives apply in writing under Article 32(2). Which administrative chief gives the order for an event abroad, and whether documents from foreign authorities (for example an official letter listing missing persons after a disaster or accident) are sufficient, is not stated in the text of the law; I have not been able to verify the administrative practice from an official source. I recommend asking the Turkish registry office or the consulate before applying.

If the documents are not sufficient, a declaration of absence remains possible. For a Turkish citizen it is decided under Turkish law (Article 11 MÖHUK); the competent court is that of the last domicile in Türkiye or, if the person never lived there, that of the place of civil registration (Article 32(2) TMK). More on this: [Article 11 MÖHUK – Declaration of Absence or Death](/en/publications/legislation/article-11-mohuk-declaration-of-absence).

### Foreign death certificates and foreign declarations of death

If the body was found and the death was registered abroad, the presumption of death is not needed. Turkish missions abroad report a death within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1) of Law No. 5490); for a person still shown as alive in the register, the registry office draws up a death report when an application is made with a document showing the death (Article 33(1)). What this means for inheritance is covered in [Certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey).

A declaration of death or absence made by a German or Dutch court, on the other hand, is a foreign court decision. Entering it in the Turkish register is an administrative act and therefore requires recognition (tanıma) (Article 58(2) and (3) MÖHUK). For the conditions and effects under German or Dutch law, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country.

If the missing person gave up Turkish citizenship and has no domicile in Türkiye, the jurisdiction of a Turkish court for a declaration of absence or death is governed by Article 42 MÖHUK: [Article 42 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-42-mohuk-absence-curatorship-foreigners).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who decides on a presumption of death in Türkiye?

Not a court but the administrative authority. The death is registered on the order of the administrative chief (Article 44(1) TMK, Article 32(1) and (3) of Law No. 5490).

### Is there a waiting period for a presumption of death?

No, the law sets none. For a declaration of absence, at least one year must have passed since the danger of death, followed by a notice period of at least six months (Article 33 TMK).

### What is the difference between presumption of death and absence?

For a presumption of death, it is documented that the person was at the scene and that their death is certain; the death is registered by administrative order. For absence, death is only highly probable and a court decision is required (Articles 32–35 TMK).

### What if several relatives die at the same time?

If it cannot be proven who died first, all are deemed to have died at the same moment (Article 29(2) TMK).

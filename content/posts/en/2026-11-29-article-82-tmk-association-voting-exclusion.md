Article 82 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets out when a member of an association may not vote (oy hakkından yoksunluk). Where a decision concerns a legal transaction or a dispute between the association (dernek) and the member, their spouse, their ascendants (üstsoy) or their descendants (altsoy), the member has no vote on it. The same applies to the person voting for a member that is a legal entity. The article matters to members who rent property to the association, sell it something, provide services to it or are in a dispute with it.

## Article 82 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **b. Exclusion from voting**
>
> Article 82 – No member of an association may vote on decisions to be taken on a legal transaction or dispute between the association and the member, their spouse, ascendants or descendants.
>
> (Paragraph added: 30.7.2003 – Law No. 4963, Art. 34) The above paragraph also applies to the person who is to vote on behalf of a legal entity.

Original Turkish text:

> **b. Oy hakkından yoksunluk**
>
> Madde 82- Hiçbir dernek üyesi, dernek ile kendisi, eşi, üstsoyu ve altsoyu arasındaki bir hukukî işlem veya uyuşmazlık konusunda alınması gereken kararlarda oy kullanamaz.
>
> (Ek fıkra: 30/7/2003-4963/34 md.) Tüzel kişi adına oy kullanacak kişi hakkında da yukarıdaki fıkra hükmü uygulanır.

As of November 2026, the amendment table on mevzuat.gov.tr lists only Law No. 4963 (in force since 30 July 2003) for Article 82; there have been no later changes (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the rule is not limited to general assembly deliberations: a member taking part in meetings of the association's other bodies may not vote either on decisions about a legal transaction or dispute between the association and the member, their spouse, ascendants or descendants. The reasoning belongs to the original text and does not cover the second paragraph added by Law No. 4963 (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Commentary

### Who, and on which decisions

The bar is tied to two kinds of decision: a legal transaction with the association (such as a lease, a sale or a service contract) and a dispute with it (such as bringing a lawsuit or a settlement). It covers the member, their spouse, their ascendants (parents, grandparents) and their descendants (children, grandchildren). Siblings and in-laws are not listed.

The wording only bars voting; it does not bar the member from attending or speaking. The article sits under the heading on general assembly decisions, but the reasoning says it also applies in other bodies such as the board of directors (yönetim kurulu). For buying or selling real estate the bar therefore reaches the board as well: the general assembly gives the authorisation and the board decides on the transaction (Associations Act No. 5253, Art. 22(1)).

### Legal entities as members

Legal entities have been able to join associations since Law No. 4963 (Art. 64(1) TMK). The chair of their board, or the person they appoint as representative, votes for them (Associations Act Art. 6). Under paragraph 2, that person may not vote either where the decision concerns a transaction or dispute between the association and that person, their spouse, ascendants or descendants.

### A vote cast despite the bar

A general assembly decision passed with a barred vote breaches the law and can be challenged under Article 83. A member who was present and did not agree with the decision has one month from the date of the decision; for absent members the month runs from when they learned of it, and in any case no later than three months from the decision (Art. 83(1) TMK). Because decisions are recorded in the minutes (Associations Regulation, Dernekler Yönetmeliği, Art. 15), a practical suggestion is to note in the minutes which member did not vote because of Article 82; that helps avoid later arguments.

### Disputed points

On the following questions the law has no express rule, and no binding court decision was found for this article. What follows are opinions; which one a court would adopt in a particular dispute is uncertain.

- Whether a member barred from voting counts among the “members attending” under [Article 81 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-81-tmk-association-general-assembly-majority).
- Whether a decision can be annulled even if the barred vote did not change the result.
- Whether the bar applies by analogy to siblings or in-laws.
- Transactions between the association and a member that is itself a legal entity. In the author's view, the legal entity is the “member of the association”, so paragraph 1 applies directly; paragraph 2 extends the bar to decisions concerning the representative personally and their relatives.

## Points for members living abroad

If a member living abroad rents or sells their property in Türkiye to the association, they may not vote on that decision. The same applies if the transaction is with their parents or children living in Türkiye. On voting in person, see the article on [Article 69 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-69-tmk-voting-rights-association-general-assembly).

## Frequently asked questions

### When may a member of a Turkish association not vote?

On decisions about a legal transaction or dispute between the association and the member, their spouse, ascendants or descendants (Art. 82(1) TMK).

### Does the voting bar also apply on the board?

The article sits under general assembly decisions; according to the reasoning, it also applies in the association's other bodies.

### Does the bar apply to the representative of a legal entity?

Yes. Paragraph 2, added by Law No. 4963, applies paragraph 1 to the person voting on behalf of a legal entity.

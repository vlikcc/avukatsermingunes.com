Article 57 of Turkish Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) governs what happens after a Turkish court has declared a foreign judgment enforceable (tenfiz). The foreign judgment is then enforced like a judgment of a Turkish court, but if an appeal is lodged against the enforcement decision, it cannot be enforced at that stage. The provision matters both to creditors who want to collect a German or Dutch maintenance or payment order in Turkey and to debtors facing one.

## Article 57 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Enforcement and appeal to the Court of Cassation**
>
> Article 57 – (1) Foreign judgments declared enforceable shall be enforced like judgments given by Turkish courts.
>
> (2) An appeal to the Court of Cassation against decisions granting or rejecting a request for enforcement is subject to the general provisions. An appeal to the Court of Cassation suspends enforcement.

Turkish original:

> **Yerine getirme ve temyiz yolu**
>
> MADDE 57 – (1) Tenfizine karar verilen yabancı ilâmlar Türk mahkemelerinden verilmiş ilâmlar gibi icra olunur.
>
> (2) Tenfiz isteminin kabul veya reddi hususunda verilen kararların temyizi genel hükümlere tâbidir. Temyiz, yerine getirmeyi durdurur.

The article has not been amended since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the Ministry of Justice’s UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds word for word to Article 41 of the former Law No. 2675 (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 56 of the bill).

## Explanation

### Enforcement on the basis of the enforcement decision

A foreign judgment can be enforced in Turkey only after an enforcement decision (Article 50(1) MÖHUK). The creditor then files the foreign judgment and the enforcement decision with an enforcement office (icra dairesi) and starts enforcement based on a judgment (ilamlı takip). Any enforcement office in Turkey may be used (Article 34 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, İİK). For a money judgment, the office serves the debtor with an enforcement order (icra emri) requiring payment within seven days (Article 32 İİK). Within the same period the debtor may apply to the enforcement court and claim that the debt has been paid, deferred or is time-barred (Article 33 İİK). The İİK provisions on enforcement of judgments (Articles 24–40) have not been amended in the last two years; the most recent change concerned Articles 25 and 28 (Law No. 7418, in force since 1 April 2023).

If the foreign judgment contains no award of interest, the 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has held that interest on the foreign-currency claim can be claimed under Article 4/a of Law No. 3095 only from the date the enforcement decision becomes final, because the foreign judgment becomes enforceable in Turkey through that decision (reference below).

### The law says “Court of Cassation”; today there are two levels

When Article 57 was enacted in 2007, Turkey’s regional courts of appeal (bölge adliye mahkemesi) were not yet operating, and the only remedy against an enforcement decision was an appeal to the Court of Cassation (temyiz). Because the article refers to the “general provisions”, today the two-tier system of the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) applies:

1. The enforcement decision is a final decision of the first-instance court. An appeal (istinaf) to the regional court of appeal must be lodged within two weeks of service (Articles 341(1)(a) and 345 HMK).
2. The regional court’s decision can be appealed to the Court of Cassation within two weeks of service (Article 361(1) HMK).

Value limits also follow from the general provisions. If the claim to be enforced is below the appeal threshold of Article 341(2) HMK or the cassation threshold of Article 362(1)(a) HMK, the decision is final at that level. The amounts written in the Code (3,000 and 40,000 lira) are increased every year by the revaluation rate (Additional Article 1(1) HMK). Since 4 June 2025 the Code states expressly that the threshold in force on the date the case was filed applies (Additional Article 1(2) HMK, as amended by Law No. 7550). In one enforcement case the 11th Civil Chamber converted a euro claim into lira at the exchange rate on the filing date and rejected the cassation appeal because the amount was below the threshold.

The cassation threshold changed twice in 2026. The Constitutional Court annulled Article 362(1)(a) HMK in so far as it applied where the appeal to the regional court had been allowed in whole or in part (E. 2026/49, K. 2026/48; effective 21 May 2026). Law No. 7589 then added a third paragraph to Article 362 (in force since 31 July 2026): where the regional court allows the appeal and decides the case afresh, a cassation appeal is possible if the part awarded or rejected exceeds the threshold of Article 341(2) HMK; it is excluded if the difference between the two decisions does not exceed that threshold or if the new decision concerns only costs or lawyers’ fees. Cases like the 11th Chamber’s one above, where the regional court decided afresh, are now assessed under this paragraph.

### Does an appeal suspend enforcement?

For judgments of Turkish courts the general rule is the opposite: neither an appeal to the regional court nor one to the Court of Cassation suspends enforcement. A debtor who wants enforcement stayed must provide security under Article 36 İİK and obtain a stay (icranın geri bırakılması) (Articles 350(1) and 367(1) HMK). Decisions on personal status, family law and rights in rem over immovable property cannot be enforced until they are final (Articles 350(2) and 367(2) HMK).

Article 57(2) MÖHUK sets a separate rule for enforcement decisions: an appeal to the Court of Cassation suspends enforcement automatically, without security. The 8th Civil Chamber held that enforcement started while such an appeal was pending must be stayed, not cancelled.

The law contains no express rule for an appeal to the regional court. It mentions only the Court of Cassation, and whether an appeal to the regional court also suspends enforcement is disputed. I have not found a binding decision in which the Court of Cassation answered this question with its own reasoning. In two decisions of the 12th Civil Chamber (2023), the regional court had held that “temyiz” must be read as covering both levels because the article was written before regional appeals existed, and that the enforcement office was right to stay enforcement once the appeal was proven; the Court of Cassation upheld these decisions with brief reasons. The reasoning is that of the regional court. In my assessment, in practice an appeal to the regional court also suspends enforcement of the enforcement decision. Whether enforcement may begin before the appeal period has expired, while no one has yet appealed, is an open question: in one of those cases the regional court said enforcement does not depend on finality, while the 12th Civil Chamber stated in 2025 that enforcement decisions cannot be enforced until they are final. It is not certain which view an enforcement court will follow in a given case.

### Relationship to other provisions

Article 57 applies by analogy to the enforcement of foreign arbitral awards (Article 61(2) MÖHUK). How the court decides on the request, including partial enforcement, is covered in [Article 56 MÖHUK – Decision on Enforcement in Turkey](/en/publications/legislation/article-56-mohuk-partial-enforcement-turkey).

## Practical points if you live abroad

**Time limits run from service.** The two-week period for both appeals starts when the decision is formally served on you. If you have a representative in Turkey, service is made on them, so agree on the deadline with them.

**Check finality before enforcing.** As a creditor, find out before starting enforcement whether an appeal has been lodged against the enforcement decision and whether it is final. Under the decisions above, enforcement is stayed while an appeal is pending. If the foreign judgment contains no award of interest, the 12th Chamber’s decision means interest runs from the date the enforcement decision becomes final.

**As a debtor.** You can go to the enforcement office with proof that you have appealed. If the decision becomes final against you, enforcement continues.

**Maintenance orders.** Under Articles 350(1) and 367(1) HMK, no stay may be granted for maintenance decisions; for Turkish maintenance judgments this rules out a stay against security under Article 36 İİK. In my assessment, for a foreign maintenance order declared enforceable, Article 57(2) MÖHUK applies as the special rule, so an appeal still suspends enforcement. I have not found a Court of Cassation decision on this point.

For questions of German or Dutch enforcement law, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. General information is available on the [Enforcement and insolvency](/en/practice-areas/enforcement-insolvency) and [Private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law) pages.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Court of Cassation, 8th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/15067, K. 2017/15607, T. 27.11.2017**

Enforcement had been started while a cassation appeal against the enforcement decision was pending, and the enforcement court cancelled it. The Chamber held that under Article 57(2) enforcement must be stayed rather than cancelled and upheld the decision as corrected. The decision was given under the procedure in force before the regional courts were set up.

**Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10608, K. 2023/3273, T. 10.05.2023**

While the enforcement decision was on appeal before the regional court, the enforcement office stayed enforcement at the debtor’s request. The regional court read “temyiz” as covering both levels of appeal and dismissed the creditor’s complaint; the Chamber upheld this with brief reasons. To the same effect: 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10373, K. 2023/3024, T. 03.05.2023.

**Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3114, K. 2023/6033, T. 23.10.2023**

The Chamber converted the claim of EUR 54,123.23 into lira at the Central Bank’s effective selling rate on the filing date; as the amount was below the cassation threshold, the appeal was rejected as inadmissible. The decision predates Laws No. 7550 and 7589.

**Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/8623, K. 2025/8451, T. 24.12.2025**

The case concerned interest in enforcement proceedings based on a French judgment declared enforceable in Turkey. The Chamber stated that enforcement decisions cannot be enforced until final and that, where the foreign judgment awards no interest, interest on the foreign-currency claim may be claimed under Article 4/a of Law No. 3095 from the date the enforcement decision became final, and it reversed the regional court’s decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an enforcement decision be enforced before it is final?

Not if a cassation appeal has been lodged; Article 57(2) MÖHUK then suspends enforcement. Regional courts take the same view for appeals to them, and the Court of Cassation has upheld such decisions, but there is no express statutory rule. Whether enforcement is possible before anyone has appealed is disputed.

### What is the deadline for appealing an enforcement decision?

Two weeks from service (Article 345 HMK). The deadline for a cassation appeal against the regional court’s decision is also two weeks from service (Article 361(1) HMK).

### Can every enforcement decision reach the Court of Cassation?

No. For money claims the value limits of Articles 341(2) and 362 HMK apply, based on the threshold on the filing date. Since 31 July 2026, Article 362(3) HMK also applies where the regional court decides the case afresh.

Article 23 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) protects a person against their own promises. No one can waive their legal capacity or capacity to act (hak ve fiil ehliyeti), not even in part. No one can give up their freedoms or restrict them in a way that is unlawful or immoral. The last paragraph deals with donating organs, tissue and blood and states that a promise to donate is not binding.

## Article 23 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **B. Protection of personality**
>
> **I. Against waiver and excessive restriction**
>
> Article 23 – No one may waive their legal capacity or capacity to act, even in part.
>
> No one may waive their freedoms or restrict them in a manner contrary to law or morality.
>
> Biological material of human origin may be removed, grafted and transplanted on the basis of written consent. However, a person who has undertaken to give biological material cannot be required to perform; no claim for pecuniary or non-pecuniary damages may be made.

Turkish original:

> **B. Kişiliğin korunması**
>
> **I. Vazgeçme ve aşırı sınırlamaya karşı**
>
> Madde 23- Kimse, hak ve fiil ehliyetlerinden kısmen de olsa vazgeçemez.
>
> Kimse özgürlüklerinden vazgeçemez veya onları hukuka ya da ahlâka aykırı olarak sınırlayamaz.
>
> Yazılı rıza üzerine insan kökenli biyolojik maddelerin alınması, aşılanması ve nakli mümkündür. Ancak, biyolojik madde verme borcu altına girmiş olandan edimini yerine getirmesi istenemez; maddî ve manevî tazminat isteminde bulunulamaz.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 23; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 23 of the former Code. Following the German text of Article 27 of the Swiss Civil Code, it was made explicit that what cannot be waived is "legal capacity and capacity to act". The rule on biological material had been added to the old Code by Law No. 3678. The reasoning concedes that keeping it is debatable, since Law No. 2238 on the removal, storage, grafting and transplantation of organs and tissue already exists, but keeps it in the Civil Code to protect personal rights (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Capacity cannot be waived

Every person has legal capacity (Article 8 TMK); capacity to act depends on the conditions set by law (Article 10 TMK). Neither can be narrowed by contract. A promise such as "I will not acquire any more property in my name" or "I will not sign any contract without my brother's approval" is a partial waiver and does not bind. Someone who feels unable to manage their affairs cannot sign away capacity; what the law offers is to ask the court to place them under restriction at their own request (Article 408 TMK).

### Freedom and excessive restriction

Paragraph 2 prohibits two things: giving up a freedom entirely, and restricting it in an unlawful or immoral way. Not every commitment is caught; every contract binds some freedom. The test is whether the core of a person's economic or personal freedom is removed. A promise never to marry, or to leave a profession completely and for an unlimited time, goes too far.

The law of obligations mirrors this: contracts contrary to personal rights are void (Article 27(1) of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK). Post-employment non-compete clauses are limited on the same basis. They may not endanger the employee's economic future unfairly through unreasonable limits as to place, time and type of work, and they may not exceed two years except in special circumstances; the judge can cut back an excessive clause (Articles 444 and 445 TBK).

### Donating biological material

Donating an organ, tissue or blood is possible with written consent. The donor can still change their mind at any point: performance cannot be demanded, and no damages, pecuniary or non-pecuniary, can be claimed because they withdrew. The medical and administrative details are governed by Law No. 2238.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

If you live in Germany or the Netherlands and are thinking of donating a kidney or part of your liver to a relative in Türkiye, Article 23(3) TMK is clear: a signed consent form does not stop you from withdrawing, and withdrawing has no legal sanction. Which hospital and which documents are involved is a matter for Law No. 2238 and health regulations, which this article does not cover.

The provision also matters if you worked for a family business or an employer in Türkiye and signed a broad non-compete clause when you left. If the employment relationship is governed by Turkish law, the validity of the non-compete clause is assessed under Articles 444–447 TBK, and other excessively binding clauses under Article 23 TMK and Article 27 TBK. Which country's law applies to the contract is a separate question, decided under the Turkish private international law act (MÖHUK).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you waive your capacity to act by contract?

No. Legal capacity and capacity to act cannot be waived, not even in part (Article 23(1) TMK). Capacity to act can only be limited by a court decision on grounds set by law.

### Can I withdraw my consent to an organ donation?

Yes. A person who has undertaken to give biological material cannot be required to perform, and no damages can be claimed from them (Article 23(3) TMK).

### Is an unlimited non-compete clause valid in Türkiye?

Under Article 445 TBK it may not exceed two years except in special circumstances, and the judge can limit an excessive clause in scope or duration. Whether a specific clause is valid depends on the contract and the type of work.

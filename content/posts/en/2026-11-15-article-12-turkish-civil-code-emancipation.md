Article 12 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) provides one route to majority before the 18th birthday: a court declaration of majority (ergin kılınma), sometimes called emancipation. A minor aged 15 or over may be declared of age by the court at their own request and with the consent of the parent holding custody (veli). The decision lets the minor carry out their own transactions before 18 without the legal representative's consent. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 12 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **c. Declaration of majority**
>
> Article 12 – A minor who has completed fifteen years of age may be declared of age by the court at their own request and with the consent of the custodial parent.

Original Turkish text:

> **c. Ergin kılınma**
>
> Madde 12 - Onbeş yaşını dolduran küçük, kendi isteği ve velisinin rızasıyla mahkemece ergin kılınabilir.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 12; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 12 of the former Code. "Kazaî rüşt" became "ergin kılınma"; since jurisdiction is a matter for procedural law, the article now simply says "the court". Consent is given by the "veli", the custodial parent, rather than "mother and father", which makes clear that a parent whose custody was removed need not consent. For minors under guardianship, Article 463 requires permission from the guardianship authority and the supervisory authority, so Article 12 does not separately require hearing the guardian (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Conditions

- **Age:** the minor must have completed 15 years.
- **The minor's own request:** the minor must want it. The custodial parent alone cannot apply.
- **Custodial parent's consent:** for a child under parental custody, the custodial parent's consent is needed; a parent without custody does not have to consent (reasoning).
- **Minors under guardianship:** after the guardianship authority's permission, the supervisory authority's permission is also required (Article 463(6) TMK).

The law says "may". Even if all conditions are met, the court is not obliged to grant the request.

### Procedure

The Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) lists the declaration of majority among non-contentious matters in the law of persons (Article 382(2)(a) HMK). Unless otherwise provided, the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) has jurisdiction (Article 383 HMK), and unless the law says otherwise, the court where the applicant or one of the parties concerned lives is competent (Article 384 HMK). The court in principle investigates the facts of its own motion (Article 385(2) HMK).

### Effect

If the person declared of age has discernment and is not under interdiction, they gain full capacity to act (Article 10 TMK), and parental custody ends, because custody applies only to children who are not of age (Article 335(1) TMK). The declaration does not change the minimum age for marriage; the age limit in Article 124 TMK still applies.

## Practical points if you live abroad

The majority of a minor Turkish citizen is governed by Turkish law as the national law (Article 9(1) MÖHUK); majority acquired under Turkish law does not end with a change of citizenship (Article 9(3) MÖHUK). How a declaration of majority issued in Turkey is treated in a transaction in Germany or the Netherlands depends on the law of that country; please check with the competent authority there or with a lawyer admitted in that country.

If the aim is only to manage an estate or a property in Turkey, the custodial parent can act for the child instead. Which route fits depends on the specific transaction.

## Frequently asked questions

### From what age can a minor be declared of age in Turkey?

From 15, at the minor's own request and with the custodial parent's consent (Article 12 TMK).

### Which court decides?

It is a non-contentious matter (Article 382(2)(a) HMK). Unless otherwise provided, the civil court of peace has jurisdiction (Article 383 HMK); territorial competence follows Article 384 HMK.

### Does parental custody end with the declaration?

Yes. Custody applies only to children who are not of age (Article 335(1) TMK).

If, before the public notice period ends, the person reappears, news is received from them, or the date of their death is established, the application for a declaration of absence (gaiplik istemi) lapses. This is Article 34 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK). The conditions for a declaration of absence are covered in the post on [Article 32 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-32-tmk-declaration-of-absence-turkey), and the waiting and notice periods in the one on [Article 33 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-33-tmk-declaration-of-absence-procedure). This post explains when the application lapses and what happens once the date of death is known.

## Article 34 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **3. Lapse of the application**
>
> Article 34 – If the person to be declared absent reappears or news is received from them before the notice period ends, or the date of their death is established, the application for a declaration of absence lapses.

Turkish original:

> **3. İstemin düşmesi**
>
> Madde 34- Gaipliğine karar verilecek kişi, ilân süresi dolmadan ortaya çıkar veya kendisinden haber alınırsa ya da öldüğü tarih tespit edilirse gaiplik istemi düşer.

As of November 2026 the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr carries no amendment note for this article. The last law in the amendment table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026), which does not touch Article 34.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 33 of the former Civil Code. The text of the 1984 preliminary draft was partly corrected and rewritten, and "gaip olan kimse" (the absent person) was replaced with "gaipliğine karar verilecek kişi" (the person to be declared absent). (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly.)

## Explanation

### Three cases

A declaration of absence is meant for the situation where nobody knows whether the person is alive or dead. If that becomes clear during the notice period, there is nothing left to decide. The law lists three cases:

- the person reappears,
- news is received from them,
- the date of their death is established.

In the first two cases the person is alive, and those who wanted to rely on rights arising from the death cannot do so. In the third case the person has died; the rules on absence give way to the consequences of an actual death.

The text says news received "from them" (kendisinden). It does not deal separately with information that the person is alive coming from a third party or an authority. There is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on this, and it is not certain which view a court would take in a given case. In my assessment, because the court investigates of its own motion in non-contentious matters (Article 385(2) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK), it will look into such information and will not declare the person absent if it turns out they are alive.

### "Before the notice period ends"

The article is tied to the notice period of at least six months in Article 33. It does not say what happens if, after that period but before the decision, it turns out that the person is alive or when they died. Article 35(1) allows the declaration "if the notice brings no result". The point is unsettled: there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision, and it is not certain which view a court would take in a given case. In my view, the condition in Article 35 is not met in that situation.

If the person returns only after the declaration has been made, Article 34 no longer applies and other rules take over. For changing the registry entry, Article 46 TMK refers to the declaration being set aside; those who received estate assets must return them under the rules on possession (Article 585(1) TMK). This is why heirs give security when they take over the estate (Article 584 TMK).

### Once the date of death is established

The person is then not absent but dead. Death is proven by the civil registry; if there is no entry or the entry is wrong, the true facts can be proven by any evidence (Article 30 TMK). Succession opens on death (Article 575(1) TMK). The security under Article 584 TMK does not come into play, because it applies only to persons who have been declared absent.

Registration of the death follows the Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). Turkish missions abroad must report a death within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1)). Where someone has died but still appears as alive in the register, the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) draws up the death record on presentation of a document showing the death; without such a document, the statement is checked and the record is processed on the order of the administrative official (Article 33(1)). Correcting an entry later, such as the date of death, as a rule requires a final court judgment (Article 35(1)).

The decision that the application has lapsed is itself a decision in non-contentious proceedings; anyone with a legal interest can appeal (istinaf) within two weeks of learning of it (Article 387(1) HMK). According to the amendment tables on mevzuat.gov.tr (as of November 2026), Articles 382–388 HMK are unchanged, and the last law in the table for Law No. 5490 is Law No. 7394 (in force since 15 April 2022), which does not affect the articles mentioned here.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

If, while proceedings are pending in Türkiye, it turns out that the person died abroad, proving and registering the death in the Turkish registry is a separate step; see [Death certificate Turkey: registering a death abroad](/en/publications/death-certificate-turkey-register-death-abroad). If you learn that the person is alive, that information and the supporting document should be filed in the absence proceedings.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens if the missing person turns up during the proceedings?

If they turn up, or news is received from them, before the notice period ends, the application lapses (Article 34 TMK). No declaration of absence is made.

### Is a declaration of absence needed if the date of death is known?

No. Once the date of death is established, the application lapses; the death is registered and succession opens on that date (Articles 34 and 575(1) TMK).

### Does Article 34 apply if the person returns after the declaration?

No. Article 34 covers only developments during the notice period. After the declaration, Article 46 TMK governs the registry entry and Article 585 TMK the return of assets.

If your mother or father lives in Turkey and can no longer manage their affairs because of mental illness or mental weakness (for example advanced dementia), the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) at the parent’s domicile restricts their legal capacity (kısıtlama) on the basis of an official medical board report and appoints a guardian (vasi) (Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK Art. 397, 405, 409, 411). The spouse and close relatives come first. Living in Germany or the Netherlands does not disqualify you under the Code, but the court takes the distance between homes into account (Art. 414, 418).

The guardian needs the court’s consent for major acts such as selling a property. Since 31 October 2026 such sales take place by auction on an electronic sales portal. This article reflects the statutory texts in UYAP Mevzuat as of November 2026.

## Grounds for guardianship of a parent

For an elderly or ill parent two grounds usually matter:

- **Mental illness or mental weakness (Art. 405).** An adult is restricted if, for this reason, they cannot manage their affairs, need constant help for their protection and care, or endanger the safety of others.
- **Own request (Art. 408).** An adult who proves that they cannot properly manage their affairs because of old age, disability, inexperience or serious illness may ask to be restricted.

Old age on its own is not a ground under Art. 405; the Code mentions it only for restriction at the person’s own request (Art. 408). If your parent understands the situation and wants this, that route is available.

Restriction for mental illness or mental weakness requires an official medical board report (resmî sağlık kurulu raporu) (Art. 409(2)). To obtain the report, the person may, on a doctor’s preliminary report, be placed in a health facility for up to twenty days (Art. 409(2) and Art. 436(6), as amended by Law No. 7499). Where restriction is requested by the person, they must be heard (Art. 409(1)).

## Competent court and application

The guardianship authority (vesayet makamı) is the civil court of peace; the supervisory authority (denetim makamı) is the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) (Art. 397(2)). Guardianship matters are non-contentious proceedings (Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK Art. 382(2)(b)(19)), and the court as a rule investigates the facts of its own motion (HMK Art. 385(2)).

The competent court is the civil court of peace at the **parent’s domicile** (TMK Art. 411). Your own residence abroad does not change this.

The Code does not list who may apply. The court can also act of its own motion, and administrative authorities, notaries and courts must report such cases to it (Art. 405(2)). A spouse or a child can apply; from abroad you can also apply through a representative in Turkey, using a power of attorney issued at a Turkish consulate.

In urgent cases the court may, before appointing a guardian, provisionally withdraw the person’s capacity and appoint a representative (Art. 420). Once final, the restriction is published at the person’s domicile and at their place of civil registration; it does not affect third parties acting in good faith before publication (Art. 410).

**If the parent lives abroad.** This is a disputed point. For a Turkish citizen without a domicile in Turkey there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision; in the author’s view jurisdiction is determined under Art. 41 MÖHUK. The applicable law is a separate question: [Article 10 MÖHUK – guardianship and restriction](/en/publications/legislation/article-10-mohuk-guardianship-turkey).

## Who is appointed guardian?

The court appoints an adult who is able to do the job (Art. 413(1)). Unless there are good reasons against it, the person proposed by the individual concerned is appointed (Art. 415); if your parent can still say whom they want, that counts. On the same condition the court prefers the spouse or a close relative, taking into account the distance between homes and personal relationships (Art. 414).

The following cannot be guardians: persons under restriction, persons barred from public service, persons whose interests conflict significantly with those of the individual, and persons on hostile terms with them (Art. 418). If siblings are in dispute over property, the court may in light of these rules appoint someone outside the family.

Several guardians can be appointed where necessary, but several persons cannot be made to run the guardianship jointly without their consent (Art. 413(2)–(3)). A guardian is normally appointed for two years, renewable for two years at a time (Art. 456). The appointee may decline within ten days of notification, and interested parties may challenge the appointment within ten days (Art. 422).

## Can a child living abroad be the guardian?

No provision excludes people who live abroad; residence is not among the disqualifications in Art. 418. Two consequences do appear in the Code. The court considers how close the homes are (Art. 414). And only persons living at the individual’s domicile are obliged to accept the office (Art. 416), so from Germany or the Netherlands you do not have to accept it.

The guardian draws up an inventory of assets (Art. 438), reports and accounts to the guardianship authority at set intervals (Art. 464) and assists the person in all personal matters (Art. 447). That is hard to do from abroad.

No binding Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) decision was found on how much weight residence abroad carries in choosing a guardian; the following two points are the author’s assessment. If a sibling lives in Turkey, the court can be expected to prefer them under Art. 414. If you still want a role, you can propose, with your consent, that more than one guardian be appointed (Art. 413(2)).

## Acts that need the court’s consent

The guardian represents the person in all legal transactions (Art. 448). For the acts listed in Art. 462, however, the consent of the civil court of peace is required. For a parent the most common are:

- buying, selling or mortgaging real property,
- lending and borrowing, and leases of real property for three years or longer,
- bringing proceedings, settlements and partition of an estate,
- placement in a care or health institution,
- changing the person’s domicile.

If you want to bring your mother or father to live with you abroad, note the last point: a person under guardianship cannot change domicile without the guardianship authority’s consent (Art. 412(1)).

Accepting or disclaiming an inheritance, a lifetime care contract (ölünceye kadar bakma sözleşmesi) and contracts between the guardian and the person also need the supervisory authority’s consent (Art. 463). Guarantees, setting up foundations and substantial gifts on the person’s behalf are prohibited (Art. 449). An act done without the required consent is treated as an act the person made without the guardian’s consent (Art. 465).

### Can the guardian sell the house? Electronic auction since 31 October 2026

Real property may be sold only if the person’s interests require it and on the guardianship authority’s instructions (Art. 444(1)). Art. 444(2) was amended by Law No. 7589 of 16 July 2026 and has applied since 31 October 2026. Unofficial translation:

> The sale is carried out by public auction on the electronic sales portal integrated into the National Judicial Network Information System, and the auction becomes final upon approval by the guardianship authority; the decision on approval must be given within ten days from the auction date.

Turkish original:

> Satış, Ulusal Yargı Ağı Bilişim Sistemine entegre elektronik satış portalında açık artırmayla yapılır ve ihale vesayet makamının onamasıyla tamam olur; onamaya ilişkin kararın ihale gününden başlayarak on gün içinde verilmesi gerekir.

Previously, a person appointed by the court ran the auction with the guardian present. The supervisory authority may exceptionally allow a private sale (Art. 444(3)). Movable property other than valuables is also sold on the portal (Art. 440(1)). Under provisional Article 1 of Law No. 7589, the changes do not apply to auctions announced before they entered into force.

## Guardian, curator (kayyım) and legal adviser

Restriction is not always necessary:

- A **curator (kayyım)** is appointed for specific matters or to manage assets (Art. 403(2)) and does not affect the person’s capacity (Art. 458(1)). If an adult cannot handle an urgent matter because of illness or absence, a representative curator is appointed (Art. 426(1)); if they cannot manage their assets but there is no sufficient ground for restriction, a management curator (Art. 427(2)).
- A **legal adviser (yasal danışman)** is appointed where there is no sufficient ground for restriction but the person needs protection. For the acts listed in Art. 429, such as bringing proceedings, buying or selling real property, loans, gifts and guarantees, the adviser’s opinion is obtained.

A power of attorney from your parent may look like a solution. Unless the contract or the nature of the matter indicates otherwise, however, a mandate ends automatically when the principal loses capacity (Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK Art. 513(1)). In the author’s view, a power of attorney therefore does not replace the appointment of a guardian once capacity is lost.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get guardianship of my mother in Turkey?

Apply to the civil court of peace at your mother’s domicile (TMK Art. 411). For mental illness or mental weakness an official medical board report is required (Art. 409(2)). The court appoints the guardian together with the restriction order.

### Can I be my parent’s guardian in Turkey if I live abroad?

The Code does not exclude it (Art. 418). The court considers the distance between homes (Art. 414), and someone who does not live at the person’s domicile is not obliged to accept (Art. 416).

### Can a guardian sell my parent’s house?

Only if the parent’s interests require it and on the court’s instructions. Since 31 October 2026 the sale is by auction on the electronic sales portal (Art. 444, 462(1)).

### Which acts require the court’s consent?

Art. 462 lists fourteen; disclaiming an inheritance and some other acts also require the supervisory authority’s consent (Art. 463).

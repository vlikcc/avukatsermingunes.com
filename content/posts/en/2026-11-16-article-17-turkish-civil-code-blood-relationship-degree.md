Article 17 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) defines blood relationship (kan hısımlığı) and how to calculate its degree. The degree is the number of births linking the relatives; persons descended from one another are related in the direct line, persons with a common ancestor in the collateral line. Marriage bans, the duty to support relatives, the exclusion of judges and the right to refuse to testify all rely on this calculation. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 17 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **IV. Relationship**
>
> **1. Blood relationship**
>
> Article 17 – The degree of blood relationship is determined by the number of births linking the relatives.
>
> Persons of whom one is descended from the other are related in the direct line (ascendant-descendant); persons who are not descended from one another but from a common ancestor are related in the collateral line.

Original Turkish text:

> **IV. Hısımlık**
>
> **1. Kan hısımlığı**
>
> Madde 17- Kan hısımlığının derecesi, hısımları birbirine bağlayan doğum sayısıyla belli olur.
>
> Biri diğerinden gelen kişiler arasında üstsoy-altsoy hısımlığı; biri diğerinden gelmeyip de, ortak bir kökten gelen kişiler arasında yansoy hısımlığı vardır.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 17; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 17 of the former Code. The old text was kept as the basis, but the French text of Article 20 of the Swiss Civil Code was also taken into account. "Usul ve füru hısımlığı" became "üstsoy-altsoy hısımlığı" (direct line) and "civar hısımlığı" became "yansoy hısımlığı" (collateral line) (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### How is the degree counted?

In the direct line you count the births directly. Between a mother and her child there is one birth: first degree. Between a grandfather and grandchild there are two: second degree.

In the collateral line you go up to the common ancestor and back down to the other person, adding all the births:

- Siblings: the common ancestors are the parents; one birth each, two in total. Second degree.
- Uncle or aunt and nephew or niece: the common ancestors are the nephew's grandparents; three births in total. Third degree.
- Cousins: the common ancestors are the grandparents; two births from each side, four in total. Fourth degree.

Relationship by marriage (affinity) is in Article 18 TMK; its line and degree follow the blood relationship defined here.

### Where the degree matters

- **Marriage bans:** Marriage is prohibited between persons related in the direct line, between siblings, and between uncles or aunts (amca, dayı, hala, teyze) and their nephews or nieces (Article 129(1) TMK). Cousins are not listed. A marriage between persons whose relationship bars marriage is absolutely void (Article 145(4) TMK).
- **Family support:** Everyone must support relatives in the direct line and siblings who would otherwise fall into poverty; for siblings only if the person obliged is well off (Article 364 TMK).
- **Exclusion of a judge:** A judge may not hear a case of their own or their spouse's ascendants or descendants, or of blood relatives or relatives by marriage up to and including the third degree (Article 34(1)(c) and (d) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). A case belonging to collateral relatives up to and including the fourth degree is a ground for challenging the judge (Article 36(1)(ç) HMK).
- **Right to refuse to testify:** Ascendants and descendants of a party, and blood relatives and relatives by marriage up to and including the third degree, may refuse to testify (Article 248(1)(c) and (d) HMK).

Statutory inheritance, by contrast, does not follow degrees but a system of classes (parentelae): first the descendants (Article 495 TMK), then the parents and their descendants (Article 496 TMK), then the grandparents and their descendants (Article 497 TMK). "I am a fourth-degree relative, so I inherit" is therefore the wrong calculation; what matters is the class.

## Practical points if you live abroad

Before Turkish authorities and courts, family relationships are usually proven through the civil registry (nüfus kaydı). If children born abroad, marriages or deaths have not been registered in Turkey, this can leave gaps, for example when applying for a certificate of inheritance in Turkey.

Capacity to marry and the conditions of marriage are governed by each party's national law at the time of the marriage (Article 13(1) MÖHUK). For a Turkish citizen, the marriage bans in Article 129 TMK therefore apply from the Turkish point of view even if the marriage takes place in Germany or the Netherlands.

## Frequently asked questions

### What degree of relationship are siblings?

Second degree in the collateral line: each is linked to the common ancestors, the parents, by one birth (Article 17 TMK).

### Can cousins marry under Turkish law?

The bans in Article 129(1) TMK cover the direct line, siblings, and uncles or aunts with nephews or nieces. Cousins, who are related in the fourth degree, are not listed.

### Which relatives may refuse to testify in Turkey?

Ascendants and descendants of a party, and blood relatives and relatives by marriage up to and including the third degree (Article 248(1) HMK).

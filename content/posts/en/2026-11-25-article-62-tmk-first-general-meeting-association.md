Article 62 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) deals with the first general meeting (ilk genel kurul) of a newly founded association: within six months of the authority's written notice, the association must hold that meeting and form its mandatory organs. It concerns the founders and the provisional board of any association whose founding review is complete.

## Article 62 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **4. First general meeting**
>
> Article 62 – (Amended: 4/11/2004 – Act No. 5253, Article 38)
>
> Associations must hold their first general meeting and form their mandatory organs within six months following the written notice given under the last paragraph of Article 60.

Original Turkish text:

> **4. İlk genel kurul toplantısı**
>
> Madde 62- (Değişik: 4/11/2004-5253/38 md.)
>
> Dernekler, 60 ıncı maddenin son fıkrası gereğince yapılan yazılı bildirimi izleyen altı ay içinde ilk genel kurul toplantılarını yapmak ve zorunlu organlarını oluşturmakla yükümlüdürler.

The present wording comes from Article 38 of the Associations Act No. 5253. As of November 2026, this is the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr; the TMK amendment table (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026) has no further entry for this article. The same Act No. 5253 repealed Article 61 TMK, which required the statute to be published in a newspaper.

## Legislative reasoning

The official reasoning was written for the original version, not for today's text. Originally, the six months ran from the day the statute was published in a newspaper, an obligation found in the now repealed Article 61. According to the reasoning, the association's capacity to act, meaning the ability to enter into legal transactions in its name, depends on its mandatory organs being formed (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised). Since 2004 the period has been linked not to newspaper publication but to the written notice under Article 60(3) TMK.

## Explanation

### Which letter starts the period

The six months start with the letter in which the authority states that the founding notice and statute have no defects, or that the defects have been fixed. It is sent to the chair of the provisional board (Associations Regulation, Article 6(2)), and Article 12 of the Regulation also counts the period from the written notice to the association. Neither the day the founding notice was filed nor the date of the receipt starts the clock. Keep the letter and a record of when you received it.

The law does not say whether the period runs if the authority never sends the letter.

### Which organs must be formed

The mandatory organs are the general meeting, the board (yönetim kurulu) and the audit committee (denetim kurulu) (Article 72 TMK). At the first meeting, the full and substitute members of the board and the audit committee are elected according to the statute (Article 4(f), Act No. 5253). A legal person acquires capacity to act once it has the organs required by law and by its founding documents (Article 49 TMK; see the article on [Article 49 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-49-tmk-capacity-to-act-legal-persons)).

### How the meeting is run

The first meeting follows the ordinary rules. Members are invited at least fifteen days in advance, with the date, time, place and agenda, by notice in a newspaper or on the association's website, in writing, by e-mail, by text message or through local media. The invitation also names the date of a second meeting in case there is no quorum; the two must be at least seven and at most sixty days apart (Regulation, Article 14). Each member has one vote and must cast it in person (Article 69 TMK; Regulation, Article 15).

### After the meeting

The elected full and substitute members of the organs are reported to the associations unit (Article 23, Act No. 5253; Regulation, Article 17). The two texts differ on the deadline: the Act says thirty days after the meeting, while Article 17 of the Regulation has said forty-five days since its 2021 amendment. This point is open; no binding court decision is cited here. In the author's assessment it is safer to work with thirty days, because the Act ranks above the Regulation.

### If the deadline is missed

If the first meeting is not held in time and the mandatory organs are not formed, the association ends automatically; anyone with an interest can ask the civil judge of the peace (sulh hâkimi) to record that it has ended (Article 87, item 2 and last paragraph, TMK). On the liquidation that follows, see the article on [Article 52 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-52-turkish-civil-code-dissolved-association-liquidation).

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

Members living in Germany or the Netherlands also vote in person; the law makes no provision for voting by proxy (Article 69 TMK). If association officers hold the meeting outside the association's seat or the place named in the statute, they face an administrative fine, and the court may annul the meeting (Article 32(b), Act No. 5253). An electronic meeting is possible, but only on systems approved by the Ministry of the Interior's Directorate General of Information Technologies and only if the invitation states this method; members log in with a secure electronic signature or two-factor authentication (Regulation, Article 15/A).

## Frequently asked questions

### When must an association in Türkiye hold its first general meeting?

Within six months of the authority's written notice under Article 60(3) TMK (Article 62 TMK; Associations Regulation, Article 12).

### What happens if it is not held?

The association ends automatically; anyone with an interest can have this recorded by the civil judge of the peace (Article 87 TMK).

### Can members living abroad take part online?

Yes, if an approved electronic system is used and the invitation says so (Associations Regulation, Article 15/A). Votes are always cast in person (Article 69 TMK).

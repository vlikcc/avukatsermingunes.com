Article 36 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) opens the section on the civil status register (kişisel durum sicili). Whether a person was born or has died, is married or divorced, and who their parents are, is shown by an official register kept for that purpose: in everyday terms, the Turkish civil registry record (nüfus kaydı). How the register is kept is not set out in the Civil Code but, today, in the Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). This concerns everyone registered in Türkiye, including Turkish citizens living in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere.

## Article 36 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **A. General**
>
> **I. Register**
>
> Article 36 – Civil status is determined by the official register kept for that purpose.
>
> The principles for keeping this register and making the mandatory notifications are set out in the relevant law.

Turkish original:

> **A. Genel olarak**
>
> **I. Sicil**
>
> Madde 36- Kişisel durum, bu amaçla tutulan resmî sicille belirlenir.
>
> Bu sicilin tutulmasına ve zorunlu bildirimlerin yapılmasına ilişkin esaslar, ilgili kanunda gösterilir.

The article has been in force unchanged since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026 it does not appear in the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr; the latest law in that table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 35 of the former Civil Code No. 743 and draws on Article 36 of the 1984 preliminary draft. The old section heading "Ahvali Şahsiye Sicil Kayıtları" was changed to the plainer "Kişisel Durum Sicili" (civil status register) (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Which register is meant?

The Civil Code speaks of the "civil status register"; Law No. 5490 speaks of "nüfus kütükleri". They are the same thing. Under Article 5(1) of Law No. 5490, the registers are official documents, to be kept permanently, in which civil status events are recorded so as to establish a person's identity, residential address, family ties, citizenship and personal status. A civil status event (nüfus olayı) is one that changes personal status, such as birth, death, marriage, divorce, adoption, acknowledgement of paternity, correction of an entry, correction of parentage or a declaration of absence (Article 3(1)(y)). Article 7 lists the information the family register contains.

### The "relevant law": Law No. 5490

The second paragraph leaves the details to a separate statute. In 2002 that was the Population Law No. 1587; Law No. 5490 repealed it (Article 71). The mandatory notifications are now found there: birth in Article 15, marriage in Articles 23 and 24, death in Article 31, and notice of court decisions in Article 55. Every entry must rest on a supporting document (dayanak belgesi) issued in the proper form by an authorised official (Article 12(1)). Articles 41 and 42 TMK likewise refer birth notifications and changes in personal status to the "relevant law".

The latest law in the amendment table of the current text of Law No. 5490 on mevzuat.gov.tr is Law No. 7394 (Article 65 and provisional Article 12, in force since 15 April 2022). None of the provisions cited here has changed since; Articles 5, 7, 15 and 44 were last amended by Law No. 7039 (in force since 3 November 2017).

### Evidential weight of the record

Official registers are evidence that the facts they record are true; proof that their content is wrong is not tied to any particular form unless a law says otherwise (Article 7 TMK). Law No. 5490 applies this to the civil registry:

- Entries in the family register and the special registers, their backups and extracts from them have the same legal value. If they differ, the supporting document behind the entry prevails until the contrary is proved (Article 5(2)).
- Registry extracts (nüfus kayıt örneği) certified by the district registry office are valid until the contrary is proved (Article 43(1)). Extracts obtained through the e-Devlet portal have the same legal value as those issued by the registry office (Article 44(5)).
- Turkish public bodies rely on the registry record in matters concerning a person. Where records differ, the registry record is not changed; the other records are corrected (Article 10(1)).

Proving in court that an entry is wrong does not change the entry. No entry may be corrected without a final court judgment; only clerical errors made when recording an event are corrected by the registry office on the basis of the supporting document (Article 39 TMK; Article 35(1) Law No. 5490). How birth and death are proved is covered in [Article 30 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-30-turkish-civil-code-birth-death); the general evidential value of official documents in [Article 7 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-7-turkish-civil-code-official-records).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

Every Turkish citizen must have themselves entered in the civil registry, in Türkiye through the registry office and abroad through the Turkish mission (Article 11(1) Law No. 5490). Citizens living abroad who have no family register in Türkiye are entered in a family register opened at a registry office designated by the Ministry of the Interior (Article 7(2)).

Only what is reported to the Turkish civil registration authorities reaches the Turkish register. A birth, marriage or death recorded by a German Standesamt or a Dutch gemeente does not appear in the Turkish record until it is reported to the Turkish consulate or a registry office in Türkiye. Since Turkish bodies generally rely on the registry record (Article 10(1)), a missing entry tends to surface in inheritance, land registry or remarriage matters. What to do for each event is explained in separate articles: [registering a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad), [registering a marriage abroad in Türkiye](/en/publications/register-marriage-abroad-in-turkey) and [registering a death abroad](/en/publications/death-certificate-turkey-register-death-abroad). Who receives these notifications is the subject of Article 37 TMK.

If you need a registry extract for a matter in Türkiye, you can obtain one for yourself and for your ancestors and descendants through e-Devlet (Article 44(5)). An extract not used within 180 days of issue loses its validity; the Ministry may shorten this to as little as 30 days depending on the purpose (Article 43(2)). What effect a Turkish extract has in Germany or the Netherlands is a matter for the law of that country.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Turkish civil status register?

The official register that shows a person's birth, death, marriage, divorce and parentage (Article 36 TMK). In Türkiye it consists of the nüfus registers kept under Law No. 5490.

### Can a registry entry be disproved?

Yes. The register is evidence of the facts it records, but they can be shown to be wrong (Article 7 TMK). Correcting the entry itself requires a final court judgment, except for clerical errors (Article 39 TMK; Article 35(1) Law No. 5490).

### Is a nüfus kayıt örneği from e-Devlet valid?

Yes. Documents obtained through e-Devlet have the same legal value as those issued by the registry office (Article 44(5) Law No. 5490).

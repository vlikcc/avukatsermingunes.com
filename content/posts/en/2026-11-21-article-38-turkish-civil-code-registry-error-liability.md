Article 38 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) decides who pays for damage caused by the keeping of the Turkish civil status register (kişisel durum sicili, nüfus kaydı). The State pays. The official at fault is not liable to you directly; the State can claim the amount back from them (recourse, rücu). Both the damages claim and the recourse claim are brought before the court of the place where the register is kept. This matters to anyone whose pension is stopped or who cannot obtain a certificate of inheritance because of a wrong entry.

## Article 38 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Liability**
>
> Article 38 – Damage arising from the keeping of the civil status register is compensated by the State, subject to recourse against the official at fault.
>
> Actions for compensation and recourse are brought before the court of the place where the civil status register is kept.

Original Turkish text:

> **III. Sorumluluk**
>
> Madde 38- Kişisel durum sicilinin tutulmasından doğan zararlar, kusurlu memura rücu edilmek kaydıyla, Devletçe tazmin edilir.
>
> Tazminat ve rücu davaları, kişisel durum sicilinin tutulduğu yer mahkemesinde açılır.

As of November 2026, Article 38 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it. The latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 37 of the former Civil Code No. 743, which made officials directly liable. The reasoning considered that rule incompatible with the Constitution and with Civil Servants Law No. 657. The article was therefore rewritten in line with Article 129 of the Constitution and in parallel with the provisions on guardianship and the land register (TMK Articles 468, 469 and 1007), and the competent court was specified (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### The State is liable, the official answers in recourse

Paragraph 1 creates two relationships: compensation between you and the State, and recourse between the State and the official. You do not sue the official. Article 129(5) of the Turkish Constitution likewise says that damages claims for faults committed by officials in the exercise of their powers may only be brought against the administration, subject to recourse. Article 13 of Law No. 657 says the same: individuals sue the institution, not its staff.

The wording does not make the State's liability depend on the official's fault; fault matters for recourse. In the author's assessment, the text means that you need not prove the official's fault, only the link between the keeping of the register and your loss. I do not cite a verified court decision on this point.

### Damage "arising from the keeping of the register"

This covers wrong or incomplete entries, an event that was never entered, or another person's data in your record. Because the register is presumed correct (TMK Articles 7 and 30), such an error has direct effects at the bank, the land registry, the pension office and in inheritance matters.

Correcting the entry is a separate matter. As a rule it requires a court decision (TMK Article 39; Population Services Law No. 5490, Article 35(1)). Clerical errors made when an event was recorded (maddî hata) are corrected by the civil registry office itself on the basis of the underlying document (Law No. 5490, Articles 35(1) and 38). A damages claim does not correct the entry, and a correction case does not compensate the loss.

### Competent court

Paragraph 2 names the court of the place where the register is kept for both claims. It does not say which branch of the judiciary or which court has subject-matter jurisdiction. In the author's assessment, Article 38 follows the same system as TMK Article 1007 on liability for the land register; which court has subject-matter jurisdiction and against whom the claim is directed should be determined for the specific case before filing. Article 38 sets no special time limit, and it does not say which limitation period applies.

## Practical points for Turkish citizens living abroad

Even if you live abroad, your Turkish record is kept in the family register (aile kütüğü) of a particular neighbourhood or village (Law No. 5490, Article 7). If an event you reported at the consulate was entered incorrectly there, the "place where the register is kept" can reasonably be read as the place of that record; this too is the author's assessment.

The first step is to find where the error comes from. If the foreign document is correct and the Turkish entry is wrong, the usual route is an application to the registry office or a correction case. Compensation is a question for afterwards, once the cost of the error is clear. For correcting a date of birth see [changing your date of birth in Turkey](/en/publications/change-date-of-birth-turkey-court-case); for the evidential value of the register extract see [Article 36 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-36-turkish-civil-code-civil-status-register).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who do I sue if a registry error caused me a loss?

The State, not the official. Under TMK Article 38(1) the State compensates the loss and has recourse against the official at fault (Constitution Article 129(5), Law No. 657 Article 13).

### Does a damages claim also correct my record?

No. Correction requires a court decision (TMK Article 39, Law No. 5490 Article 35(1)); clerical errors are corrected by the registry office (Law No. 5490 Article 38).

### Where is the case filed?

Before the court of the place where the civil status register is kept (TMK Article 38(2)). The article does not say which court has subject-matter jurisdiction.

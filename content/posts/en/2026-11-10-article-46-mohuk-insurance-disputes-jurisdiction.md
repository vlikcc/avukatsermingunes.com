Article 46 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which Turkish court has jurisdiction over insurance disputes with a foreign element (milletlerarası yetki). A claim against the insurer goes to the court where the insurer’s principal place of business, or the branch or agency that concluded the policy, is located in Turkey. A claim against the policyholder, the insured or the beneficiary goes to the court of their domicile or habitual residence in Turkey. You will come across this article if you hold home insurance or compulsory earthquake insurance (DASK) for a flat in Turkey, are the beneficiary of a life policy taken out in Turkey, or are in a dispute with a Turkish insurer while living in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe.

## Article 46 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Actions concerning insurance contracts**
>
> Article 46 – (1) In disputes arising from an insurance contract, the court of the place in Turkey where the insurer’s principal place of business, or the branch or agency that concluded the insurance contract, is located has jurisdiction. However, in actions brought against the policyholder, the insured or the beneficiary, the competent court is the court of their domicile or habitual residence in Turkey.

Turkish original:

> **Sigorta sözleşmesine ilişkin davalar**
>
> MADDE 46 – (1) Sigorta sözleşmesinden doğan uyuşmazlıklarda, sigortacının esas işyeri veya sigorta sözleşmesini yapan şubesinin ya da acentasının Türkiye’de bulunduğu yer mahkemesi yetkilidir. Ancak sigorta ettirene, sigortalıya veya lehdara karşı açılacak davalarda yetkili mahkeme, onların Türkiye’deki yerleşim yeri veya mutad meskeni mahkemesidir.

The article has not changed since the law came into force on 12 December 2007; as of November 2026 the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system shows no amendment note for it.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article was new; the former Law No. 2675 had no equivalent. Insurers had used freedom of contract, and standard terms in particular, to make it harder for the other side to use its legal remedies, or to remove them altogether. To protect the economically and socially weaker party, the legislator gave jurisdiction over claims against the insurer to the court of its principal place of business and of the branch or agency that concluded the contract, and over claims against the policyholder, insured or beneficiary to the court of their domicile or habitual residence. Because an insurer may have many branches and agents, only the one that concluded the contract counts. The reasoning also notes that this jurisdiction cannot be excluded by agreement. (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 45 of the bill; the bill’s Article 46(2) cited there became Article 47(2) of the law.)

## Explanation

As a rule, the international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 MÖHUK; see [Article 40 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-40-mohuk-international-jurisdiction-turkey)). Article 46 sets a special rule for insurance contracts and separates the two directions of a claim. A claim against the insurer belongs to the court where its principal place of business, or the branch or agency that concluded the policy, is located in Turkey. A claim against the policyholder, the insured or the beneficiary belongs only to the court of their domicile or habitual residence (mutad mesken) in Turkey.

The first sentence does not mention the claimant’s domicile. If you want to sue the insurer, you look for the court by the address of the insurer or of the branch or agency that issued the policy, not by your own Turkish address.

The jurisdiction of these courts cannot be excluded by agreement (Article 47(2) MÖHUK). A clause in the policy saying that “only the courts of country X” have jurisdiction does not stop you from suing in the Turkish court that has jurisdiction under Article 46. Under domestic law, jurisdiction agreements can in any case only be made between merchants or public legal entities (Article 17 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK).

The HMK also has its own rule for insurance cases. Where property insurance relates to immovable property, the claim may also be brought where the property is located (Article 15(1) HMK). In personal insurance (can sigortaları), the court of the domicile of the policyholder, insured or beneficiary has exclusive jurisdiction over claims brought by or against them (Article 15(2) HMK). Life insurance is regulated as personal insurance in the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 (TTK, Article 1487 onwards). Whether Article 15 HMK adds a further ground of jurisdiction next to Article 46 in a cross-border case is discussed below.

For liability claims arising from motor vehicle accidents, the Highway Traffic Law No. 2918 (KTK) has a separate rule: they may be brought before the courts where the insurer’s branch or the agency that concluded the policy is located, or before the court of the place of the accident (Article 110(2) KTK). The Constitutional Court annulled the words “head office” (merkez) and “or” in this paragraph by its decision of 14 March 2024, E. 2023/79, K. 2024/80; according to the amendment table in UYAP the annulment took effect on 18 April 2024.

## What this means if you live abroad

### DASK and home insurance for your flat in Turkey

Independent units under condominium ownership and residential buildings on land registered in the title register are subject to compulsory earthquake insurance; the owner or usufructuary must take it out and renew it every year (Article 10(1) and (3) of the Disaster Insurance Law No. 6305). The cover is in principle provided by the Natural Disaster Insurance Institution (Doğal Afet Sigortaları Kurumu, DASK) (Article 7(1) Law No. 6305). If there is a dispute about a claim payment, you can sue from abroad before the Turkish court where the insurer’s principal place of business, or the branch or agency that issued the policy, is located (Article 46(1), first sentence). The agency is named on the policy.

Whether the court where the flat is located also has jurisdiction is disputed. Article 15(1) HMK allows this in domestic cases; I could not find a Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) decision that directly settles whether this rule also applies to international jurisdiction through Article 40. In the decisions below, the courts applied Article 46 as the special rule for insurance disputes. How a court will decide in a specific case is not certain.

### Beneficiary of a Turkish life policy living abroad

A beneficiary who lives in Germany or the Netherlands and wants to claim the sum insured can sue the insurer under the first sentence of Article 46(1), where its principal place of business or the branch or agency that concluded the policy is located. Article 15(2) HMK gives exclusive jurisdiction in personal insurance to the court of the beneficiary’s domicile; if that domicile is abroad, the rule points to no court in Turkey. In my assessment Article 46 then applies as the special rule, so the claim can be brought where the insurer is located in Turkey. This is an assessment: the law does not expressly settle how the two provisions relate, and I could not find a binding decision on the point.

### You live abroad and are being sued

For claims against the policyholder, insured or beneficiary, the second sentence of Article 46 names only their domicile or habitual residence **in Turkey**. If the insurer wants to sue, for example for unpaid premiums, an insured who lives in Germany and has neither domicile nor habitual residence in Turkey, this sentence points to no Turkish court.

This question is disputed. The law has no express rule for this situation, and I could not find a binding court decision. One view is that the second sentence (“however, the competent court is …”) is exhaustive, so Article 46 gives Turkish courts no jurisdiction over an insured with no domicile or habitual residence in Turkey. Another possibility is that the court falls back, through Article 40, on the general domestic rules (for example the place of performance, Article 10 HMK). Since the article is meant to protect the weaker party, I find the first view more convincing; how a court will decide in a specific case is not certain.

If such a claim is brought against you in Turkey while you live abroad, raise the lack of international jurisdiction in your statement of defence; where jurisdiction is not exclusive, the objection must be made there (Article 19(2) HMK). Because the law does not say expressly whether jurisdiction under Article 46 is exclusive, objecting at that stage is the safe course.

### Accident in Turkey with a foreign-registered car

Traffic accidents in Turkey, the Green Card and compulsory motor insurance are covered in the post on [Article 34 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-34-mohuk-tort-accident-turkey). On jurisdiction, the only addition here is that Article 110(2) KTK also gives jurisdiction to the court of the place of the accident (see above).

### Policy concluded abroad

If a policy was issued at the head office of an insurer in Germany and not through a branch or agency in Turkey, the first sentence of Article 46 points to no Turkish court. In the decisions below, upheld by the Yargıtay, claims against foreign insurers were dismissed for this reason for lack of international jurisdiction.

### Before you sue: mediation, a claim to the insurer, the Insurance Arbitration Commission

Because insurance law is part of the TTK, actions arising from insurance contracts are commercial cases whether or not the parties are merchants (Article 4(1)(a) TTK). For actions for a sum of money, such as receivables and damages, applying to a mediator (arabuluculuk) before going to court is a procedural requirement (Article 5/A TTK). If the policyholder is a consumer, the consumer court may have jurisdiction (Articles 73 and 83(2) of the Consumer Protection Law No. 6502); there, too, mediation is a precondition for suing, apart from the statutory exceptions (Article 73/A Law No. 6502). Which court has subject-matter jurisdiction is outside the scope of this post.

The mediation application is filed with the mediation office at the place of the court that has jurisdiction (Article 18/A(4) of the Mediation Law No. 6325). Since Article 46 determines that court, it also determines the office. From the application until the final report, limitation is suspended (Article 18/A(15) Law No. 6325).

Besides the courts there is the Insurance Arbitration Commission (Sigorta Tahkim Komisyonu) at the Association of Insurance, Reinsurance and Pension Companies (Article 30(1) of the Insurance Law No. 5684). For disputes about compulsory insurance this route is open even if the insurer has not joined the arbitration system. Before applying, you must have made a claim to the insurer and be able to show that it was rejected in whole or in part; it is also enough if the insurer has not answered in writing within fifteen working days (Article 30(13) Law No. 5684). A dispute already before a court or a consumer arbitration committee cannot be taken to the Commission (Article 30(14)). Fees and value thresholds change, so no amounts are given here.

Watch the limitation period: claims arising from an insurance contract are time-barred two years after they fall due, and claims for insurance compensation or the sum insured in any event six years after the insured event; rules in other laws and Article 1482 TTK on liability insurance are reserved (Article 1420 TTK).

For other compensation claims, see [compensation law](/en/practice-areas/compensation-law).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2022/2901, K. 2023/7087, T. 05.12.2023**

A cargo insurer sought to recover what it had paid for cargo lost when a ship sank from the shipowner’s liability insurer, based in England. The first-instance court found that the insurer was based abroad and that the policy had been issued abroad, not by a branch or agency in Turkey, and held that Turkish courts lacked jurisdiction under Article 46. It added that the jurisdiction clause in the policy did not bind a claimant who was not party to it, and that jurisdiction had to be determined under Article 46. The regional court of appeal dismissed the appeal and the Yargıtay upheld the decision.

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2022/5278, K. 2024/1898, T. 07.03.2024**

A Turkish insurer sued a reinsurer based in Vienna. The first-instance court held the contractual jurisdiction clause invalid because it did not name a specific court (Articles 17 and 18 HMK). As the contract had been concluded by the defendant itself, not through a branch or agency, and its principal place of business was in Vienna, the court found that Turkish courts had no jurisdiction under Article 46. The appeal and the appeal on points of law failed. To the same effect, for a policy issued at the insurer’s head office: Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/5533, K. 2026/2617, T. 05.05.2026.

These decisions concern commercial insurance, and the Yargıtay upheld the first-instance rulings without detailed reasons of its own. For consumer policies they only show how the first sentence is applied.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I sue an insurance company in Turkey?

Before the court where the insurer’s principal place of business, or the branch or agency that concluded the policy, is located in Turkey (Article 46 MÖHUK). Liability claims from traffic accidents may also be brought where the accident happened (Article 110(2) KTK).

### My policy names a foreign court – can I still sue in Turkey?

Yes. Jurisdiction under Article 46 cannot be excluded by agreement (Article 47(2) MÖHUK).

### Do I need mediation before suing an insurer in Turkey?

For claims for money and damages, yes: Article 5/A TTK, and for cases before the consumer court Article 73/A Law No. 6502 (with exceptions). The application goes to the mediation office where the court competent under Article 46 sits.

### I live in Germany – can the insurer sue me in Turkey?

Article 46 names only your domicile or habitual residence in Turkey; if you have neither, the law is unclear and the point is disputed. Object to jurisdiction in your statement of defence (Article 19(2) HMK).

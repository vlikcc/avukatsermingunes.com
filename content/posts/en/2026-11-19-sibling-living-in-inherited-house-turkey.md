If one of your siblings lives alone in the house your parents left in Turkey, the other heirs can claim compensation from them for the use of their share. Turkish law calls this ecrimisil (compensation for unlawful occupation). There is no specific provision on ecrimisil between co-owners or heirs; the claim rests on the rules on ownership and possession and on conditions developed by the courts, the main one being that the sibling has excluded the others from using the house.

If you live in Germany or elsewhere in Europe and get nothing out of the house in Turkey, this article explains what has to be shown, how far back you can claim and where the case is brought. Selling or dividing the house is a separate topic, covered in [inherited property in Turkey: partition and sale](/en/publications/inherited-property-turkey-partition-lawsuit).

As of November 2026, the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr show no change to the articles of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK) cited here. Law No. 7589, however, abolished the unquantified claim (belirsiz alacak davası) in the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) from 31 July 2026; the effect is explained below.

## What is ecrimisil and where is it regulated?

Ecrimisil is the payment an owner claims from someone who uses a property without a right to do so. The word does not appear in the Civil Code (TMK), the Code of Obligations (TBK) or the HMK. For privately owned property the claim is based on these rules:

- The owner can sue to stop any unlawful interference (TMK Art. 683(2)).
- A possessor in bad faith must compensate the rightful owner for the harm caused by wrongfully withholding the property and for the fruits obtained or neglected (TMK Art. 995(1)).
- A possessor in good faith who uses the property in line with their presumed right owes no compensation for that use (TMK Art. 993(1)). Good faith is presumed (TMK Art. 3).
- The rules on tort (TBK Art. 49) and unjust enrichment (TBK Art. 77) are also discussed as a basis.

For property of the Treasury (Hazine) and the other authorities listed in the law, Art. 75 of State Tender Law No. 2886 regulates ecrimisil expressly; that is not the subject of this article.

## Does a sibling living in the inherited house have to pay?

On the death of the deceased, the heirs own the estate jointly (TMK Art. 640(2)). In joint ownership (elbirliği mülkiyeti) there are no defined shares; each owner's right extends to the whole property (TMK Art. 701(2)). If the heirs have moved to co-ownership in shares (paylı mülkiyet), each co-owner may use the property as far as this is compatible with the rights of the others (TMK Art. 693(1)).

The sibling in the house has a right to use it too. The question is whether they use it in a way that shuts the others out. In practice, in ecrimisil claims between co-owners and heirs, the courts require that the defendant has kept the others from using the property (intifadan men, exclusion from use). This condition is not in the statute; it comes from case law.

> This point is disputed. The exclusion requirement, its scope and its exceptions are not expressly regulated by statute; they rest on court decisions. Those decisions could not be checked in an official database for this article, so no case references are given. How a court will apply the requirement in a particular case is uncertain.

In the author's assessment, the clearest way to show exclusion is to tell your sibling in writing that you want to use the house or be paid for its use, and to keep the reply. A changed lock, or the house being let without the rent being shared, also counts. The mere fact that you lived abroad and did not use the house may not be enough on its own.

Use can also be arranged without going to court. Co-owners can agree unanimously on how the property is used (TMK Art. 689); if they cannot agree, the judge decides, which may include dividing use by time or by space (TMK Art. 693(2)). Letting the house requires a majority of shares and of co-owners in co-ownership (TMK Art. 691(1)); in joint ownership, management decisions require unanimity (TMK Art. 702(2)).

## Rights of heirs living abroad

Not living in Turkey does not reduce your rights as an heir. For a claim brought from abroad, the real question is who brings it.

If the estate has not been divided and the land registry (tapu) still shows joint ownership, the heirs act together in respect of the estate's rights (TMK Art. 640(2)). Each heir may ask for the estate's rights to be protected, and all heirs benefit (TMK Art. 640(4); also Art. 702(4)). Whether one heir can sue alone for their own part of the ecrimisil while joint ownership continues is not expressly regulated. In the author's assessment, three routes reduce the risk of the claim being dismissed on procedural grounds:

1. sue together with the other heirs (other than the sibling in the house),
2. ask the magistrate's court (sulh mahkemesi) to appoint a representative for the community of heirs (TMK Art. 640(3)),
3. first convert joint ownership into co-ownership in shares (TMK Art. 644). In co-ownership each co-owner has the rights and duties of an owner for their share (TMK Art. 688(3)).

How the transfer in the land registry and the move to co-ownership work is covered in [inheriting property in Turkey: title deed transfer](/en/publications/inheriting-property-in-turkey-title-deed); who inherits which share in [inheritance shares in Turkey](/en/publications/inheritance-shares-turkey-spouse-children). You do not need to travel to Turkey for the case or the mediation; the lawyer files a power of attorney drawn up or certified by a notary (HMK Art. 76(1)). You can grant it at a Turkish consulate.

## Limitation period: five years?

There is no specific limitation period for ecrimisil between private persons. In practice, ecrimisil is claimed for the five years before the claim is filed, and this period is linked to the five-year limitation for rent and other periodic payments in TBK Art. 147(1). For ecrimisil between private persons, five years is not written in the statute; it rests on court decisions.

> This point is disputed. There is no statutory limitation rule specific to ecrimisil; the five-year period applied in practice rests on court decisions that could not be checked in an official database for this article, so no case references are given. Which period a court will apply in a particular case is uncertain.

The statute can also be read differently: for tort (TBK Art. 72(1)) and unjust enrichment (TBK Art. 82(1)) the periods are two and ten years, and the general rule is ten years (TBK Art. 146). The only provision that states five years expressly concerns state property: ecrimisil is claimed for no more than five years back from the date it is assessed (Law No. 2886, Art. 75).

Two procedural rules:

- The court does not apply limitation of its own motion (TBK Art. 161). If your sibling does not raise it, an earlier period may also be included.
- Limitation is interrupted by filing a claim or starting enforcement proceedings (TBK Art. 154(2)).

In the author's assessment, waiting works against you: since the claim is accepted only for a limited period back, each month that passes can push the oldest month out.

## Is mediation mandatory for an ecrimisil claim?

Since 1 September 2023, Art. 18/B(1) of Law No. 6325 on Mediation in Civil Disputes (HUAK) makes prior mediation a condition for bringing these disputes to court:

> a) Kiralanan taşınmazların 2004 sayılı Kanuna göre ilamsız icra yoluyla tahliyesine ilişkin hükümler hariç olmak üzere, kira ilişkisinden kaynaklanan uyuşmazlıklar.
> b) Taşınır ve taşınmazların paylaştırılmasına ve ortaklığın giderilmesine ilişkin uyuşmazlıklar.
> c) 23/6/1965 tarihli ve 634 sayılı Kat Mülkiyeti Kanunundan kaynaklanan uyuşmazlıklar.
> ç) Komşu hakkından kaynaklanan uyuşmazlıklar.

Unofficial translation: a) disputes arising from a tenancy, except eviction without a judgment under Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004; b) disputes on the division of movable and immovable property and the ending of co-ownership; c) disputes arising from Condominium Law No. 634; ç) disputes arising from neighbour law.

Ecrimisil is not on this list; the claim concerns payment for past use, not the division of the property. In the author's assessment, a claim for ecrimisil alone therefore does not clearly fall within the wording of Art. 18/B. This is disputed: there is no express provision, and no verified court decision on the point was reviewed for this article. If the siblings have signed a lease, the dispute arises from a tenancy under point (a) and mediation is mandatory.

Where mediation is a precondition and a claim is filed without it, the claim is dismissed on procedural grounds (HUAK Art. 18/A(2)). Voluntary mediation is always available for disputes the parties can freely dispose of (HUAK Art. 1(2)). If you also want the house divided, the author considers it practical to raise the use payment in the mediation that is mandatory for partition anyway.

## Which court, and how to file

**Subject-matter jurisdiction.** The cases heard by the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) are listed in HMK Art. 4(1) (tenancy, partition and ending co-ownership, protection of possession only, cases assigned by statute); ecrimisil is not among them. For claims concerning property rights, the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) has jurisdiction unless otherwise provided (HMK Art. 2(1)). An ecrimisil claim therefore normally goes there; where the parties have a tenancy, it goes to the civil court of peace (HMK Art. 4(1)(a)).

**Venue.** The general venue is the court where the defendant, the sibling in the house, is domiciled (HMK Art. 6(1)). For tort claims, the court of the place where the tort was committed also has jurisdiction (HMK Art. 16). In the author's assessment, where the claim is based on tort, the place where the house is located may also qualify; there is, however, no venue rule specific to ecrimisil.

**The unquantified claim is gone.** The amount of ecrimisil is often not known exactly when the claim is filed. The unquantified claim under HMK Art. 107 has not been available since 31 July 2026 (Law No. 7589). If you claim part of the amount, you may increase it once in the same proceedings until the end of the evidence stage; for the increased part, limitation is treated as interrupted from the filing date (HMK Art. 109(4)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a sibling living in an inherited house have to pay rent?

Not automatically. If the other heirs show that the sibling excluded them from using the house, they can claim ecrimisil for their shares; this condition comes from case law.

### How far back can occupation compensation be claimed?

There is no specific statutory period between private persons. In practice, five years before the claim is used; this rests on court decisions and is disputed.

### Is mediation required before suing?

HUAK Art. 18/B does not name ecrimisil. In the author's assessment, a claim for ecrimisil alone is not covered by its wording; a lease changes that. The point is disputed.

### Which court hears an ecrimisil claim?

Normally the civil court of first instance (HMK Art. 2(1)), where the defendant is domiciled (HMK Art. 6).

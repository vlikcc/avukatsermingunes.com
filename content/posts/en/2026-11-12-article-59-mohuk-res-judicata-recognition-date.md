Article 59 of Turkey's Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK, Official Gazette of 12 December 2007, No. 26728) sets the date from which a foreign judgment recognised (tanıma) or declared enforceable (tenfiz) in Turkey has res judicata effect (kesin hüküm) and counts as conclusive evidence (kesin delil). That date is not the day the Turkish court rules on recognition. It is the day the judgment became final in the country where it was given. If you divorced in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere and have the divorce recognised in Turkey years later, this rule decides your divorce date, later claims and limitation periods.

## Article 59 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Res judicata and conclusive evidence effect**
>
> Article 59 – (1) The res judicata or conclusive evidence effect of a foreign judgment applies from the moment the foreign court decision became final.

Original Turkish text:

> **Kesin hüküm ve kesin delil etkisi**
>
> MADDE 59 – (1) Yabancı ilâmın kesin hüküm veya kesin delil etkisi yabancı mahkeme kararının kesinleştiği andan itibaren hüküm ifade eder.

The article has not changed since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article is new; the former Law No. 2675 had no equivalent. In practice it was unclear from when the res judicata or evidentiary effect of a recognised foreign judgment applied in Turkish law. The article settles this: what counts is the moment the judgment became final under its own law (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 58 of the bill).

## Explanation

### Effect depends on recognition but reaches back

A foreign judgment does not automatically have res judicata or evidentiary effect in Turkey. Under Article 58(1) MÖHUK, the court must first find that it meets the conditions for enforcement. Article 59 says how far back that finding reaches. The Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation (Hukuk Genel Kurulu) has put it this way: recognition decisions take effect retroactively, as of the date the foreign judgment became final (E. 2022/766, K. 2023/975).

Before recognition or enforcement, you cannot rely on the foreign judgment as res judicata before a Turkish court, and Article 59 does not change that. Once recognition is granted, the effect starts from the date of finality abroad. Actual enforcement still requires an enforcement decision (Articles 50(1) and 57(1) MÖHUK).

### What the two effects mean

Res judicata bars the same dispute from being litigated again. Under Article 303(1) of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure (HMK), the parties, the cause of action, and the operative part of the first judgment and the relief sought in the second case must be the same. That the same case has not already been finally decided is a procedural requirement (Article 114(1)(i) HMK). A Turkish claim on a dispute already decided by a recognised German judgment is therefore dismissed as inadmissible (see the 11th Civil Chamber below).

With the evidentiary effect, the judgment is used in another case as evidence that binds the judge. Judgments of Turkish courts are conclusive evidence unless proven forged (Article 204(1) HMK). According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, recognition or enforcement gives the foreign judgment the force and character of a Turkish court judgment (E. 2022/1205, K. 2023/1188).

As of November 2026, the current HMK text in the UYAP Mevzuat system (last law in the amendment table: No. 7589, in force since 31 July 2026) contains no amendment to Articles 114, 204 or 303.

## Practical points if you live abroad

**Your divorce date is the date of finality abroad.** According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, once a foreign divorce is recognised the spouses are treated as divorced from the date the foreign judgment became final, and the consequences of divorce run from that date. The same decision states that under Turkish civil registration rules this date is entered in the family register as the divorce date (E. 2022/766, K. 2023/975).

**There is no deadline for recognition.** According to the same decision, the law sets no time limit for recognising foreign divorce judgments; the case can be brought at any time. In that case, a divorce that became final in the Netherlands in 2004 was brought for recognition in 2018. For the general framework, see [Article 50 MÖHUK – enforcement in Turkey](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey).

**Limitation of matrimonial property claims: case law is not settled.** The Assembly of Civil Chambers has given two different answers on when the ten-year limitation period for a matrimonial property claim starts after a foreign divorce. In 2021 it relied on the date the Turkish recognition decision became final (E. 2018/471, K. 2021/1586); in 2023, relying on Article 59, it took the date the foreign judgment became final (E. 2022/1205, K. 2023/1188). Both were majority decisions. The law does not answer this question directly, and which approach a court will follow in a given case is uncertain. My assessment is that it is safer to assume the period starts when the judgment became final abroad, because the most recent decision of the Assembly says so. On the law applicable to limitation, see [Article 8 MÖHUK – limitation periods](/en/publications/legislation/article-8-mohuk-limitation-periods).

**No fault can be assigned afterwards.** Where an uncontested divorce granted in Germany without any finding of fault had been recognised in Turkey, the Court of Cassation held that a later Turkish damages claim (Article 178 Turkish Civil Code) could not assign fault to either spouse (2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/1003, K. 2024/2481). If you are considering an uncontested divorce abroad, bear in mind that it can affect such claims under Turkish law.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2022/766, K. 2023/975, 18 October 2023**

Recognition of a divorce judgment of the Arnhem family court, final since 9 August 2004, was sought in 2018. The Assembly held that no time limit applies to recognition, that under Article 59 the personal consequences of divorce arise from the date of finality abroad, and that the spouses' continued cohabitation did not make the request contrary to good faith. It reversed the lower court's insistence decision (majority).

**Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2022/1205, K. 2023/1188, 29 November 2023**

The Krefeld divorce judgment became final on 16 January 2007, the Turkish recognition on 28 September 2015, and the matrimonial property claim was filed on 18 October 2017. Applying Article 59, the Assembly started the limitation period from finality abroad and upheld the dismissal on limitation grounds (majority; the dissent relies on the date the recognition became final).

**Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/1003, K. 2024/2481, 16 April 2024**

An uncontested divorce in Germany became final on 14 June 2013 and was recognised in Turkey. The Chamber held that under Articles 58 and 59 the parties were divorced from the date of finality abroad, and that since the foreign judgment contained no finding of fault, the husband could not later be found at fault and ordered to pay damages. It reversed the judgment.

**Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/3298, K. 2025/6806, 18 November 2025**

The first-instance court dismissed a payment claim as inadmissible because a German higher regional court had decided the same claim and its judgment had been recognised in Turkey with final effect; it relied on Articles 58 and 59 MÖHUK and Article 114(1)(i) HMK. The Chamber upheld the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### From when am I divorced in Turkey once my foreign divorce is recognised?

From the date the foreign judgment became final (Article 59 MÖHUK). The date of the Turkish recognition decision does not matter for this.

### Is there a time limit for recognising a foreign divorce in Turkey?

The law sets none. The Assembly of Civil Chambers has held that recognition can be requested at any time (E. 2022/766, K. 2023/975). Claims arising from the divorce have their own limitation periods, and when they start is disputed.

### Can I rely on an unrecognised foreign judgment before a Turkish court?

Not as res judicata or conclusive evidence. The court must first find that the judgment meets the conditions for enforcement (Article 58(1) MÖHUK).

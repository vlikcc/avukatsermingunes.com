Article 72 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) is the first provision on the organs of an association (dernek). Every association has three mandatory organs: the general assembly (genel kurul), the board (yönetim kurulu) and the audit committee (denetim kurulu). It may set up other organs, but it cannot transfer the duties, powers and responsibilities of the mandatory organs to them. The article concerns founders, those drafting a statute and anyone holding office in an association in Türkiye.

## Article 72 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **C. Organs**
>
> **I. General**
>
> Article 72 – The mandatory organs of an association are the general assembly, the board and the audit committee.
>
> Associations may set up other organs in addition to their mandatory organs. However, the duties, powers and responsibilities of the mandatory organs may not be transferred to these organs.

Original Turkish text:

> **C. Organlar**
>
> **I. Genel olarak**
>
> Madde 72- Derneğin zorunlu organları, genel kurul, yönetim kurulu ve denetim kuruludur.
>
> Dernekler zorunlu organları dışında başka organlar da oluşturabilirler. Ancak, bu organlara zorunlu organların görev, yetki ve sorumlulukları devredilemez.

As of November 2026, Article 72 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The reasoning says the Code deliberately does not list what other organs an association may have, so that associations can organise themselves freely; the duties, powers and responsibilities of the mandatory organs, however, cannot be transferred to those organs (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Organs in the statute

The Code sets minimum sizes: the board has at least five full and five substitute members (Article 84(1)), the audit committee at least three full and three substitute members (Article 86(1)). A statute may provide for larger bodies, not smaller ones. The Associations Act No. 5253 also requires the statute to state how and when the general assembly meets, its duties and powers, and the duties, powers, election procedure and number of full and substitute members of the board and audit committee (Article 4(d), (e) and (f) of Act No. 5253). These organs are first elected at the first general assembly (Article 62 TMK).

### Other organs and the ban on transfer

An advisory board, a disciplinary committee or an honour committee can be created by the statute. Article 72(2) sets the limit. The general assembly's powers are listed in Article 80: the final decision on admitting and expelling members, electing the organs, supervising the other organs and removing them for just cause. A statute cannot hand these powers to an additional body.

For auditing, the Associations Act says the same in so many words: an audit by the general assembly, the board or an independent audit firm does not remove the audit committee's obligation (Article 9(1) of Act No. 5253). An association that uses an external auditor still needs a working audit committee.

### If an organ is missing

An association acquires capacity to act once it has the required organs (Article 49). If the first general assembly is not held within the statutory period and the mandatory organs are not formed, the association ends automatically (Article 87(1), point 2). The same applies if forming the board under the statute becomes impossible, or if the ordinary general assembly cannot be held twice in a row (Article 87(1), points 4 and 5). Any interested person can ask the justice of the peace (sulh hâkimi) to establish that the association has ended automatically (Article 87(2)).

If vacancies bring the board below half its full membership, the general assembly must be called within one month. If nobody calls it, the justice of the peace, at the request of a member, appoints three members to call the meeting (Article 84(2)). Each branch (şube) must also have a general assembly, a board and an audit committee or an auditor (Article 95(1)).

### Notifying those elected, and who cannot hold office

Those elected to the organs are notified to the authorities. Article 23 of Act No. 5253 says this is done "within thirty days following the general assembly"; Article 17 of the Associations Regulation (Dernekler Yönetmeliği) says the general assembly results notice listing those elected is filed "within forty-five days" after the meeting. The two texts differ; check current practice with the provincial directorate for relations with civil society (il sivil toplumla ilişkiler müdürlüğü) responsible for the association.

A person convicted of terrorist financing offences, or under the Turkish Penal Code of manufacturing and trafficking narcotic or stimulant substances or of laundering proceeds of crime, may not serve on any organ of an association other than the general assembly (Article 3(3) of Act No. 5253, added by Act No. 7262). Because the provision leaves out the general assembly, such a person can still attend it as a member but cannot be elected to the board or audit committee; a conviction after election ends the office.

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

If you live in Germany or the Netherlands and want to sit on the board of a hometown or village association in Türkiye, note the following. Neither the association provisions of the TMK nor the Associations Act contain an express rule barring a Turkish citizen who lives abroad from holding office. The requirement of a right to settle in Türkiye (Article 93) applies only to foreigners. Check whether the statute adds conditions of its own. Board meetings may also be held online if the association chooses to (Article 15/A of the Associations Regulation). On the first general assembly, see the post on [Article 62 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-62-tmk-first-general-meeting-association); on capacity to act, see [Article 49 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-49-tmk-capacity-to-act-legal-persons).

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the mandatory organs of an association in Türkiye?

The general assembly, the board and the audit committee (Article 72(1) TMK). A branch may have an auditor instead of an audit committee (Article 95(1)).

### How many members does the board have?

At least five full and five substitute members; the audit committee at least three full and three substitute members (Articles 84(1) and 86(1)). The statute can provide for more.

### Does an association with an external auditor still need an audit committee?

Yes. An independent audit does not remove the audit committee's obligation (Article 9(1) of Act No. 5253).

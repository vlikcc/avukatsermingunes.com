Joint custody (ortak velayet) after divorce is not provided for in the Turkish Civil Code (TMK). Under the Code, custody on divorce belongs to the parent the child is entrusted to (Article 336(3) TMK). Since 2017, however, the 2nd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has held that joint custody is not manifestly contrary to Turkish public policy. Several judgments also show Turkish family courts ordering joint custody themselves.

This article covers the statute, the case law, and how a German joint custody order (gemeinsames Sorgerecht) is recognised in Turkey. For the general custody criteria, see [child custody after divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey).

## What does Turkish law say about joint custody?

Article 336 TMK covers three situations. While the marriage lasts, both parents exercise custody together (para. 1). If the parents have stopped living together or are legally separated, the judge may give custody to one of them (para. 2). On divorce, custody belongs to the parent the child is entrusted to (para. 3).

The divorce court settles the parents' rights and their personal relations with the child (Article 182(1) TMK). Paragraph 3 of the same article refers to "the spouse who has not been given custody": contact with that parent is arranged, and he or she contributes to the child's costs according to their means. The Code is therefore built around sole custody. It does not expressly prohibit joint custody either.

In an uncontested divorce the spouses submit an arrangement for the children. The judge must approve it and may change it in the children's interest (Article 166(3) TMK). In one of the cases below, joint custody was created in exactly this way.

As of November 2026, Article 336 TMK does not appear in the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr; it has applied unchanged since 1 January 2002. The latest law in the table is Law No. 7589, which only amends Articles 440 and 444 (in force since 31 October 2026). There is talk of a new judicial reform package with family law changes, but as of 21 November 2026 no law on joint custody has been published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete).

## How has the case law developed?

### Before 2017: "no joint custody in Turkish law"

In a case where a foreign divorce judgment had kept joint custody in place, the 2nd Civil Chamber held that parents cannot exercise custody together after divorce, and that Article 336(3) TMK makes it a legal necessity to give custody to one parent (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2015/2140, K. 2015/1932, 16.02.2015**).

A year later, in a case where the foreign judgment said nothing about custody, the Chamber wrote: "Türk Hukukunda müşterek velayet müessesesi bulunmamaktadır" (Turkish law has no institution of joint custody). It treated the claim as a request to settle custody (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2016/18398, K. 2016/15709, 08.12.2016**).

### Since 2017: not manifestly contrary to public policy

The turning point was a case brought by two British nationals who asked for joint custody of their child born outside marriage. The first-instance court dismissed it on the ground that joint custody violates Turkish public policy. The Court of Cassation reversed (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2016/15771, K. 2017/1737, 20.02.2017**). It relied on Article 5 of Protocol No. 7 to the European Convention on Human Rights, whose ratification Turkey approved by Law No. 6684 (Official Gazette, 25.03.2016). Under that article, spouses have equal rights and responsibilities of a private law character between them and in their relations with their children, during marriage and on its dissolution; states may still take measures in the interests of the children.

Reading this together with the last paragraph of Article 90 of the Constitution and Article 5 MÖHUK (Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and Procedure), the Chamber found that foreign law is set aside only where it is "manifestly" contrary to public policy, and that joint custody is not. The court should have applied the parties' common national law (Article 17(1) MÖHUK).

In the same year the Chamber dealt with two Turkish citizens divorced in Sweden. The family court had recognised the divorce but refused to recognise the joint custody part. The Court of Cassation held that the custody part must also be recognised, since the other conditions of Article 58 MÖHUK were met and the parents did not dispute joint custody (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2016/18674, K. 2017/13800, 04.12.2017**).

Both judgments concerned foreign law or a foreign judgment. They set no test for a divorce governed by Turkish law.

### Divorces under Turkish law

Here the picture is less clear. In one case the first-instance court ordered joint custody, with the child living with the mother; the regional appeal court found joint custody to be wrong and gave custody to the father. The Court of Cassation set that decision aside because no expert report had been obtained and the child had not been heard where appropriate. It did not rule on whether joint custody is permissible (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2021/8413, K. 2021/9546, 14.12.2021**). Another judgment records that the parents divorced by agreement under Article 166(3) TMK and that joint custody was ordered for the children (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2022/1902, K. 2022/2470, 15.03.2022**).

This point is contested. No statutory provision says whether joint custody can be ordered in a divorce governed by Turkish law, and no binding judgment (such as a decision unifying case law) answers the question in general terms. Academic opinions are not covered in this article. It is not certain which view a court will take in a particular case.

## Where does the child live, and is child support paid?

Because the law does not define what joint custody involves, the answers are found in the judgment or the divorce protocol. A judgment of the Court of Cassation's General Assembly of Civil Chambers describes the first-instance ruling in that case: joint custody, the children living with the mother, contact with the father, and child support (iştirak nafakası) payable by the father (**Yargıtay HGK, E. 2022/146, K. 2023/242, 22.03.2023**; the dispute before the General Assembly concerned fault and compensation). In case 2022/1902, too, the children lived with the mother and contact with the father had been arranged. Joint custody does not necessarily mean the child spends equal time in two homes.

## Can joint custody be ended later?

Yes. If new facts such as one parent remarrying or moving require it, the judge takes the necessary measures (Article 183 TMK). Removing custody and transferring it from one parent to the other are non-contentious proceedings (çekişmesiz yargı; Article 382(2)(b)(13) HMK, the Code of Civil Procedure). Regional appeal court decisions in such matters cannot be appealed to the Court of Cassation (Article 362(1)(ç) HMK). On that basis the Court of Cassation rejected an appeal seeking to end joint custody and leave custody with the mother (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2026/5739, K. 2026/8199, 16.09.2026**). In these cases the regional appeal court has the final say.

## Is a German joint custody order recognised in Turkey?

On German law, only the general framework: where parents with joint parental responsibility live apart, either parent may ask the family court to transfer all or part of it to him or her alone (§ 1671(1) BGB). Under joint responsibility, matters of considerable importance need both parents' agreement, while the parent the child lives with decides everyday matters (§ 1687(1) BGB). A German divorce judgment may therefore contain no custody ruling at all. For questions of German law, please consult a lawyer admitted in Germany or the youth welfare office (Jugendamt).

In Turkey, a foreign custody judgment takes effect as res judicata only once a court has found that it meets the conditions for enforcement (tenfiz), a process called recognition (tanıma; Article 58(1) MÖHUK). One condition is that the judgment is not manifestly contrary to public policy (Article 54(1)(c) MÖHUK). Judgment 2017/13800 means joint custody does not fail on that ground. For the procedure, see [Article 58 MÖHUK on recognition](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-mohuk-recognition-foreign-judgments-turkey).

If the German judgment contains no custody ruling, there is no custody part to recognise. In judgment 2016/15709 the Court of Cassation treated such a case as a claim to settle custody, but that was before the 2017 shift. How a Turkish court would decide today is uncertain.

Joint custody also matters in child abduction cases. A foreign court had ordered joint custody and given the right to determine the child's residence to the mother alone; the mother then moved to Turkey with the child. The Court of Cassation found no wrongful removal or retention in the circumstances of that case (**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2018/7114, K. 2018/13831, 29.11.2018**). See also [the Hague Convention and child abduction to Turkey](/en/publications/hague-convention-child-abduction-turkey).

## The author's assessment

This section is neither statute nor settled case law; it is the author's assessment. On the judgments cited here, joint custody stands on the firmest ground where the parents agree; judgment 2017/13800 also stresses that the parents did not dispute it. If you are seeking joint custody, the protocol should state clearly where the child will live, how contact with the other parent works, who pays support, and which decisions (school, health, travel abroad) are taken jointly. For other topics, see [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is joint custody possible in Turkey after divorce?

Not under the Code: on divorce, custody belongs to the parent the child is entrusted to (Article 336(3) TMK). The Court of Cassation, however, does not regard joint custody as manifestly contrary to public policy (2nd Civil Chamber, K. 2017/1737 and K. 2017/13800).

### What are the requirements for joint custody?

The law sets none. As with any custody decision, the test is the child's best interests (Articles 182 and 339 TMK). In an uncontested divorce the judge must approve the arrangement for the children (Article 166(3) TMK).

### Do you pay child support with joint custody?

There is no specific statutory rule. In the two examples cited above, the parent the child did not live with paid child support. The actual arrangement is set out in the judgment or protocol.

### Is German joint custody valid in Turkey?

To take effect as a final judgment it needs a recognition decision (Article 58 MÖHUK). The Court of Cassation has held that the custody part of a foreign judgment ordering joint custody can be recognised (2nd Civil Chamber, K. 2017/13800).

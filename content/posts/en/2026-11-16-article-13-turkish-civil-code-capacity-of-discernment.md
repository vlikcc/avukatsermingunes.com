Article 13 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) defines capacity of discernment (ayırt etme gücü). Everyone has it unless young age, mental illness, mental weakness, intoxication or a similar cause deprives them of the ability to act rationally. The article often comes up when a will or a property transfer made by an elderly parent in Turkey is challenged after the parent's death. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 13 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **d. Capacity of discernment**
>
> Article 13 – Every person who is not deprived of the ability to act rationally by reason of young age, mental illness, mental weakness, intoxication or similar causes has capacity of discernment within the meaning of this Code.

Original Turkish text:

> **d. Ayırt etme gücü**
>
> Madde 13- Yaşının küçüklüğü yüzünden veya akıl hastalığı, akıl zayıflığı, sarhoşluk ya da bunlara benzer sebeplerden biriyle akla uygun biçimde davranma yeteneğinden yoksun olmayan herkes, bu Kanuna göre ayırt etme gücüne sahiptir.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 13; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 13 of the former Code. Only the heading was modernised ("temyiz kudreti" became "ayırt etme gücü"); the rule itself was not changed (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

The Code does not define capacity of discernment positively. It lists the causes that exclude it. If none of them applies, the person is treated as having capacity, so the burden of proof lies with whoever claims the opposite.

There is no fixed age. Capacity is assessed separately for each transaction and each moment. The same person may understand an everyday purchase but not the consequences of selling land. Saying "my mother was 85" is therefore not enough on its own; what matters is whether she understood the transaction on the day she made it.

Capacity of discernment matters in many places:

- Full capacity to act requires capacity of discernment, majority and not being under legal interdiction (Article 10 TMK).
- Acts of a person lacking capacity of discernment have no legal effect, except where the law provides otherwise (Article 15 TMK).
- To make a will you must have capacity of discernment and be at least 15 (Article 502 TMK).
- A person without capacity of discernment cannot marry (Article 125 TMK). If a spouse permanently lacked capacity at the time of the marriage, the marriage is absolutely void (Article 145(2) TMK).

Lack of capacity of discernment and legal interdiction (kısıtlılık) are different things. Interdiction on grounds of mental illness requires a separate court decision (Article 405 TMK), which may only be made on the basis of an official medical board report (Article 409(2) TMK). A person who has never been placed under interdiction may still be found to have lacked capacity for a particular transaction.

## Practical points if you live abroad

A common scenario: a parent in Turkey, late in an illness, transfers a property to one child or makes a will, and the siblings living abroad only find out after the death. Two cases must be kept apart. A disposition upon death, such as a will, can be challenged with an action for annulment (iptal davası) if the testator lacked capacity to dispose when making it (Article 557(1) TMK). The time limits are in Article 559 TMK: one year from the date the claimant learned of the disposition, the ground for annulment and their own entitlement, and in any case ten years (against defendants in good faith) or twenty years (against defendants in bad faith) from the opening of the will, or from the passing of the estate for other dispositions. A transfer of property during the parent's lifetime is not a disposition upon death. If it was made without capacity of discernment, it has no legal effect under Article 15 TMK; this is pursued with a claim for cancellation of the land registry entry and re-registration (tapu iptali ve tescil).

In both cases, capacity is assessed as at the date of the transaction. Hospital records, discharge reports (epikriz) and records of medication from that period are therefore important.

The capacity to act of a Turkish citizen is governed by Turkish law as the law of nationality (Article 9(1) MÖHUK). How the same person's capacity is assessed for a transaction in Germany or the Netherlands depends on that country's rules; for that question you should consult a lawyer admitted there.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does capacity of discernment mean in Turkish law?

The ability to act rationally. Everyone has it unless young age, mental illness, mental weakness, intoxication or a similar cause takes it away (Article 13 TMK).

### Who has to prove lack of capacity?

The law presumes capacity. The party claiming that a transaction is invalid on this ground must prove that the person lacked capacity at the time of the transaction (Articles 6 and 13 TMK).

### Is a property transfer by a mother with Alzheimer's disease invalid?

The diagnosis alone does not decide it. The question is whether she could understand the meaning of the transfer on the day it was made. If she could not, the transaction in principle has no legal effect (Article 15 TMK).

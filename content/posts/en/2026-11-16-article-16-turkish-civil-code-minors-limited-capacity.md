Article 16 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) governs persons with limited capacity: minors and persons under interdiction (kısıtlılar) who have capacity of discernment. Without their legal representative's consent they cannot take on obligations through their own transactions; for gratuitous benefits and strictly personal rights no consent is needed. They are liable for their own torts. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 16 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **3. Minors and persons under interdiction who have capacity of discernment**
>
> Article 16 – Minors and persons under interdiction who have capacity of discernment cannot take on obligations through their own transactions without the consent of their legal representative. This consent is not required for gratuitous acquisitions or for the exercise of strictly personal rights.
>
> Minors and persons under interdiction who have capacity of discernment are liable for their torts.

Original Turkish text:

> **3. Ayırt etme gücüne sahip küçükler ve kısıtlılar**
>
> Madde 16- Ayırt etme gücüne sahip küçükler ve kısıtlılar, yasal temsilcilerinin rızası olmadıkça, kendi işlemleriyle borç altına giremezler. Karşılıksız kazanmada ve kişiye sıkı sıkıya bağlı hakları kullanmada bu rıza gerekli değildir.
>
> Ayırt etme gücüne sahip küçükler ve kısıtlılar haksız fiillerinden sorumludurlar.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 16; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 16 of the former Code and regulates the capacity of persons who have discernment but are minors or under interdiction. Only the terminology changed: "yasal temsilci" for "kanunî mümessil", "karşılıksız kazanma" for "ivazsız iktisap", and "kişiye sıkı sıkıya bağlı haklar" for "münhasıran şahsa merbut haklar" (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Transactions requiring consent

A person with limited capacity who wants to take on an obligation through their own transaction needs the consent of the legal representative, that is the parents or the guardian. For persons under guardianship, Article 451 TMK describes the form: express or implied permission of the guardian, or later approval. If the transaction is not approved within a reasonable period set by the other party, or by the judge at that party's request, the other party is released. Where a transaction is not approved, each side may reclaim what it gave; the person under guardianship, however, is liable only for what was spent for their benefit or is still in their assets when it is reclaimed, or for what they disposed of in bad faith (Article 452 TMK). A child under parental custody has the same capacity as a person under guardianship (Article 343 TMK).

### Transactions without consent

No consent is needed for:

- **gratuitous acquisitions**, meaning transactions that bring only benefits, such as accepting an unconditional gift;
- **the exercise of strictly personal rights**, such as making a will, which requires capacity of discernment and being at least 15 (Article 502 TMK).

For some of these rights the law still requires permission: minors and persons under interdiction cannot marry without their legal representative's permission (Articles 126 and 127 TMK).

Other provisions widen the scope. A child manages property the parents gave them for a profession or trade, and their own earnings (Article 359 TMK). If the guardianship authority has allowed a person under guardianship to carry on a profession or trade, that person may enter into the ordinary transactions it involves and is liable for them with all their assets (Article 453 TMK).

### Liability for torts

The second paragraph is clear: minors and persons under interdiction who have discernment are liable for their own torts, regardless of any consent. In addition, the head of the household is liable for damage caused by a minor or a person under interdiction living in the household, unless they prove they exercised the required care (Article 369 TMK).

## Practical points if you live abroad

The capacity of a young Turkish citizen is governed by Turkish law (Article 9(1) MÖHUK). Under Article 9(2) MÖHUK, however, a person who lacks capacity under their national law but has it under the law of the country where the transaction is made is bound by that transaction; transactions in family and inheritance law, and rights in rem over immovable property in another country, are excluded. This matters if, for example, a Turkish court has to decide on the validity of a contract concluded in Germany.

The law applicable to obligations arising from torts is determined separately; as a rule it is the law of the country where the tort was committed (Article 34(1) MÖHUK). Damage caused by a young person living abroad while on holiday in Turkey is assessed under this rule.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a minor sign a contract alone in Turkey?

If the minor has capacity of discernment, they can take on obligations with their legal representative's consent. No consent is needed for gratuitous acquisitions or strictly personal rights (Article 16 TMK).

### Who is liable for damage caused by a minor?

A minor with capacity of discernment is personally liable (Article 16(2) TMK). The liability of the head of the household is regulated separately in Article 369 TMK.

### Can a person under interdiction make a will?

Making a will is a strictly personal right. Article 502 TMK requires capacity of discernment and being at least 15, but not freedom from interdiction. To conclude an inheritance contract, by contrast, a person must not be under interdiction (Article 503 TMK).

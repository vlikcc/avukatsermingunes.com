Article 36 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which law governs the non-contractual liability for damage caused by a manufactured product. The injured person chooses the law from a limited set of options. The provision matters to you if a product bought in Turkey and taken to Germany or the Netherlands, or a product made by a Turkish manufacturer, has caused you damage.

## Article 36 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Non-contractual liability of the manufacturer**
>
> Article 36 – (1) Liability for damage caused by manufactured goods shall be governed, at the choice of the injured person, by the law of the habitual residence or place of business of the person causing the damage, or by the law of the country where the manufactured goods were acquired. The law of the place of acquisition may only be applied if the person causing the damage has failed to prove that the product was brought into that country without their consent.

Turkish original:

> **İmalâtçının sözleşme dışı sorumluluğu**
>
> MADDE 36 – (1) İmal edilen şeylerin sebep olduğu zarardan doğan sorumluluğa, zarar görenin seçimine göre, zarar verenin mutad meskeni veya işyeri hukuku ya da imal edilen şeyin iktisap edildiği ülke hukuku uygulanır. İktisap yeri hukukunun uygulanabilmesi için zarar verenin, mamulün o ülkeye rızası dışında sokulduğunu ispat edememiş olması gerekir.

The provision has not changed since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the current text in the Ministry of Justice’s UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note for this article.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the explanatory memorandum, the former Law No. 2675 had no specific rule, so such disputes were decided under the general tort rule, which did not protect the interests involved well enough. The new provision gives the injured person a limited choice of law. The manufacturer is already closely connected with the law of its habitual residence or place of business. It may have no connection at all with the country where the product was acquired, so that law does not apply if the manufacturer proves that the product reached that country without its consent. (Source: explanatory memorandum to the bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly, Article 35 of the bill.)

## Explanation

The provision covers the manufacturer’s non-contractual liability: there is usually no contract between the injured person and the manufacturer, because the product was bought from a dealer. Claims against the seller for defects are governed by the law applicable to the contract (Articles 24 et seq. MÖHUK; for consumer contracts, Article 26).

The injured person chooses between

- the law of the country where the person causing the damage has its habitual residence or place of business, and
- the law of the country where the product was acquired.

The second option has a limit. If the manufacturer proves that the product was brought into that country without its consent, the law of the place of acquisition does not apply. The burden of proof lies with the manufacturer, not with the injured person. The substantive rules of the chosen law apply, not its conflict-of-laws rules (Article 2(4) MÖHUK).

The law does not say when or how the choice must be made, or what happens if the injured person makes no choice. This point is disputed; there is no express provision and I have not found a verified, binding decision of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) on it. In my view the safest course is to state the choice expressly in the statement of claim.

## Practical points for people living abroad

If an appliance you bought in Turkey caused damage in your home in Germany and the manufacturer is based in Turkey, both options before a Turkish court lead to Turkish law: the manufacturer’s place of business and the place of acquisition are both in Turkey. If you bought the product in Germany, you can choose German law only if the manufacturer cannot prove that the product reached Germany without its consent.

If Turkish law applies, Article 6 of Law No. 7223 on Product Safety and Technical Regulations is the key provision. If a product causes damage to a person or to property, the manufacturer or importer must make good the damage. The injured person must prove the damage and the causal link between the non-conformity (uygunsuzluk) and the damage. Several responsible manufacturers or importers are jointly and severally liable, and contract terms excluding or limiting their liability are void. The amount of pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages is determined under the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098. The limitation period is three years from the date the injured person learns of the damage and the person liable, and in any case ten years from the date the damage occurred (Article 6(6) of Law No. 7223). Liability rules in other laws remain unaffected (Article 6(7)). As of November 2026, the text of Law No. 7223 in the UYAP Mevzuat system has no amendment note for Article 6.

The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on venue (Article 40 MÖHUK); in tort cases the court of the place where the act was committed, where the damage occurred or is likely to occur, and of the injured person’s domicile also has jurisdiction (Article 16 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK). If you sue in Germany or the Netherlands, that court applies its own rules; for the law of that country, consult a lawyer qualified there.

For your rights against the seller see [consumer law](/en/practice-areas/consumer-law); for damages see [compensation law](/en/practice-areas/compensation-law). The general tort rule: [Article 34 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-34-mohuk-tort-accident-turkey).

## Frequently asked questions

### A product I bought in Turkey caused damage in Germany. Which law applies?

Before a Turkish court you choose between the law of the manufacturer’s place of business and the law of the country of acquisition (Article 36 MÖHUK). If you bought it in Turkey and the manufacturer is there too, both lead to Turkish law.

### When does a claim against the manufacturer become time-barred under Turkish law?

Under Article 6(6) of Law No. 7223, three years from learning of the damage and the person liable, and at most ten years from the date the damage occurred.

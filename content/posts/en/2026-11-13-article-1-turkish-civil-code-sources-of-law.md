Article 1 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) tells a Turkish court which sources to use and in what order: first the statute; if it has no applicable provision, customary law; and if there is none, the rule the judge would make as legislator. The article matters not only in family and inheritance cases but in every private law dispute governed by Turkish law. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and under Article 1029 entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 1 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **A. Application of law and sources of law**
>
> Article 1 – The law applies to all matters that it addresses by its wording and its spirit.
>
> Where the law contains no applicable provision, the judge decides according to customary law and, where there is none, according to the rule he would make if he were the legislator.
>
> In reaching a decision, the judge draws on legal scholarship and court decisions.

Original Turkish text:

> **A. Hukukun uygulanması ve kaynakları**
>
> Madde 1 - Kanun, sözüyle ve özüyle değindiği bütün konularda uygulanır.
>
> Kanunda uygulanabilir bir hüküm yoksa, hâkim, örf ve âdet hukukuna göre, bu da yoksa kendisi kanun koyucu olsaydı nasıl bir kural koyacak idiyse ona göre karar verir.
>
> Hâkim, karar verirken bilimsel görüşlerden ve yargı kararlarından yararlanır.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system (recorded 31 July 2026) does not list Article 1; the last law in the table is Law No. 7589, which amended Articles 440 and 444 (those changes in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 1 of the former Civil Code No. 743; its content was kept and its language simplified. "Lâfziyle veya ruhiyle" became "sözüyle ve özüyle" (by its wording and spirit), and the judge is to apply the law by looking at wording and spirit together, not at only one of them. The old heading ("Application of the Civil Code") was changed because the sources listed apply not only to the Civil Code but to other branches of private law as well (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Statute first, but not just the words

Paragraph 1 sets the standard of interpretation. The court first looks for a provision that fits the case and applies it according to its wording and purpose. The "wording and spirit" standard comes in where the words seem to cover a case but the purpose addresses a different situation, or where the words are too narrow but the purpose clearly covers the case.

### When the statute is silent

Paragraph 2 sets the order where there is no applicable provision: customary law (örf ve âdet hukuku) first, then the rule the judge would make as legislator. That rule is meant not as a one-off answer for the case at hand but as a general rule that would also fit similar cases.

Courts turn to this paragraph where the legislator has left a question open. When a statute is amended, details such as which reference date applies are not always written into it. The Court of Cassation decision below is a recent example.

### How it works with the other introductory articles

Article 1 is read together with the other provisions in the introductory part of the Code. Under Article 4, where the law gives the judge discretion or tells him to consider the circumstances or good cause, he decides according to law and equity. Under Article 5, the general provisions of the Civil Code and the Code of Obligations apply, where suitable, to all private law relationships. Article 1(3) lists scholarship and court decisions as sources the judge "draws on".

## Practical points if you live abroad

**First, which country's law applies.** If a case has a foreign element (a party is a foreign national, lives abroad, or the events took place in another country), the applicable law is determined by Turkey's private international law act No. 5718 (MÖHUK) (Article 1(1) MÖHUK). The judge applies the Turkish conflict-of-law rules and the foreign law they designate of his own motion (Article 2(1) MÖHUK). The order in Article 1 TMK matters where Turkish law turns out to apply. If the content of the foreign law cannot be established despite all inquiries, Turkish law also applies (Article 2(2) MÖHUK). More on this: [Article 2 MÖHUK – Foreign Law in Turkish Courts](/en/publications/legislation/article-2-mohuk-foreign-law-turkish-courts).

**Reference date for market value in pre-emption cases.** If you own a share in property in Turkey through inheritance or purchase, the decision below may concern you directly. The Constitutional Court has annulled Article 734(2) and Provisional Article 1(2) TMK, which the decision applied; according to the text in the UYAP Mevzuat system, the annulment takes effect on 6 August 2027 (E. 2026/23, K. 2026/162). Until then, the provisions continue to apply. Details: [Pre-emption right in Turkey: market value rule annulled](/en/publications/pre-emption-right-turkey-shared-property).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Court of Cassation, 7th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/3449, K. 2026/3685, 7 September 2026**

The pre-emption claim (önalım davası) had been filed before Law No. 7571 entered into force on 25 December 2025. Under the amended Article 734(2), the court sets the market value of the share, and Provisional Article 1(2) extends this rule to pending cases. The law did not say as of which date the market value is to be set. Referring to Articles 1 and 4 TMK, the Chamber filled this gap: for claims filed after the amendment, the filing date counts; for cases pending when it took effect, 25 December 2025 counts. Because the first-instance court had used the sale price shown in the land register, the judgment was reversed (unanimous).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does Article 1 of the Turkish Civil Code regulate?

How the judge applies the law (by its wording and spirit) and which sources he uses, in what order, where the law has no provision: customary law, then the rule he would make as legislator.

### Are Court of Cassation decisions binding on the judge?

Article 1(3) TMK lists court decisions among the sources the judge "draws on". The article does not describe them as binding.

### Does Article 1 TMK apply in cases with a foreign element?

With a foreign element, the applicable law is first determined under MÖHUK. Article 1 TMK matters where Turkish law applies.

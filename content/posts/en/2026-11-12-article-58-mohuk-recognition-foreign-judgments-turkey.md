Article 58 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) governs the recognition (tanıma) of foreign court decisions in Turkey. Once recognised, a foreign judgment counts in Turkey as res judicata (kesin hüküm) or as conclusive evidence (kesin delil). The court checks that the judgment meets the conditions for enforcement (tenfiz), but it does not require reciprocity. If you divorced in Germany or the Netherlands and want the divorce entered in the Turkish civil registry, this provision applies to you, together with the administrative route next to it: Article 27/A of the Civil Registry Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu).

## Article 58 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Recognition**
>
> Article 58 – (1) Whether a foreign court judgment can be accepted as conclusive evidence or as res judicata depends on the court establishing that the foreign judgment meets the conditions for enforcement. Article 54(1)(a) does not apply to recognition.
>
> (2) The recognition of decisions in non-contentious proceedings is subject to the same rule.
>
> (3) The same procedure applies where an administrative act is to be carried out in Turkey on the basis of a foreign court judgment.

Turkish original:

> **Tanıma**
>
> MADDE 58 – (1) Yabancı mahkeme ilâmının kesin delil veya kesin hüküm olarak kabul edilebilmesi yabancı ilâmın tenfiz şartlarını taşıdığının mahkemece tespitine bağlıdır. Tanımada 54 üncü maddenin birinci fıkrasının (a) bendi uygulanmaz.
>
> (2) İhtilâfsız kaza kararlarının tanınması da aynı hükme tâbidir.
>
> (3) Yabancı mahkeme ilâmına dayanılarak Türkiye'de idarî bir işlemin yapılmasında da aynı usul uygulanır.

The article has not changed since the Law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

The article partly corresponds to Article 42 of the former Law No. 2675. The old text also excluded the right-to-be-heard condition from recognition. According to the reasoning, that reference was removed because it could be read as leaving unchecked whether the person against whom recognition is sought had been able to defend themselves (Source: article-by-article reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly, Article 57 of the bill).

## Explanation

### Recognition and enforcement

Enforcement is needed to execute a foreign judgment in Turkey (Article 50 MÖHUK). Recognition lets the judgment take effect without execution: it binds as res judicata or serves as conclusive evidence in other proceedings. A divorce judgment ends the marriage and there is nothing to execute, so recognition is usually enough for a divorce to have effect in Turkey. The Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has treated a decision on a foreign divorce judgment with no enforceable order as recognition under Article 58 (see below).

### Which conditions apply

Because the Law refers to the conditions for enforcement, recognition also requires a final judgment in a civil matter (Article 50(1)) and the conditions of Article 54: no exclusive jurisdiction of the Turkish courts (b), no clear breach of Turkish public policy (c) and respect for the right to be heard (ç). Only reciprocity is left out (Article 54(1)(a)). See [Article 54 MÖHUK – Conditions for Enforcement](/en/publications/legislation/article-54-mohuk-enforcement-conditions-turkey) for the details.

The procedure follows the enforcement rules too: the competent court (Article 51), the documents to attach (Article 53) and service on the other party (Article 55). The documents are covered in [Article 53 MÖHUK – Documents, Finality and Apostille](/en/publications/legislation/article-53-mohuk-documents-apostille-turkey).

### Non-contentious decisions

Paragraph 2 covers decisions that do not resolve a dispute but are made on application (ihtilâfsız kaza). The obstacles in Article 54 apply to them as well. For example, the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation (Hukuk Genel Kurulu) upheld the refusal to recognise a decision of a Leverkusen court appointing a guardian (vasi) for a Turkish national, because Turkish courts have exclusive jurisdiction in guardianship matters (reference below).

### Administrative acts and the civil registry

Under paragraph 3 the same procedure applies when a Turkish authority is asked to act on a foreign judgment. Article 60(1) of the Civil Registry Services Implementing Regulation (Nüfus Hizmetleri Uygulama Yönetmeliği) repeats this for the registry: foreign court decisions are processed only after recognition or enforcement by the competent Turkish court.

For divorces, Article 27/A of Law No. 5490 (added by Decree-Law No. 690 of 17 April 2017, confirmed by Law No. 7077) provides a route without a court. Decisions of foreign courts or authorities on divorce, nullity, annulment or the existence of a marriage are entered in the civil registry if

- the spouses apply together, in person or through representatives; if one spouse has died or is a foreign national, the Turkish spouse or their representative may apply alone (possible since 26 March 2020, added by Law No. 7226),
- the decision was given by an authority competent under the law of the state where it was issued and is final there,
- it is not clearly contrary to Turkish public policy.

Abroad, the entry is made by the Turkish mission in the country where the decision was given; in Turkey, by the registry offices designated by the Ministry of the Interior (Article 27/A(2)). If the application is refused because these conditions are not met, recognition takes place under Law No. 5718 (Article 27/A(3)). The Assembly of Civil Chambers has confirmed that, where the conditions of Article 27/A are met, registration can be requested without a recognition action (E. 2022/1205, K. 2023/1188, 29.11.2023).

The procedure is set out in the Regulation on the Registration of Decisions of Foreign Judicial or Administrative Authorities in the Civil Registry (Official Gazette of 7 February 2018, No. 30325, amended on 30 August 2020). The rules you are most likely to meet:

- The spouses need not apply at the same time; separate applications may be no more than ninety days apart (Article 6(1)).
- A commission examines the application. If the decision also contains orders on custody, child maintenance, contact, matrimonial property or damages, the commission decides only on the divorce; for those orders an action may be brought under Law No. 5718 (Article 9(5)).
- If proceedings on the same decision are pending in Turkey or have already been dismissed, the application is refused (Article 9(6)).
- If the commission refuses registration, an action for recognition or enforcement may be brought before the family court (Article 11).

## Practical points if you live abroad

**If both spouses are Turkish and the other spouse will not take part,** the administrative route is closed and a recognition action in Turkey is the way forward. No provision requires you to apply to the consulate before going to court. In a case concerning a divorce judgment from Neuss, the defendant argued that the claimant should first have applied to the registry office. The regional court of appeal rejected this because the regulation requires a joint application, and the Court of Cassation upheld that decision (2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1831, K. 2023/2697).

**The mission in the country of the decision is the right place.** The Law assigns registration abroad to the mission “in the country where the decision was given”. A German divorce is handled by a Turkish mission in Germany, a Dutch divorce by one in the Netherlands. If you have no residence in Turkey, you can apply there to one of the provincial directorates listed in Article 5 of the regulation.

**Maintenance and custody are separate.** Registration only records that the marriage has ended. To enforce a German or Dutch maintenance order in Turkey, you need enforcement.

General information is on the [Private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law) page.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2010/17159, K. 2011/6541, 12.04.2011**

The Chamber upheld the decision, holding that the foreign judgment concerned a divorce and contained no order requiring execution, that the court’s decision was recognition within Article 58 of Law No. 5718, and that reciprocity is not required for recognition.

**Court of Cassation, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1831, K. 2023/2697, 25.05.2023**

A divorce judgment from Neuss had been recognised. The defendant appealed only on costs, arguing that there was no legal interest in suing while an administrative route existed. The regional court of appeal dismissed the appeal because the regulation requires both parties to apply to the registry office together; the Court of Cassation upheld it.

**Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2009/2-557, K. 2009/527, 18.11.2009**

Recognition was sought of a Leverkusen decision appointing a guardian (vasi) for a Turkish national. The 2nd Civil Chamber had reversed the dismissal, noting that non-contentious decisions can be recognised under Article 58(2). The Assembly nevertheless upheld the dismissal: Turkish courts have exclusive international jurisdiction in guardianship matters. Its reasoning relies largely on the former Law No. 2675.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is reciprocity required for recognition in Turkey?

No. Article 58(1) MÖHUK expressly excludes Article 54(1)(a) for recognition. Reciprocity is only required for enforcement.

### Do I need a court case to register my foreign divorce in Turkey?

Not necessarily. If both spouses apply, or if the other spouse has died or is a foreign national, the divorce can be registered under Article 27/A of Law No. 5490 through the consulate or a registry office. If that is not possible or the application is refused, a recognition action before the family court follows.

Article 40 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) governs the international jurisdiction (milletlerarası yetki) of Turkish courts: whether a court in Turkey may hear a case with a foreign element. The test is whether a Turkish court would have territorial jurisdiction under the domestic rules. You will meet this article if you want to sue someone in Turkey while living in Germany or the Netherlands, have a claim against someone living in Turkey, or are being sued in Turkey yourself.

## Article 40 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **International jurisdiction**
>
> Article 40 – (1) The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts is determined by the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction.

Turkish original:

> **Milletlerarası yetki**
>
> MADDE 40 – (1) Türk mahkemelerinin milletlerarası yetkisini, iç hukukun yer itibariyle yetki kuralları tayin eder.

The article has not changed since the law came into force on 12 December 2007; as of November 2026 the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning of the government bill, the article reproduces Article 27 of the former Law No. 2675 without change. (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 39 of the bill.)

## Explanation

Article 40 does not list any grounds of jurisdiction itself. It refers to the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (HMK) and to special statutes. The court asks: if this case had no foreign element, which court in Turkey would be competent? If there is one, Turkish courts also have international jurisdiction.

In cases with a foreign element, these HMK rules come up most often:

- the defendant’s domicile (yerleşim yeri) when the action is filed (Article 6),
- the defendant’s habitual residence (mutad mesken) in Turkey if they have no domicile there; and, for claims concerning property rights, the place where the disputed asset is located (Article 9),
- the place where the contract is to be performed (Article 10),
- for rights in rem over immovable property, the place where the property lies, as mandatory jurisdiction (kesin yetki) (Article 12),
- the place where the tort was committed or the damage occurred, or the injured party’s domicile (Article 16).

Article 5 HMK preserves jurisdiction rules in other statutes. So Article 6 of the Labour Courts Law No. 7036 for employment cases, Article 73(5) of the Consumer Protection Law No. 6502 for consumer cases and Article 168 of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK) for divorce can also give Turkish courts international jurisdiction. Under Article 168 TMK, divorce proceedings belong to the court of either spouse’s domicile or of the place where the spouses last lived together for six months before the action. As of November 2026, the UYAP Mevzuat amendment tables show no change in the last two years to Articles 5–19 HMK, Article 6 of Law No. 7036, Article 73 of Law No. 6502 or Article 168 TMK.

### How it relates to Articles 41–47

Article 40 is the general rule; Articles 41–46 add to it in specific areas. For personal status cases of Turkish citizens that have not been or cannot be brought abroad, where no Turkish court has territorial jurisdiction, Article 41 points in turn to the place in Turkey where the person resides, their last domicile in Turkey, and otherwise to the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir. Articles 42 and 43 fill gaps for certain orders concerning foreigners without a domicile in Turkey and for inheritance disputes. Articles 44–46 protect the weaker party in employment, consumer and insurance disputes, and the parties cannot contract out of these courts’ jurisdiction (Article 47(2)).

Under Article 47(1), parties to a dispute with a foreign element arising from obligations, where territorial jurisdiction is not exclusive, may agree that a foreign court will hear it. The agreement must be proven by written evidence. If there is one, the case goes ahead in Turkey only if the foreign court declines jurisdiction or the defendant does not object to jurisdiction (yetki itirazı) before the Turkish court. The restriction in Article 17 HMK to merchants and public legal entities does not appear in the wording of Article 47.

### Objecting to jurisdiction

Where jurisdiction is mandatory, the court checks it of its own motion. In other cases the defendant must object in the statement of defence and name the competent court; if the objection is not made in time and in proper form, the court where the case was filed becomes competent (Article 19(2) and (4) HMK). If you live abroad and leave a statement of claim served from Turkey unanswered, you may therefore also lose the right to object to jurisdiction.

## Points to note if you live abroad

### Can someone living in Germany be sued in Turkey?

If the defendant is domiciled in Germany, Article 6 HMK does not give a Turkish court jurisdiction. A case can still be brought in Turkey if the contract is to be performed in Turkey (Article 10 HMK), the claim concerns a right in rem over property in Turkey (Article 12 HMK), the tort was committed or the damage occurred in Turkey (Article 16 HMK; for the applicable law see [Article 34 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-34-mohuk-tort-accident-turkey)), or, for a claim concerning property rights, the disputed asset is in Turkey (Article 9 HMK). Article 9 refers to the asset in dispute. In my assessment, a bank account or a flat the defendant has in Turkey that has nothing to do with the dispute does not, on its own, create jurisdiction under that provision.

For divorce, Article 168 TMK is enough if one spouse lives in Turkey. If both spouses live abroad, Turkish citizens can bring the case in Turkey under the conditions of Article 41.

### Suing a debtor in Turkey from abroad

A Turkish court always has jurisdiction over a debtor domiciled in Turkey (Article 6 HMK). If you are thinking of suing in Germany or the Netherlands, that court’s jurisdiction depends on its own law; for that part, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. From the Turkish side, what matters is the next step: a foreign judgment can be enforced in Turkey only after an enforcement order (tenfiz) ([Article 50 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey)). Enforcement is refused if the judgment concerns a matter within the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts or, on the defendant’s objection, if the court assumed jurisdiction without any real connection to the subject matter or the parties (Article 54(1)(b) MÖHUK).

### Proceedings already pending abroad

MÖHUK has no rule on what effect a case pending abroad has on a case in Turkey. The Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) said exactly this in the decision listed below: a divorce case pending abroad does not bar a case in Turkey as lis pendens (derdestlik), even if it is the same case. The only exception it mentioned is an international agreement between Turkey and that state regulating jurisdiction. So having filed for divorce in Germany does not in itself stop your spouse from suing in Turkey. The decision concerns divorce. I expect the same approach to apply to money claims, but that is my view; the decisions I was able to check deal with divorce cases.

### Forum non conveniens

This question is disputed. What follows is my assessment; the law contains no express provision on it, and there is no binding court decision. How a court would decide in a given case is uncertain. The common-law doctrine of forum non conveniens, under which a competent court may decline a case because a foreign court is better placed to hear it, appears neither in MÖHUK nor in the HMK. In my view, a Turkish court with jurisdiction under Article 40 has no statutory basis to dismiss a case on that ground. If the parties want a foreign court to decide, the route is a jurisdiction agreement under Article 47.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/1214, K. 2026/1586, 20.04.2026**

A manufacturer in Turkey had started enforcement proceedings against a company in Germany over a claim under a contract for work and sued to set aside the company’s objection. The Chamber held that the jurisdiction clause in the parties’ confidentiality agreement did not cover this claim, so there was no valid agreement under Article 47. Because the claimant’s goods were handed over in Turkey, Turkish courts had jurisdiction under Article 10 HMK through Article 40, and the objection to jurisdiction should have been rejected. The decision was by majority.

**Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2014/12316, K. 2014/23427, 20.11.2014**

The first-instance court had dismissed a divorce case in Turkey because divorce proceedings were pending in Moscow. The Chamber found that Law No. 5718 contains no rule on lis pendens and that a foreign case can be taken into account only where an international agreement regulates jurisdiction, and reversed the judgment.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do Turkish courts have international jurisdiction?

When a court in Turkey would be competent under the Turkish rules on territorial jurisdiction in the HMK and special statutes (Article 40 MÖHUK).

### When must you object to jurisdiction in Turkey?

Unless jurisdiction is mandatory, in the statement of defence, naming the competent court; otherwise the court becomes competent (Article 19(2) and (4) HMK).

### How can I sue someone in Turkey who lives abroad?

Only if a Turkish court has a ground of jurisdiction, for example the place of performance of the contract (Article 10 HMK) or, for rights in rem, the location of property in Turkey (Article 12 HMK).

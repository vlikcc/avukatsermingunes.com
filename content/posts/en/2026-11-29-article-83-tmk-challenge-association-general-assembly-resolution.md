Article 83 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) governs the action to annul (iptal davası) a resolution of an association's (dernek) general assembly (genel kurul). A member can go to court against a resolution that breaches the law or the statute, as a rule within one month and in any event within three months of the date of the resolution. Decisions of other organs can only be challenged once the association's internal remedies are exhausted, and resolutions that are non-existent or absolutely null fall outside these time limits. The article concerns every member affected by a general assembly resolution.

## Article 83 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **c. Annulment of the resolution**
>
> Article 83 – Every member who was present at the meeting and did not agree to general assembly resolutions adopted in breach of the law or the statute may, within one month of the date of the resolution, and every member who was not present at the meeting may, within one month of learning of the resolution and in any event within three months of the date of the resolution, apply to the court and request the annulment of the resolution.
>
> No action for annulment may be brought against decisions of the other organs until the association's internal review procedures have been exhausted.
>
> Cases in which general assembly resolutions are deemed non-existent or absolutely null and void are reserved.

Original Turkish text:

> **c. Kararın iptali**
>
> Madde 83- Toplantıda hazır bulunan ve kanuna veya tüzüğe aykırı olarak alınan genel kurul kararlarına katılmayan her üye, karar tarihinden başlayarak bir ay içinde; toplantıda hazır bulunmayan her üye kararı öğrenmesinden başlayarak bir ay içinde ve her hâlde karar tarihinden başlayarak üç ay içinde mahkemeye başvurmak suretiyle kararın iptalini isteyebilir.
>
> Diğer organların kararlarına karşı, dernek içi denetim yolları tüketilmedikçe iptal davası açılamaz.
>
> Genel kurul kararlarının yok veya mutlak butlanla hükümsüz sayıldığı durumlar saklıdır.

As of November 2026, Article 83 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest act: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The reasoning is brief: the article sets out in detail who may request the annulment of general assembly resolutions and under what conditions (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Commentary

### Who can sue, and by when

The law distinguishes two groups. For a member who was present and did not agree to the resolution, the one-month period runs from the date of the resolution. For a member who was absent, it runs from the day the member learns of the resolution; but that member, too, can no longer sue once three months have passed since the resolution. Learning of it late does not extend this outer limit.

On its wording, the article gives no right of action to a member who was present and voted in favour. The ground for annulment is a breach of the law or the statute; a meeting held without the required quorum is one example ([Article 78 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-78-tmk-association-general-assembly-quorum)).

### Nature of the time limits

The law does not say expressly that these are time bars that extinguish the right itself (hak düşürücü süre). In the decision cited below, the 8th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) described the one- and three-month periods as such and stated that if no action is brought in time, the resolution can no longer be attacked as annullable. This is a chamber decision, not a binding decision unifying case law, and it is not certain that a court will take the same view in a particular case. The author's view is that it is safer to proceed on the basis that they are.

### Decisions of other organs

Decisions of other organs, such as the board of directors, cannot be challenged directly in court; the internal review procedures under the statute must be exhausted first (Article 83(2)). The general assembly takes the final decision on admission and expulsion of members (Article 80(1)). For expulsion, see the post on [Article 67 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-67-turkish-civil-code-expulsion-member).

### Non-existent and null resolutions

Paragraph 3 reserves resolutions that are non-existent (yok) or absolutely null (mutlak butlan); the law does not define these terms. In the same decision, the 8th Civil Chamber explained that non-existence results from missing constitutive elements, can only be raised through an action for a declaratory judgment and is not subject to the time limits in Article 83(1). It describes nullity as invalidity that anyone with a legal interest may raise without a time limit. Whether a defect makes a resolution annullable, null or non-existent has to be assessed case by case.

### Court and procedure

Article 83 only says "by applying to the court". Neither Article 83 TMK nor the Associations Act No. 5253 contains a separate rule on subject-matter jurisdiction for this action; under Article 2 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (HMK), the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) has jurisdiction unless otherwise provided. The court generally competent is that of the defendant's domicile (Article 6 HMK); a legal person's domicile is the place where its affairs are managed unless its founding document provides otherwise (Article 51 TMK). Article 14(2) HMK gives exclusive jurisdiction to the court of the legal person's seat for certain actions arising from membership; that this also covers a member's annulment action against the association is the author's assessment, as the law does not say so expressly and no binding decision on the point was found for this post. Nor does the law name the defendant; in the Court of Cassation decisions reviewed, the association (in the foundation case, the foundation) was the defendant.

As of November 2026, this action is not among the disputes in which mediation (arabuluculuk) is a precondition for suing (Mediation Act No. 6325, Article 18/B: tenancy, partition of co-owned property, condominium, neighbour law), and neither the TMK nor Act No. 5253 requires it. Article 32(1)(b) of Act No. 5253 adds that the court may annul general assembly meetings held in breach of the law or the statute.

## Points for members living abroad

For a member who did not attend and learns of the resolution later, the one-month period runs from that knowledge, but three months from the resolution date remain the outer limit in every case. If you need to instruct a lawyer in Türkiye, you can grant the power of attorney at a Turkish consulate or before a notary in your country of residence with an apostille and a Turkish translation; details are in the post on [powers of attorney for Turkey](/en/publications/power-of-attorney-for-turkey-from-abroad).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay, 8th Civil Chamber (8. Hukuk Dairesi), E. 2025/3778, K. 2026/335, 21 January 2026**

The case concerned a request for a declaration that resolutions of a foundation's board of trustees (mütevelli heyet) were non-existent; as foundation law has no rule on this, the Chamber applied Article 83 TMK by analogy. It held that the one- and three-month periods in Article 83(1) extinguish the right, but that a request for a declaration of non-existence under Article 83(3) is not subject to any time limit, and it reversed the regional court's decision dismissing the action.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I have to challenge a general assembly resolution in Türkiye?

If you were present and did not agree, one month from the resolution date; if you were absent, one month from learning of it, but no later than three months from the resolution date (TMK Article 83(1)).

### Who is the defendant?

The law does not say expressly; in the Court of Cassation decisions reviewed, the association was the defendant.

### Is mediation required first?

As of November 2026, neither the TMK, Act No. 5253 nor Article 18/B of the Mediation Act requires mediation for this action.

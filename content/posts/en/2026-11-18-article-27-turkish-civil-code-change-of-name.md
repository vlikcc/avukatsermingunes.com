Article 27 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) allows a name to be changed only for just cause (haklı sebep) and only by a court. The decision is entered in the civil registry and, under the current text, also published on the notice portal of the Press Advertising Agency (Basın İlan Kurumu). Changing the name does not change personal status, and anyone harmed by the change may sue to have it set aside within one year of learning of it. Paragraph 2 was amended in 2024 and partly annulled by the Constitutional Court (Anayasa Mahkemesi, AYM); the annulment takes effect on 1 January 2027.

## Article 27 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Change of name**
>
> Article 27 – A change of name may be requested from the court only on just grounds.
>
> (Paragraph 2 amended: 14/11/2024 – Law No. 7532, Art. 12) The change of name is entered in the civil registry and published on the notice portal of the Press Advertising Agency. The notice states the court that gave the judgment, the date of the decision, the case and decision numbers, and the place of civil registration, date of birth, mother's and father's names, previous first name and surname, and the new first name and surname given by the court of the person whose name was changed.
>
> A change of name does not change personal status.
>
> Anyone harmed by the change of name may bring an action to set aside the decision within one year of the day they learned of it.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 26 of the former Code; it was simplified in wording, together with its side heading, without any change in substance (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly). This reasoning concerns the original text, not the 2024 amendment or the Constitutional Court decision.

## Explanation

### Just cause and a court decision

A registered name cannot be changed by simple declaration. The Code does not define "just cause"; the court decides case by case whether the name is a real burden for the person (Article 4 TMK). The procedure is in the Civil Registry Services Act: actions to correct civil registry records are brought before the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) of the place of domicile and heard in the presence of the registry director or an official they appoint (Article 36(1)(a) of Law No. 5490). Where there is just cause, a further correction on the same matter may be requested (Article 36(1)(b)).

### Registration and publication: before and after 1 January 2027

The decision is entered in the civil registry. Under the text in force until 31 December 2026, it is also published on the Press Advertising Agency's portal, giving the court, date, case numbers, place of registration, date of birth, parents' names and the old and new names. When the annulment takes effect on 1 January 2027, unless the legislature has made a new rule by then, the paragraph will only require registration. How publication is handled for decisions after that date should be checked again under the law then in force.

### The name changes, personal status does not

Parentage, marital status and citizenship are not affected. Contracts, debts and rights acquired under the old name continue. Under the Civil Registry Services Act, when a first name is changed, the registry office also corrects the father's or mother's name in the children's records (Article 36(1)(b) of Law No. 5490). When a surname is changed, the spouse's and minor children's surnames are corrected; that is a separate rule for surnames.

### One-year action to set aside

A third party harmed by the change, for example because the new name causes confusion with them, may sue to have the decision set aside. The time limit is one year from learning of the change (Article 27(4) TMK). Because the time limit runs from knowledge, the end of publication may indirectly affect when this action can be brought; this is an assessment, and the Code has no express rule on it.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

If a Turkish citizen living in Germany or the Netherlands changes their name in Türkiye, the new name is entered in the Turkish registry; whether and how German or Dutch records are updated is for the authorities there under their own law. Expect Turkish and local documents to differ for a while. Which court has jurisdiction, how to run the case through a power of attorney, and how a name changed in Germany is entered in the Turkish registry are covered in [How to change your name in Türkiye](/en/publications/how-to-change-your-name-in-turkey).

The rule that personal status stays the same also helps abroad: diplomas, land registry entries or contracts in the old name remain valid. To show that the old and new names belong to the same person, the key documents are the name change decision with its certificate of finality and a current civil registry extract.

## Frequently asked questions

### What counts as just cause for a name change in Türkiye?

The Code does not define it. The court decides case by case whether the name is a real burden for the person (Article 27(1) and Article 4 TMK).

### Is a name change published?

Under the text in force until 31 December 2026, yes, on the Press Advertising Agency's portal. The Constitutional Court annulled this rule; the annulment takes effect on 1 January 2027.

### Will my children's records change?

When your first name changes, the registry office corrects the father's or mother's name in your children's records (Article 36(1)(b) of Law No. 5490).

### Can someone challenge a name change?

Anyone harmed by it may sue to have it set aside within one year of learning of it (Article 27(4) TMK).

Article 30 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says how birth and death are proved: by the entry in the Turkish civil registry (nüfus sicili). If there is no entry, or the entry turns out to be wrong, the true facts may be proved by any evidence. The rule matters whenever a date of birth or death decides something, such as who inherits. For Turkish citizens abroad whose Turkish record is missing, or does not match a German or Dutch certificate, it is a very practical question.

## Article 30 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Means of proof**
>
> **a. In general**
>
> Article 30 – Birth and death are proved by the entries in the civil registry.
>
> If there is no entry in the civil registry, or if the existing entry is found to be incorrect, the true facts may be proved by any evidence.

Turkish original:

> **2. İspat araçları**
>
> **a. Genel olarak**
>
> Madde 30- Doğum ve ölüm, nüfus sicilindeki kayıtlarla ispat olunur.
>
> Nüfus sicilinde bir kayıt yoksa veya bulunan kaydın doğru olmadığı anlaşılırsa, gerçek durum her türlü kanıtla ispat edilebilir.

The article has been in force unchanged since 1 January 2002. It does not appear in the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 29 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The old heading ("Ahvali şahsiye beyyineleri") was changed to "Means of proof" and the first paragraph was simplified. The old second paragraph, on correcting the registry after gender reassignment, was taken out and placed as a separate provision after Article 39 on corrections; today that is Article 40 (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### The burden is in Article 29, the means in Article 30

Anyone who, in order to exercise a right, claims that a person is alive or dead, or was alive when someone else died, must prove it (Article 29(1) TMK). Article 30 says how: first by the registry entry. It applies to birth and death the general rule that official registers prove the facts they record (Article 7 TMK), covered in [Article 7 TMK – Evidential value of official records](/en/publications/legislation/article-7-turkish-civil-code-official-records).

The order of deaths can change inheritance shares. If the registry does not show who died first, or the entry is wrong, the order may be proved by any evidence (Article 30(2)). If it cannot be proved at all, the persons are treated as having died at the same time (Article 29(2)).

### Proving a fact in court is not correcting the record

The second paragraph lets a court work from the true facts when the entry is wrong. The entry itself stays as it is. Without a final court judgment no registry entry may be corrected; only clerical errors made when recording an event are corrected by the registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) on the basis of the underlying document (Article 39 TMK; Article 35(1) of the Civil Registry Services Act No. 5490). Under that Act, declaratory judgments create a presumption for later actions to cancel or correct the entry (Article 36(1)(c)). How a date of birth is corrected is covered in [Change date of birth in Turkey](/en/publications/change-date-of-birth-turkey-court-case).

In everyday use, the entry is shown by a civil registry extract (nüfus kayıt örneği) certified by the registry office. It is valid until proven otherwise and expires if not used within 180 days of issue; the Ministry may shorten that period to as little as 30 days depending on the purpose (Article 43 of Law No. 5490).

### When a death was never recorded

The most common missing entry is a deceased person who still appears alive in the registry. Usually no court case is needed. If a document showing the death is submitted, the registry office draws up a death record (ölüm tutanağı). If there is no document, the office has the statement investigated and the record is entered on the order of the district governor or provincial governor (mülkî idare amiri) (Article 33(1) of Law No. 5490). Death records received after the statutory deadline are still accepted if they are based on records of public health institutions or other official documents (Article 31(2)). The General Directorate of Population and Citizenship Affairs may also identify and investigate people whose records have not been used for a long time and who, given their age, have probably died (Article 33(2)). If the deceased had no record at all, once Turkish citizenship and family are established, the birth is entered first and then the death (Article 31(4)).

Where someone disappears in circumstances that make death certain, the presumption of death applies (Article 31 TMK); where death is only highly likely, a declaration of absence (Article 32 ff. TMK). Those are the subject of the following articles, not of Article 30.

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

A birth abroad is reported by handing the official document or report of the foreign authority to the Turkish consulate or embassy (Article 15(6) of Law No. 5490). For a death, the mission must notify Türkiye within ten days of learning of it (Article 31(1)). Since the consulate only learns of a death if someone tells it, the death of a relative in Germany or the Netherlands may be missing from the Turkish registry until the death certificate is submitted there. This causes problems above all with the certificate of inheritance: [Certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey).

A foreign birth or death certificate has the status of an official document in Turkish proceedings only once it is confirmed by the competent authority of the issuing state or by the Turkish consulate; treaties to which Türkiye is party are reserved (Article 224 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure, HMK). Türkiye, Germany and the Netherlands are parties to the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 abolishing the requirement of legalisation for foreign public documents, so the confirmation is an apostille. A party relying on a document in a foreign language must also submit a translation, and the court may ask for an official translation (Article 223 HMK).

If the Turkish entry and a foreign certificate conflict, Turkish authorities and courts start from the Turkish entry (Article 30(1)). If the entry is missing or wrong, the apostilled foreign certificate is one of the pieces of evidence that can prove the true facts under Article 30(2). The author's assessment: if something tied to the death has to be done in Türkiye (a certificate of inheritance, transfer of land, a case where the date of death matters), it is usually quicker to have the death entered in the Turkish registry under Article 33 first rather than prove it again in each procedure. If information from a correct foreign birth certificate was copied wrongly into the Turkish registry, it can be corrected as a clerical error (Article 38 of Law No. 5490); for how the birth entry is made, see [Birth registration in Turkey for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad). What effect a Turkish entry has in Germany or the Netherlands is a matter for local law and the local registry office or a lawyer admitted there.

## Frequently asked questions

### How are birth and death proved in Türkiye?

By the entry in the civil registry (Article 30(1) TMK). If there is none, or it is wrong, any evidence may be used (Article 30(2)).

### My late father still appears alive in the Turkish registry. What can I do?

Submit the death certificate to the consulate or to a registry office in Türkiye so the death is entered (Article 31(1) and Article 33(1) of Law No. 5490). Without a document, the registry office has the matter investigated.

### Is a German or Dutch death certificate valid in a Turkish court?

With an apostille and a Turkish translation, it can be used there as an official document (Articles 223 and 224 HMK).

### Does the record change if the court finds it wrong?

No. A final judgment ordering the correction is needed (Article 39 TMK; Article 35(1) of Law No. 5490).

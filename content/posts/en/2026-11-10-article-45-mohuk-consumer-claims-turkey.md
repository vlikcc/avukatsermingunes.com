Article 45 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which Turkish court has international jurisdiction (milletlerarası yetki) over disputes arising from consumer contracts with a foreign element. A consumer who sues may choose between his or her own domicile (yerleşim yeri) or habitual residence (mutad mesken) and the seller’s place of business, domicile or habitual residence. A claim against the consumer, on the other hand, can only be brought at the consumer’s habitual residence in Turkey. You may come across this article if you live in Germany or the Netherlands and order from a Turkish online shop, if you live in Turkey and buy from abroad, or if you signed a contract with a Turkish company and later moved abroad.

## Article 45 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Actions concerning consumer contracts**
>
> Article 45 – (1) In disputes arising from the consumer contracts defined in Article 26, the Turkish courts of the consumer’s domicile or habitual residence, or of the other party’s place of business, domicile or habitual residence, have jurisdiction, at the consumer’s choice.
>
> (2) In actions against the consumer concerning consumer contracts concluded in accordance with paragraph one, the competent court is the court of the consumer’s habitual residence in Turkey.

Original Turkish text:

> **Tüketici sözleşmesine ilişkin davalar**
>
> MADDE 45 – (1) 26 ncı maddede tanımlanan tüketici sözleşmelerinden doğan uyuşmazlıklarda, tüketicinin seçimine göre, tüketicinin yerleşim yeri veya mutad meskeni ya da karşı tarafın işyeri, yerleşim yeri veya mutad meskeninin bulunduğu Türk mahkemeleri yetkilidir.
>
> (2) Birinci fıkra uyarınca yapılan tüketici sözleşmeleri hakkında tüketiciye karşı açılacak davalarda yetkili mahkeme, tüketicinin Türkiye’deki mutad meskeni mahkemesidir.

The article has not changed since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment list of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system shows only one change to Law No. 5718, in Article 27 (Law No. 7550, in force since 4 June 2025); Article 45 carries no amendment note.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning of the government bill, the article is new; the former Law No. 2675 had no such rule. Because the law applicable to consumer contracts was built around consumer protection, the jurisdiction rules were designed in the same spirit. When the consumer sues, the courts of the consumer’s own domicile and habitual residence are available in addition to those of the other party’s place of business, domicile and habitual residence; when the consumer is sued, only the court of the consumer’s habitual residence has jurisdiction. This jurisdiction cannot be excluded by agreement. (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 44 of the bill; article numbers in the reasoning are those of the bill: today’s Article 26 was Article 25, and today’s Article 47(2) was Article 46(2).)

## Explanation

Article 45 applies only to consumer contracts within the meaning of Article 26 MÖHUK, that is, contracts made for a purpose outside the person’s trade or profession. If you bought the same item for your business, the general rule applies.

The general rule is Article 40 MÖHUK: Turkish courts have international jurisdiction where the domestic venue rules give them jurisdiction. For consumer claims these are the defendant’s domicile (Article 6 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK), the place where the contract is to be performed (Article 10 HMK) and the consumer court at the consumer’s domicile (Article 73(5) of the Consumer Protection Law No. 6502). Article 45 adds the consumer’s habitual residence and the other party’s place of business, and treats the two directions differently:

- **If the consumer sues** (paragraph 1), the consumer picks one of the listed places.
- **If the consumer is sued** (paragraph 2), the seller or service provider can only sue at the consumer’s habitual residence in Turkey.

This jurisdiction cannot be excluded by agreement (Article 47(2) MÖHUK). A clause in the terms and conditions saying that “only the courts of country X” have jurisdiction does not stop you from suing in Turkey. Under domestic law, too, only merchants and public-law entities may agree on a forum (Article 17 HMK).

The consumer court (tüketici mahkemesi) hears these cases (Article 73(1) Law No. 6502) under the simplified procedure (Article 73(4) Law No. 6502, Articles 316 et seq. HMK). Even where a Turkish court has jurisdiction, the applicable law is determined separately under Article 26 MÖHUK ([Article 26 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-26-mohuk-consumer-contracts)).

The law does not say whether Article 45 takes over all the conditions of Article 26 or only its definition of a consumer contract. This point is disputed; there is no express provision, and I have not found a binding court decision that answers it directly. Article 26(4) excludes contracts of carriage (other than package travel) and contracts under which the service must be provided in a country other than that of the consumer’s habitual residence. If “the consumer contracts defined in Article 26” is read as a reference to the whole article, these contracts also fall outside Article 45 and jurisdiction follows the general rule (Article 40); it can also be argued that the reference is only to the definition in Article 26(1). I consider the first reading closer to the wording, but that is a view; how a court will decide in a given case is not certain.

## What to watch for if you live abroad

### Ordering online from a shop in Turkey

If you live in Germany or the Netherlands and ordered online from a seller in Turkey, you can sue before the Turkish consumer court at the seller’s place of business, domicile or habitual residence (Article 45(1)). Whether you can also sue in the courts of your country of residence depends on German, Dutch and EU law; for that you need a lawyer admitted in that country.

### Holidays, hotels and clinics in Turkey

If you contract directly with a hotel, a dental clinic or a cosmetic surgery centre in Turkey, the service has to be provided in Turkey. Such contracts fall outside Article 26 because of Article 26(4), and on the view described above also outside Article 45. In practice this often changes little: the provider is based in Turkey and the contract is performed there, so the Turkish court already has jurisdiction under Article 40 MÖHUK together with Articles 6 and 10 HMK. Package travel remains expressly covered by Article 26(4).

### If a company wants to sue you in Turkey

Under Article 45(2), a consumer can only be sued at the court of his or her habitual residence in Turkey. MÖHUK does not define habitual residence; the term refers to the place where you actually live, not to the address in the population register. An address still registered in Turkey does not mean that your habitual residence is there.

The law does not expressly say whether a consumer with no habitual residence in Turkey can be sued in Turkey at all. This point is disputed. There is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision; the following assessment is my own. The wording and the protective purpose suggest that no Turkish court has jurisdiction in that case and the seller must sue in the consumer’s country of residence. It can also be argued, however, that paragraph 2 only covers consumers who live in Turkey and that otherwise the general rule applies (Article 40 MÖHUK, Article 10 HMK). How a court will decide in a given case is not certain. If such a claim has been filed against you in Turkey, you must object to jurisdiction in your statement of defence; otherwise, where jurisdiction is not exclusive, the court becomes competent (Article 19(2) and (4) HMK).

### Consumer arbitration committee and mediation

Disputes below a certain value must first go to the consumer arbitration committee (tüketici hakem heyeti); above that value the committee cannot be used (Article 68(1) Law No. 6502). The statutory threshold is raised every year by the revaluation rate (Article 68(4)); please check the amount for 2026 in the announcement of the Turkish Ministry of Trade. The application goes to the committee at the consumer’s domicile or at the place where the consumer transaction took place (Article 68(3)). The law does not say separately where an online purchase is deemed to have taken place.

Before suing in the consumer court, you must apply to a mediator (dava şartı olarak arabuluculuk); exceptions include disputes within the committee’s remit and objections to its decisions (Article 73/A(1) Law No. 6502). The application goes to the mediation office at the place of the competent court (Article 18/A(4) of the Mediation Law No. 6325). Law No. 6325 expressly covers disputes with a foreign element (Article 1(2)), and Article 73/A makes no exception for international cases, so applying for mediation before suing is the safe course.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 6. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2022/4525, K. 2023/3918, T. 27.11.2023**

The claimant, who lived in Turkey, had ordered a sailing yacht for private use from a French shipyard at a boat show in Istanbul, terminated the contract and sued for repayment of the deposit before the consumer court at his domicile. The shipyard argued that French courts had jurisdiction and asked the court to wait for the proceedings it had brought in France. The lower courts treated the claimant as a consumer and based the Turkish court’s jurisdiction on Article 73(5) of Law No. 6502 and Articles 24, 26 and 45 MÖHUK. The Chamber quoted Articles 26 and 45, took into account that claims against the consumer must be brought at the consumer’s habitual residence, found that the court was right not to wait for the French proceedings, and upheld the judgment.

**Yargıtay 5. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/14446, K. 2026/4313, T. 09.03.2026**

In a damages claim over a cosmetic procedure, the Antalya consumer court declined jurisdiction because the procedure had taken place in Istanbul; the Istanbul consumer court also declined the case, noting that the claimant had exercised the right to choose, and the Court of Cassation designated the competent court. The Chamber listed Articles 6 and 10 HMK, Article 73(5) of Law No. 6502 and Article 45 MÖHUK as the relevant law and held that Article 73(5) creates an additional venue alongside the HMK rules. As the claimant, whose habitual residence in Turkey was in Antalya, had chosen the court of his or her own domicile, the Chamber designated the Antalya consumer court.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where can I sue a seller in Turkey as a consumer?

Before the Turkish consumer court at the seller’s place of business, domicile or habitual residence; if you have a domicile or habitual residence in Turkey yourself, also there (Article 45(1) MÖHUK).

### The terms and conditions name a foreign court. Can I still sue in Turkey?

Yes. Jurisdiction under Article 45 cannot be excluded by agreement (Article 47(2) MÖHUK).

### I live in Germany. Can a Turkish company sue me in Turkey?

Claims against consumers belong before the court of their habitual residence in Turkey (Article 45(2) MÖHUK). What happens if you have no habitual residence there is not regulated by the law and is disputed; you must raise the objection to jurisdiction in your statement of defence.

Article 15 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets the consequence for acts of a person who lacks capacity of discernment: apart from statutory exceptions, they have no legal effect. The rule applies directly when a sale, gift or power of attorney given by someone who was seriously ill or very old is later challenged. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 15 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Lack of capacity of discernment**
>
> Article 15 – Subject to the exceptions provided by law, the acts of a person who lacks capacity of discernment have no legal effect.

Original Turkish text:

> **2. Ayırt etme gücünün bulunmaması**
>
> Madde 15- Kanunda gösterilen ayrık durumlar saklı kalmak üzere, ayırt etme gücü bulunmayan kimsenin fiilleri hukukî sonuç doğurmaz.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 15; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 15 of the former Code; the two paragraphs of the old text were merged into one. The article deliberately speaks of "acts" (fiiller) rather than "dispositions": it is not only the dispositive transactions of a person without discernment that lack effect, but all of their acts. The statutory exceptions are expressly reserved (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

The consequence is strict. A contract, power of attorney or gift made by a person without capacity of discernment is, in principle, entirely without effect. Three points follow:

- **Later approval does not cure it.** A transaction by a minor or a person under interdiction who has discernment can become valid through the later approval of the legal representative (Articles 16 and 451 TMK). Article 15 provides no such route for a person without discernment.
- **The other party's good faith is not protected.** Article 15 contains no exception for someone who dealt with the person without knowing their condition.
- **The rule is not limited to contracts.** As the reasoning stresses, "acts" also covers conduct that is not a legal transaction, such as a tort.

Statutory exceptions remain. The best known is equitable liability: where fairness requires, the judge orders the person without discernment to compensate the damage they caused in whole or in part (Article 65 of the Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK). Separately, the head of the household is liable for damage caused by a member of the household who is a minor, under interdiction, mentally ill or mentally weak (Article 369 TMK).

The party relying on invalidity must prove the lack of discernment; the law presumes capacity (Article 13 TMK). What counts is the moment of the transaction.

## Practical points if you live abroad

The rule usually comes up within a family after a parent's death: a transfer of land or a general power of attorney given by a very old or seriously ill parent to one of the children. If the transfer was made while the parent lacked discernment, it has no legal effect, and the other heirs can pursue this with a claim for cancellation of the land registry entry and re-registration (tapu iptali ve tescil). For dispositions upon death such as wills, the action for annulment under Articles 557 et seq. TMK and its time limits also apply.

For heirs living abroad the practical difficulty is evidence. Hospital records, discharge reports (epikriz) and the land registry file (tapu müdürlüğü) are in Turkey. These can be requested through the court during the proceedings and used as the basis for an expert report.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a sale by a person without capacity of discernment valid?

No. Apart from statutory exceptions, the acts of a person without capacity of discernment have no legal effect (Article 15 TMK).

### Is a person without discernment liable for damage?

As a rule, no. But where fairness requires, the judge orders full or partial compensation (Article 65 TBK).

### Does the transaction become valid if relatives approve it later?

Article 15 does not provide for later approval. Approval is regulated for transactions by minors and persons under interdiction who have discernment (Articles 16 and 451 TMK).

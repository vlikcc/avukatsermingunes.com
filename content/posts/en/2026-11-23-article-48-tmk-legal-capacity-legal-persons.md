Article 48 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) is one of the general rules on legal persons and sets the limit of their legal capacity (hak ehliyeti). A legal person can hold every right and obligation except those tied to purely human characteristics such as sex, age or kinship. The article matters to the boards of associations and foundations and to anyone who wants to give or leave something to a legal person.

## Article 48 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **B. Legal capacity**
>
> Article 48 – Legal persons have capacity for all rights and obligations other than those that depend on characteristics inherent in human beings by nature, such as sex, age and kinship.

Original Turkish text:

> **B. Hak ehliyeti**
>
> Madde 48- Tüzel kişiler, cins, yaş, hısımlık gibi yaradılış gereği insana özgü niteliklere bağlı olanlar dışındaki bütün haklara ve borçlara ehildirler.

As of November 2026, Article 48 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 46 of the former Civil Code No. 743 and does not change its substance. Only the heading ("Hak ehliyeti" instead of "Medenî haklardan istifade") and the wording, which was simplified, were changed. The reasoning stresses that sex, age and kinship are examples, not a closed list, and that the principle of equal capacity of natural and legal persons is kept. A paragraph "special provisions are reserved" from the 1984 preliminary draft was considered unnecessary, following the Swiss model (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Capacity to hold a right, and who exercises it

Article 48 only answers whether a legal person can have a right. Who exercises that right, and by which decision, is governed elsewhere. Land shows the difference well: an association can own property, but under Article 22 of the Associations Act No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu) it buys or sells land by a board decision after the general assembly has authorised it, and it must notify the local administrative authority within one month of registration in the land register. The organs are covered by the next two articles: [Article 49 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-49-tmk-capacity-to-act-legal-persons) and Article 50.

Equal capacity also means that legal persons can found and join other legal persons. An association is formed by at least seven natural or legal persons (Article 56(1) TMK); legal persons may become members of associations (Article 64(1) TMK, the words "and legal persons" were added by Law No. 4963), and legal persons with capacity to act may found associations without prior permission (Article 3(1) of Law No. 5253).

The legal capacity of natural persons is governed by Article 8 TMK; for comparison see the [post on Article 8 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-8-turkish-civil-code-legal-capacity).

### Does the purpose limit legal capacity?

Article 48 names only human characteristics as the limit, not the legal person's purpose. The reasoning to Article 90 TMK, on the other hand, says that the aims and fields of activity stated in an association's statutes also define the scope of its legal capacity and capacity to act. This point is disputed. The Code has no express rule on what happens to a transaction outside the purpose, and different views are put forward in legal writing; which view a court would follow in a given case is uncertain. The author's assessment: if you are entering into a significant transaction with a legal person, checking whether it fits the aims and activities in its statutes avoids this argument from the start.

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you want to leave assets in Türkiye to a German e.V. or a Dutch stichting, its legal capacity is not determined by Turkish law but, under Article 9(4) MÖHUK (Turkish Private International Law Act), by the law of the administrative seat stated in its statutes; if its actual administrative seat is in Türkiye, Turkish law may be applied ([Article 9 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-9-mohuk-legal-capacity)). The law governing the succession itself is a separate question (Article 20 MÖHUK). For questions of German or Dutch law, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. Acquisition of land in Türkiye by foreign legal persons follows its own rules and is not covered here. How legal personality arises is explained in the [post on Article 47 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-47-turkish-civil-code-legal-personality).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an association or foundation inherit in Türkiye?

Yes. Being an heir does not depend on any purely human characteristic, so a legal person has this capacity too (Article 48 TMK).

### Which rights does a legal person not have?

Rights tied to purely human characteristics such as sex, age or kinship. According to the reasoning, this list is only illustrative.

### Can a Turkish association buy land?

Yes. The general assembly authorises, the board decides, and the acquisition is notified to the administrative authority within one month of registration (Article 22 of Law No. 5253).

Under Turkish law, a person’s domicile (yerleşim yeri) is the place where they live with the intention of staying permanently, and no one can have more than one domicile at a time. The rule is in Article 19 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK). Because court jurisdiction in Turkey usually follows domicile, it directly concerns anyone who lives in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere and has a case in Turkey.

## Article 19 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **V. Domicile**
>
> **1. Definition**
>
> Article 19 – A person’s domicile is the place where they live with the intention of staying permanently.
>
> No one may have more than one domicile at the same time.
>
> This rule does not apply to commercial and industrial establishments.

Turkish original:

> **V. Yerleşim yeri**
>
> **1. Tanım**
>
> Madde 19- Yerleşim yeri bir kimsenin sürekli kalma niyetiyle oturduğu yerdir.
>
> Bir kimsenin aynı zamanda birden çok yerleşim yeri olamaz.
>
> Bu kural ticarî ve sınaî kuruluşlar hakkında uygulanmaz.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 19; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the provision corresponds to Article 19 of the former Code. Only the wording changed: “ikametgâh” became “yerleşim yeri”, and “ticarî ve sınaî müesseseler” became “ticarî ve sınaî kuruluşlar” (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### What domicile is used for

Which court hears a case mostly depends on domicile. The court of general jurisdiction is the court of the defendant’s domicile on the date the action is filed, and that domicile is determined under the Civil Code (Article 6(1) and (2) of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). In divorce and separation cases, the competent court is that of either spouse’s domicile or of the place where the spouses last lived together for six months before the case (Article 168 TMK).

The one-domicile rule does not apply to commercial and industrial establishments. For legal persons, Article 19 is complemented by Article 51 TMK: their domicile is the place where their affairs are managed, unless the founding document says otherwise.

### Domicile and the address register

Addresses are recorded under the Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu). It defines the “domicile address” (yerleşim yeri adresi) in the same words as the TMK: the place where one lives with the intention of staying permanently (Article 3 of Law No. 5490). The registration itself, however, is based on the person’s written declaration (Article 50(1)); it is made to the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) or to a Turkish mission abroad and must be made within twenty working days (Article 50(2) and (3)). For Turkish citizens whose domicile address is abroad, the record follows the address data of the country of residence, or the country and the competent Turkish mission (Article 49(3)).

Article 19 TMK and Article 6 HMK tie domicile to actual residence and the intention to stay, not to the register. In my assessment the registered address is therefore strong evidence of domicile but does not decide the question alone where it contradicts the facts. This is an assessment; I have not been able to read the full text of a Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) decision on the point in an official database.

### Domicile, place of residence and habitual residence

Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) uses three different concepts. Its jurisdiction rules mention, beside domicile, the place where a person actually lives (sâkin olunan yer; Articles 41, 42 and 51 MÖHUK). Its choice-of-law rules often use habitual residence (mutad mesken): for the divorce of spouses of different nationalities, their common habitual residence (Article 14 MÖHUK); for maintenance, the habitual residence of the person entitled (Article 19 MÖHUK). Where the applicable law depends on domicile or habitual residence, the situation on the date of the action counts unless a rule provides otherwise (Article 3 MÖHUK).

MÖHUK does not define habitual residence. Legal writers take the view that it is the place where a person actually conducts their life with some degree of permanence, without any requirement of intention to stay permanently. As there is no statutory definition and no binding decision I am aware of, this is an opinion; a court may assess an individual case differently.

## Practical points if you live abroad

If you live permanently in Germany or the Netherlands, your domicile is, as a rule, there. Owning a home in Turkey or spending your summers there does not change that, because the law requires the intention to stay permanently and allows only one domicile (Article 19(1) and (2) TMK).

This is most noticeable in divorce. If neither spouse has a domicile in Turkey and they did not last live together in Turkey for six months, Article 168 TMK points to no Turkish court, and jurisdiction follows the order in Article 41 MÖHUK. That order is explained in our post on [Article 41 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-41-mohuk-divorce-jurisdiction-turkey).

If you return to Turkey for good, Article 20(2) TMK is relevant: a person who has given up a domicile abroad but has not yet acquired one in Turkey is domiciled where they currently live.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does domicile mean under Turkish law?

The place where a person lives with the intention of staying permanently; one person can have only one domicile at a time (Article 19 TMK).

### Is my address in the Turkish population register my domicile?

The registered address is based on your own declaration (Article 50(1) of Law No. 5490). Domicile depends on where you actually live with the intention of staying (Article 19 TMK). The two usually match, but they are not the same thing.

### Where in Turkey can someone living in Germany be sued?

General jurisdiction follows the defendant’s domicile (Article 6 HMK). If the defendant has no domicile in Turkey, whether a Turkish court has jurisdiction depends on the type of dispute and follows from special jurisdiction rules or from MÖHUK (Articles 40 and 41 MÖHUK).

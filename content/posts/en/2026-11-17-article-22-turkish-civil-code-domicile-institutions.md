Article 22 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) provides that staying somewhere to attend an educational institution, or being placed in an educational, health-care, care or penal institution, does not create a new domicile (yerleşim yeri). A student who moves to another city or country, an elderly person who moves into a care home or a patient in long-term treatment keeps their previous domicile as a rule.

## Article 22 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **4. Stay in institutions**
>
> Article 22 – Staying in a place in order to attend an educational institution, or being placed in an educational, health-care, care or penal institution, does not result in the acquisition of a new domicile.

Turkish original:

> **4. Kurumlarda bulunma**
>
> Madde 22- Bir öğretim kurumuna devam etmek için bir yerde bulunma ya da eğitim, sağlık, bakım veya ceza kurumuna konulma, yeni yerleşim yeri edinme sonucunu doğurmaz.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 22; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 22 of the former Code; it was rewritten in modern Turkish without any change in substance (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

Domicile is the place where you live with the intention of staying permanently (Article 19 TMK), and it changes only when a new one is acquired (Article 20 TMK). Article 22 does not let a stay at a school or in an institution count, on its own, as evidence of that intention.

The article deals only with the fact of being in an institution; it says nothing about someone who builds other ties to the same place. In my assessment, a person who later rents a flat and starts working in the city where they went for treatment would again be judged by the general test in Article 19. The Code does not list which facts are enough; it depends on the case.

For persons under guardianship, Article 21(2) TMK also applies: their domicile is where the guardianship authority is located. Placing them in a care or health institution requires the authority's consent (Article 462(13) TMK).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

- A person living in Türkiye who goes to Germany or the Netherlands only to study keeps their Turkish domicile as a rule. For a claim against them in Türkiye, the court of general jurisdiction is therefore usually the court of that Turkish domicile (Article 6 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK).
- A person living in Germany who stays in a hospital or care home in Türkiye does not, by that alone, move their domicile to Türkiye.
- For residents of care homes, student dormitories or prisons, the institution registers their address (Article 50(6) of the Population Services Law No. 5490). The registered address may therefore show the institution. In my assessment, because of Article 22 this does not mean the domicile has changed; domicile is determined under the TMK (Article 6(2) HMK).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is a student's domicile under Turkish law?

Not where they study, but at their previous domicile (Articles 22 and 20(1) TMK); this may differ if the student has settled there with the intention to stay for reasons other than studying.

### Does my mother's domicile change if she moves into a care home?

Placement in a care home alone does not create a new domicile (Article 22 TMK). If your mother is under guardianship, her domicile is where the guardianship authority is located (Article 21(2) TMK).

If one partner is a foreign national, a marriage in Turkey is performed by a municipal marriage officer (belediye evlendirme memuru) or by the head of a population registry office (nüfus müdürü); the mufti’s office (müftülük) cannot marry a couple in which one partner is a foreigner (Article 22(2) of the Turkish Population Services Law No. 5490). The foreign partner must bring a certificate of capacity to marry (evlenme ehliyet belgesi) from the authorities of their own country; for a German resident in Germany this is the Ehefähigkeitszeugnis issued by the local Standesamt.

This article is for Turkish citizens living in Germany or the Netherlands who want to marry in Turkey, and for couples where one partner is German or Dutch. If you have already married at a Standesamt or gemeente, you need a different article: [register a marriage abroad in Turkey](/en/publications/register-marriage-abroad-in-turkey).

## Who can marry you in Turkey?

The marriage officer is the mayor, or an official the mayor appoints, in places with a municipality, and the village head (muhtar) in villages (Article 134(2) of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK; Article 22(2) of Law No. 5490). The Ministry of the Interior may also give marriage powers to population registry offices, to Turkish missions abroad and to provincial and district mufti offices. The mufti offices were added by Article 6 of Law No. 7039, in force since 3 November 2017.

The rule is narrower for mixed couples. If one partner is a foreigner, only municipal marriage offices and heads of population registry offices may perform the marriage (Article 22(2), last sentence; Articles 7 and 12 of the Marriage Regulation, Evlendirme Yönetmeliği). A mufti or a village head cannot marry you to a German or Dutch partner.

If both partners are Turkish citizens, they can also marry at a Turkish consulate abroad. Article 10 of the Regulation requires both to be Turkish citizens, and honorary consuls have no power to marry.

## Which law applies: Article 13 MÖHUK and the age limit

Under Article 13(1) of the Turkish Private International Law Act No. 5718 (MÖHUK), capacity to marry and the conditions of marriage are governed, for each partner, by the law of their nationality at the time of the marriage. The Turkish partner is assessed under the TMK, a German partner under German law, a Dutch partner under Dutch law. The form of the marriage follows the law of the country where it takes place (Article 13(2)), so a wedding in Turkey follows Turkish formalities. See also [Article 13 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-13-mohuk-marriage-general-effects). For someone who holds Turkish and another nationality, Turkish authorities apply Turkish law (Article 4(1)(b) MÖHUK).

For the Turkish partner, the TMK requires among other things:

- A person under 17 cannot marry; in exceptional cases and for a very important reason a judge may allow a 16-year-old to marry (Article 124). A minor needs the consent of their legal representative (Article 126).
- Marriage is prohibited between ascendants and descendants, between siblings, and between uncles or aunts and their nieces or nephews, as well as in the in-law and adoption cases listed in Article 129.
- Anyone remarrying must prove that the earlier marriage has ended (Article 130). A woman whose marriage has ended cannot remarry until 300 days have passed; the period ends with a birth, and the court lifts it in certain cases (Article 132).

If you divorced in Germany or the Netherlands, watch this rule. Under Article 15(b) of the Marriage Regulation, a person shown as married in the Turkish register cannot remarry. Even if a court has ended the earlier marriage, the new wedding cannot take place until the divorce is entered in the Turkish family register.

## Documents for the Turkish partner

Each partner hands in their identity card and a population register extract, proof that any earlier marriage has ended, the certified consent of the legal representative for a minor, and a medical report stating that there is no illness preventing the marriage (Article 136 TMK). Article 20 of the Regulation adds the application form (beyanname) and a passport photo. Without the medical report, no marriage takes place.

Living abroad changes little on the Turkish side. Marriage offices connected to the Identity Sharing System (Kimlik Paylaşımı Sistemi) pull the register extract themselves, and no marriage is performed on the basis of the identity card alone (Article 20 of the Regulation). If something is missing from your record, such as an unregistered divorce, it surfaces here.

## Certificate of no impediment, apostille and translation for the foreign partner

For a foreigner, Article 20 of the Regulation accepts a document issued by the competent central authorities or by a mission of that state, showing the person’s first name, surname, parents’ names and date of birth, stating whether there is any impediment to the marriage, and duly certified. The partner should obtain it in person from their own country; if that is not possible, the marriage officer may request it through the General Directorate (Article 12). Documents issued by honorary consulates are not accepted.

- **German partner:** The Ehefähigkeitszeugnis a German needs to marry abroad is issued by the Standesamt where they live or habitually reside. It is valid for six months, and the Standesamt does not ask for a certificate for the other partner (§ 39 of the German Civil Status Act, PStG).
- **Dutch partner:** The verklaring van huwelijksbevoegdheid is issued by the civil registry (burgerlijke stand) of the last place of residence. Dutch nationals living in Turkey who have never lived in the Netherlands apply to the Dutch embassy in Ankara or the consulate-general in Istanbul; that certificate is valid for six months.

Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are all parties to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention. The German Federal Foreign Office advises checking with the foreign authority whether the certificate must be translated and carry an apostille or legalisation. Ask the Turkish marriage office in advance which certification and which Turkish translation it requires. The German or Dutch procedure is a matter of that country’s law; for details, contact the Standesamt or gemeente or a lawyer there.

As of November 2026, also confirm with the marriage office which identity documents and proof of stay in Turkey it will ask the foreign partner for.

## Application and wedding day

The couple applies together to the marriage office where one of them lives (Article 134(1) TMK). There is a practical option for people abroad: an agent holding a special power of attorney can handle the application. The power of attorney must give the full identity of the principal, the agent and the future spouse and state clearly that it is given for the marriage formalities (Article 17 of the Regulation).

The wedding itself cannot take place by proxy (Article 27 of the Regulation). Both partners attend in person, with two adult witnesses of sound mind (Article 141 TMK). If the foreign partner does not speak Turkish, the marriage officer may use an interpreter, whom the couple brings to the ceremony (Article 27 of the Regulation).

On request, the marriage officer issues a marriage permit (evlenme izni belgesi). It entitles the couple to marry before any marriage officer within six months (Article 139 TMK), in Turkey or before a competent authority abroad, without a new file (Article 22 of the Regulation).

A religious ceremony does not replace the civil one. No religious ceremony may take place unless the family booklet is shown, and the validity of the marriage does not depend on it (Article 143 TMK).

## Registration in Turkey and Formül B for use abroad

You do not need to report the marriage to the Turkish register. The marriage officer sends the marriage notice to the population registry office within ten days, and that office registers it (Article 23(1) of Law No. 5490). The 30-day reporting rule at the consulate applies only to marriages concluded abroad before foreign authorities.

After the ceremony the couple receives a family booklet (aile cüzdanı) (Article 143 TMK). The foreign partner receives a family booklet and, on request, a multilingual marriage certificate (Article 34 of the Regulation).

For use in Germany or the Netherlands you can obtain a multilingual extract of the marriage record (Formül B). It is issued under CIEC Convention No. 16, in force for Turkey since 30 June 1985, for Germany since 18 July 1997 and for the Netherlands since 26 April 1987. Under Article 8 of the Convention, such extracts are accepted in the contracting states without legalisation or equivalent formality. The Turkish e-Government portal (e-Devlet) offers a “Çok Dilli Evlenme Kayıt Örneği (Formül B) Sorgulama” service.

Residents of the Netherlands must register a marriage concluded abroad in the Personal Records Database (BRP) at their gemeente. Residents of Germany should ask the Standesamt how the marriage is recorded there and whether anything beyond Formül B is needed. These are questions of German and Dutch law; for details, contact the authority or a lawyer there.

Matrimonial property is a separate question: see [prenuptial agreement in Turkey](/en/publications/prenuptial-agreement-turkey-marital-property) and [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### What documents are needed to get married in Turkey as a foreigner?

A certificate of capacity to marry from your own country’s authorities, duly certified, plus a medical report, a photo and the application form (Article 136 TMK; Article 20 of the Marriage Regulation). Ask the marriage office which certification and translation it requires.

### Can a mufti marry a Turkish citizen and a foreigner?

No. If one partner is a foreigner, only municipal marriage offices and heads of population registry offices are competent (Article 22(2) of Law No. 5490).

### Do I have to report a marriage in Turkey to the consulate?

No. The marriage officer reports it to the population registry office within ten days (Article 23(1) of Law No. 5490). The 30-day rule applies to marriages concluded abroad before foreign authorities.

### I divorced in Germany. Can I remarry in Turkey?

Only once the divorce is entered in the Turkish register (Article 15(b) of the Marriage Regulation).

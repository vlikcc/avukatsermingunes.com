If someone disappeared in mortal danger, or nobody has heard from them for a long time, and their death is highly probable, a Turkish court can declare them absent (gaiplik kararı) at the request of those whose rights depend on that death. This is Article 32 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK). The same article names the competent court in a fixed order: the person's last domicile in Türkiye; if they never lived in Türkiye, the place where they are entered in the civil registry; failing that, the place where their mother or father is registered. It matters to families whose Turkish relative went missing abroad and to anyone who inherits or owns property in Türkiye.

## Article 32 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Declaration of absence**
>
> **1. General**
>
> Article 32 – Where there is a strong probability that a person who disappeared in mortal danger, or from whom no news has been received for a long time, has died, the court may declare that person absent upon the application of those whose rights depend on that death.
>
> The competent court is the court of the person's last domicile in Türkiye; if the person never had a domicile in Türkiye, the court of the place where they are entered in the civil registry; if there is no such entry either, the court of the place where their mother or father is registered.

Turkish original:

> **III. Gaiplik kararı**
>
> **1. Genel olarak**
>
> Madde 32- Ölüm tehlikesi içinde kaybolan veya kendisinden uzun zamandan beri haber alınamayan bir kimsenin ölümü hakkında kuvvetli olasılık varsa, hakları bu ölüme bağlı olanların başvurusu üzerine mahkeme bu kişinin gaipliğine karar verebilir.
>
> Yetkili mahkeme, kişinin Türkiye'deki son yerleşim yeri; eğer Türkiye'de hiç yerleşmemişse nüfus sicilinde kayıtlı olduğu yer; böyle bir kayıt da yoksa anasının veya babasının kayıtlı bulunduğu yer mahkemesidir.

As of November 2026 the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr carries no amendment note for this article. The last law in the amendment table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026), which does not touch Article 32.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 31 of the former Civil Code. In paragraph 1 the old wording "hakları ölüme muallâk kimselerin talebi" was replaced by the plainer Turkish "hakları bu ölüme bağlı olanların başvurusu üzerine"; both mean "at the request of those whose rights depend on the death". In paragraph 2 the old text named, as the last option, only the place where the father was registered. To remove rules that offended equality between women and men, this became "the place where the mother or father is registered". (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly.)

## Explanation

### Presumption of death or declaration of absence?

The TMK has two routes. A person who disappeared in circumstances that make death certain is treated as dead even if the body is never found (Article 31, ölüm karinesi); the death is registered on the order of the highest administrative official of the place (Article 44(1)). No court is involved. The declaration of absence covers cases where death is not certain but highly probable, and it always requires a court decision.

### Who can apply?

The law does not list applicants. The test is whether the applicant's rights depend on the missing person's death. Other provisions of the TMK name some of them:

- Those who would receive a share of an inheritance that fell to the missing person, if he or she is no longer alive, can ask for a declaration of absence and for the share to be handed over (Article 586(2)).
- The spouse can ask for the marriage to be dissolved together with the application for a declaration of absence (Article 131(2)).
- If a person's assets, or an inheritance share that fell to them, have been officially administered for ten years, or the time in which they would have reached the age of 100 has passed, the court declares them absent at the request of the Treasury (Hazine) (Article 588(1)).

### Which court?

A declaration of absence is a non-contentious matter (çekişmesiz yargı) under the law of persons (Article 382(2)(a)(4) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). Unless the law says otherwise, non-contentious matters go to the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi, Article 383(1) HMK). Article 384 HMK gives jurisdiction to the court where the applicant or another party lives, but only "unless the law provides otherwise". Article 32(2) TMK is such a special rule. What counts is not where you, the applicant, live, but the missing person's connection with Türkiye. On the meaning of domicile (yerleşim yeri), see [Article 19 TMK – Domicile under Turkish law](/en/publications/legislation/article-19-turkish-civil-code-domicile).

### Time limits and effects, in brief

The application can be made at the earliest one year after the mortal danger or five years after the last news. The court calls on anyone with information about the person by public notice, with a period of at least six months from the first notice (Article 33). If the person reappears, sends news, or the date of death is established before that period ends, the application lapses (Article 34).

If the notice brings no result, the court declares the person absent. Rights that depend on the death can then be exercised as if the death had been proven. The decision takes effect from the day of the mortal danger or the last news (Article 35) and is entered in the death register on notification by the judge (Article 45). Two consequences are worth knowing:

- The marriage does not end automatically. The spouse cannot remarry until the court has dissolved the marriage (Article 131(1)).
- Heirs receive the estate only against security. Security is given for five years where the person disappeared in mortal danger, fifteen years where there has been no news for a long time, and in any case at most until the absent person would have turned 100 (Article 584). If the absent person returns, the assets must be given back under the rules on possession (Article 585(1)).

### What happens to the assets in the meantime?

For the assets of someone who has not been found for a long time and whose residence is unknown, the guardianship authority appoints a curator to manage them (yönetim kayyımı, Article 427(1)(1)). The share of an heir who is absent when the succession opens, and whose survival cannot be proven, is officially administered (Article 586(1)). If an heir has not been found for a long time and has no representative, the civil judge of peace orders an inventory of the estate (Article 590(1)(2)).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

For a Turkish citizen who went missing abroad, Turkish law governs the declaration of absence, because it follows the person's national law. The details are in [Article 11 MÖHUK – Declaration of Absence or Death](/en/publications/legislation/article-11-mohuk-declaration-of-absence). Local jurisdiction also follows Article 32(2) TMK. The same applies to dual nationals who kept Turkish citizenship. For foreigners without a domicile in Türkiye, for example Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holders who gave up Turkish citizenship, Article 42 MÖHUK applies instead ([Article 42 MÖHUK – Foreigners: Absence and Curatorship](/en/publications/legislation/article-42-mohuk-absence-curatorship-foreigners)).

The order in the law looks at whether and where the person lived in Türkiye. Someone who grew up in Türkiye and later moved to Germany has a last domicile in Türkiye, and the court of that place is competent. Someone born in Germany or the Netherlands who never lived in Türkiye goes before the court of their registry place. If emigration was decades ago, proving the last Turkish domicile can be hard. Before applying, it helps to collect a civil registry extract and whatever records you have of the person's former addresses in Türkiye.

If the spouse lives abroad, one point is unsettled. If dissolution of the marriage is sought in a separate action, the court of the claimant's domicile is competent (Article 131(3)). Where that domicile is abroad, the article does not say directly which Turkish court has jurisdiction. There is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on this, and it is not certain which view a court would take in a given case. In my assessment, Article 41 MÖHUK, which governs jurisdiction over personal status actions of Turkish citizens, may come into play here. The question does not arise if dissolution is requested together with the application for a declaration of absence (Article 131(2)).

Germany and the Netherlands have their own procedures for missing persons. Whether a decision given there can be entered in the Turkish civil registry is a question of recognition, also covered in the Article 11 MÖHUK post. For the requirements and procedure in Germany or the Netherlands, please ask the competent authorities there or a lawyer admitted in that country. For proving inheritance rights after a declaration of absence, see [Certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which court issues a declaration of absence in Türkiye?

The civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi; Article 382(2)(a)(4) and Article 383(1) HMK). Which local court is competent follows from Article 32(2) TMK.

### Who can apply to have a missing person declared absent?

Anyone whose rights depend on the person's death, for example those who would receive an inheritance share that fell to the missing person (Article 586(2) TMK) or a spouse seeking dissolution of the marriage (Article 131(2) TMK). Under certain conditions the Treasury can also apply (Article 588(1) TMK).

### How long do you have to wait?

At least one year after the mortal danger or five years after the last news, plus a public notice period of at least six months (Article 33 TMK).

### Does a declaration of absence end the marriage?

No. The spouse can remarry only after the court has dissolved the marriage (Article 131(1) TMK).

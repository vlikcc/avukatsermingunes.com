Article 47 of Turkey's Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) governs international jurisdiction agreements (yetki anlaşması): the parties to a contract with a foreign element may agree that their disputes will be heard by a court of a foreign state. If the agreement is valid, the chosen foreign court has jurisdiction; a Turkish court will hear the case only if the foreign court declines jurisdiction or the defendant does not object to jurisdiction in Turkey. You may come across this article if your contract with a company in Turkey says something like “the courts of Düsseldorf have jurisdiction”, or if you accepted the standard terms of a foreign company.

## Article 47 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Jurisdiction agreements and their limits**
>
> Article 47 – (1) Where venue is not determined on the basis of exclusive jurisdiction, the parties may agree that a dispute between them that has a foreign element and arises from obligations is to be heard by a court of a foreign state. The agreement is valid if it is proven by written evidence. The case is heard by the competent Turkish court only if the foreign court declares itself without jurisdiction or no objection to jurisdiction is raised before the Turkish courts.
>
> (2) The jurisdiction of the courts specified in Articles 44, 45 and 46 cannot be excluded by agreement of the parties.

Original Turkish text:

> **Yetki anlaşması ve sınırları**
>
> MADDE 47 – (1) Yer itibariyle yetkinin münhasır yetki esasına göre tayin edilmediği hâllerde, taraflar, aralarındaki yabancılık unsuru taşıyan ve borç ilişkilerinden doğan uyuşmazlığın yabancı bir devletin mahkemesinde görülmesi konusunda anlaşabilirler. Anlaşma, yazılı delille ispat edilmesi hâlinde geçerli olur. Dava, ancak yabancı mahkemenin kendisini yetkisiz sayması veya Türk mahkemelerinde yetki itirazında bulunulmaması hâlinde yetkili Türk mahkemesinde görülür.
>
> (2) 44, 45 ve 46 ncı maddelerde belirlenen mahkemelerin yetkisi tarafların anlaşmasıyla bertaraf edilemez.

The article has not changed since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment list of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system shows only Article 27, amended by Law No. 7550.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article essentially keeps Article 31 of the former Law No. 2675; the “public policy” criterion, whose function could not be explained, was removed. The second sentence on form was added to settle doubts: the agreement may be proven by any written evidence and may be made by any means of communication that allows its content to be established. The third sentence was rewritten so that there is no longer any doubt that the chosen foreign court gains exclusive jurisdiction. Paragraph 2 protects the weaker party.

In the reasoning, paragraph 2 refers to “Articles 43, 44 and 45”. Those are the numbers in the bill; in the law they became Article 44 (employment), Article 45 (consumers) and Article 46 (insurance). Article 47 itself was Article 46 in the bill. (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly.)

## Explanation

As a rule, the international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic venue rules ([Article 40 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-40-mohuk-international-jurisdiction-turkey)). Article 47 lets the parties hand that jurisdiction to a foreign court by agreement. According to the decision of the Court of Cassation's General Assembly of Civil Chambers cited below, there are four conditions: the dispute must be identifiable, arise from an obligation, have a foreign element, and must not fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Turkish courts. In addition, the agreement must be proven by written evidence.

Claims for the purchase price, a contractual penalty or freight charges are covered. Personal status and family matters such as divorce, custody or parentage cannot be the subject of an agreement under Article 47.

Exclusive international jurisdiction (münhasır yetki) is not the same as a mandatory domestic venue (kesin yetki). The General Assembly stresses that not every mandatory domestic venue rule creates exclusive international jurisdiction. In practice, actions concerning rights in rem in immovable property and bankruptcy matters are treated as falling within the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts. An action to cancel and re-register title (tapu iptali ve tescil) to a flat in Turkey is therefore heard in Turkey, whatever the contract says.

Under domestic Turkish law, only merchants and public legal entities may agree on a court (Article 17 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK), and the agreement must be made in writing (Article 18(2) HMK). Article 47 MÖHUK does not require merchant status; the General Assembly has said so expressly. As to form, proof by written evidence is enough. As of November 2026, Articles 17 and 18 HMK are unchanged and do not appear in the amendment list.

If a case is filed in Turkey despite a clause choosing a foreign court, the court does not take the clause into account of its own motion. Where there is no mandatory venue, the defendant must raise the objection to jurisdiction as a preliminary objection (ilk itiraz) in the statement of defence (Articles 116(1)(a) and 117(1) HMK). The time limit for the defence is two weeks from service of the statement of claim; a one-time extension of up to one month may be granted (Article 127(1) HMK). Without an objection, the Turkish court hears the case.

Each court applies its own procedural law. An agreement that is valid under Turkish law does not oblige the chosen foreign court to accept jurisdiction. If it declines, the competent Turkish court may hear the case.

The jurisdiction of Turkish courts under Article 44 for employees, Article 45 for consumers and Article 46 for policyholders, insured persons and beneficiaries cannot be excluded by agreement (Article 47(2)).

## What matters if you live abroad

### A German court clause in a contract with a Turkish company

If your contract with a company in Turkey says “the courts of Düsseldorf have jurisdiction” and the clause meets Article 47, the Turkish courts lose jurisdiction. If the company still sues you in Turkey, you must raise the objection in your statement of defence; otherwise the case continues in Turkey. Also check whether the clause covers the dispute at hand: the Court of Cassation refused to apply a German court clause from a confidentiality agreement to a payment claim arising from orders between the same parties (see below).

### Sale and rental contracts

When a flat in Turkey is sold, money claims such as the purchase price, a contractual penalty or damages for delay arise from an obligation, and a foreign court may be chosen for them. Actions concerning rights in rem, such as cancelling and re-registering title, fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts (the mandatory venue in Article 12(1) HMK and the General Assembly's finding cited above). Rent claims are obligations too; whether a particular tenancy action falls under Article 12 HMK has to be checked separately depending on the type of action.

### Employees and consumers: protection under Article 47(2)

Even if your employment contract names a foreign court, you keep your right to sue before a Turkish court that has jurisdiction under Article 44 ([Article 44 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-44-mohuk-employment-disputes-jurisdiction)). The same applies to consumer contracts: for contracts described in Article 26, Article 45 lets the consumer choose among several Turkish courts, and a jurisdiction clause in standard terms cannot remove that jurisdiction ([Article 26 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-26-mohuk-consumer-contracts)). Note that Article 47(2) only protects the jurisdiction of Turkish courts. How the same clause is assessed in Germany or the Netherlands depends on the law of that country; for that, please consult a lawyer admitted there.

### Contracts choosing a Turkish court

Article 47 only deals with choosing a foreign court. If two non-merchants agree in a contract with a foreign element that “the courts of Istanbul have jurisdiction”, the law contains no express rule on the validity of that clause, and I have not found a binding decision that answers this question directly. It is possible that the merchant requirement of Article 17 HMK will be applied here too; this is an assessment, and how a court will decide is not certain.

### Enforcing the foreign judgment in Turkey

If you want to enforce a judgment of the chosen German court in Turkey, you need an enforcement order (tenfiz). One of the conditions is that the judgment was not given on a matter within the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts (Article 54(1)(b) MÖHUK). On the procedure: [Article 50 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu, E. 2024/176, K. 2025/618, T. 08.10.2025**

A share transfer agreement gave jurisdiction to the courts of London; the creditors started enforcement proceedings in Turkey. The General Assembly set out the conditions of Article 47 and held that the legislature “did not require the condition in Article 17 HMK of being a merchant or a public legal entity”. A mandatory domestic venue (here Article 14(2) HMK) does not in itself create exclusive international jurisdiction; without a proper objection the agreement is not taken into account of the court's own motion; and Article 47 does not apply to the jurisdiction of enforcement offices. The Turkish court rules only on its own jurisdiction. The decision was taken by majority.

**Yargıtay 6. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2026/1214, K. 2026/1586, T. 20.04.2026**

A manufacturer in Kocaeli claimed payment in Turkey for steel profiles supplied to a company based in Germany; the German company relied on the court clause in a confidentiality agreement. The Chamber held that the clause covered only disputes under the confidentiality agreement and that there was no jurisdiction agreement proven by written evidence for the payment claim. Since the place of performance was in Turkey, it held the Turkish court competent and reversed the decision declining jurisdiction. The decision was taken by majority; the dissent took the view that the clause covered the parties' entire business relationship.

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2026/26, K. 2026/4321, T. 07.09.2026**

In a case about cargo shipped from Kenya to Mersin, the bill of lading gave exclusive jurisdiction to the courts of Marseille. The regional court of appeal held the clause valid under Article 18(2) HMK and Article 47(1) MÖHUK and upheld the dismissal for lack of jurisdiction; the Court of Cassation affirmed.

## Frequently asked questions

### My contract names a foreign court – can a case still be brought in Turkey?

Yes, but if the defendant objects to jurisdiction in the statement of defence and the agreement meets Article 47, the case is dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. Without an objection, the Turkish court hears it.

### Do you have to be a merchant to make such an agreement?

Not when a foreign court is chosen; the General Assembly of the Court of Cassation has held that Article 47 does not require the merchant status set out in Article 17 HMK.

### Does a foreign court clause bind me as an employee or consumer?

It does not remove the jurisdiction of the Turkish courts available to you under Articles 44 and 45 (Article 47(2)).

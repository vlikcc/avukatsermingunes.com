You do not have to travel to Turkey to sell a house or a plot of land there. An attorney-in-fact can complete the sale at the land registry office (tapu müdürlüğü) with a power of attorney made at a Turkish consulate, or you can sign at a land registry unit abroad while the buyer signs at the same time in Turkey (Article 26(2) of Land Registry Law No. 2644). As of November 2026 the title deed fee (tapu harcı) on a sale is 20 per mille for the seller and 20 per mille for the buyer, calculated on the declared price but never on less than the property tax value (emlak vergisi değeri).

A sale of real property in Turkey is valid only in official form, meaning a deed (resmî senet) drawn up by the land registry office (Article 706 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK; Article 237 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK). A private agreement, a deposit receipt or a contract signed before a notary in Germany or the Netherlands does not transfer ownership of property in Turkey.

## Three ways to sell property in Turkey from abroad

1. **Travel to Turkey.** Seller and buyer sign together at the land registry office.
2. **Sale through an attorney-in-fact.** A relative or your lawyer signs on your behalf.
3. **Signing at different offices.** Since 1 January 2020, a paragraph added to Article 26 of the Land Registry Law by Law No. 7181 provides that, where the parties to a contract transferring ownership are at different land registry offices or at the registry's organisation abroad, officials may take each party's declaration separately and complete the contract.

The details of the third route are set out in a regulation published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) on 3 December 2024 (Yetki Alanı Dışında ve Tarafların Farklı Birimlerde Bulunmaları Halinde Tapu İşlemlerinin Yapılmasına İlişkin Usul ve Esaslar Hakkında Yönetmelik). Sales, gifts and exchanges can be done this way (Article 10(1)). Both parties must be present at their own unit at the same appointed time; otherwise no signatures are taken (Article 11(6)).

## Power of attorney for the sale: what it must contain

Powers of attorney for transactions that require registration in the land register must be drawn up by the notary as a full notarial deed (düzenleme şeklinde); a certified signature alone is not enough (Article 89 of Notaries Law No. 1512). Abroad, Turkish consulates perform these notarial acts, and Article 89 applies to them as well (Articles 191 and 193 of the Notaries Law). If you live in Germany or the Netherlands, you can give a power of attorney for the land registry at the Turkish consulate responsible for your area.

Check the following points:

- **Express authority to sell.** Without special authority, an attorney-in-fact may not transfer real property or encumber it with a right (Article 504(3) TBK).
- **A clear description of the property.** Province, district, neighbourhood, block (ada) and parcel (parsel) make it clear which property the power covers.
- **Price and fees.** State whether the attorney-in-fact may receive the purchase price and pay the fees.
- **Substitution (tevkil).** If the attorney-in-fact has appointed someone else, the land registry also asks for the original power of attorney (Article 18(4) of the Land Registry Regulation, Tapu Sicili Tüzüğü).

The power of attorney must bear your signature, and the registry checks that you, as the principal, are the registered owner (Article 18(4) of the Land Registry Regulation). If the attorney-in-fact will act at a registry office other than the one where the property is registered, note that powers of attorney made before 21 November 2023 cannot be used for such out-of-area transactions (provisional Article 1 of the 2024 regulation).

You can give the attorney-in-fact instructions, such as a minimum price, and he or she must follow express instructions (Article 505 TBK). You may revoke the power of attorney at any time (Article 512 TBK); registry units check electronically for a revocation before the signing (Article 7(2) of the regulation).

Whether a land registry office accepts a power of attorney made before a German or Dutch notary, with an apostille and translation, depends on internal rules of the General Directorate of Land Registry and Cadastre (TKGM); this article does not go into them. The Land Registry Regulation requires a power of attorney drawn up under the Turkish Notaries Law, and a consular power of attorney meets that requirement directly. If you want to use a foreign notarial power of attorney, ask the land registry office beforehand.

## Selling through the consulate: the land registry office in Berlin

Under the regulation, a "unit" means land registry offices and units of the registry's organisation abroad (Article 4(1)(b)). According to the information sheet of the Land Registry and Cadastre Office (Tapu ve Kadastro Temsilciliği) at the Turkish Consulate General in Berlin, its service area is not limited, and it accepts applications from other German cities and from other countries, which means sellers living in the Netherlands can use it too. The information sheet also says:

- Applications are made through Web Tapu with an e-Devlet login, through the Alo 181 call centre, or by e-mail to the office; an appointment with the consulate's notarial service cannot be used for a land registry application.
- The transaction is prepared once the land registry office where the property is registered has given its authorisation; signatures are taken on the appointment date you are given.
- The title deed fee is calculated in lira and paid in euro at the consulate, at the exchange rate set by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs on the day of the transaction. The registry's service charge (döner sermaye) is paid online with an e-tahsilat number.

The information sheet is undated, so ask the office about the current procedure before you apply. This route is not available where both units are in the same Turkish province (Article 10(1)(c) of the regulation).

## Documents you need

For a sale, the Berlin office lists:

- an identity document (Turkish ID card, Blue Card; for foreign nationals a passport or national ID card),
- if someone represents you, the power of attorney or guardianship decision,
- for buildings, the compulsory earthquake insurance (DASK) policy,
- if the property was inherited or received as a gift, a tax office certificate that no inheritance and gift tax (veraset ve intikal vergisi) is outstanding,
- if the seller or buyer is a foreign national without a Blue Card, an identity information form and one photograph.

If you have taken German or Dutch citizenship, see [Blue Card in Turkey](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights) for your rights. For out-of-area transactions, a document showing the property tax value and that no property tax is owed is also required (Article 6(2) of the regulation). According to the Berlin information sheet, the transaction is cancelled if property tax is owed or if the owner never filed a return with the municipality, so settle your [property tax in Turkey](/en/publications/property-tax-turkey-payment-november) first.

## Title deed fee: rate, basis and who pays

Under section I, item 20(a) of Schedule (4) to Fees Law No. 492 (Harçlar Kanunu), the fee on a sale is **20 per mille each for the transferor and the transferee**, 40 per mille of the price in total. For example, on a sale for TRY 3,000,000 the seller and the buyer each pay TRY 60,000. These figures only illustrate the calculation.

The basis was rewritten by Law No. 7566 of 4 December 2025 and, since 19 December 2025, is the declared transfer price, but not less than the property tax value (Article 63(2) of the Fees Law; Schedule I/20(a)). If it later turns out that the fee was paid on less than the property tax value, or that the declared price did not reflect the real one, the difference is assessed afterwards. A tax loss penalty (vergi ziyaı cezası) equal to that amount is added; Law No. 7566 raised it from 25 percent to this level (Article 63(4)).

The President may raise the rate up to double and lower it to one tenth (Schedule I/20(a)). As of November 2026, the current text in UYAP Mevzuat shows 20 per mille. The registry's service charge (döner sermaye) is separate and set each year; ask the land registry office for the amount.

Who pays the title deed fee? Under the law, the fee on a sale is charged to each party separately. In practice the parties sometimes split it differently. In the author's assessment, such a private arrangement does not change the fact that the law makes both parties liable for the fee.

## What to check before the sale

- **Family home.** If the house is the family home (aile konutu), the owner cannot transfer it without the express consent of the other spouse (Article 194(1) TMK). See [family residence annotation](/en/publications/family-residence-turkey-title-deed-annotation).
- **Inherited property.** The heirs must first be registered as owners (intikal), which requires a certificate of inheritance: [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey). If the heirs cannot agree, there is the [partition lawsuit](/en/publications/inherited-property-turkey-partition-lawsuit).
- **Selling a co-ownership share.** If you sell your share to a third party, the other co-owners may exercise their right of pre-emption (Article 732 TMK). On the Constitutional Court's decision on market value, see [pre-emption right in Turkey](/en/publications/pre-emption-right-turkey-shared-property).
- **Income tax in Turkey.** A gain from disposing of property acquired for consideration within five years of acquisition is a capital gain (değer artışı kazancı); property acquired without consideration, such as by inheritance or gift, is excluded in this item (Repeated Article 80(1)(6) of Income Tax Law No. 193). The exemption amount, the tax return and the tax position in Germany or the Netherlands are outside the scope of this article; ask a tax adviser.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I sell my property in Turkey at the Turkish consulate?

Article 26(2) of the Land Registry Law and the 2024 regulation allow a sale where the parties sign at different land registry units or at the registry's organisation abroad. In Germany the land registry office at the Consulate General in Berlin handles this, and it also accepts applications from other countries. Ask Alo 181 beforehand whether another consulate has a land registry unit; you can give a power of attorney at any Turkish consulate.

### What power of attorney do I need to sell property in Turkey?

A power of attorney drawn up as a full notarial deed at the consulate, expressly authorising the sale of the property and bearing your signature (Articles 89 and 193 of the Notaries Law; Article 504(3) TBK; Article 18(4) of the Land Registry Regulation).

### How much is the title deed fee in Turkey in 2026?

Seller and buyer each pay 20 per mille, that is 2 percent each, 4 percent in total. The basis is the declared price, but not less than the property tax value.

### How can I stop my attorney-in-fact from selling?

You can revoke the power of attorney at any time (Article 512 TBK). A revocation received by the land registry is entered in the revocation register with the date, hour and minute (Article 77 of the Land Registry Regulation), and registry units check for a revocation before the signing (Article 7(2) of the regulation). In the author's assessment, having the revocation drawn up at the consulate and also notifying the attorney-in-fact makes sure it reaches both the land registry and the attorney-in-fact.

An inheritance waiver agreement (mirastan feragat sözleşmesi) is a contract in which a future heir, while the person they would inherit from is still alive, gives up their inheritance rights; under Turkish law the person who waives loses the status of heir (Article 528 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). It is a type of inheritance contract and is valid only if made in the form of an official will, before a public officer (a civil court of peace judge, a notary or another officer authorised by law) and two witnesses (TMK Articles 545 and 532). This article covers the form requirement, how a waiver for consideration affects your children, how it differs from disclaiming an inheritance, cancellation, and what applies if you live in Germany or the Netherlands.

The information is current as of November 2026. It is based on the TMK as last amended by Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026); the provisions on the waiver (Articles 528–530), the form rule (Article 545) and Articles 573–574 were not affected.

## What is an inheritance waiver under Turkish law?

Under Article 528(1) TMK, the person whose estate is concerned (the testator) may agree with one of their heirs on a waiver, either free of charge or in return for something (ivaz). A typical case: a parent gives a child money or a flat during their lifetime, and the child waives the inheritance in return.

The person who waives loses the status of heir (Article 528(2)). When the testator dies, they can claim neither an inheritance share nor the reserved share (saklı pay), which is the Turkish form of forced heirship. According to the official legislative reasoning, the agreement is mostly used to exclude an heir with a reserved share, such as a child or spouse; it can also be made with an heir who has none. For the reserved shares themselves, see [Forced heirship in Turkey: the reduction claim](/en/publications/forced-heirship-turkey-reduction-claim-time-limit).

The testator must be a party. If siblings agree among themselves, without the participation or consent of their living parent, that one of them "gives up the inheritance", that agreement is not valid, and anything handed over under it can be reclaimed (Article 678 TMK).

## Form: notary or judge, two witnesses

The TMK places the waiver among inheritance contracts. An inheritance contract is valid only if drawn up in the form of an official will (Article 545(1)). In practice this means:

- A public officer draws up the document: a civil court of peace judge (sulh hâkimi), a notary or another officer authorised by law (Article 532(2)).
- Both parties state their wishes to the officer at the same time and sign in the presence of the officer and two witnesses (Article 545(2)).
- Some people cannot act as witnesses, including the testator's spouse, relatives in the direct line, siblings and their spouses (Article 536).
- The testator must have the capacity to judge, be of age and not be under guardianship (Article 503).

A private written document, a handwritten statement or a text with only a certified signature does not meet this requirement. A waiver made without the required form can be challenged in an action for annulment (Article 557(4)). More on the official form: [Making a will in Turkey](/en/publications/making-a-will-in-turkey).

The law does not say expressly whether the agreement can be signed through a representative. Turkish legal writers take the view that the testator must act in person; opinions differ on the person waiving. The safe route is for both parties to appear before the officer themselves.

## Does the waiver also bind your children?

Article 528(3) TMK is clear: a waiver made for consideration also takes effect for the descendants of the person waiving, unless the agreement says otherwise. If a son received a flat from his father and waived in return, his children cannot inherit in his place even if he dies before his father.

This paragraph does not cover a waiver made free of charge. According to the legislative reasoning, the descendants are then not affected and can inherit in place of the person who waived. Whether a free waiver can be extended to the descendants by agreement, and what that would mean for their reserved share, is disputed; the law has no express rule on it.

If the waiver was made in favour of a specific person and that person does not become an heir for any reason, the waiver lapses (Article 529(1)). If no beneficiary is named, the waiver is treated as made in favour of the descendants of the nearest common ancestor, for example the siblings of the child who waived; if none of them becomes an heir, it lapses as well (Article 529(2)).

## Consideration, reduction and the estate's creditors

If the testator gave the person waiving more during their lifetime than the disposable part of the estate allows, the other heirs may ask for a reduction (tenkis); only the amount exceeding the reserved share of the person who waived is subject to reduction (Article 573(1) TMK). If that person must return something as a result, they can choose: return the reducible value, or return everything they received to the estate and share in it as if they had never waived (Article 574). Gifts made to settle inheritance rights before death are also subject to reduction (Article 565(1)(2)).

If the estate cannot cover its debts at the time of death and the heirs do not pay them, the person who waived and their heirs are liable to the creditors for the consideration received for the waiver in the five years before the death, up to the amount by which they are still enriched when the estate passes (Article 530).

## Waiver versus disclaimer

A waiver is a two-sided contract with the living testator and requires official form. A disclaimer (mirasın reddi) is a one-sided declaration made after the death (Article 605 TMK), as a rule within three months (Article 606). Once the person has died, a waiver is no longer possible; the only route then is a disclaimer: [Renouncing an inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/renouncing-inheritance-in-turkey).

## Can the waiver be cancelled or annulled?

The law expressly provides:

- An inheritance contract can be cancelled at any time by written agreement of the parties (Article 546(1) TMK).
- A party entitled to lifetime performance may withdraw under the rules of the law of obligations if that performance is not delivered or secured as agreed (Article 547).
- A disposition upon death can be annulled for lack of capacity, mistake, fraud, threat or duress, illegality or immorality, or lack of form (Article 557). The action must be brought within one year of learning of the ground and in any case within ten years of the passing of the estate against defendants in good faith, twenty years against those in bad faith (Article 559).

How these rules apply to an inheritance waiver is disputed. What follows reflects views in Turkish legal writing and the author's own assessment; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision verified for this article. It is not certain which view a court will take in a given case.

- In the author's view, Articles 546 and 547 apply to all inheritance contracts and therefore to the waiver too. Agreeing the cancellation in official form as well avoids later disputes about proof and form.
- It is accepted that the person waiving can also rely on defects in their own consent, such as fraud; opinions differ on the time limit and on whether this can be raised during the testator's lifetime or only after the death.
- If the agreed consideration was never paid, withdrawal under Article 547 comes into question.

## If you live in Germany or the Netherlands

Under Turkish private international law, succession is governed by the deceased's national law, and Turkish law always applies to immovable property in Turkey (Article 20(1) of Law No. 5718, MÖHUK). The form of a disposition upon death follows Article 7 MÖHUK: the law of the place where it is made, or the law governing the transaction itself. Dispositions made in the form of the deceased's national law are also valid (Article 20(4)). Capacity to make a disposition is governed by the national law at the time it is made (Article 20(5)). More detail: [Article 20 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-20-mohuk-inheritance-turkey).

Abroad, notarial functions are carried out by consuls (Article 191 of the Notaries Law No. 1512), and Article 193 extends the provision on wills (Article 69) to consuls. In the author's view this means a waiver can be drawn up in official form at a Turkish consulate; no separate provision says so expressly. Ask the consulate responsible for you about appointments and documents.

Whether an agreement signed before a German notary is recognised in Turkey is also disputed. In the author's view, an agreement that meets the form rules of the country where it was made should be formally valid under Articles 7 and 20(4) MÖHUK; however, no confirming decision of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) was checked for this article, and how a land registry or court in Turkey will treat the document is not certain. Under German law the waiver (Erbverzicht) is governed by § 2346 of the German Civil Code (BGB) and requires a notarial deed (§ 2348 BGB). German and Dutch courts decide under their own rules which law applies to the estate; for questions of German or Dutch law, consult a lawyer or notary admitted there.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an inheritance waiver in Turkey have to be notarised?

Yes. It is made in the form of an official will before a notary, judge or authorised officer, with two witnesses and both parties signing together (Articles 545 and 532 TMK). A certified signature alone is not enough.

### Can someone who waived still claim the reserved share?

No. The person who waives loses the status of heir (Article 528(2)) and with it the reserved share. If the consideration exceeded the disposable part, the other heirs can ask for a reduction of the amount above that person's reserved share (Article 573).

### Can an inheritance waiver agreement be cancelled?

The parties can cancel an inheritance contract in writing at any time (Article 546(1)). The grounds for annulment are listed in Article 557; how they apply to a waiver is disputed.

### Do the children of the person who waived inherit?

Not if the waiver was made for consideration, unless the agreement says otherwise (Article 528(3)). For a free waiver, the legislative reasoning says the descendants are not affected.

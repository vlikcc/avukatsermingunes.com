Article 11 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets the start of majority (erginlik): on completing 18 years of age. A person who marries becomes of age earlier. Majority is one of the three conditions for capacity to act (Article 10 TMK); from their 18th birthday, a person with discernment who is not under interdiction signs contracts, powers of attorney and inheritance documents themselves. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 11 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **b. Majority**
>
> Article 11 – Majority begins on completing eighteen years of age.
>
> Marriage confers majority.

Original Turkish text:

> **b. Erginlik**
>
> Madde 11 - Erginlik onsekiz yaşın doldurulmasıyla başlar.
>
> Evlenme kişiyi ergin kılar.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 11; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 11 of the former Code. The old term "rüşt" was replaced with the modern "erginlik", and "ikmal" with "doldurma" (completion) (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Eighteen years

Majority begins on the day the 18th year is completed. Unless the contrary is proven, the date of birth in the Turkish civil registry is decisive (Articles 7, 30 TMK). If that date is wrong, correcting it requires a court decision (Article 39 TMK).

Parental custody (velayet) ends with majority, because the Code places only children who are not of age under their parents' custody (Article 335(1) TMK). The duty of support does not necessarily end: it lasts in principle until majority, but if the child is still in education, the parents must support the child until the education ends, to the extent that can reasonably be expected of them in the circumstances (Article 328 TMK). More on child support: [Child support in Turkey](/en/publications/child-support-turkey-increase-enforcement-abroad).

### Majority through marriage

Under Turkish law nobody may marry before completing 17 years of age; in exceptional circumstances and for a very important reason, a judge may allow someone aged 16 to marry (Article 124 TMK). A minor needs the consent of their legal representative (Article 126 TMK). Someone who marries at that age becomes of age under Article 11(2). The Code contains no provision under which majority is lost when the marriage ends by divorce or death.

The third route to majority before 18 is a court declaration of majority (Article 12 TMK).

## Practical points if you live abroad

A Turkish citizen's majority is governed by Turkish law, because legal capacity and capacity to act are subject to the person's national law (Article 9(1) MÖHUK, Law No. 5718). Majority acquired under the national law does not end with a change of citizenship (Article 9(3) MÖHUK). Someone who later gives up Turkish citizenship keeps the majority acquired under Turkish law.

In practice it looks like this: when a young person living abroad who is an heir in Turkey or appears in a Turkish land register turns 18, they sign powers of attorney and handle land registry and estate matters themselves, or grant their own power of attorney. The law does not expressly say whether a power of attorney granted by a parent on the child's behalf before the 18th birthday can still be used afterwards; in the author's assessment, for new transactions it is safest for the young person to grant their own power of attorney. On powers of attorney: [Selling property in Turkey from abroad](/en/publications/selling-property-in-turkey-from-abroad).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the age of majority in Turkey?

18. Majority begins on completing 18 years of age (Article 11(1) TMK).

### Does a 17-year-old who marries become of age?

Yes. Marriage confers majority (Article 11(2) TMK).

### Is support owed to an adult child in education under Turkish law?

Yes, while the education continues, to the extent reasonably expected of the parents in the circumstances (Article 328(2) TMK).

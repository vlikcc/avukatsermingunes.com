If your tenant in Turkey is not paying rent, you can give them written notice with at least thirty days to pay and warn that you will terminate the lease if they do not. If the rent is still unpaid when that period ends, you can terminate and ask for eviction through the enforcement office or the court (Art. 315 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK). If the tenant has caused two written notices for unpaid rent within one lease year, there is a second route: an eviction claim based on two justified notices (iki haklı ihtar, Art. 352(2) TBK).

This article is for landlords living in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe who let a flat or business premises in Turkey. It explains the routes, their time limits and where a guarantor fits in.

As of November 2026, the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr shows that Articles 315 and 352 TBK have not changed since they came into force on 1 July 2012; the latest amendment to the TBK, Law No. 7589 (in force 31 July 2026), only changed Article 55. The rent enforcement provisions of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (İİK), Articles 269 to 269/d, were last amended in 2003 by Law No. 4949. Mandatory mediation in tenancy disputes has applied since 1 September 2023 under Law No. 7445; the last amendment to the Mediation Law (HUAK) was made by Law No. 7531 (in force 14 November 2024).

## Tenant not paying rent: Article 315 TBK and the 30-day period

Unless agreed otherwise, rent is due at the end of each month (Art. 314 TBK). On the tenant’s default (temerrüt), Article 315 TBK reads, in an unofficial translation:

> If, after the leased property has been handed over, the tenant fails to pay rent or ancillary costs that have fallen due, the landlord may set the tenant a time limit in writing and give notice that the contract will be terminated if payment is not made within that time limit.
>
> The time limit given to the tenant is at least ten days, and at least thirty days for residential and covered business premises. The time limit starts on the day after the written notice is given to the tenant.

The Turkish text:

> Kiracı, kiralananın tesliminden sonra muaccel olan kira bedelini veya yan gideri ödeme borcunu ifa etmezse, kiraya veren kiracıya yazılı olarak bir süre verip, bu sürede de ifa etmeme durumunda, sözleşmeyi feshedeceğini bildirebilir.
>
> Kiracıya verilecek süre en az on gün, konut ve çatılı işyeri kiralarında ise en az otuz gündür. Bu süre, kiracıya yazılı bildirimin yapıldığı tarihi izleyen günden itibaren işlemeye başlar.

So you need unpaid rent or ancillary costs, a **written** notice, a period of **at least thirty days** and a warning that the lease will be terminated. The period runs from the day after the notice reaches the tenant, not from the day you send it. In the author’s assessment, a notarial notice (ihtarname) is the safest option because it records that date.

If the tenant’s spouse has become a party to the lease by notifying you that the flat is the family home, you must send the notice with the payment deadline to the tenant and the spouse **separately** (Art. 349(3) TBK).

Clauses such as “if one month’s rent is unpaid, all remaining rent becomes due” or a penalty for late payment are void (Art. 346 TBK).

## Eviction through the enforcement office (Art. 269 İİK)

The route most used in practice is enforcement proceedings for the unpaid rent at the enforcement office (icra dairesi), with an eviction warning; in practice this payment order is known as “örnek 13” (form 13). If you also ask for eviction in the enforcement request, the payment order tells the tenant the payment period and that, once it has passed, their eviction can be requested from the enforcement court (icra mahkemesi) (Art. 269(1) İİK). The İİK still refers here to Article 260 of the old Code of Obligations; under Article 8 of Law No. 6101 that reference is deemed to be to the corresponding new provision, which is Article 315 TBK on the tenant’s default. For residential and business premises the payment order therefore contains the thirty-day period, and the notice under Article 315 TBK is part of the payment order.

The statutory time limits then run:

1. The tenant can object to the enforcement office within **seven days**; an objection halts the proceedings (Art. 269(2)–(3) İİK).
2. If the tenant does not object and does not pay within the thirty days, the enforcement court orders eviction on your application, made within **six months** after the notice period ends (Art. 269/a İİK).
3. If the tenant objects, you can apply to the enforcement court within **six months** of being served with the objection to have it set aside and the tenant evicted. If you miss that deadline, you cannot bring enforcement without a judgment for the same claim again (Art. 269(3), 269/b İİK).
4. The enforcement court’s eviction order does not have to become final before it is carried out, but **ten days** must pass after it is pronounced to or served on the tenant (Art. 269/c İİK).

If the tenant’s objection does not clearly and expressly deny the lease and their signature, they are deemed to have accepted the lease (Art. 269(2) İİK). If they do deny it, setting the objection aside in the enforcement court requires a lease drawn up by a notary or with a notarially certified signature (Art. 269/b İİK). A tenant who claims to have paid must prove it with a document drawn up or certified by a notary or acknowledged by you, or with an official receipt (Art. 269/c İİK).

Mandatory mediation does not apply to this route: Article 18/B(1)(a) HUAK expressly excludes eviction of leased property through enforcement without a judgment (ilamsız icra) under the İİK. If an objection forces you to bring a claim in the ordinary courts, mediation comes first for that claim.

## Notarial notice, mediation and an eviction claim

Instead of enforcement, you can have a notary serve a notice that meets Article 315 TBK and bring an eviction claim once the period has run. Claims arising from a lease are heard by the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) (Art. 4(1)(a) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). Applying to a mediator first is a precondition for the claim (Art. 18/B(1)(a) HUAK); a claim filed without it is dismissed on procedural grounds (Art. 18/A(2)). The mediator must conclude the process within three weeks of appointment, extendable by at most one week where necessary (Art. 18/A(9)).

Article 315 TBK sets no separate deadline for this claim; the six-month period in Article 269/a İİK belongs to the enforcement route.

## Two justified notices: requirements and time limit for the claim

If the tenant pays in the end but keeps paying late, Article 352(2) TBK applies. In an unofficial translation:

> If, in leases of less than one year, during the lease term, and in leases of one year or longer, within one lease year or a period exceeding one lease year, the tenant has given cause for two justified written notices for failing to pay the rent, the landlord may end the lease by bringing a claim within one month from the end of the lease term and, for leases of more than one year, from the end of the lease year in which the notices were given.

The Turkish text:

> Kiracı, bir yıldan kısa süreli kira sözleşmelerinde kira süresi içinde; bir yıl ve daha uzun süreli kira sözleşmelerinde ise bir kira yılı veya bir kira yılını aşan süre içinde kira bedelini ödemediği için kendisine yazılı olarak iki haklı ihtarda bulunulmasına sebep olmuşsa kiraya veren, kira süresinin ve bir yıldan uzun süreli kiralarda ihtarların yapıldığı kira yılının bitiminden başlayarak bir ay içinde, dava yoluyla kira sözleşmesini sona erdirebilir.

What follows from the text:

- The notices must be **in writing** and based on unpaid rent.
- For leases of one year or longer, both notices must fall within the **same lease year**. A lease year is not a calendar year; it runs from the start date of the lease.
- The law does not treat payment after a notice as a bar to eviction; what counts is that the tenant “gave cause” for the notice.
- This route is only available by **court claim**, not through enforcement.
- The claim must be brought within **one month** from the end of the lease year in which the notices were given (for leases under one year, from the end of the term). If you tell the tenant in writing within that month that you will sue, the time limit is extended by one lease year (Art. 353 TBK).

Mediation is again required first. From the application to the mediation office until the final report, time limits that extinguish a right (hak düşürücü süre) do not run (Art. 18/A(15) HUAK); the law does not expressly say whether the one-month period is such a time limit. In the author’s assessment, the safest course is to apply for mediation within the month after the lease year ends and to file the claim promptly after the final report.

Some details are not in the statute and have been left to the case law: whether the two notices must concern rent for different months, when a notice counts as “justified”, and whether it must have reached the tenant. No Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) decision on these points was verified for this article, which is why they are not in the list above as firm rules. The author recommends sending each notice for a different month’s late rent, in a way that proves it reached the tenant.

## What happens to the guarantor?

Eviction is sought only against the tenant; from the guarantor (kefil) you can only claim the rent owed. A guarantee is valid only if it is in writing, states the maximum amount and the date, and the guarantor writes the maximum amount, the date and, where applicable, joint and several liability (müteselsil kefalet) **in their own handwriting** (Art. 583 TBK). A married guarantor also needs their spouse’s written consent (Art. 584). If any of these is missing, the guarantee is invalid.

Under a valid guarantee, the guarantor is liable up to the agreed maximum (Art. 589). With an ordinary guarantor (adi kefil) you must pursue the tenant first; you can go straight to the guarantor only in the cases the law lists, for example once a final certificate of insolvency (kesin aciz belgesi) has been issued against the tenant (Art. 585). A joint and several guarantor can be pursued without first pursuing the tenant if the tenant is late in paying and the reminder has failed, or if the tenant is clearly unable to pay (Art. 586). A guarantee given by a natural person ends automatically ten years after it was made (Art. 598(3)). Whether the guarantor is liable for rent from extended lease years depends on the wording of the guarantee and can be disputed; no decision on this was reviewed for this article.

## Landlords living abroad: practical points

You do not need to travel to Turkey for enforcement, notices, mediation or a claim. Your lawyer in Turkey must file a power of attorney drawn up or certified by a notary (Art. 76(1) HMK); you can grant it at a Turkish consulate in Germany or the Netherlands. You can take part in mediation in person or through your lawyer (Art. 15(6) HUAK); even if you have a lawyer, the mediator also informs you directly (Art. 18/A(7)).

The six-month period for setting an objection aside runs from service of the objection on you, so keep your address for service in Turkey up to date. The author recommends agreeing in the lease that rent is paid into a bank account and keeping a list of when each month’s rent arrived; for the two-notices route, that list shows which lease year you are in.

## Frequently asked questions

### How soon can I send a notice if my tenant in Turkey does not pay?

The law sets no waiting period: as soon as the rent is due and unpaid, you can send written notice under Article 315 TBK. For residential and business premises, the notice must give at least thirty days to pay.

### Can I start enforcement proceedings in Turkey for unpaid rent?

Yes. You can bring enforcement proceedings for the rent; if you also ask for eviction, the tenant receives a payment order with an eviction warning (Art. 269 İİK). Mediation is not required for this (Art. 18/B(1)(a) HUAK).

### When must an eviction claim based on two justified notices be filed?

Within one month from the end of the lease year in which the notices were given; for leases under one year, from the end of the term (Art. 352(2) TBK). Mediation comes first.

### Is the guarantor liable if a tenant in Turkey does not pay rent?

Under a valid guarantee, the guarantor is liable for the rent up to the agreed maximum (Arts. 583 and 589 TBK). Only the tenant is evicted; the guarantor is pursued only for money.

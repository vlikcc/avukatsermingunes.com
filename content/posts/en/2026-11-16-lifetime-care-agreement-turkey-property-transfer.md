Under Turkish law, a lifetime care agreement (ölünceye kadar bakma sözleşmesi) is a contract in which one person undertakes to look after another until death, and the person being cared for undertakes to transfer property in return, usually a house, flat or plot of land (Article 611 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK). The agreement is valid only if made in the form of an inheritance contract, that is, before an official and two witnesses (Article 612 TBK); where real estate is transferred, this is done at the land registry office (tapu müdürlüğü) with two witnesses.

It is common for a parent living in Turkey to transfer their home this way to the child who cares for them. Brothers and sisters living in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere often learn of the transfer only after the parent dies. This post covers the form, when the title passes, the carer's duties, how the agreement ends, and how it relates to reserved shares and sham transfer claims. Current as of November 2026; Articles 611 to 619 TBK have not been amended since they came into force.

## What is a lifetime care agreement?

The Code calls the person cared for the “care creditor” (bakım alacaklısı) and the carer the “care debtor” (bakım borçlusu). The care creditor undertakes to transfer all of their assets or certain assets (Article 611(1) TBK). On signing, the care creditor joins the carer's household community (Article 614(1) TBK).

If the care creditor appoints the carer as heir, the rules on inheritance contracts also apply (Article 611(2) TBK). Whether the property passes during the parent's lifetime or after death therefore changes the legal consequences.

## Required form: where and how is it signed?

Under Article 612(1) TBK, a lifetime care agreement is invalid unless made in the form of an inheritance contract, even if it does not appoint an heir. An inheritance contract follows the form of an official will: the parties state their wishes to the official at the same time and sign before the official and two witnesses (Article 545 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, TMK). The official may be a civil court judge (sulh hâkimi), a notary or another official authorised by law (Article 532(2) TMK).

The care creditor's spouse, descendants, siblings and their spouses cannot act as witnesses (Article 536(1) TMK), so the parent's other children cannot be witnesses either. The witnesses state in writing that they consider the care creditor capable of making the disposition (Article 534(2) TMK).

Contracts to transfer ownership of land must be made in official form (Article 706(1) TMK), and the land registry office draws up these deeds (Article 26 of the Land Registry Law No. 2644). Article 24(1) of the Land Registry Regulation (Tapu Sicili Tüzüğü) expressly requires two witnesses for a lifetime care agreement. In practice the agreement is therefore usually signed at the land registry, and the transfer is registered at the same time.

A state-recognised care institution that follows the conditions set by the authorities only needs written form (Article 612(2) TBK). This exception does not apply to agreements within the family.

### Is a medical report required?

Neither the law nor the Land Registry Regulation requires a medical report from a certain age. If the land registry, judging by what a person says and how they behave, has doubts about their capacity to act, it asks for a medical report with a photo on their capacity to understand (Article 19(2) of the Regulation). The report's date and number are written into the deed.

My assessment: if the care creditor is very old, a report obtained shortly before the appointment makes proof easier if capacity is disputed later, even when the registry does not ask for one. If the care creditor is under guardianship (vasi), the agreement needs the consent of both the guardianship authority and the supervisory authority (Article 463, point 4, TMK). See: [Guardianship of an elderly parent in Turkey](/en/publications/guardianship-elderly-parent-turkey).

## When is the title deed transferred?

The agreement sets the time. There are two routes:

1. **Transfer during the parent's lifetime.** The property is registered in the carer's name on the same day. The care creditor is protected by a statutory mortgage over the transferred property, like a seller (Article 613 TBK). A seller's statutory mortgage must be registered within three months of the transfer of ownership (Articles 893 and 894 TMK). That this period should also be observed by the care creditor, because Article 613 says “like a seller”, is my assessment.
2. **Transfer after death.** If the carer is to receive the property only after the care creditor dies, the agreement appoints an heir and the rules on inheritance contracts apply (Article 611(2) TBK). The care creditor remains free to deal with their assets, but dispositions on death and gifts that conflict with the agreement can be challenged (Article 527(2) TMK).

If the care creditor refuses to make an agreed transfer, the carer can ask the court to transfer ownership by judgment (Article 716(1) TMK).

## The carer's duties

The carer must provide what fairness requires, given the value of what they received and the care creditor's previous social standing. In particular, the carer must provide suitable food and housing and, in illness, care for the care creditor with due diligence and arrange treatment (Article 614(1) and (2) TBK). The care creditor cannot assign this right to anyone else (Article 619(1) TBK).

The law does not say whether care provided through others, for example a paid carer, is enough. My assessment: it depends on the wording of the agreement and the fairness standard in Article 614 TBK; writing down how and by whom care will be given avoids disputes later.

## How does the agreement end? Termination and annulment

The agreement is fulfilled when the care creditor dies. The law also provides:

- **Termination with notice.** If there is a significant imbalance between what each side gives and the side receiving more cannot prove that a gift was intended, the other side may terminate at any time on six months' notice (Article 616(1) TBK). What was performed until then is balanced out (Article 616(2)).
- **Termination without notice.** If a breach makes it unbearable to continue, or other important reasons make it impossible or excessively difficult, either party may terminate immediately. The party at fault returns what it received and pays the other appropriate compensation (Article 617(1) TBK). Instead, the court may end the shared household and order a life annuity for the care creditor (Article 617(2)).
- **Death of the carer.** The care creditor may ask for termination within one year (Article 618 TBK).
- **Annulment by maintenance creditors.** If the agreement leaves the care creditor unable to meet legal maintenance obligations, those deprived of maintenance may ask for annulment; the court may order maintenance instead (Article 615(1) and (2) TBK).

A claim to recover the property after termination is brought before the court where the property is located (Article 12(1) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK).

This point is contested. The termination rights in Articles 616 and 617 TBK belong to the parties. Whether the heirs, after the care creditor's death, can terminate on the ground that no care was given is not expressly regulated; how a court would decide in a given case is uncertain.

## Reserved shares, reduction and sham transfer claims

The transferred home may infringe the reserved share (saklı pay) of the other children. Article 615(3) TBK makes this clear: the heirs' right to bring a reduction claim and creditors' right to bring an annulment claim are preserved.

Descendants, parents and the surviving spouse have reserved shares (Article 506 TMK). A reduction claim (tenkis davası) must be brought within one year of learning of the infringement and in any case within ten years of the death (Article 571(1) TMK). Details: [Forced heirship in Turkey and the reduction claim](/en/publications/forced-heirship-turkey-reduction-claim-time-limit).

This point is contested. Article 565 TMK subjects only “gratuitous” transfers to reduction, while a lifetime care agreement involves performance on both sides. According to a view put forward in legal writing, reduction applies to the extent that the difference between the value of the property and the value of the care amounts to a gift. The law contains no express calculation rule, and no binding court decision is reported in this post; which standard a court would apply in a given case is uncertain. If the agreement appoints the carer as heir (Article 611(2) TBK), a carer whose benefit is reduced may claim back the consideration they gave in proportion to the reduction (Article 566(2) TMK).

A sham transfer claim (muris muvazaası) is different: the land registry shows a lifetime care agreement, but no care was actually intended and the transaction hid a gift. The type and content of a contract are determined by the parties' real and common intention, regardless of words used to conceal it (Article 19(1) TBK). A registration based on a non-binding transaction is unjustified, and its correction can be claimed in court (Articles 1024(2) and 1025(1) TMK). The law does not list the criteria (whether care was actually given, the value of the property compared with the care, whether the other children received other assets); they come from case law, and each case turns on its evidence. Requirements and the difference from reduction: [Sham sale of property by a parent](/en/publications/inheritance-law-turkey-sham-sale-property).

## If you live in Germany or the Netherlands

Rights in rem over property in Turkey, and the form of transactions concerning them, are governed by Turkish law (Article 21(1) and (4) of the Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure, MÖHUK).

Article 26 of the Land Registry Law allows the parties' declarations to be taken separately for contracts transferring land when the parties are at different land registry offices or at a Turkish mission abroad. This point is contested: because Article 545(2) TMK requires the parties to state their wishes to the official at the same time, the law does not make clear whether this route is available for a lifetime care agreement. Ask the relevant land registry office in advance.

Turkish law always governs succession to property in Turkey (Article 20(1) MÖHUK). The tax, social security or inheritance consequences of the transfer in Germany or the Netherlands depend on the law of that country; please consult a lawyer or tax adviser admitted there.

## Frequently asked questions

### What form does a lifetime care agreement in Turkey need?

The form of an inheritance contract: before an official and two witnesses (Article 612(1) TBK, Article 545 TMK). For real estate it is signed at the land registry with two witnesses (Article 24(1) Tapu Sicili Tüzüğü).

### When is the title deed transferred to the carer?

When the agreement says. For a transfer during the parent's lifetime, on the same day at the land registry; if the property is to pass only after death, the rules on inheritance contracts apply (Article 611(2) TBK).

### Is a medical report required?

Not from a certain age. The land registry asks for one if it doubts a person's capacity to act (Article 19(2) Tapu Sicili Tüzüğü).

### What if the agreement infringes my reserved share?

The heirs' right to reduction is preserved (Article 615(3) TBK); to what extent it applies is contested. If it is argued that the agreement hid a gift, a claim to cancel the registration on the ground of a sham transfer may be considered.

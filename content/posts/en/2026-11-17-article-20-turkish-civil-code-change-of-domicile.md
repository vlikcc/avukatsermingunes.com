Article 20 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says when a domicile (yerleşim yeri) changes and where a person whose domicile cannot be established is treated as domiciled. The old domicile continues until a new one is acquired. Someone who has given up a domicile abroad but has not yet settled anywhere in Türkiye is domiciled where they currently live. If you move back from Germany or the Netherlands for good, this second rule decides which court has jurisdiction during the first months after your return.

## Article 20 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **2. Change of domicile and place of residence**
>
> Article 20 – A change of domicile depends on the acquisition of a new one.
>
> Where a person's previous domicile is unknown, or where a person has given up a domicile abroad but has not yet acquired a domicile in Türkiye, the place where that person currently lives is deemed to be the domicile.

Turkish original:

> **2. Yerleşim yerinin değiştirilmesi ve oturma yeri**
>
> Madde 20- Bir yerleşim yerinin değiştirilmesi yenisinin edinilmesine bağlıdır.
>
> Önceki yerleşim yeri belli olmayan veya yabancı ülkedeki yerleşim yerini bıraktığı hâlde Türkiye'de henüz bir yerleşim yeri edinmemiş olan kimsenin hâlen oturduğu yer, yerleşim yeri sayılır.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 20; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589 (Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 20 of the former Code and was taken from Article 20 of the 1984 preliminary draft. A person who has not yet acquired a domicile is domiciled where they live temporarily, without intending to settle. This follows from the principle that everyone must have a domicile (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### Moving out is not enough

Domicile is the place where a person lives with the intention of staying permanently, and nobody can have more than one domicile at a time (Article 19 TMK). Article 20(1) adds a rule on timing: your old domicile does not end on the day you move out, but only when you start living somewhere new with the intention to stay. In between, you remain legally tied to the old place.

### When does the place of residence count?

Paragraph 2 replaces domicile with the place of actual residence (oturma yeri) in two situations: where the previous domicile is unknown, and where someone has left a domicile abroad without yet acquiring one in Türkiye. No intention to stay is needed; the place where you actually are at the time is enough. The second situation only covers moving from abroad to Türkiye. If you move from Türkiye to another country, the general rule in paragraph 1 applies.

### What depends on domicile

Mostly, which court has jurisdiction:

- The court of general jurisdiction is the court of the defendant's domicile on the date the claim is filed; domicile is determined under the TMK (Article 6 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK).
- If the defendant has no domicile in Türkiye, the court of their habitual residence (mutad mesken) in Türkiye has general jurisdiction (Article 9 HMK).
- A divorce may be filed at the domicile of either spouse or where the spouses last lived together for six months (Article 168 TMK).
- Succession opens at the deceased's domicile, and claims for abatement, partition and recovery of the estate are heard there (Article 576 TMK).

## What this means in practice if you live abroad

### The first months after moving back

Take someone who gives up their home in Germany, returns to Türkiye and stays at a holiday flat in Bodrum while looking for a home in Antalya. The German domicile is gone, and there is no place in Türkiye yet where they live with the intention to stay. Under Article 20(2), Bodrum counts as their domicile. A debt claim against them falls, as a rule, to the court in Bodrum (Article 6 HMK). If they die during this period, reading Article 20(2) together with Article 576 TMK, the succession opens in Bodrum. Once they live in Antalya intending to stay, Antalya becomes the domicile.

### When does the domicile in Germany end?

If you keep your domicile in Germany or the Netherlands and spend a few months in Türkiye, it does not change (Article 20(1) TMK). The law does not tie the date of a permanent return to any particular document; it is a question of fact, and there is no statutory list of decisive circumstances. In my assessment, a court would look at several facts together: deregistration in Germany, renting or buying a home in Türkiye, moving your work or pension to Türkiye, and registering your address in Türkiye.

### Address registration and domicile

The Turkish Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu) also defines the "domicile address" as the place where a person lives with the intention of staying permanently (Article 3(ee)). The address is registered with the civil registry office (nüfus müdürlüğü) or with a Turkish consulate or embassy, and registration within twenty working days is mandatory (Article 50(2) and (3) Law No. 5490). For Turkish citizens whose domicile address is abroad, the address is recorded according to the data of their country of residence or the mission they belong to (Article 49(3)). My assessment: the registered address is strong evidence of domicile, but Article 6(2) HMK refers to the TMK for the definition of domicile. If the register and the facts differ, it depends on the case which one the court will follow.

## Frequently asked questions

### I have moved back for good but have no home yet. Where is my domicile?

If you have given up your domicile abroad and have not yet settled anywhere in Türkiye, the place where you currently live is treated as your domicile (Article 20(2) TMK).

### Does my domicile change if I spend a few months in Türkiye?

No. Domicile changes only when a new one is acquired (Article 20(1) TMK). A holiday or short work stay in Türkiye does not end your domicile in Germany.

### Is domicile the same as my registered address in Türkiye?

Both use the same test (Article 19 TMK, Article 3(ee) Law No. 5490). For court jurisdiction, however, domicile is determined under the TMK (Article 6(2) HMK); the registered address is one of the main pieces of evidence.

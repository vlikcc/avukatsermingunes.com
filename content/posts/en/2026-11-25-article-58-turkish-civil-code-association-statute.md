Article 58 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) deals with the statute of an association (dernek tüzüğü): every association has one, it must contain certain items, and it may not conflict with mandatory law. It matters to anyone drafting a statute to found an association in Türkiye and to associations that want to amend theirs.

## Article 58 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Statute**
>
> Article 58 – Every association has a statute.
>
> The statute of an association must state its name, purpose, (...) sources of income, conditions of membership, organs and organisation, and the provisional board of directors.
>
> The statute of an association may not conflict with the mandatory provisions of the law.
>
> Matters not regulated in the statute are governed by the provisions of the law.

Original Turkish text:

> **III. Tüzük**
>
> Madde 58- Her derneğin bir tüzüğü bulunur.
>
> Dernek tüzüğünde derneğin adı, amacı, (...) gelir kaynakları, üyelik koşulları, organları ve örgütü ile geçici yönetim kurulunun gösterilmesi zorunludur.
>
> Dernek tüzüğü, kanunun emredici hükümlerine aykırı olamaz.
>
> Dernek tüzüğünde düzenlenmemiş konularda kanun hükümleri uygulanır.

Footnote to “(...)” in paragraph 2 of the official text: Article 38 of Law No. 5253 of 4 November 2004 removed the words “yerleşim yeri, kurucuları” (domicile, founders) from the text.

As of November 2026, the amendment table on mevzuat.gov.tr shows only this Law No. 5253 entry for Article 58 (in force since 23 November 2004); the provision has not changed since (latest law in the table: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The official reasoning calls the statute “in effect the constitution” of the association and requires the members of the provisional board to be named in it. The last two paragraphs state that the statute may not contradict mandatory law and that the law applies where the statute is silent (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised). The reasoning refers to the original wording, which also required the domicile and the founders to be stated.

## Explanation

### The detailed list is in Article 4 of the Associations Law

Article 58(2) TMK names five items. The more detailed list is in Article 4 of the Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu). Under it the statute must state:

1. The name of the association and its seat (merkez).
2. Its purpose, the fields and methods of work used to pursue it, and its area of activity.
3. The conditions and form for joining and leaving.
4. How and when the general meeting (genel kurul) is convened, its duties and powers, and its voting and decision rules.
5. The duties and powers of the board and the audit committee, how they are elected, and the number of full and substitute members.
6. Whether there will be branches (şube) and, if so, how they are set up, their powers and how they are represented at the general meeting.
7. How the joining fee and annual dues are set.
8. Borrowing procedures and forms of internal audit.
9. How the statute is amended.
10. How the assets are wound up if the association is dissolved.

Even though “domicile” was removed from Article 58 TMK, the statute still states a place: the Associations Law requires the seat (merkez), meaning the province or district where the association is domiciled (Articles 2(c) and 4(a) of Law No. 5253). The difference between domicile and seat is explained in the post on [Article 51 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-51-turkish-civil-code-domicile-legal-person). The provisional board is not listed in Article 4, but Article 58(2) TMK is still in force, so it is named in the statute.

### The statute sets the limits of what the association may do

An association may not act outside the purpose and fields of work stated in its statute (Article 30(a) of Law No. 5253). It may open branches only if the statute provides for them (Article 4(g); Associations Regulation, Article 7), and it may set up a mutual fund for members only if the statute says so (Article 12). How you word the purpose decides what the association can do later.

Where the statute is silent, the association provisions of the TMK and Law No. 5253 apply; where that Law is silent too, the TMK applies (Article 58(4) TMK; Article 36 of Law No. 5253). At formation the authority also reviews the statute (Article 60 TMK); see the post on [Article 57 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-57-turkish-civil-code-right-to-form-association).

### Amending the statute

The general meeting amends the statute by a two-thirds majority of the members present (Article 81 TMK). The amendment is reported to the authority as an annex to the general meeting results notice (genel kurul sonuç bildirimi), together with the minutes, the old and new wording of the amended articles and the full new statute, each page signed by a majority of the board members (Associations Regulation, Articles 17(1) and 92(2)).

The Law and the Regulation give different deadlines. Article 23(1) of Law No. 5253 sets “thirty days following the general meeting” for the results notice; Articles 17 and 92 of the Regulation have set forty-five days since the amendment of 21 October 2021. This point is disputed: there is no express provision resolving the difference and no binding court decision, and it is not certain which deadline a court would apply. In the author's assessment, filing within thirty days carries the least risk, because the Law ranks above the Regulation.

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

Associations keep their records and correspond with public authorities in Turkish (Article 31 of Law No. 5253), and the statute is drawn up in Turkish. At formation every founder signs every page of the statute (Associations Regulation, Article 5(a)), including founders who live abroad. The Regulation does not say where or in what form that signature is given.

An association in Türkiye may open branches or representative offices abroad and cooperate with organisations there for the purposes in its statute (Article 5(1) of Law No. 5253). If you plan to work with the community in Germany or the Netherlands, put this in the purpose and fields of work from the start, because of the limit in Article 30(a). An association registered in Germany or the Netherlands is governed by that country's law; for that, consult a lawyer authorised to practise there.

## Frequently asked questions

### What must the statute of an association in Türkiye contain?

At least the name, purpose, sources of income, conditions of membership, organs and organisation, and the provisional board (Article 58(2) TMK). The detailed list in Article 4 of Law No. 5253 adds, among other things, the seat, dues, borrowing, internal audit, amendment and winding-up.

### Does an authority have to approve the statute in advance?

As a rule, no. The statute is filed with the founding notice, the association has legal personality from that moment, and the authority reviews the statute afterwards within sixty days (Articles 59 and 60 TMK).

### How is the statute amended?

By a decision of the general meeting with a two-thirds majority of the members present (Article 81 TMK). The amendment is reported to the authority with the general meeting results notice (Associations Regulation, Article 92(2)).

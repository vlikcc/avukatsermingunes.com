Article 64 is one of the final provisions of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK). It repeals the former private international law act, Law No. 2675, and two individual provisions of other laws. Law No. 5718 was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 12 December 2007, No. 26728, and under Article 65 entered into force on the day of publication. In practice you meet Article 64 in cases from before 2007 and in provisions that still refer to the old law.

## Article 64 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Repealed provisions**
>
> Article 64 – (1) Law No. 2675 of 20.5.1982 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure,
>
> (2) the second paragraph of Article 866 of Turkish Commercial Code No. 6762 of 29.6.1956,
>
> (3) Article 88 of Law No. 5846 of 5.12.1951 on Intellectual and Artistic Works
>
> are repealed.

Turkish original:

> **Yürürlükten kaldırılan hükümler**
>
> MADDE 64 – (1) 20/5/1982 tarihli ve 2675 sayılı Milletlerarası Özel Hukuk ve Usul Hukuku Hakkında Kanun,
>
> (2) 29/6/1956 tarihli ve 6762 sayılı Türk Ticaret Kanununun 866 ncı maddesinin ikinci fıkrası,
>
> (3) 5/12/1951 tarihli ve 5846 sayılı Fikir ve Sanat Eserleri Kanununun 88 inci maddesi,
>
> yürürlükten kaldırılmıştır.

The article has not changed since 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment list of the current text in UYAP Mevzuat shows only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, Article 866(2) TTK was repealed because the new provision on rights in rem in means of transport deals with the matter more clearly, and Article 88 FSEK because of the new provision covering all intellectual property rights. The reasoning uses the bill’s numbering (Articles 21 and 22 there); in the law these are Article 22 (means of transport) and Article 23 (intellectual property) (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 63 of the bill).

## Explanation

### Where the repealed matters are governed today

Rights in rem in aircraft, ships and rail vehicles are now governed by the law of the country of origin under Article 22 MÖHUK. Intellectual property rights are governed under Article 23 by the law of the country for which protection is claimed.

In the current text of Law No. 5846, Article 88 sits under the subheading “Kanunlar ihtilafı” (conflict of laws) and now carries only the note “(Mülga: 27/11/2007-5718/64 md.)”, meaning “repealed by Article 64 of Law No. 5718”. Commercial Code No. 6762 itself was later repealed in full by Article 1533 of Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102.

### Cases from the time of the old law

MÖHUK has no transitional provision; it ends with the final provisions in Articles 64 to 66 and contains no provisional articles. In the Court of Cassation decisions I have read, Law No. 2675 “in force on the date the action was filed” was applied to proceedings started before 12 December 2007 (see below).

This question is disputed. The law has no express transitional rule, and the decisions I read rely on the filing date only for procedural matters (security for costs, reciprocity in enforcement). Which law governs the applicable-law question, for example for a legal relationship created before 2007, cannot be derived from them, and I have not found a binding decision on it. In my assessment, an action filed today to recognise or enforce a foreign judgment given before 2007 is governed by Law No. 5718, in force on the filing date. Which view a court will take in a particular case is uncertain.

### Provisions that still refer to Law No. 2675

Article 4(1)(2) of Law No. 4787 on Family Courts still lists among the family courts’ duties the recognition and enforcement of foreign family-law judgments “under Law No. 2675”; the reference has not been updated to Law No. 5718. In my assessment, the provision today covers family-law recognition and enforcement proceedings conducted under Law No. 5718. On recognition of foreign divorces, see [Article 58 MÖHUK – recognition](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-mohuk-recognition-foreign-judgments-turkey).

## What this means if you live in Germany or the Netherlands

If you have a divorce granted in Germany or the Netherlands before 2007 that has not yet been recognised in Turkey, a recognition action filed today is governed by Articles 58 et seq. MÖHUK (per the assessment above). For Article 59, the date on which the judgment became final matters: [Article 59 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-59-mohuk-res-judicata-recognition-date).

## Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) case law

**Yargıtay 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/4033, K. 2015/11495, 03.11.2015**

In an action to enforce a judgment of the Syrian Court of Cassation, the Chamber examined reciprocity under Article 38 of Law No. 2675, “in force on the date the action was filed”. Granting the action although de facto reciprocity between Turkey and Syria had not been proven led to reversal.

**Yargıtay 18th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/23247, K. 2016/2964, 23.02.2016**

In an action filed on 26.02.2007 to lift a curatorship (kayyımlık), the Chamber found that the claimant was a foreigner and sent the file back to the lower court to document whether security had been provided under Article 32 of Law No. 2675, “in force on the date the action was filed”.

## Frequently asked questions

### When did Law No. 2675 cease to be in force?

On 12 December 2007, when Law No. 5718 was published and entered into force (Articles 64(1) and 65 MÖHUK).

### Is there a transitional provision between the old and new law?

No. The Court of Cassation decisions I read applied Law No. 2675 to procedural matters in proceedings filed before 2007.

Article 54 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) governs who receives the assets left after a legal person that has come to an end is liquidated. If neither the law nor the founding document says otherwise, and the competent organ has not decided otherwise, the assets pass to the public body or institution pursuing the closest purpose. This concerns members, donors and board members of a dissolved association.

## Article 54 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Allocation of assets**
>
> Article 54 – Unless the law or the founding document provides otherwise, or the competent organ decides otherwise, the assets of a legal person pass to the public body or institution pursuing the closest purpose.
>
> These assets are, as far as possible, used for the purpose to which they were previously devoted.
>
> The assets of a legal person whose personality was ended by court decision because it pursued a purpose contrary to law or morality pass in every case to the competent public body.

Turkish original:

> **III. Malvarlığının özgülenmesi**
>
> Madde 54- Tüzel kişinin malvarlığı, kanunda veya kuruluş belgesinde başka bir hüküm bulunmadıkça ya da yetkili organı başka türlü karar vermedikçe, en yakın amacı güden kamu kurum veya kuruluşuna geçer.
>
> Bu malvarlığı olanak ölçüsünde daha önce özgülendiği amaç için kullanılır.
>
> Hukuka veya ahlâka aykırı amaç güttüğü için kişiliği mahkeme kararıyla sona eren tüzel kişinin malvarlığı her hâlde ilgili kamu kuruluşuna geçer.

As of November 2026, Article 54 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The article corresponds to Article 50 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The old marginal heading “Malların tahsisi” was replaced by the modern “Malvarlığının özgülenmesi”, and the article was split into three paragraphs, following Article 57 of the Swiss Civil Code and Article 51 of the 1984 preliminary draft. According to the reasoning, the substance was not changed (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Order: law, founding document, organ resolution, public body

Paragraph 1 puts the public body last. For associations and for foundations set up under the Civil Code there is “another provision of law”, which applies first:

- In associations, liquidation follows the statutes; if the statutes leave it to the general assembly and no resolution is passed, or the association was dissolved by court, its money, property and rights pass by court decision to the association with the closest purpose and the most members on the date of closure (Art. 15(1) Associations Law No. 5253). The fallback recipient is therefore another association, not a public body.
- In foundations (yeni vakıf), what remains passes according to the foundation deed or, if the deed is silent, by court decision after consulting the Directorate General of Foundations, to a foundation with a similar purpose; for dissolved foundations, it passes to the Directorate General (Art. 27 Foundations Law No. 5737).

These rules are covered in more detail in the post on [Article 52 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-52-turkish-civil-code-dissolved-association-liquidation). What is specific to Article 54 is in paragraphs 2 and 3.

### Tied to the former purpose

Paragraph 2 asks that assets passing under paragraph 1 be used, as far as possible, for their former purpose. A building that passes from an education association to a public body should, where possible, still serve education. The words “as far as possible” set a direction, not a strict duty of use.

Whether the competent organ, such as the general assembly, may decide to distribute the assets among the members is not expressly regulated. Associations cannot be formed to share profits (Art. 56 TMK), and a member who leaves or is expelled has no claim to the association's assets (Art. 68(3) TMK). The question is disputed; there is no express statutory rule on it and no binding court decision verified for this post, and how a court would decide in a given case is not certain.

### Unlawful or immoral purpose: “in every case”

Paragraph 3 overrides the founding document and any organ resolution. If personality was ended by court decision because the purpose was contrary to law or morality, the assets pass to the competent public body. For associations, this is dissolution by court where the purpose becomes contrary to law or morality (Art. 89 TMK). A foundation with a prohibited purpose or activities is dissolved (Art. 116(2) TMK), and its remaining assets pass to the Directorate General of Foundations (Art. 27 Law No. 5737), which points the same way as paragraph 3.

For associations, however, Article 15(1) of Law No. 5253 sends the assets of an association dissolved by court to the association with the closest purpose. How this relates to Article 54(3) TMK is disputed. The law does not say expressly how the two fit together, and there is no binding court decision on it verified for this post; which provision a court will apply in a given case is not certain. If an investigation or court case for dissolution is pending, nothing is transferred until it is concluded (Art. 15(3) Law No. 5253).

## Practical points for people living abroad

If a Turkish association of which you are a member or donor is dissolved, the liquidation clause in its statutes is the first place that shows where the remaining assets go. If you have left or been expelled, you have no claim to the association's assets (Art. 68(3) TMK). For the rules on how liquidation is carried out, see the post on [Article 53 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-53-turkish-civil-code-liquidation-assets).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do the assets of a dissolved association in Türkiye go to the state?

Not automatically. The statutes and Article 15 of Law No. 5253 apply first, and under them the fallback recipient is the association with the closest purpose and the most members. The transfer to a public body under Article 54(1) TMK applies where the law, the founding document and the organ's resolution say nothing.

### Who gets the assets of an association closed by court?

Article 54(3) TMK gives the assets of a legal person dissolved for an unlawful or immoral purpose to the competent public body; Article 15(1) of Law No. 5253 names the association with the closest purpose for associations dissolved by court. How the two relate is disputed.

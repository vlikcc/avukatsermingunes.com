In a Turkish salary garnishment (maaş haczi), the amount seized may not be less than one quarter of the salary (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, İİK Art. 83(2)). For employees covered by the Labour Law, no more than one quarter of the wage may be seized (Labour Law No. 4857 Art. 35), and that provision leaves the rights of maintenance creditors untouched. A pension paid by the Turkish Social Security Institution (SGK) is in principle exempt: apart from maintenance debts and the SGK's own claims, it can only be garnished with the debtor's consent (muvafakat) (Law No. 5510 Art. 93(1)).

This article covers the stage after the enforcement proceedings have become final. For objecting to a payment order and the deadlines, see [Turkish payment order: objection deadline](/en/publications/turkey-payment-order-objection-deadline).

## How much of a salary can be garnished? What the law says

Under İİK Art. 83(1), salaries, wages of every kind, retirement pensions (tekaüt maaşları) and annuities paid by insurers can be seized after deducting the amount the enforcement officer considers necessary for the living costs of the debtor and their family. Paragraph 2 sets a floor: the amount seized may not be less than one quarter.

For employees a second rule applies. Under Art. 35 of the Labour Law, no more than one quarter of an employee's monthly wage may be seized. The amount a judge sets for family members the employee must support is not included in that figure, and the rights of maintenance creditors are reserved. Read together, the two provisions mean that one quarter of an employee's wage is withheld for a debt other than maintenance. The law does not say whether the quarter is calculated on net or gross pay; that is left to practice.

I found no separate garnishment rate for civil servants' salaries in the Civil Servants Law No. 657, so İİK Art. 83 applies. The family allowance (aile yardımı ödeneği) cannot be seized for debts (657 Art. 203).

There is no rule saying "the minimum wage cannot be garnished". Under Art. 83(1) the enforcement officer decides how much is left for living costs. The minimum wage appears in the İİK in other contexts (for example Arts. 338 and 354), not as a limit on salary garnishment.

**Has the garnishment rate changed?** As of November 2026, no. In the current text of the İİK on mevzuat.gov.tr, the amendment table ends with Law No. 7589, in force since 31 July 2026, which changed only Art. 114. Arts. 83, 83/a and 355–357 have not been amended in the last two years; the last change to Art. 82 (Law No. 7445, 2023) concerned household goods. I found no law changing the rate in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) issues published since then. Tiered garnishment rates mentioned in the press do not apply unless they are enacted.

## The employer's duties and the response deadline

The enforcement office (icra dairesi) notifies the employer or the paying institution of the garnishment in writing. Under İİK Art. 355, the recipient must report **within one week at the latest** that the garnishment has been applied and how much the debtor earns. Until the debt is paid, it withholds the garnished amount and sends it to the office. If the debtor changes post or leaves the job, the employer must report that at once.

If the employer fails to withhold, no separate lawsuit is needed: the enforcement office collects the amount directly from the employer's own assets, and the employer may claim it back from the debtor (İİK Art. 356). Anyone who fails without good reason to carry out the office's notices and orders is prosecuted directly by the public prosecutor (Art. 357).

## Several garnishments: the order of priority

The law says that where there are several garnishments, they are ranked, and no deduction may be made for a later garnishment until the deductions for the one ahead of it have ended (İİK Art. 83(2)). If three creditors have garnished your salary, the employer does not make three deductions at once; it pays off the first debt and then moves to the second. You can ask the employer or paying institution and the enforcement office which garnishment is first in line.

Agreeing in advance that a salary may be seized has no effect. Under İİK Art. 83/a, prior agreements that the assets and rights listed in Arts. 82 and 83 may be seized are invalid.

## Salary garnishment for maintenance (nafaka)

The law treats maintenance separately in three places. SGK benefits can be garnished for maintenance debts (5510 Art. 93(1)). The Labour Law's one-quarter cap does not affect the rights of maintenance creditors (4857 Art. 35). In the distribution schedule (sıra cetveli), individual enforcement follows the ranking used in bankruptcy (İİK Art. 140(2)), where family-law maintenance claims that fell due in the last year rank first (İİK Art. 206(4)).

Whether maintenance takes priority over earlier salary garnishments is not answered expressly by the law. This point is disputed. What follows are views taken in practice and in legal writing; there is no express statutory rule, and I could not verify a binding court decision in an official database for this article. Which view a court will adopt in a given case is uncertain. In practice, a maintenance garnishment is often deducted without waiting for its turn under Art. 83(2), and the one-quarter figure is not treated as a ceiling for maintenance. A complaint (şikâyet) against the enforcement office's decision is available (İİK Art. 16). On enforcing child maintenance, see [Child support in Turkey](/en/publications/child-support-turkey-increase-enforcement-abroad).

## Can a Turkish pension be garnished?

5510 Art. 93(1) reads (unofficial translation): "Income, pensions and allowances may not be seized except for claims to be pursued and collected under Article 88 and for maintenance debts. Requests to seize income, pensions and allowances whose seizure is prohibited under this paragraph shall be rejected by the enforcement director if the debtor's consent is absent."

İİK Art. 83(1) lists retirement pensions as partly seizable, but Art. 82(1)(1) exempts assets that a special law declares unseizable, and 5510 Art. 93 is such a special law. Your SGK pension can therefore be garnished for a bank loan, a credit card or rent arrears only with your consent. Maintenance debts and the SGK's claims under Art. 88 fall outside this protection.

When consent has to be given is disputed. The following is the author's assessment; there is no express statutory rule, and I could not verify a binding court decision in an official database for this article. Which view a court will adopt in a given case is uncertain. A clause in a loan agreement such as "I consent to the garnishment of my pension" runs into the ban on prior agreements in İİK Art. 83/a. The view that consent must be given at the garnishment stage therefore fits the wording of the law better.

5510 Art. 93 has not been amended in the last two years (the amendment table of the current text ends with Law No. 7594 of 18 August 2026; the last change to Art. 93 was made by Law No. 5838 in 2009). The protection covers income, pensions and allowances that the SGK pays under Law No. 5510. For pensions that include bought-back years abroad, see [Turkish pension: buying back years abroad](/en/publications/turkish-pension-buy-back-years-abroad).

## Salary garnishment when you live abroad

If you live in Germany or the Netherlands but receive a wage from an employer in Turkey or an SGK pension, the garnishment is served on that employer or institution in Turkey. İİK Arts. 83 and 355 and 5510 Art. 93 apply in the same way; where you live makes no difference.

It is different if your salary comes from an employer abroad. A Turkish enforcement office's measures take effect in Turkey; it cannot require an employer in Germany or the Netherlands to withhold under İİK Art. 355. A creditor who wants to reach that salary has to use the procedure provided by that country's law. Whether and how a Turkish judgment or enforcement file is recognised and enforced there is governed by local law, and you need a lawyer admitted in that country for it.

A creditor living abroad can conduct enforcement proceedings in Turkey through an authorised representative; the request for salary garnishment is made to the enforcement office. For the Turkish side, see [Enforcement and Insolvency Law](/en/practice-areas/enforcement-insolvency).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much of my salary can be garnished in Turkey?

No less than one quarter (İİK Art. 83(2)). For employees, no more than one quarter can be seized (4857 Art. 35); the rights of maintenance creditors are not affected by that cap.

### Can my Turkish pension be garnished?

An SGK pension can only be garnished with the debtor's consent, except for maintenance and SGK claims. Without consent, the enforcement director rejects the request (5510 Art. 93(1)).

### Are several salary garnishments deducted at the same time?

No. They are ranked, and a later one is deducted only after the earlier one has ended (İİK Art. 83(2)). The law does not say expressly whether maintenance moves ahead in that order.

### How quickly must the employer respond to a garnishment?

Within one week at the latest, it reports the garnishment and the amount of the salary to the enforcement office (İİK Art. 355).

### Can a Turkish enforcement office garnish my salary abroad?

Not directly. Its measures take effect in Turkey; a salary in Germany or the Netherlands is governed by that country's law.

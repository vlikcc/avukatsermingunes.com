Article 44 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) is the register-side counterpart of the presumption of death (ölüm karinesi, Article 31): a person who disappears in circumstances that make death certain is registered as dead on the order of the chief administrative officer, even if the body is never found. Under paragraph 2, any interested party can ask a court to establish whether the person is dead or alive. The article concerns families who lost relatives in earthquakes, floods, mining accidents or shipwrecks.

## Article 44 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Person whose body has not been found**
>
> Article 44 – If a person disappears in circumstances in which death must be regarded as certain, the person is entered in the register as dead on the order of the highest administrative officer of the place, even if the body has not been found.
>
> Nevertheless, any interested party may bring an action for a court to establish whether this person is dead or alive.

Original Turkish text:

> **II. Cesedi bulunamayan kişi**
>
> Madde 44- Bir kimse, ölümüne kesin gözle bakılmayı gerektiren durumlar içinde ortadan kaybolursa cesedi bulunamamış olsa bile, o yerin en büyük mülkî amirinin emriyle kütüğe ölü kaydı düşürülür.
>
> Bununla birlikte her ilgili, bu kişinin ölü veya sağ olduğunun mahkemece tespitini dava edebilir.

As of November 2026, Article 44 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 42 of the former Civil Code No. 743. It was simplified and split into two paragraphs following the Swiss Civil Code (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### How is the death entered?

That the person is treated as dead follows from Article 31 TMK; Article 44 deals with the register. No court decision is needed. The procedure is in Article 32 of the Population Services Law No. 5490 (Nüfus Hizmetleri Kanunu) and Article 39 of its Implementing Regulation:

- The application is made in writing by a descendant, a parent or grandparent, or a sibling, or failing them by the heirs, who document the event. A competent authority may also notify the registry office by official letter (Article 32(2)).
- If the documents, and any inquiry the registry office orders, prove the event and that the person was there at the time, the death is entered on the order of the administrative officer (Article 32(3)).

Article 44 TMK speaks of "the highest administrative officer of the place"; Article 32(1) of Law No. 5490 refers to the administrative officer "of the place where the application is made". No provision explains the difference. There is no waiting period and no public notice, which sets it apart from a declaration of absence, where a court decides after at least one year (Articles 32–33 TMK). Conditions of the presumption and its effects on inheritance and marriage: [Article 31 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-31-turkish-civil-code-presumption-of-death).

### Paragraph 2: action to establish death or life

The officer's order is not final. "Any interested party" may ask a court to establish whether the person is dead or alive. The law does not list who counts as interested. Examples are someone who claims the registered person is still alive, or a relative whose application was refused.

The law does not say expressly which court hears this action. Actions to correct civil register entries are brought before the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) of the place of residence (Law No. 5490, Article 36(1)(a)); under Article 36(1)(c), declaratory actions create a presumption for later actions to cancel or correct the entry. In the author's assessment the action under Article 44(2) follows these rules as well; there is no express provision. On corrections in general: [Article 39 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-39-turkish-civil-code-correcting-civil-registry).

If the person later reappears, the change is written in the remarks column of their register entry (Article 46 TMK).

## Practical points for people living abroad

The law has no separate procedure for Turkish citizens who disappear in a disaster abroad, and no provision gives consulates power to enter a death on the basis of the presumption. The wording "place where the application is made" in Article 32(1) of Law No. 5490 suggests that the application can be filed with a registry office in Türkiye; this is an assessment. If the documents are not enough, the remaining route is a court declaration of absence; the applicable law in cases with a foreign element is set out in Article 11 MÖHUK ([Article 11 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-11-mohuk-declaration-of-absence)). Foreign documents (accident report, passenger list, official letters) should carry an apostille and a Turkish translation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who orders the death entry when no body is found?

The chief administrative officer (the governor in a province, the kaymakam in a district) orders it and the registry office enters it; no court is involved (Article 44(1) TMK, Article 32 of Law No. 5490).

### Who can apply?

Descendants, ascendants or siblings, or failing them the heirs. A competent authority can also report the case officially (Law No. 5490, Article 32(2)).

### What if the officer refuses?

Any interested party can sue to establish death or life (Article 44(2) TMK). If death is only highly probable, a declaration of absence can be sought (Article 32 TMK).

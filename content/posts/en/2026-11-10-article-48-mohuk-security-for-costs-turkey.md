Article 48 of Turkish Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) requires foreigners who sue, join proceedings or start enforcement proceedings before Turkish authorities to give security for costs (yabancılık teminatı). Whether you are exempt depends on your nationality and where you live. A Turkish citizen living in Germany falls under Article 84 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure (HMK), not Article 48. Someone who holds only German or Dutch citizenship, Blue Card (Mavi Kart) holders included, falls under Article 48, but is usually exempt under the 1954 Hague Convention.

## Article 48 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Security**
>
> Article 48 – (1) Foreign natural and legal persons who bring an action, join proceedings or initiate enforcement proceedings before a Turkish court must give the security determined by the court to cover the costs of the proceedings and of enforcement and the losses and damage of the other party.
>
> (2) The court exempts the claimant, the joining party or the enforcing creditor from security on the basis of reciprocity.

Turkish original:

> **Teminat**
>
> MADDE 48 – (1) Türk mahkemesinde dava açan, davaya katılan veya icra takibinde bulunan yabancı gerçek ve tüzel kişiler, yargılama ve takip giderleriyle karşı tarafın zarar ve ziyanını karşılamak üzere mahkemenin belirleyeceği teminatı göstermek zorundadır.
>
> (2) Mahkeme, dava açanı, davaya katılanı veya icra takibi yapanı karşılıklılık esasına göre teminattan muaf tutar.

The article has not changed since the law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article partly corresponds to Article 32 of the former Law No. 2675. The duty to give security was kept, and paragraph 2 was redrafted because of difficulties in practice. The reasoning adds that the court may exempt a party even without reciprocity, depending on the nature of the case or enforcement, and that the judge has discretion here. (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly, Article 47 of the bill.)

That last sentence is not in the text in force. The law only provides for exemption based on reciprocity and phrases it as an obligation ("exempts"). As far as I could find, the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has not directly decided whether a court can exempt a party without reciprocity, based only on the nature of the case. In my assessment the reasoning cannot create an exemption the statute does not contain, and the court is bound by the text; how a court will decide in a given case is not certain.

## Explanation

The article applies only to claimants, joining parties (müdahil) and enforcing creditors; no security is required from a foreign defendant. The test is nationality, not residence.

The procedure is in Articles 84 to 89 HMK. The court orders security of its own motion (Art. 86) and sets the amount and form at its discretion (Art. 87); the law sets no fixed amount. If security is not given within the final time limit set by the judge, the claim is dismissed on procedural grounds (Art. 88); complying with the order is a procedural requirement (dava şartı, Art. 114(1)(ğ)). Once the reason for the security disappears, the court orders its return on request (Art. 89). As of November 2026 these HMK provisions are unchanged (the UYAP text is updated up to Law No. 7589 of 31 July 2026).

Reciprocity (karşılıklılık) means that the foreigner's home state does not require security from Turkish citizens in the same position. It can come from a treaty, a statute or actual practice. International treaties to which Turkey is a party take precedence (Art. 1(2) MÖHUK).

For Germany and the Netherlands the key treaty is the Hague Convention of 1 March 1954 on civil procedure. According to the status table of the Hague Conference, Turkey acceded on 23 October 1972 and the Convention has applied to it since 11 July 1973; Germany (1 January 1960) and the Netherlands (27 June 1959) are also parties. Under Article 17, no security, bond or deposit may be imposed, by reason of their foreign nationality or lack of domicile or residence in the country, on nationals of a contracting state who have their domicile in a contracting state and are claimants or intervening parties before the courts of another contracting state. Article 17 refers to nationals; the 12th Civil Chamber of the Yargıtay held, in enforcement proceedings brought by a Dutch company, that legal persons are not covered (E. 2015/26555, K. 2016/3489, 11 February 2016).

## What matters if you live abroad

### Turkish citizens living in Germany

As a Turkish citizen you are not a foreigner, so Article 48 does not apply to you. But Article 84(1)(a) HMK also requires security from a Turkish citizen with no habitual residence (mutad mesken) in Turkey who sues, joins proceedings or starts enforcement. If you live in Germany or the Netherlands, the court can therefore ask you for security as well. Having German citizenship in addition does not change this: the Yargıtay required security under Article 84 HMK from a dual national living abroad who had started enforcement proceedings (see below).

No security may be required (Art. 85 HMK) if you receive legal aid (adli yardım), own real estate in Turkey or hold a claim secured by a real right that covers the amount, the case is brought solely to protect a minor's interests, or you are enforcing a court judgment (ilamlı takip).

Article 17 of the Hague Convention does not help here. It protects nationals before the courts of "another" contracting state and, on its wording, does not cover a Turkish citizen suing in a Turkish court. This is my reading of the text. The result is that a Turk living in Germany may be asked for security while a German living in Germany may not.

### German or Dutch citizens only, and the Blue Card

Someone who gave up Turkish citizenship with permission and now holds only German or Dutch citizenship is legally a foreigner and falls under Article 48. With a domicile in Germany or the Netherlands, Article 17 of the Hague Convention means no security may be required on the ground of foreign nationality or lack of a domicile in Turkey. The court examines the exemption of its own motion; proof of nationality and domicile makes this easier.

For Blue Card holders, Article 28 of Turkish Citizenship Law No. 5901 also comes into play. Under it, persons who were Turkish by birth and lost citizenship with permission, and their descendants up to the third degree, keep the rights granted to Turkish citizens except for the listed exceptions (para. 1): voting and standing for election, duty-free import of vehicles and household goods, military service, and permanent civil service posts (paras. 2 and 3). Security for costs is not mentioned. This point is disputed. The law does not expressly exempt Blue Card holders, and I found no Yargıtay decision on it in UYAP Emsal. In my assessment, even if Article 28 puts the holder in the position of a Turkish citizen, Article 84(1)(a) HMK could then apply to someone living abroad. For Blue Card holders in Germany or the Netherlands the real basis is therefore the Hague Convention. How a court will decide is not certain. More on the card: [Blue Card Turkey (Mavi Kart)](/en/publications/blue-card-turkey-mavi-kart-rights).

### Recognition and enforcement

A foreign claimant must also give security in proceedings to enforce a foreign judgment (tenfiz) (see the 13th Civil Chamber decision below). If a Turkish citizen without habitual residence in Turkey applies for recognition (tanıma) of a German divorce, the court considers whether security is due under Article 84(1)(a) HMK. For the conditions see [Article 50 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey), and for the competent court [Article 51 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-51-mohuk-competent-court-turkey).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 12. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2015/4972, K. 2015/15090, T. 02.06.2015**

The debtor complained that the creditor did not live in Turkey and had started enforcement without giving security. The chamber found that the creditor was a dual national who had given a foreign address in the enforcement request and the power of attorney. As a Turkish citizen with no habitual residence in Turkey under Article 84(1)(a) HMK, he had to give security; the decision rejecting the complaint was reversed.

**Yargıtay 3. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/1328, K. 2025/4863, T. 15.10.2025**

In a payment claim by a foreign claimant living in England, the chamber stressed that Article 48 is based on foreign nationality, that reciprocity can rest on a treaty, a statute or practice, and that Article 17 of the 1954 Hague Convention is such a treaty. Because the first-instance court had not examined the exemption, it had to ask the Ministry of Justice and, if security was needed, set a final time limit.

**Yargıtay 13. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2019/1026, K. 2019/7846, T. 27.06.2019**

In proceedings for recognition and enforcement of a Dutch judgment, the chamber noted that security is regulated both in Articles 84 et seq. HMK and in Article 48 MÖHUK. The claimant should have given security under Article 48(1); since he had not, the claim should have been dismissed on procedural grounds rather than on the merits. The decision was upheld with corrected reasoning.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Turkish citizen living in Germany have to give security when suing in Turkey?

Not under Article 48, but under Article 84(1)(a) HMK if they have no habitual residence in Turkey. Exceptions include legal aid, sufficient real estate in Turkey and claims brought solely for a minor (Art. 85 HMK).

### Does a Blue Card holder with only German citizenship have to give security?

They are a foreigner under Article 48. With a domicile in Germany they should be exempt under Article 17 of the Hague Convention. Whether Article 28 of Law No. 5901 exempts them on its own is disputed.

### What happens if the security is not given?

The court sets a final time limit; if it passes, the claim is dismissed on procedural grounds (Art. 88 and Art. 114(1)(ğ) HMK).

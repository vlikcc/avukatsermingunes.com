Under Turkish law, custody (velayet) can be changed after a divorce if new facts arise that make it necessary, in the child's interest, for custody to pass to the other parent (Articles 183 and 349 of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK). The application is made by the mother or the father, and the judge can also act on their own motion. The family court (aile mahkemesi) decides.

How custody is first awarded on divorce is explained in [child custody after divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey). This article covers what comes after. I checked the provisions cited here against the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr in November 2026. Articles 182 and 324 TMK each gained a paragraph through Law No. 7343 in 2021; Articles 183 and 348–351 TMK have not changed.

## When can custody be changed?

The basis is Article 183 TMK. Unofficial translation:

> Where new facts, such as the mother or father marrying another person, moving to another place or dying, make it necessary, the judge takes the required measures of their own motion or at the request of one of the parents.

Turkish original:

> Ana veya babanın başkasıyla evlenmesi, başka bir yere gitmesi veya ölmesi gibi yeni olguların zorunlu kılması hâlinde hâkim, re'sen veya ana ve babadan birinin istemi üzerine gerekli önlemleri alır.

There are two conditions. First, there must be a **new fact** that arose after the divorce judgment. Marriage, moving and death are only examples (“gibi”, “such as”). Second, that fact must **make the change necessary**. It is not enough that it happened; the child's interest must call for custody to move.

The Code deals with some situations separately:

- **Remarriage.** If the parent with custody remarries, that alone is no reason to remove custody. If the child's interest requires it, custody can be transferred to the other parent (Article 349 TMK).
- **Contact arrangements ignored.** If the parent with custody does not comply with the contact order, custody can be changed, provided this is not against the child's interest. The court warns the parents of this in its decision (Articles 182(2) and 324(3) TMK).
- **Removal of custody.** If other protective measures fail and a parent cannot exercise custody, for example because of inexperience, illness or absence, or grossly neglects their duties towards the child, the judge removes custody (Article 348 TMK). If circumstances change, the measures are adjusted; once the reason has gone, custody is restored (Article 351 TMK).

The author's assessment: facts already argued in the divorce case carry little weight without a new development. Your application should show concretely when the new fact arose and how it affects the child's life.

## Who can apply to change custody?

Article 183 TMK gives the right to apply to the mother and the father. The parent who asks for custody names the parent who has custody as the other party. The judge can also act without an application. The mother or father can likewise ask for custody that was removed to be restored (Article 351(2) TMK).

## Which court, and how the case runs

**Subject-matter jurisdiction** lies with the family court (Law No. 4787 on Family Courts, Article 4(1)(1)). Where no family court has been set up, a civil court of first instance designated under that Law hears the case (Article 2(2)).

**Territorial jurisdiction.** The Turkish Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) lists “removal of custody, transfer of custody from one spouse to the other and restoration of custody that was removed” among non-contentious matters (çekişmesiz yargı, Article 382(2)(b)(13) HMK). For these, the court where the applicant or one of the persons concerned lives has jurisdiction (Article 384 HMK). The TMK has no specific rule on jurisdiction for changing custody; the court at the child's place of residence is named only for contact arrangements (Article 326(1) TMK). I do not cite a judgment, read in full in an official database, on whether jurisdiction follows Article 384 or the general rule (Article 6 HMK: the defendant's domicile). In my assessment, filing where the parent with custody and the child live means choosing a court that has jurisdiction under both rules.

**Procedure.** Custody matters follow the simplified procedure (basit yargılama, Article 316(1)(ç) HMK). The judge investigates the facts of their own motion (Article 385(2) HMK). Custody cases are also heard during the judicial recess (Article 103(1)(b) HMK). Family law judgments cannot be enforced until they are final (Articles 350(2) and 367(2) HMK); a custody change therefore takes effect once final. Decisions of the regional courts of appeal (bölge adliye mahkemesi) in non-contentious matters cannot be appealed to the Court of Cassation (Article 362(1)(ç) HMK).

## Hearing the child and the social inquiry report

Parents take the child's views into account on important matters, as far as the child's maturity allows (Article 339(3) TMK). Neither the TMK nor the HMK sets an age from which a child must be heard in custody proceedings. The family court relies on experts: psychologists, educationalists and social workers assigned to the courts look into the questions the court puts to them, report back and, if needed, give their view at the hearing (Article 5(1) Law No. 4787). This is what is known as the social inquiry report (sosyal inceleme raporu). An expert can be challenged on the same grounds as a judge (Article 5(3)).

Hearing the child also matters across borders. Under the 1996 Hague Child Protection Convention, recognition of a measure may be refused if, except in a case of urgency, it was taken without the child having been given the opportunity to be heard, in violation of fundamental principles of procedure of the requested State (Article 23(2)(b)).

## When a parent or the child moves abroad

A parent “moving to another place” is expressly named in Article 183 TMK. The child's interest must still call for the change. When the move is abroad, the real question is which country's authorities decide.

**The 1996 Hague Convention.** Turkey is a party to the Convention on Jurisdiction, Applicable Law, Recognition, Enforcement and Co-operation in respect of Parental Responsibility and Measures for the Protection of Children. Accession was approved by Law No. 6707 (Resmî Gazete, 5 May 2016, No. 29703). Under Council of Ministers Decision No. 2016/9638, the Convention entered into force for Turkey on 1 February 2017 (Resmî Gazete, 3 January 2017, No. 29937). According to the Hague Conference status table it has applied in Germany since 1 January 2011 and in the Netherlands since 1 May 2011. Under the Turkish Private International Law Act, international conventions to which Turkey is a party take precedence (Article 1(2) MÖHUK).

The main custody rules of the Convention:

- The authorities of the Contracting State where the child is habitually resident have jurisdiction to take protective measures. If the child's habitual residence moves to another Contracting State, that State's authorities have jurisdiction (Article 5). Attributing and terminating parental responsibility are among these measures (Article 3).
- If the child was wrongfully removed or retained, the former State of habitual residence keeps jurisdiction until the conditions of Article 7 are met. A [return application under the Hague Abduction Convention](/en/publications/hague-convention-child-abduction-turkey) then also comes into play.
- Measures already taken stay in force, even if the basis for jurisdiction has gone, until the authorities that now have jurisdiction change them (Article 14).
- The authorities apply their own law (Article 15(1)).

If the child has lawfully moved with a parent to Germany or the Netherlands and is now habitually resident there, the power to change custody passes to that country's authorities under the Convention. The earlier Turkish decision remains in force until it is changed there. If the child moves to Turkey, the reverse applies. I do not cite a judgment, read in an official database, showing how Turkish courts apply Article 5 in custody modification cases. Where the Convention does not apply (for example, the child lives in a non-Contracting State), Turkish jurisdiction follows Articles 40 and 41 MÖHUK. The applicable law follows from Article 14(3) or Article 17 MÖHUK; the Act does not say expressly which of the two covers a change after divorce.

## A foreign custody decision and recognition

A custody decision given in Germany or the Netherlands has the effect of res judicata in Turkey only once a Turkish court has found that it meets the conditions for enforcement (Article 58(1) MÖHUK). The same applies to non-contentious decisions (Article 58(2)). The family court hears these cases (Article 4(1)(2) Law No. 4787). Direct registration through a consulate or civil registry office exists only for decisions on divorce, annulment or the existence of a marriage (Article 27/A Civil Registration Services Law No. 5490); custody is not listed there. The conditions are set out in [Article 58 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-mohuk-recognition-foreign-judgments-turkey).

The 1996 Convention, by contrast, says that measures taken in another Contracting State are recognised by operation of law (Article 23(1)). Any interested person may ask for a decision on recognition or non-recognition, under the procedure of the requested State (Article 24). If the decision needs enforcing, a declaration of enforceability or registration must be sought (Article 26(1); Article 50 MÖHUK).

This point is disputed. No statutory provision expressly settles how recognition by operation of law under the Convention relates to the recognition judgment required by Article 58 MÖHUK, and I do not cite a binding decision on the question whose full text I have seen in an official database. My view: if you want a foreign decision entered in the Turkish civil register, or changed by a Turkish court, obtaining a recognition judgment first avoids objections later. How a court will decide in a particular case is not certain.

Under German law, changes to custody decisions are governed by § 1696 BGB; in the Netherlands, a Dutch court decides on any change. For the content of those laws, please contact a lawyer qualified in that country. Other topics are covered under [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I apply to change custody in Turkey?

At the family court (Article 4 Law No. 4787). The law treats the matter as non-contentious; the court where the applicant or one of the persons concerned lives has jurisdiction (Articles 382(2)(b)(13) and 384 HMK). If the child is habitually resident in another State party to the 1996 Hague Convention, that State's authorities have jurisdiction (Article 5).

### Are custody cases heard during the judicial recess in Turkey?

Yes. Custody matters are heard during the judicial recess as well (Article 103(1)(b) HMK).

### Does a custody change apply before the judgment is final?

No. Family law judgments cannot be enforced until they are final (Articles 350(2) and 367(2) HMK).

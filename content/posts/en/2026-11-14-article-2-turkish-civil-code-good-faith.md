Article 2 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) sets out the principle of good faith (dürüstlük kuralı) and the prohibition of abuse of rights (hakkın kötüye kullanılması): everyone must act in good faith when exercising their rights and performing their obligations, and the legal order does not protect the manifest abuse of a right. The article is not tied to one type of contract or lawsuit. You can meet it in any private law relationship governed by Turkish law, and especially when buying or selling property in Turkey.

## Article 2 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **B. Scope of legal relationships**
>
> **I. Acting in good faith**
>
> Article 2 – Everyone must act in accordance with the rules of good faith when exercising their rights and performing their obligations.
>
> The legal order does not protect the manifest abuse of a right.

Original Turkish text:

> **B. Hukukî ilişkilerin kapsamı**
>
> **I. Dürüst davranma**
>
> Madde 2 - Herkes, haklarını kullanırken ve borçlarını yerine getirirken dürüstlük kurallarına uymak zorundadır.
>
> Bir hakkın açıkça kötüye kullanılmasını hukuk düzeni korumaz.

The article has not changed since the Code entered into force on 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system (recorded 31 July 2026) does not list Article 2; the last law in the table is Law No. 7589, which amended Articles 440 and 444 (those changes in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 2 of the former Civil Code No. 743. The heading "Dürüst davranma" (acting in good faith) was chosen to separate it from subjective good faith, which Article 3 governs. The text now says "dürüstlük kurallarına uymak" instead of "iyiniyet", and "borç" is used in a broad sense that also covers duties (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### How the article comes up in court

Article 2 is usually raised in reply to the other side's defence: "You have the right, but in this case you cannot use it this way." One common setting is a property sale that was never registered at the land registry (tapu müdürlüğü) ([real estate law](/en/practice-areas/real-estate-law)). The law requires the official form for the sale of land (Article 706 TMK, Article 237 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK). A private sale outside the land registry ("harici satış") is therefore void as a rule. If the seller has received almost the full price and let the buyer use the property for years, and then relies on the formal defect to refuse the transfer, the buyer invokes Article 2(2). The rulings below show when the courts accept this.

The Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation also draws a clear line: a person who relies on a formal defect does not abuse a right for that reason alone, even if the other side suffers from it. Otherwise the form requirement would lose its point.

### How it shows up in the Code of Obligations

Good faith appears in many specific provisions of the Turkish Code of Obligations (TBK), for example:

- Article 25 TBK: standard terms may not contain clauses that, contrary to good faith, work against the other party.
- Article 34 TBK: a party who was mistaken may not rely on the mistake contrary to good faith.
- Article 138 TBK: if an unforeseeable extraordinary event changes the situation against the debtor so much that demanding performance would be contrary to good faith, the debtor may ask the court to adapt the contract.
- Article 175 TBK: if a party prevents a condition from being fulfilled contrary to good faith, the condition is treated as fulfilled.

Where such a specific rule fits, it applies first; Article 2 is used where the specific rule gives no answer.

### Other provisions of the Civil Code and civil procedure

Under Article 166(2) TMK, where the spouse who is more at fault files for divorce, the other spouse may object; if that objection amounts to an abuse of rights and the marriage no longer holds any interest worth protecting for the defendant and the children, divorce may be granted. The same standard applies during a lawsuit: under Article 29 of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (HMK), the parties must act in good faith and state the facts truthfully.

## Practical points if you live abroad

**Turkish law governs property in Turkey.** Even if you signed the contract in Germany or the Netherlands, contracts concerning immovable property are governed by the law of the country where the property is located (Article 25(1) of the Turkish Private International Law Act No. 5718, MÖHUK). If you bought a flat in Turkey without a transfer at the land registry, on the promise that "we will do the title deed later", Article 2 and the case law below become relevant to you.

**Pay in a way you can prove.** In the case below, upheld by the 7th Civil Chamber, the 150,000 TL sent by bank transfer was not disputed; the 25,000 TL allegedly paid in cash could not be proven, and the buyer had to deposit its current equivalent. Bank transfers from abroad are evidence in your favour here. You can have the title transfer done under a power of attorney without travelling to Turkey; see [selling property in Turkey from abroad](/en/publications/selling-property-in-turkey-from-abroad).

**If the property passes to a third party, the position changes.** If the seller has transferred the flat at the land registry to someone else, a person who acquired a right relying in good faith on the register is protected (Article 1023 TMK). The buyer under the private sale must then prove that the transfer was a sham (muvazaa) or was made knowingly to harm him (Assembly of Civil Chambers ruling below).

**Off-plan purchases follow a separate rule.** If you bought a home as a consumer under a pre-paid housing sale (ön ödemeli konut satışı), Article 41(1) of the Consumer Protection Law No. 6502 applies: if the sale is not registered at the land registry and the promise to sell is not drawn up by a notary, the seller cannot later rely on invalidity against the consumer. As of November 2026 this provision is unchanged in the UYAP Mevzuat text (recorded 1 July 2026).

**This question is disputed.** The Court of Cassation's unifying decision, as the Assembly of Civil Chambers quotes it, concerns the sale of units in buildings constructed under the Condominium Law (Kat Mülkiyeti Kanunu). The case upheld by the 7th Civil Chamber, by contrast, concerned a plot of land, and the regional court relied on a 1979 ruling of the Assembly of Civil Chambers. The law does not say expressly whether this principle applies as a general rule to private sales of other land such as building plots or fields. What is reported here is the outcome of two specific cases; how a court would decide your case is not certain.

**Germany and the Netherlands have similar principles, but their own law.** The German Civil Code (BGB) provides for performance in good faith in § 242 and prohibits the exercise of a right solely to harm another in § 226; in the Netherlands, Article 6:2 of the Burgerlijk Wetboek requires creditor and debtor to act according to "redelijkheid en billijkheid" (reasonableness and fairness). For how these rules apply to a specific case, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country.

## Court of Cassation rulings

**Yargıtay (Court of Cassation), Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/421, K. 2025/381, 18.06.2025**

A consumer had bought a flat in a project under construction in 2015 by a private written contract and paid part of the price; the selling company transferred the flat to someone else at the land registry before handing it over. The Assembly quoted the unifying decision of 30.09.1988 (E. 1987/2, K. 1988/2): if the buyer has performed, the seller has handed over the unit and the buyer uses it as an owner, the court may, depending on the case and having regard to Article 2 TMK, grant the claim for registration. Because the contract was concluded after Law No. 6502, the dispute had to be decided under Article 41(1) of that law, and the court had to examine whether the transfer to the third party was a sham. The regional court's decision to stand by its earlier ruling was reversed (majority decision).

**Yargıtay (Court of Cassation), 7th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/2300, K. 2026/63, 07.01.2026**

In a land sale never registered at the land registry, 150,000 TL had been paid by bank and the land handed over. Relying on a ruling of the Assembly of Civil Chambers of 06.06.1979 (14/190-799), the regional court held that the seller's reliance on the formal defect would be an abuse of rights. It ordered registration in the buyer's name against deposit and payment to the seller of 171,478.57 TL, the value at the filing date of the unproven 25,000 TL. The Court of Cassation rejected the appeal on this point; it changed only the costs and attorney's fees, because under the rule of simultaneous performance (Article 97 TBK) the seller had not caused the lawsuit, and otherwise upheld the judgment.

**Yargıtay (Court of Cassation), 7th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5262, K. 2026/3399, 22.06.2026**

A counterexample: the buyer relied on an undated private protocol, and the defendants disputed the document and the signatures. The first-instance court dismissed the claim, holding that under a void contract the parties can only reclaim what they have given. The Court of Cassation did not treat the buyer's argument that relying on the formal defect breached good faith as a ground for reversal and upheld the judgment. The decision gives brief reasons and does not discuss that argument separately.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Article 2 TMK make a property sale without a title deed valid?

No, the contract does not become valid. Depending on the case, the court can only treat the seller's reliance on invalidity as an abuse of rights and order registration. Payment of the price, handover and the buyer's use of the property as an owner matter in that assessment.

### What is abuse of rights under Turkish law?

Using a right that formally exists in a way that is clearly incompatible with justice. The law requires a "manifest" abuse. Exercising one's right is lawful as a rule.

Article 52 of Turkish Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (Milletlerarası Özel Hukuk ve Usul Hukuku Hakkında Kanun, MÖHUK) says who may apply for enforcement (tenfiz) of a foreign judgment in Turkey and what the application must state. In short, anyone with a legal interest (hukuki yarar) in enforcement may apply; you do not have to have been a party to the foreign proceedings. If you want to enforce a German, Dutch or other foreign judgment in Turkey, this is the provision your application follows.

## Article 52 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Application for enforcement**
>
> Article 52 – (1) Anyone who has a legal interest in the enforcement of the decision may apply for enforcement. The application is made by petition. As many copies of the petition as there are opposing parties are attached. The petition contains the following:
>
> a) The names, surnames and addresses of the applicant, the opposing party and, if any, their legal representatives and attorneys.
>
> b) The state whose court gave the judgment, the name of the court, the date and number of the judgment and a summary of the judgment.
>
> c) If enforcement is sought for only part of the judgment, which part.

Original Turkish text:

> **Tenfiz istemi**
>
> MADDE 52 – (1) Kararın tenfiz edilmesinde hukukî yararı bulunan herkes tenfiz isteminde bulunabilir. Tenfiz istemi dilekçe ile olur. Dilekçeye karşı tarafın sayısı kadar örnek eklenir. Dilekçede aşağıdaki hususlar yer alır:
>
> a) Tenfiz isteyenle, karşı tarafın ve varsa kanunî temsilci ve vekillerinin ad, soyad ve adresleri.
>
> b) Tenfiz konusu hükmün hangi devlet mahkemesinden verilmiş olduğu ve mahkemenin adı ile ilâmın tarih ve numarası ve hükmün özeti.
>
> c) Tenfiz, hükmün bir kısmı hakkında isteniyorsa bunun hangi kısım olduğu.

The article has not been amended since the Law entered into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

The official reasoning is a single sentence: the article corresponds exactly to Article 36 of the former Law No. 2675 (reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, where it was Article 51). The rule that had applied since 1982 was carried over unchanged in 2007.

## Explanation

### Who may apply?

The Law says "anyone who has a legal interest". You need not have been the claimant or the defendant abroad; it is enough that enforcement affects an interest of yours that the law protects. Legal interest is also a procedural requirement (dava şartı) under Article 114(1)(h) of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (HMK). The court checks it of its own motion at every stage (Article 115(1) HMK). According to the Yargıtay (Court of Cassation), it must exist not only when the case is filed but until judgment (see the 2nd Civil Chamber's 2023 decision below).

### Against whom?

Article 52 does not say. The 6th Civil Chamber of the Yargıtay holds that, as a rule, the person who was the defendant in the foreign judgment must also be named as the respondent in the enforcement case (see below). That person's Turkish domicile also determines which court has venue; see the post on [Article 51 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-51-mohuk-competent-court-turkey).

### The petition and its contents

You cannot apply orally. You file a petition with one copy for each opposing party; the copy is served on the other side together with the hearing date (Article 55(1) MÖHUK). The petition must include:

- the names and addresses of the applicant, the opposing party and, if any, their legal representatives and attorneys,
- the state and court that gave the judgment, its date and number, and a summary,
- for partial enforcement, which part is meant.

Under Article 55(1) MÖHUK, the application is examined under the simplified procedure (basit yargılama usulü). Where that procedure has no rule, the provisions on the written procedure apply (Article 322(1) HMK). In my view, it follows that the petition should also contain the general items listed in Article 119 HMK, such as the name of the court, the applicant's Turkish identity number, a clear statement of the relief sought and a signature; MÖHUK contains no express reference to Article 119 HMK. If certain of these items are missing, the judge sets a one-week final deadline under Article 119(2) HMK; if the defect is not cured, the case is deemed not to have been filed.

The documents to be attached (certified judgment, proof of finality, translations) are dealt with separately in Article 53 MÖHUK. The post on [Article 50 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey) briefly explains why proof of finality is needed.

### Partial enforcement

Subparagraph (c) lets you seek enforcement of only part of a judgment. If, for example, you only want the maintenance order in a German divorce judgment enforced in Turkey, you say so expressly in the petition. The court may grant enforcement in whole or in part, or reject the application (Article 56(1) MÖHUK).

## Practical points for Turks living abroad

**Your parents divorced abroad, and the divorce was not recognised before one of them died.** This has a direct effect on the estate. Without recognition, the marriage remains in the Turkish population register until death, and the former spouse may appear there as an heir. In decisions from 2025 and 2026, the 2nd Civil Chamber of the Yargıtay accepted that the heirs have a legal interest in such a case. Yet decisions of the same chamber from 2023 and January 2025 contain a general statement that a foreign divorce can no longer be recognised once the marriage has ended under Turkish law, for example by death. The 2026 decision was taken by majority, and the dissent argued that the right to seek recognition is strictly personal. This question is disputed and the case law is not settled. How a court will decide in a particular case is uncertain.

**If you were also divorced in Turkey.** If, after the divorce abroad, a Turkish divorce judgment has also become final and been entered in the population register, the Yargıtay holds that there is no longer a legal interest in recognising or enforcing the foreign judgment. Thinking about this order early on avoids a pointless case.

**Only for the population register.** If all you need is to have your civil status updated, court proceedings are not always necessary. General information is available on the [Turkey–Germany](/en/turkiye-germany) page.

**Addresses.** The opposing party's address must appear in the petition (Article 52(1)(a)). If that person also lives abroad, you should know their current address there before you apply. If a representative in Turkey brings the case for you, their name and address are given too. See also the page on [private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law).

## Decisions of the Yargıtay

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/7717, K. 2026/6780, T. 17.06.2026**

The heirs of a father who had divorced in Rotterdam and died without having the divorce recognised in Turkey applied for recognition and enforcement. The first-instance court dismissed the case, holding that the marriage had ended by death under Turkish law and that the heirs could not bring the case. The chamber reversed: the divorce affects the heirs' inheritance rights, so they fall within "anyone who has a legal interest". The decision was taken by majority.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/2070, K. 2025/7823, T. 25.09.2025**

In a similar case, the regional court of appeal had dismissed the claim for lack of legal interest because the marriage had ended with the father's death. The chamber held that Article 52(1) also applies to applications for recognition, that on recognition the res judicata effect runs from the date the foreign judgment became final (Article 59 MÖHUK), and that the claimant therefore had an interest worth protecting. The decision was reversed unanimously.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/5707, K. 2023/6196, T. 13.12.2023**

A spouse divorced in Belgium applied for recognition and enforcement; while the case was pending, a Turkish divorce judgment became final and was registered. The chamber held that legal interest must continue until judgment and had ceased once the Turkish divorce became final, and reversed the decision granting the application. The same reasoning appears in a reversal in the interest of the law concerning a Dutch judgment (2. HD, E. 2024/10660, K. 2025/696, T. 23.01.2025).

**Yargıtay 6. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/3924, K. 2025/10, T. 13.01.2025**

Enforcement of an Algerian money judgment was sought against Turkish companies. The chamber noted that Article 52 does not say against whom the application is directed; as a rule, the defendant in the foreign judgment is the respondent. The court had to establish whether the defendant named in the Algerian judgment had legal personality under Algerian law and how it was connected to the respondent companies. To the same effect: 6. HD, E. 2024/407, K. 2025/481, T. 12.02.2025.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can apply to enforce a foreign judgment in Turkey?

Anyone with a legal interest in enforcement (Article 52(1) MÖHUK). You need not have been a party to the foreign proceedings.

### What must the application contain?

The names and addresses of the parties and of any legal representatives and attorneys; the state and court, the date, number and a summary of the judgment; and, for partial enforcement, the part concerned. Attach one copy for each opposing party.

### Can I enforce only the maintenance part of a foreign judgment?

Yes. The petition states which part is meant (Article 52(1)(c)), and the court may grant partial enforcement (Article 56(1)).

### My parents divorced abroad and my father has died. Can I apply for recognition as his heir?

Decisions of the 2nd Civil Chamber from 2025 and 2026 accept that heirs have a legal interest. Because older decisions say otherwise and the 2026 decision has a dissent, the outcome is not certain.

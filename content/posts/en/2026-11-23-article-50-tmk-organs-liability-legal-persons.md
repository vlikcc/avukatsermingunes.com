Article 50 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) governs how a legal person exercises its capacity to act: it expresses its will through its organs, their legal transactions and other acts bind it, and an organ member who is at fault is also personally liable. The article matters to board members of associations and foundations and to anyone who has suffered loss caused by a legal person.

## Article 50 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Exercise**
>
> Article 50 – The will of a legal person is expressed through its organs.
>
> The organs bind the legal person through their legal transactions and all their other acts.
>
> The organs are, in addition, personally liable for their fault.

Original Turkish text:

> **II. Kullanılması**
>
> Madde 50- Tüzel kişinin iradesi, organları aracılığıyla açıklanır.
>
> Organlar, hukukî işlemleri ve diğer bütün fiilleriyle tüzel kişiyi borç altına sokarlar.
>
> Organlar, kusurlarından dolayı ayrıca kişisel olarak sorumludurlar.

As of November 2026, Article 50 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31/10/2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 48 of the former Civil Code No. 743; the language was simplified and the content supplemented. Paragraph 2 makes clear that organs can bind the legal person not only through legal transactions but through all their other acts. Paragraph 3 reflects that it is not the abstract "organ" that is liable but the person or persons who make it up, and the word "in addition" (ayrıca) was inserted: personal liability does not remove the legal person's liability but sits alongside it (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Acts of an organ and acts of an employee

Whether the person who caused the loss is an organ or an employee of an association decides which rule makes the legal person liable. Under Article 50(2), the act of an organ counts directly as the act of the legal person. The executive and representative organ of an association is its board of directors (Article 85(1) TMK). For loss caused by an employee who is not an organ, employer's liability applies (Article 66 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, Türk Borçlar Kanunu, TBK); there the employer escapes liability by proving that it took due care in selecting, instructing and supervising the employee (Article 66(2) TBK). Article 50 contains no such defence.

### Personal liability of the organ member at fault

Paragraph 3 requires fault. The injured party must prove the loss and the fault of the person who caused it (Article 50(1) TBK). Having acted on behalf of the legal person does not release a board member from this liability.

Article 50 does not say expressly how the legal person and the organ member at fault are liable together towards the injured party. Legal writing takes the view that the rules on joint and several liability in Article 61 TBK apply and that the internal split follows Article 62 TBK; this is a view in legal writing, not an express rule of the Code. A claim for damages in tort is time-barred two years after the injured party learns of the loss and of the person liable, and in any event ten years after the act; if criminal law provides a longer limitation period for the act, that period applies (Article 72(1) TBK).

Commercial companies are governed by their own legislation (Article 55 TMK); this post deals with associations and foundations. On how organs are formed see the post on [Article 49 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-49-tmk-capacity-to-act-legal-persons), and on the rights a legal person can hold the post on [Article 48 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-48-tmk-legal-capacity-legal-persons).

## Practical points for people living abroad

If you live in Germany or the Netherlands and sit on the board of a hometown association (hemşehri derneği) or a foundation in Türkiye, living abroad does not change your personal liability under Article 50(3): for a decision or act made with fault, you can be held liable alongside the legal person. Article 3 of the Foundations Act No. 5737 (Vakıflar Kanunu) defines a foundation manager as a person authorised to manage and represent the foundation or a member of its competent organs. The general framework on legal persons is in the [post on Article 47 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-47-turkish-civil-code-legal-personality); general information on damages claims is on the [compensation law](/en/practice-areas/compensation-law) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a board member personally liable for an association's debts in Türkiye?

The association's debts are, as a rule, the association's (Article 50(2) TMK). A board member is additionally personally liable for loss caused by their own fault (Article 50(3) TMK).

### Whom can the injured party sue?

Both the legal person and the organ member at fault. According to the reasoning, the member's liability sits alongside that of the legal person.

### Does it matter whether an organ or an employee caused the loss?

Yes. For an employee, the employer can escape liability by proving due care (Article 66(2) TBK); the act of an organ counts directly as the act of the legal person (Article 50(2) TMK).

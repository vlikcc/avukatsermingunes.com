Article 7 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) says that official registers (resmî sicil) and official deeds (resmî senet) prove the truth of the facts they record, while leaving room to prove the opposite. If you are a party to proceedings in Turkey, or you think something in your Turkish civil registry record (nüfus kaydı) is wrong, this article and the Code of Civil Procedure together decide how much weight that record carries. The Code was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 8 December 2001, No. 24607, and entered into force on 1 January 2002.

## Article 7 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Proof by official documents**
>
> Article 7 – Official registers and deeds constitute proof of the truth of the facts they record.
>
> Unless the law provides otherwise, proof that their content is incorrect is not subject to any particular form.

Original Turkish text:

> **II. Resmî belgelerle ispat**
>
> Madde 7 - Resmî sicil ve senetler, belgeledikleri olguların doğruluğuna kanıt oluşturur.
>
> Bunların içeriğinin doğru olmadığının ispatı, kanunlarda başka bir hüküm bulunmadıkça, her hangi bir şekle bağlı değildir.

The article has not changed since 1 January 2002. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text on mevzuat.gov.tr does not list Article 7; the latest law in the table is Law No. 7589, which amended Articles 440 and 444 (in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, the article corresponds to Article 7 of the former Civil Code No. 743; the old, hard-to-read wording was replaced with a short, positive sentence. The phrase "unless the law provides otherwise" covers every law on evidence, including procedural codes, and "register" is used as an umbrella term for entries, annotations and registrations (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Turkish Grand National Assembly).

## Explanation

### The record is presumed correct, but can be rebutted

If you rely on an official register or deed, you do not have to prove separately that it is correct. That applies, for example, to the date of birth in the civil registry or the owner shown in the land registry (tapu sicili). The party claiming the record is wrong bears the burden of proving it, which follows from Article 6 TMK ([Article 6 TMK – burden of proof](/en/publications/legislation/article-6-turkish-civil-code-burden-of-proof)). Proof to the contrary is in principle not bound to any form, so witnesses are allowed, unless a law says otherwise.

### Article 204 HMK: how strong is each document?

The key exception is in the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 (Hukuk Muhakemeleri Kanunu, HMK). Court judgments (ilam) and notarial deeds drawn up by the notary (düzenleme şeklindeki noter senetleri) are conclusive evidence unless proven forged (Article 204(1) HMK). Documents certified by a notary on the basis of the parties' statements, and documents issued in due form by other competent officials within their duties, are conclusive evidence until the contrary is proven (Article 204(2)). If the court has doubts, it may ask the issuing office for an explanation (Article 204(3)).

So against a notarial deed drawn up by the notary it is not enough to say the content is wrong; forgery has to be proven. Legal transactions intended to cancel or reduce the effect of a written deed also cannot be proven by witnesses (Article 201 HMK).

### Civil registry and correction

Birth and death are proven by civil registry entries; if there is no entry or it turns out to be wrong, the true facts may be proven by any evidence (Article 30 TMK). Proving this in a lawsuit does not change the entry. No entry in the civil status register may be corrected without a court decision (Article 39 TMK). The Civil Registry Services Law No. 5490 repeats the rule: no correction without a final judgment, except clerical errors made during registration, which the registry office corrects on the basis of the underlying document (Article 35(1)). The correction action is filed with the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) at the place of residence (Article 36(1)(a)).

## Practical points if you live abroad

If you submit a German, Dutch or other foreign birth, marriage or death certificate to a Turkish court, it has the status of an official document in Turkey only if it is certified by the competent authority of the issuing state or by the Turkish consulate; international conventions to which Turkey is a party remain reserved (Article 224 HMK). For documents from states party to the Hague Apostille Convention, this means an apostille in practice.

If something in your Turkish record (date of birth, name, parents' names) does not match your foreign documents, Turkish authorities and courts will in principle go by the Turkish record. The way to remove the discrepancy is a correction. On dates of birth: [Changing your date of birth in Turkey](/en/publications/change-date-of-birth-turkey-court-case).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Turkish civil registry record final?

No. It proves the recorded fact, but the contrary can be proven (Article 7(2), Article 30(2) TMK). Changing the entry itself requires a final court decision (Article 39 TMK).

### Can witnesses be called against a notarial deed?

Notarial deeds drawn up by the notary are conclusive unless proven forged (Article 204(1) HMK). Transactions against the content of a written deed cannot be proven by witnesses (Article 201 HMK).

### How do I submit a foreign document to a Turkish court?

With an apostille or Turkish consular certification (Article 224 HMK), together with a Turkish translation.

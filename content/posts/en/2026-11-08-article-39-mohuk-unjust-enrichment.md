Article 39 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which law governs claims for unjust enrichment (sebepsiz zenginleşme) with a foreign element. If the enrichment rests on an existing or alleged legal relationship between the parties, the law of that relationship applies; otherwise, the law of the country where the enrichment took place. You may come across this article if you sent money from abroad to the wrong Turkish bank account, or want a refund of a payment made under a contract that turned out to be void.

## Article 39 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Unjust enrichment**
>
> Article 39 – (1) Claims arising from unjust enrichment are governed by the law applicable to the existing or allegedly existing legal relationship that gave rise to the enrichment. In other cases, unjust enrichment is governed by the law of the country where the enrichment took place.
>
> (2) After the unjust enrichment has occurred, the parties may expressly choose the applicable law.

Turkish original:

> **Sebepsiz zenginleşme**
>
> MADDE 39 – (1) Sebepsiz zenginleşmeden doğan talepler, zenginleşmeye sebep olan mevcut veya mevcut olduğu iddia edilen hukukî ilişkiye uygulanan hukuka tâbidir. Diğer hâllerde sebepsiz zenginleşmeye, zenginleşmenin gerçekleştiği ülke hukuku uygulanır.
>
> (2) Taraflar, sebepsiz zenginleşmenin meydana gelmesinden sonra, uygulanacak hukuku açık olarak seçebilirler.

The article has not changed since the law came into force on 12 December 2007; as of November 2026 the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning of the government bill, the article partly corresponds to Article 26 of the former Law No. 2675. The term “acquisition” (iktisap) was replaced with “enrichment”, which describes the situation more accurately. Where the enrichment rests on an existing or alleged legal relationship, the law of that relationship is treated as most closely connected; where there is none, the law of the place of enrichment still applies, as before. Paragraph 2 permits a choice of law only after the enrichment and only expressly. (Source: reasoning of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 38 of the bill.)

## Explanation

The first sentence of paragraph 1 covers the most common case: money was paid under a contract, and the contract later proves void or never came into existence. Because of the words “or allegedly existing”, invalidity does not break the link, and the refund is governed by the law of that contract. That law is determined under Articles 24 ff. MÖHUK ([Article 24 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-24-mohuk-choice-of-law-contracts)).

The second sentence applies where there is no such relationship, typically a transfer made by mistake to a stranger. The law of the country where the enrichment took place then applies.

Where Turkish law applies, the rules are in Articles 77–82 of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 (TBK). A person enriched without just cause from another’s assets or work must return the enrichment, in particular where the cause was invalid, did not materialise or has ceased (Article 77 TBK). Someone who voluntarily pays a debt they did not owe can recover it only by proving they believed they were liable (Article 78(1) TBK). As of November 2026, Articles 77–82 TBK carry no amendment note.

## Points to note if you live abroad

### Money sent to the wrong Turkish account

If you sent money from Germany or the Netherlands to a stranger’s account in Turkey by mistake, there is no legal relationship between you, so the second sentence of paragraph 1 applies. Since the enrichment occurs in the recipient’s account, it is my assessment that a Turkish court would in principle apply Turkish law; the law does not expressly define whether the place of enrichment is the country of the account or somewhere else. Banks’ recall procedures are outside the scope of this article.

### Payment for property in Turkey under a void contract

Contracts concerning immovable property in Turkey are governed by the law of the country where the property is located, that is Turkish law ([Article 25 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-25-mohuk-immovable-property-contracts)). Under Turkish law, a sale of land or a promise to sell is not valid unless made in official form (Article 237 TBK). If you paid under a merely private written contract, the refund is also governed by Turkish law under the first sentence of paragraph 1.

### Limitation

Under Turkish law, an unjust enrichment claim becomes time-barred two years after the claimant learns of the right to recover and in any event ten years after the enrichment (Article 82(1) TBK). Limitation follows the law governing the claim ([Article 8 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-8-mohuk-limitation-periods)).

### Where to sue

Turkish courts’ international jurisdiction follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 MÖHUK). The court of general jurisdiction is the court of the defendant’s domicile at the time the action is filed (Article 6(1) of the Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). If you sue in Germany or the Netherlands, that court applies its own conflict rules; for questions of German or Dutch law, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country. More on related topics: [commercial and contract law](/en/practice-areas/commercial-and-contract-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get back money sent to the wrong account in Turkey?

If the recipient does not return it voluntarily, it can be claimed under the rules on unjust enrichment. Where Turkish law applies, the basis is Articles 77 ff. TBK; Article 39(1) MÖHUK determines the applicable law.

### What is the limitation period for unjust enrichment in Turkey?

Two years from learning of the right to recover, and at most ten years from the enrichment (Article 82(1) TBK).

### Can the parties choose the law for an unjust enrichment claim?

Yes, but only after the enrichment has occurred and only expressly (Article 39(2) MÖHUK).

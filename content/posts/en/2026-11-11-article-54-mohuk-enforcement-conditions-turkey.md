Article 54 of Turkish Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK; Official Gazette of 12 December 2007, No. 26728) lists the conditions a Turkish court checks before it declares a foreign judgment enforceable (tenfiz). There are four: reciprocity, jurisdiction, public policy and the right to be heard. The article matters to you if you want to enforce a German or Dutch maintenance, payment or damages judgment in Turkey, and equally if someone wants to enforce such a judgment against you there.

## Article 54 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Conditions for enforcement**
>
> Article 54 – (1) The competent court shall grant enforcement subject to the following conditions:
>
> a) There is an agreement based on reciprocity between the Republic of Türkiye and the state in which the judgment was given, or in that state a provision of law or a de facto practice that allows judgments of Turkish courts to be enforced.
>
> b) The judgment was given on a matter that does not fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Turkish courts, or, provided that the defendant objects, the judgment was not given by a court of a state that assumed jurisdiction although it had no real connection with the subject matter or the parties.
>
> c) The judgment is not manifestly contrary to public policy.
>
> ç) The person against whom enforcement is sought has not objected before the Turkish court, on the ground that under the laws of that state he or she was not duly summoned to the court that gave the judgment or was not represented before it, or that the judgment was given in his or her default or absence in breach of those laws.

Turkish original:

> **Tenfiz şartları**
>
> MADDE 54 – (1) Yetkili mahkeme tenfiz kararını aşağıdaki şartlar dâhilinde verir:
>
> a) Türkiye Cumhuriyeti ile ilâmın verildiği devlet arasında karşılıklılık esasına dayanan bir anlaşma yahut o devlette Türk mahkemelerinden verilmiş ilâmların tenfizini mümkün kılan bir kanun hükmünün veya fiilî uygulamanın bulunması.
>
> b) İlâmın, Türk mahkemelerinin münhasır yetkisine girmeyen bir konuda verilmiş olması veya davalının itiraz etmesi şartıyla ilâmın, dava konusu veya taraflarla gerçek bir ilişkisi bulunmadığı hâlde kendisine yetki tanıyan bir devlet mahkemesince verilmiş olmaması.
>
> c) Hükmün kamu düzenine açıkça aykırı bulunmaması.
>
> ç) O yer kanunları uyarınca, kendisine karşı tenfiz istenen kişinin hükmü veren mahkemeye usulüne uygun bir şekilde çağrılmamış veya o mahkemede temsil edilmemiş yahut bu kanunlara aykırı bir şekilde gıyabında veya yokluğunda hüküm verilmiş ve bu kişinin yukarıdaki hususlardan birine dayanarak tenfiz istemine karşı Türk mahkemesine itiraz etmemiş olması.

The article has not changed since 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning of the government bill, the article partly corresponds to Article 38 of the former Law No. 2675 and adds two things. The first is the check on exorbitant jurisdiction in point (b). The old law only looked at the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts, so judgments of courts with no real link to the dispute could still be enforced. The reasoning states that the judge examines exclusive jurisdiction of their own motion, but exorbitant jurisdiction only if the defendant objects. The second is the addition of “or in his or her absence” to point (ç), so that judgments given in a person's absence in breach of the laws of the state of origin are also covered.

## Explanation

The Turkish judge does not review whether the foreign judgment was right; only these four conditions are checked. The other side, too, may only argue that the conditions are not met, or that the judgment has been satisfied or something has arisen that prevents its performance (Article 55(2)).

**Reciprocity (a).** Judgments of Turkish courts must be enforceable in the state of origin as well. The law names three possible bases: an agreement, a provision of law or a de facto practice. One is enough. This condition applies only to enforcement, not to recognition (tanıma; Article 58(1)).

**Jurisdiction (b).** Point (b) contains two checks. The judgment must not concern a matter within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Turkish courts. And the foreign court must not have taken jurisdiction without a real connection to the subject matter or the parties. The text joins the two with “or” (veya); the legislative reasoning, however, describes them as two conditions that both apply, the first examined of the court's own motion and the second on objection. This article follows the reading in the reasoning.

**Public policy (c).** A difference between the foreign law and Turkish law is not enough; the result of the judgment in Turkey must be *manifestly* contrary to public policy. How this differs from the public policy test when a Turkish court applies foreign law is explained in [Article 5 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-5-mohuk-public-policy-turkey).

**Right to be heard (ç).** Enforcement is refused if, under the laws of the state of origin, the person was not duly summoned, was not represented, or judgment was given in their default or absence in breach of those laws. But the person must raise this in the Turkish proceedings; the court does not apply point (ç) of its own motion. Under the wording, whether the summons and the default judgment were proper is measured by the laws of the state of origin.

Treaties to which Turkey is a party remain unaffected (Article 1(2)); where a treaty covers the matter, its conditions come first. The documents to be filed are listed in Article 53, and the requirement that the judgment be final is in Article 50; see [Article 50 MÖHUK – enforcement](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey).

## Practical points if you live abroad

**No reciprocity check for divorce.** If a German divorce only needs to be entered in the Turkish civil register, recognition is enough and reciprocity is not examined. The Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has corrected a judgment that both “recognised and enforced” a foreign divorce where only recognition was relevant (2nd Civil Chamber 2025/7382, below). If you want the maintenance part of the judgment enforced in Turkey, you need enforcement, and Article 54(1)(a) applies.

**Reciprocity with Germany and the Netherlands.** The court establishes reciprocity anew in each case. In the Court of Cassation decisions I have read in full, for Germany the first-instance court accepted that there was an agreement based on reciprocity between Turkey and Germany, and the Court of Cassation upheld the judgment; the decision does not name the agreement. For the Netherlands, in a case where the Ministry of Justice had only reported that there was no agreement on payment claims, the Court of Cassation required the lower court to examine whether Dutch law or de facto practice allows Turkish judgments to be enforced; in a later case it upheld an appeal judgment that found reciprocity on the basis of Dutch legislation. Reciprocity is therefore not assumed; it is established in the proceedings. Questions of German or Dutch law itself should be put to a lawyer admitted in that country.

**If enforcement is sought against you.** If you knew nothing of the proceedings in Germany or the Netherlands, or were not properly summoned, you have to raise this as an objection in the Turkish proceedings; without an objection, point (ç) is not examined. The same applies to an objection of exorbitant jurisdiction.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2014/4148, K. 2014/10274, T. 02.06.2014**

Enforcement of an Amsterdam payment judgment was refused at first instance on the ground that there was neither an agreement nor a de facto practice between the Netherlands and Turkey. The Chamber reversed: the Ministry's letter only said that there was no agreement on payment claims and did not address de facto practice or whether Dutch law allows Turkish judgments to be enforced. These points had to be investigated.

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2021/8322, K. 2023/2287, T. 13.04.2023**

In a case on a Rotterdam judgment, the regional court of appeal found that, although there is no agreement, Dutch legislation provides for the enforcement of foreign judgments and reciprocity was therefore met. Because the claim had been served on the defendants a reasonable time before the hearing, judgment in their absence did not breach their right to be heard. The Chamber upheld the decision.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/501, K. 2023/2145, T. 04.05.2023**

The claimant sought recognition and enforcement of a child maintenance order of a local court (Amtsgericht) in Frankfurt am Main. The first-instance court found an agreement based on reciprocity between Turkey and Germany, no exclusive Turkish jurisdiction and no breach of public policy, and granted the claim; the appeal failed and the Chamber upheld the judgment.

**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/884, K. 2025/7382, T. 16.09.2025**

A court had ordered that a foreign divorce be “recognised and enforced”. The Chamber held that correcting the civil register does not require enforcement and that the conditions specific to enforcement (Article 54(1)(a)) had not been examined. It deleted the words “and enforced” and upheld the judgment as corrected.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are the conditions for recognition and enforcement the same?

Largely, yes. Recognition also requires the conditions of Article 54, except reciprocity under point (a) (Article 58(1)).

### Which countries have reciprocity with Turkey?

The law contains no list. The court checks in each case whether there is an agreement, a provision of law or a de facto practice. In practice it asks the Ministry of Justice for information (e.g. 11th Civil Chamber 2014/10274).

### Can a default judgment be enforced in Turkey?

Default alone is no bar. If you were not summoned in line with the laws of the state of origin, or judgment was given in your default in breach of those laws, and you object on that ground in the Turkish proceedings, enforcement is refused (Article 54(1)(ç)).

A divorce in Turkey for adultery (zina) is based on Article 161 of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK). You must file within six months of finding out and in any case within five years of the adultery, and if you forgave your spouse you can no longer rely on this article. If the adultery cannot be proven or the time limit has passed, the same events can be raised in a divorce action for breakdown of the marriage (Article 166(1) TMK).

I explain all grounds for divorce and how the proceedings work in [contested divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/contested-divorce-turkey-grounds), and the general conditions for compensation in [divorce compensation in Turkey](/en/publications/divorce-compensation-turkey-damages). This article only covers what is specific to cheating. The provisions cited were checked against the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr in November 2026. The most recent amendment to the TMK (Law No. 7589, Articles 440 and 444, in force since 31 October 2026) does not touch the divorce provisions.

## Divorce for adultery: Article 161 or Article 166(1) TMK?

The Code does not use the word “cheating”. It treats adultery as a specific ground for divorce: “If one spouse commits adultery, the other spouse may file for divorce” (Article 161(1) TMK, unofficial translation). Adultery is not defined in the Code. In legal usage it means sexual intercourse between a married person and someone other than their spouse. Unfaithfulness without intercourse, such as an emotional affair or flirting by message, falls outside this narrow meaning. Spouses owe each other fidelity (Article 185(3) TMK); breaches that fall short of adultery are raised in an action under Article 166(1) TMK.

The difference matters in practice:

- **Article 161 TMK:** once adultery is proven, the court grants the divorce without having to find that married life has become intolerable. In return, the time limits are short and forgiveness ends the right to sue.
- **Article 166(1) TMK:** the Code sets no specific time limit. The claimant must prove that the marriage has broken down so deeply that the spouses cannot be expected to continue living together. If the claimant is more at fault, the defendant may object (Article 166(2) TMK).
- **Property:** only “in a divorce on grounds of adultery or attempt on life” may the court reduce or remove the at-fault spouse's share of the surplus value (artık değer) on equitable grounds (Article 236(2) TMK; for separation of property with sharing, Article 252 TMK). The general rules are in [division of property on divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/division-of-property-divorce-turkey).

This point is disputed. The Code does not say whether Article 236(2) applies where the divorce is granted only under Article 166(1) but the court finds the cheating to be fault. Because the wording refers to a divorce “on grounds of adultery”, it is the author's assessment that a spouse who wants this reduction is safer also basing the action on Article 161; no binding court decision on this is cited here.

## Time limits for divorce on grounds of adultery, and forgiveness

The right to sue lapses “six months after learning of the ground for divorce and in any case five years after the act of adultery” (Article 161(2) TMK). These are limitation periods that extinguish the right itself (hak düşürücü süre), not ordinary prescription; whichever expires first ends the right.

The **six months** start on the day you learn of the adultery. Suspicion is not knowledge. Because your spouse may dispute when you found out, keep anything that shows the date separately from the start. The **five years** run from the act itself; once they have passed, Article 161 is closed even if you never knew. Where there were several acts over time, in the author's view the period runs separately for each act, based on the wording “after the act of adultery”.

“The party who has forgiven has no right to sue” (Article 161(3) TMK). The Code does not say how forgiveness must be given; it can be stated expressly or inferred from conduct. Whether simply carrying on the marriage after finding out counts as forgiveness depends on the facts of each case; the Code sets no test.

The Code is also silent on whether the same events can be relied on in an Article 166(1) action as evidence of breakdown once the time limits have passed or after forgiveness. That this is possible is the author's assessment; no verified decision of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) is cited on it.

## Proving adultery: the ban on unlawfully obtained evidence

In divorce proceedings the court may treat facts as proven only if it is convinced of them. It may not put the parties on oath about them, an admission does not bind it, and it weighs the evidence freely (Article 184 TMK). So if your spouse admits the affair, that does not by itself decide the case.

The Code of Civil Procedure (HMK) is clear: “Evidence obtained unlawfully shall not be taken into account by the court in proving a fact” (Article 189(2) HMK). The court decides whether a piece of evidence is admissible (Article 189(4) HMK). Secretly installing a tracking app on your spouse's phone, logging into their accounts with a cracked password or covertly recording conversations you are not part of may make the evidence unusable and may also be a criminal offence under Turkish law: violation of the confidentiality of communications (Article 132 TCK), listening to and recording conversations (Article 133 TCK), and violation of privacy (Article 134 TCK). Recording a private conversation you take part in without the others' consent is also an offence under Article 133(2) TCK.

The Code does not list which evidence counts as unlawful in a given case, for example messages left open on a shared family computer; that is for the court. I do not cite a verified decision on this point. The ban does not arise for witness statements or documents obtained lawfully, so think about how you are getting evidence before you collect it.

Adultery is not a crime in Turkey; the Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) contains no such offence. The consequences are purely matters of family law.

## Compensation for adultery and claims against the third party

Adultery can be relied on when fault is assessed and for both pecuniary damages (maddi tazminat) and non-pecuniary damages (manevi tazminat). The basis is Article 174 TMK: pecuniary damages may be claimed by the spouse who is not at fault or less at fault, and non-pecuniary damages are paid by the spouse at fault to the spouse whose personal rights were infringed. There is no separate “adultery compensation”, and the Code sets no amount. A separate action must be brought within one year of the divorce judgment becoming final (Article 178 TMK).

Whether you can claim compensation from the person your spouse had the affair with is a separate question. Article 174 TMK only governs claims between the spouses; a claim against a third party could only rest on the general provision on infringement of personal rights in the Code of Obligations (Article 58 TBK). There is no express statutory rule on this, and this article does not report any court decision whose official full text has been checked. If you are considering an action against the third party, have the effect of current case law on your situation assessed.

## Does the cheating spouse lose custody?

The Code does not tie custody (velayet) to the ground for divorce or to fault. Where possible the court hears both parents and settles parental rights and contact with the child (Article 182(1) TMK). In arranging contact, the child's interests, in particular health, education and moral development, are decisive (Article 182(3) TMK).

Adultery is not listed in the Code as a reason for losing custody. In the author's assessment what counts is whether the events have a concrete effect on the child's care and development, which the court examines in each case. The criteria are explained in [child custody after divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey).

## If you live abroad: which court and which law

**Which court.** A divorce action belongs to the court of either spouse's domicile or of the place where the spouses last lived together for six months before the action (Article 168 TMK). If both of you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe, none of these places is usually in Turkey. For Turkish citizens, Article 41 of the Turkish Act on Private International and Procedural Law (MÖHUK) then applies: if the action has not been or cannot be brought before a foreign court, it is heard where the person is resident in Turkey, failing that at their last domicile in Turkey, and failing that by a court in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir. Details are in [Article 41 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-41-mohuk-divorce-jurisdiction-turkey).

**Which law.** A Turkish court applies the spouses' common national law to the grounds and consequences of divorce; if they have different nationalities, the law of their common habitual residence, and failing that Turkish law (Article 14(1) MÖHUK). Custody follows the same law (Article 14(3) MÖHUK). Nationality is determined as at the date of the action (Article 3 MÖHUK), and for dual nationals who are also Turkish, Turkish law applies (Article 4(b) MÖHUK). For adultery this means:

- If both spouses are Turkish citizens, even with a German or Dutch passport as well, the Turkish court applies Articles 161 and 166(1) TMK.
- If one spouse has given up Turkish citizenship (for example a Blue Card holder, Mavi Kart) and both live in Germany, the Turkish court applies German law as the law of the common habitual residence. Article 161 TMK and its time limits then do not apply. For what German law provides, ask a lawyer admitted in Germany.

A German or Dutch court applies its own conflict-of-laws rules. A judgment given there must be recognised in Turkey to have effect there.

The press reports that a 13th judicial package will change divorce procedure. As at 26 November 2026, no law amending Articles 161, 166, 174 or 236 TMK has been published in the Official Gazette; drafts are not in force.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the time limit for divorce on grounds of adultery in Turkey?

Six months from finding out and in any case five years from the act of adultery (Article 161(2) TMK). After forgiveness this article cannot be relied on. For an action under Article 166(1) TMK the Code sets no separate time limit.

### Does adultery affect the division of property?

In a divorce on grounds of adultery the court may reduce or remove the at-fault spouse's share of the surplus value on equitable grounds (Article 236(2) TMK). Otherwise fault does not affect the division of property.

### Can messages from my spouse's phone be used as evidence?

Unlawfully obtained evidence is not taken into account (Article 189(2) HMK). Secretly accessing the phone may also be a criminal offence (Articles 132–134 TCK). The court decides on admissibility.

### Is adultery a crime in Turkey?

No. The Turkish Criminal Code has no offence of adultery; its consequences lie in family law, such as divorce, compensation and division of property.

If you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe, you can give a power of attorney (vekâletname) for matters in Turkey in two ways. You can have it drawn up at the Turkish consulate, which acts as a Turkish notary, or you can sign it before a local notary and have an apostille added in that country. A consulate power of attorney needs no apostille. One made before a foreign notary goes to Turkey with an apostille and a Turkish translation, and you should check in advance whether the land registry will accept it.

This article is about the power of attorney itself: its form, the apostille, its content, how long it lasts and how to revoke it. The steps of a property sale and the title deed fee are covered in [selling property in Turkey from abroad](/en/publications/selling-property-in-turkey-from-abroad), and the inheritance certificate in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey).

## A power of attorney at the Turkish consulate

Outside Turkey, notarial work is done by consuls (Article 191 of the Turkish Notaries Act No. 1512). It is carried out inside the consulate building under the Act's rules on notarial acts (Article 192). Article 193 lists the provisions that apply, among them Articles 78 to 108 on drawn-up and certified instruments. A power of attorney from the consulate is therefore used in Turkey as if a Turkish notary had made it.

The Act knows two forms, and the difference matters in practice:

- **Drawn-up power of attorney (düzenleme şeklinde).** The consul records your declaration in a deed; you read it and sign if it reflects what you want (Articles 84, 86). A deed made this way is valid until proven forged (Article 82(2)).
- **Certified power of attorney (onaylama şeklinde).** You bring the text, and the consul only confirms that the signature is yours (Article 90). The certification does not cover the content (Article 82(3)).

A power of attorney that requires a transaction at the land registry (tapu) must be drawn up (Article 89). If you do not speak Turkish, a sworn interpreter takes part (Article 74). Which acts require a photograph is set by regulation (Article 80). Check on your consulate's page or via konsolosluk.gov.tr which documents and photographs it asks for before your appointment.

Consuls may not charge a notary's fee (Article 194). That does not make the service free; ask your consulate for the current consular fee.

## A power of attorney before a foreign notary, with apostille

You can also sign the power of attorney before a notary in Germany or the Netherlands. Under Turkish conflict-of-laws rules a legal act may follow the form of the country where it is made (Article 7 MÖHUK, Act No. 5718). To use it in Turkey, the document needs an apostille.

Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are parties to the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents. According to the official status table of the Hague Conference (HCCH), it entered into force for Turkey on 29 September 1985, for Germany on 13 February 1966 and for the Netherlands on 8 October 1965 (as of November 2026). Notarial acts are public documents under the Convention (Article 1). The only formality that may be required to certify the signature, the capacity of the signer and the seal is the apostille issued by the competent authority of the state where the document was made (Article 3). Article 195 of the Notaries Act provides for a Turkish consul to confirm the signature of a foreign authority, but it reserves special legislation; between parties to the Convention the apostille replaces that legalisation (Articles 2 and 3 of the Convention).

The apostille comes from the country where the document was made. According to the justice portal of North Rhine-Westphalia, in that state the Landgericht issues apostilles for notarial deeds. In the Netherlands you get an apostille from a rechtbank (Nederland Wereldwijd). Check the official pages of those countries for the competent office and the procedure.

To use the document in Turkey you also need a Turkish translation. A notary in Turkey or the consulate can prepare it (Article 60(6) and Article 193 of the Notaries Act).

A power of attorney made at the consulate falls outside the Apostille Convention: the Convention does not apply to documents executed by diplomatic or consular agents (Article 1(3)(a)). You do not need an apostille for it.

## What the land registry requires

Under Article 18(4) of the Turkish Land Registry Regulation (Tapu Sicili Tüzüğü), an application made through a representative requires a power of attorney drawn up under the Notaries Act No. 1512 that authorises the specific transaction. The principal's signature is mandatory. If the representative has appointed a substitute (tevkil), the underlying power of attorney is also required. The registry then compares the principal with the owner shown in the register.

Content matters as much as form. Without special authority, an agent may not transfer or encumber real property, make a gift, give a guarantee or bring a lawsuit (Article 504(3) of the Turkish Code of Obligations, TBK No. 6098). A "general power of attorney" that does not name these acts is not enough for them.

A power of attorney drawn up at the consulate meets the Regulation's requirement directly. The conditions under which the land registry accepts one made before a foreign notary are set by internal rules of the General Directorate of Land Registry and Cadastre (TKGM). I could not consult the official text for this article, so I do not go into detail. My assessment: for land registry matters the consulate route raises fewer questions. If you want to use a power of attorney from a German or Dutch notary, ask the land registry office concerned or the TKGM helpline Alo 181 first. There is also a restriction for land registry work that depends on the date of the power of attorney; it is explained in the [article on selling property](/en/publications/selling-property-in-turkey-from-abroad).

## Power of attorney for court cases and special powers under Article 74 HMK

For a lawsuit in Turkey, the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK No. 6100) applies. The lawyer must file the original power of attorney certified or drawn up by a notary, or a copy certified by the lawyer (Article 76 HMK). Except where the law requires special authority, a litigation power of attorney covers every step needed to conduct the case until the judgment becomes final, enforcement and recovery of costs (Article 73).

Under Article 74 HMK, unless expressly authorised, the representative may not, among other things:

- settle, waive the claim or a legal remedy, release the other side or accept the claim,
- challenge the judge, amend the whole claim (ıslah), offer, accept, return or refuse an oath,
- appoint a substitute, lift an attachment, petition for the client's bankruptcy,
- enter into an arbitration agreement or use alternative dispute resolution,
- seek a retrial or sue the State over acts of judges,
- bring or pursue claims concerning strictly personal rights, unless it is stated which ones are covered.

The law does not list the strictly personal rights. In my assessment, divorce and parentage cases belong to this group, so have the type of case written into the power of attorney expressly.

Can a power of attorney from a German notary be used for a court case? This point is disputed. What follows is the author's opinion; there is no express statutory provision and no binding court decision on it. It is not certain which view a court would take in a given case. Article 76 HMK refers to a "notary" and does not mention foreign notaries. Since an apostilled foreign notarial deed is a public document under the Convention, it can be argued that, with a Turkish translation, it meets the requirement. With a litigation power of attorney from the consulate, the question does not arise.

## How long is a power of attorney valid?

Turkish law sets no period of validity. If you have stated a time limit or a specific transaction, the authority is limited to that; otherwise it remains valid until you revoke it.

The mandate ends automatically on the death, loss of capacity or bankruptcy of the agent or the principal (Article 513(1) TBK; for the authority to represent, also a declaration of absence, Article 43). This does not apply where the contract or the nature of the business indicates otherwise. If the termination puts the principal's interests at risk, the agent must continue until the principal or the heirs can act themselves (Article 513(2)).

## Revoking a power of attorney given at the consulate

You can revoke it at any time. The principal may end the mandate unilaterally whenever they wish; a party who ends it at an inappropriate time must compensate the resulting loss (Article 512 TBK). The authority can also be merely restricted, and this right cannot be waived in advance (Article 42(1) and (2)).

When revoking, keep three points in mind:

1. Tell the agent. You are liable for acts the agent performs before learning of the termination as if the mandate were still in force (Article 514 TBK). You can have a notice of revocation (azilname) drawn up at the consulate or by a notary in Turkey and served on the agent (Article 60(7) and Article 193 of the Notaries Act).
2. Tell the offices concerned. If you notified third parties of the authority, you cannot rely on its revocation against third parties acting in good faith unless you notify them as well (Article 42(3) TBK). At the land registry, a revocation is entered in the register of revocations with the date, hour and minute of receipt (Article 77 of the Land Registry Regulation).
3. Ask for the document back. Once the authority ends, the agent must return the document (Article 44(1) TBK). If you fail to take the necessary steps, you are liable to third parties in good faith for their loss (Article 44(2)).

If you dismiss your lawyer in a pending case, the dismissal takes effect towards the court and the other side only once it is notified by petition or recorded in the minutes (Article 81 HMK). If you neither appoint a new lawyer within two weeks nor pursue the case yourself, the rules on a party's default apply (Article 83).

According to the current texts on mevzuat.gov.tr (as of November 2026), the Notaries Act, the TBK and the HMK were last amended by Act No. 7589 of 31 July 2026, which does not affect the articles cited here. The official text of the Land Registry Regulation carries no amendment note.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I give a power of attorney for Turkey from abroad?

Book an appointment at the Turkish consulate responsible for your area and have it drawn up there (Articles 191 to 193 of the Notaries Act). The alternative is a local notary, followed by an apostille and a Turkish translation.

### Does a power of attorney from the Turkish consulate need an apostille?

No. The Apostille Convention does not apply to documents executed by consular agents (Article 1(3)(a)); the consulate acts as a Turkish notary.

### Does a Turkish power of attorney expire?

Not by law. Without a time limit it remains valid until revoked or until it ends for a reason such as death, loss of capacity or bankruptcy (Articles 512 and 513 TBK).

### How do I cancel a power of attorney in Turkey?

Revoke it, which you can do at any time (Article 512 TBK), and notify the agent and the offices where the agent acts. At the land registry, the revocation is recorded in the register of revocations (Article 77 of the Land Registry Regulation).

Article 68 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) is the first provision on the rights of association members. Members have equal rights, every member may take part in the association's activities and management, and a member who leaves or is expelled has no claim to the association's assets. It concerns every member of an association (dernek) in Türkiye and the boards that draft or apply a statute.

## Article 68 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **III. Scope**
>
> **1. Rights of members**
>
> **a. Principle of equality**
>
> Article 68 – Members of an association have equal rights. The association may not discriminate between its members on grounds of language, race, colour, sex, religion and sect, family, group and class; it may not adopt practices that undermine equality or grant privileges to some members on these grounds.
>
> Every member has the right to take part in the activities and management of the association.
>
> A member who leaves or is expelled from the association may not claim any right in the association's assets.

Original Turkish text:

> **III. Kapsamı**
>
> **1. Üyelerin hakları**
>
> **a. Eşitlik ilkesi**
>
> Madde 68- Dernek üyeleri eşit haklara sahiptirler. Dernek, üyeleri arasında dil, ırk, renk, cinsiyet, din ve mezhep, aile, zümre ve sınıf farkı gözetemez; eşitliği bozan veya bazı üyelere bu sebeplerle ayrıcalık tanıyan uygulamalar yapamaz.
>
> Her üyenin, derneğin faaliyetlerine ve yönetimine katılma hakkı vardır.
>
> Dernekten çıkan veya çıkarılan üye, dernek malvarlığında hak iddia edemez.

As of November 2026, Article 68 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The reasoning says the article repeats the equality principle of Article 10 of the Turkish Constitution for association members and stresses that members also have equal rights to take part in the association's activities. Paragraph 3 keeps the rule of Article 66 of the former Civil Code No. 743 (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Equal rights and the ban on discrimination

The first sentence of paragraph 1 is the general rule: members have equal rights. The second sentence lists specific grounds; the association may not grant privileges based on language, race, colour, sex, religion and sect, family, group and class.

Voting is where equality is most concrete: each member has one vote in the general assembly and must cast it in person (Article 69(1)). Dues are set by the statute; if it says nothing, members contribute equally to the necessary dues (Article 70(1)). The Code itself makes one distinction: honorary members have no vote and do not pay dues (Articles 69(2) and 70(2)).

> This question is disputed. What follows are views put forward in legal writing; there is no express provision in the law and no binding court decision on the point. Which view a court will adopt in a given case is uncertain.

The Code does not expressly answer two questions. First, whether a statute may make distinctions based not on the listed grounds but on an objective criterion, such as lower dues for students. Second, whether reserving membership from the outset to a particular group, such as people from one village or women, is compatible with Article 68. In the author's assessment, Article 68(1) by its wording prohibits discrimination "between its members", so it governs the relationship after admission; at the admission stage, no association can be compelled to admit members (Article 63).

### Taking part in activities and management

The right in paragraph 2 is given concrete form elsewhere in the Code: voting in the general assembly (Article 69), calling an extraordinary meeting on the written request of one fifth of the members (Article 75), putting an item on the agenda when at least one tenth of the members present ask for it in writing (Article 79(2)), and suing to annul general assembly decisions that breach the law or the statute (Article 83).

### Leaving, expulsion and the association's assets

A member who leaves voluntarily (Article 66) or is expelled (Article 67) cannot ask for a share of the association's assets (paragraph 3). Dues and donations stay with the association. This follows from the fact that an association pursues a purpose other than sharing profit (Article 56). Dues for the period of membership remain payable (Article 70(1)). On expulsion and how to challenge it, see the post on [Article 67 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-67-turkish-civil-code-expulsion-member).

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

Because the vote must be cast in person (Article 69(1)), a member living in Germany or the Netherlands cannot vote by proxy at the general assembly of an association in Türkiye. Decisions taken in writing by all members without meeting are valid, but they do not replace the ordinary meeting (Article 76). On the general assembly, see the post on [Article 62 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-62-tmk-first-general-meeting-association); on membership, see [Article 63 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-63-turkish-civil-code-association-membership).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an association give some members privileges?

Not on grounds of language, race, colour, sex, religion and sect, family, group and class (Article 68(1) TMK). The Code sets separate rules on voting and dues only for honorary members (Articles 69(2) and 70(2)).

### Do I get my dues back when I leave an association?

No. A member who leaves or is expelled has no claim to the association's assets (Article 68(3)).

### Can someone else vote for me at the general assembly?

No. Members must vote in person (Article 69(1)).

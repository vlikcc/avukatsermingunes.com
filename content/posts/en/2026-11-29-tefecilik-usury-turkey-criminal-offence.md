Under Article 241 of the Turkish Criminal Code (Türk Ceza Kanunu, Law No. 5237, TCK), the offence of tefecilik (usury, or illegal moneylending) is committed by anyone who lends money to another person in order to make a profit. The penalty is two to six years' imprisonment and a judicial fine of 500 to 5,000 days; if the offence is committed as part of the activities of an organisation, the penalty is doubled (“bir kat artırılır”).

“Tefecilik” is usually translated as usury, but the Turkish wording does not require an excessive interest rate. Lending money with the aim of making a profit is enough.

Searches for “tefecilik” rose in Turkey this week. This article is not about any investigation, incident or person. It only explains the legal framework as of November 2026. Anyone under investigation is presumed innocent.

## Article 241 TCK: the text

The article sits in the part on “Offences against Society”, in the chapter on “Offences relating to the Economy, Industry and Trade”.

Unofficial translation:

> Usury
>
> Article 241 (1) Any person who lends money to another for the purpose of making a profit shall be punished with imprisonment from two to six years and a judicial fine from five hundred to five thousand days.
>
> (2) (Added: 14/4/2020 – Law No. 7242, Art. 14) Where the offence is committed within the activities of an organisation, the penalty to be imposed shall be increased by one fold.

Turkish original:

> Tefecilik
>
> Madde 241- (1) Kazanç elde etmek amacıyla başkasına ödünç para veren kişi, iki yıldan altı yıla kadar hapis ve beşyüz günden beşbin güne kadar adlî para cezası ile cezalandırılır.
>
> (2) (Ek:14/4/2020-7242/14 md.) Suçun bir örgütün faaliyeti çerçevesinde işlenmesi hâlinde verilecek ceza bir kat artırılır.

The official text carries this footnote to paragraph 1: “14/4/2020 tarihli ve 7242 sayılı Kanunun 14 üncü maddesiyle, bu fıkrada yer alan “beş yıla kadar hapis ve” ibaresi “altı yıla kadar hapis ve beşyüz günden” şeklinde değiştirilmiştir.” In other words, Law No. 7242 replaced “up to five years' imprisonment and” with “up to six years' imprisonment and from five hundred days”. That change and paragraph 2 have applied since 15 April 2020. The last entry in the amendment table of the TCK text on mevzuat.gov.tr is Law No. 7593 (in force since 18 August 2026; Articles 31 and 233). Article 241 has not been amended since 2020, and my search for August to November 2026 found no change either.

## Elements of the offence

Three elements follow from the wording:

1. **Act:** lending money to another person.
2. **Object:** money; the text says “ödünç para” (money on loan).
3. **Purpose:** making a profit. Article 241 does not provide for a negligent form, so only intentional conduct is punishable (Article 22(1) TCK); the offender must also act with the aim of profit.

According to the wording, the offender is the person who lends the money. The text does not require a particular interest rate, a professional or habitual activity, or loans to several people.

Some questions are not answered by the wording: whether a single loan is enough, whether gold or foreign currency counts as “money”, and where the limits of “profit” lie. These points are disputed. The law contains no express provision on them, and I could not verify a binding court decision in an official database for this article; the answer depends on interpretation. Which view a court will take in a particular case is uncertain.

## Usury within an organisation; legal persons

If the offence is committed within the activities of an organisation, the penalty is doubled (Article 241(2)). Founding or leading a criminal organisation, and membership of one, are separate offences under Article 220 TCK. Since Law No. 7571 (in force since 25 December 2025), founders and leaders face five to ten years' imprisonment (Article 220(1)) and members two to five years (Article 220(2)); an organisation requires at least three members. Offences committed within the organisation's activities are punished in addition (Article 220(4)).

If a legal person obtained an unlawful benefit through the offence, the security measures specific to legal persons are ordered against it (Article 242). Article 60 lists them: revocation of an official licence and confiscation.

## Is a complaint required? Is mediation available?

Tefecilik is not a complaint-based offence. Where the TCK requires a complaint (şikâyet), the article says so expressly, as the immediately preceding Article 239 does with “şikayet üzerine” (upon complaint). Article 241 contains no such wording. The public prosecutor starts investigating as soon as he or she learns, by a report or otherwise, of facts suggesting that an offence has been committed (Article 160(1) of the Turkish Code of Criminal Procedure, Law No. 5271, CMK). Withdrawing a complaint therefore does not end the case; that effect applies only to complaint-based offences (Article 73(4) TCK). Reports can be made to the chief public prosecutor's office (Cumhuriyet başsavcılığı) or the police (Article 158(1) CMK).

Criminal mediation (uzlaştırma) under Article 253 CMK is not available either. Article 253(1) covers complaint-based offences, the TCK offences listed one by one in point (b), and, for children involved in criminal proceedings, offences with a maximum sentence of up to three years. Article 241 is not on the list, is not complaint-based, and carries a maximum of six years. Article 253 was last amended by Law No. 7593 (in force since 18 August 2026), which replaced “suça sürüklenen” with “adli süreçteki” in point (c).

## How is usury proved?

There is no special rule of evidence. The general rule applies: the offence may be proved by any lawfully obtained evidence, and the judge assesses the evidence freely according to his or her conviction (Article 217 CMK). What must be shown is that money was lent and that it was lent to make a profit. Promissory notes (senet), cheques, bank records, messages and witness statements can all serve as evidence. The court decides in each case what is sufficient.

## Limitation period and competent court

The limitation period for prosecution depends on the statutory maximum penalty; where imprisonment and a fine are alternatives, imprisonment is decisive (Article 66(4) TCK). The maximum term in Article 241(1) is six years. Under Article 66(1)(d), offences carrying “more than five and less than twenty years” of imprisonment become time-barred after **fifteen years**. Aggravated forms are taken into account according to the evidence in the file (Article 66(3)). In the organisation case the doubled maximum is twelve years, which falls under the same point, so the period remains fifteen years. It runs from the day the offence was committed (Article 66(6)). A statement taken before the prosecutor, a detention order or an indictment interrupts the period (Article 67(2)); interruption can extend it by at most half, to a maximum of twenty-two years and six months (Article 67(4)).

The competent court is determined by Law No. 5235 on the organisation of the courts. Under Article 12, the heavy penal court (ağır ceza mahkemesi) hears certain listed offences and offences punishable by more than ten years' imprisonment; Article 241 is not listed. Under Article 14, jurisdiction depends on the statutory maximum penalty without regard to aggravating or mitigating circumstances. With a maximum of six years, the offence under Article 241(1) is therefore heard by the general criminal court of first instance (**asliye ceza mahkemesi**) (Article 11). Whether the increase in Article 241(2) is an “aggravating circumstance” within the meaning of Article 14 may affect jurisdiction in the organisation case; since this cannot be derived with certainty from the text, I draw no conclusion for that case.

## Notes and cheques given to a moneylender

The criminal case and the debt are separate matters. What happens to a promissory note or cheque given to a moneylender is decided by the civil courts and under enforcement law.

The statutory rules are these. Contracts that violate mandatory provisions of law, morality or public order are null and void (“kesin hükümsüz”, Article 27(1) of the Turkish Code of Obligations, Law No. 6098, TBK). The contractually agreed annual interest rate may not exceed the rate that applies by law in the absence of an agreement by more than fifty per cent (Article 88(2) TBK). That base rate is the statutory interest rate; Article 1 of Law No. 3095 was amended by Law No. 7589 with effect from 31 July 2026 and sets it at eighty per cent of the Central Bank's rediscount rate. Special rules for commercial transactions are outside the scope of this article. A debtor may bring an action for a negative declaration (menfi tespit davası) before or during enforcement proceedings to prove that nothing is owed; if the debt has already been paid, the action continues as an action for restitution (istirdat davası) (Article 72 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, Law No. 2004, İİK).

How these rules apply to a usurious loan is not expressly stated in the law. Whether the loan is void under Article 27 TBK as a whole or only as to interest, whether the capital paid out can be reclaimed, and how findings in the criminal case affect the civil case depend on interpretation and on the facts. These points are disputed: there is no express statutory provision, I could not verify a binding court decision, and which view a court will take in a particular case is uncertain. In statutory terms, the question people search for as “cancelling the note” (senedin iptali) is raised through the negative declaration and restitution actions in Article 72 İİK. Under paragraph 1 of that article, the debtor must prove that nothing is owed.

## If you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe

Turkish law applies to offences committed in Turkey (Article 8(1) TCK). If you live abroad and gave a note or cheque for a loan in Turkey, both the criminal side and an action for a negative declaration against enforcement are governed by Turkish law. Negative declaration and restitution actions may be brought at the court where the enforcement office is located or at the defendant's place of residence (Article 72 İİK, last paragraph). For grounds and time limits in detention matters, see [pre-trial detention in Turkey](/en/publications/pre-trial-detention-turkey-grounds-appeal).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the penalty for tefecilik?

Two to six years' imprisonment and a judicial fine of 500 to 5,000 days (Article 241(1) TCK); within an organisation the penalty is doubled (Article 241(2)).

### Does tefecilik require a complaint?

No. Article 241 does not contain the words “şikayet üzerine”; withdrawing a complaint does not end the case.

### What is the limitation period for tefecilik?

Fifteen years (Article 66(1)(d) TCK); with interruptions, at most twenty-two years and six months (Article 67(4)).

### Which court hears tefecilik cases?

For the offence under Article 241(1), the asliye ceza mahkemesi (Articles 11, 12 and 14 of Law No. 5235). This article draws no conclusion for the organisation case.

If the date of birth in your Turkish civil register (nüfus kaydı) is wrong and the error is not a simple clerical mistake made when the entry was recorded, you can only change it with a final court judgment (Population Services Law No. 5490, Art. 35(1); Turkish Civil Code – TMK, Art. 39). The case, known in Turkish as yaş düzeltme davası, is filed at the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) for your place of residence and heard in the presence of the head of the civil registry office (nüfus müdürü) or an officer he appoints (Law No. 5490, Art. 36(1)(a)).

Winning the case does not make the new date count everywhere. Turkish social security law ignores corrections made after you were first insured when it calculates a pension, and changes made after you reach military service age are, as a rule, ignored for military service. I checked the provisions below against the current texts in UYAP Mevzuat in November 2026. Turkish speakers also call these cases "yaş küçültme" (making yourself younger) or "yaş büyütme" (older); the same rules apply to both.

## Clerical error or court case?

Not every wrong date needs a court. If the correct date appears in the source document (dayanak belge) but was copied wrongly or incompletely into the register, it is a clerical error (maddî hata), and the General Directorate of Population or the registry office corrects it itself (Law No. 5490, Arts. 35(1) and 38(1)). If the error does not come from copying the source document, the register can only be corrected by court order (Population Services Implementing Regulation, Art. 41(2)).

The law leaves two situations to the authorities. The General Directorate converts dates recorded in the Hijri or Rumi calendar into the Gregorian year and fills in missing months and days (Art. 39(1)). Where only the year of birth is recorded, age is calculated from 1 July of that year; where the month is recorded but not the day, from the first day of that month (Art. 39(2)).

If your birth was reported abroad, the source document is the official certificate or report issued by the foreign authority (Law No. 5490, Art. 15(6)). If your German or Dutch birth certificate shows the right date but the Turkish register does not, my assessment is that asking the registry office or the consulate to correct it as a clerical error is the shorter route. How the birth entry is made is explained in [Birth registration in Turkey for a child born abroad](/en/publications/birth-registration-turkey-child-born-abroad).

## Requirements and evidence

Birth is proven by the entry in the civil register. If there is no entry, or the entry turns out to be wrong, the true facts can be proven by any evidence (TMK, Art. 30). Official registers are evidence of the facts they record; unless the law says otherwise, proving that their content is wrong is not tied to any particular form (TMK, Art. 7). As the claimant, you must prove that the recorded date is wrong and what your real date of birth is.

The law does not list the evidence. Two decisions of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay), which I read in full in UYAP Emsal, show what the court looks at:

- **Yargıtay, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2021/6260, K. 2021/7662, 25.10.2021.** Witnesses had said the claimant was born in a state hospital. The chamber found the court had not investigated enough because it granted the claim without asking the hospital for its birth records and birth certificate. The judgment was reversed in the interest of the law (kanun yararına), at the Ministry of Justice's request, without affecting the outcome.
- **Yargıtay, 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2026/4751, K. 2026/5580, 14.05.2026.** The chamber stated that cases to correct a date of birth are closely linked to public order and that the judge must investigate of their own motion, without being bound by the request. The file contained birth certificates and a medical board report prepared with bone X-rays. The chamber added that correcting the date of birth does not by itself change parentage. This too was a reversal in the interest of the law.

Both decisions concern single files, and a reversal in the interest of the law does not remove the legal effects of the judgment (Code of Civil Procedure – HMK, Art. 363(2)). They are not settled case law; they are examples of how official records and medical examinations are used as evidence.

## Which court, and who takes part

### Jurisdiction

Cases to correct civil register entries are filed by the person concerned or, at the request of the relevant authority, by the public prosecutor, at the competent civil court of first instance for the place of the residential address (Law No. 5490, Art. 36(1)(a); Regulation, Art. 30(1)).

For people living abroad, territorial jurisdiction is not clearly settled. The address records of Turkish citizens resident abroad are kept according to the address data of their country of residence (Law No. 5490, Art. 49(3)), and the international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (MÖHUK, Art. 40). The law does not say which court in Turkey hears the case of someone whose only residence is in Germany or the Netherlands. I am not citing a verified court decision on this point; it should be assessed for your situation before the case is filed.

### Is it non-contentious?

The HMK lists "correction of an entry in the civil status register" among non-contentious matters (çekişmesiz yargı, HMK, Art. 382(2)(a)(5)). Non-contentious matters normally go to the magistrates' court (sulh hukuk mahkemesi), but only "unless otherwise provided" (HMK, Art. 383); for civil register entries, Art. 36 of Law No. 5490 provides otherwise.

The case is heard in the presence of the registry office head or an appointed officer. Under the Regulation, he takes part as a party for the registry office and tells the court whether the request complies with the rules (Regulation, Art. 31(1)–(2)). The requirement that the public prosecutor be present was removed in 2011 by Art. 31 of Law No. 6217. Registry offices may appeal against judgments and are exempt from court fees and costs (Law No. 5490, Art. 37).

## How many times can you apply? Finality

The current text of the law sets no numerical limit. Under the provision as amended by Law No. 7039 in 2017, where there are justified reasons a further correction on the same subject may be requested from the judge (Law No. 5490, Art. 36(1)(b); Regulation, Art. 30(2)).

The first-instance judgment can be appealed to a regional court (istinaf). Regional court decisions in cases to correct civil register entries cannot be taken to the Court of Cassation, except where they affect parentage (HMK, Art. 362(1)(d)). For this reason the 2nd Civil Chamber upheld the rejection of an appeal in a case to correct a date of birth (E. 2024/1559, K. 2024/1047, 21.02.2024). Many published Yargıtay decisions on this subject are therefore appeals in the interest of the law (HMK, Art. 363).

Once final, the judgment is entered in the register. If the court corrected only the year of birth, the month and day stay as they are (Regulation, Art. 34(1)(c)).

## Pension and military service: the new date does not count everywhere

### Turkish social security (Law No. 5510, Art. 57)

For age rules in invalidity, old-age and survivors' insurance, the date of birth recorded in the register on the day you first became subject to that insurance applies (Law No. 5510, Art. 57(2)). Paragraph 3 reads (unofficial translation):

> In calculating income and pension awards and capital value under the work accident, occupational disease, invalidity, old-age and survivors' insurance, age corrections made after the date of the work accident, the date on which the occupational disease was first established by a medical report, or the date on which the insured first started work subject to this Law or to the laws repealed by this Law, are not taken into account.

> İş kazası, meslek hastalığı, malûllük, yaşlılık ve ölüm sigortalarından gelir ve aylık tahsisleri ile sermaye değerinin hesabında, iş kazasının olduğu veya meslek hastalığının hekim raporuyla ilk defa tespit edildiği veya sigortalıların bu Kanuna ve bu Kanunla yürürlükten kaldırılmış kanunlara tâbi olarak ilk defa çalışmaya başladığı tarihten sonraki yaş düzeltmeleri dikkate alınmaz.

The 10th Civil Chamber of the Yargıtay upheld a judgment refusing an old-age pension to a claimant who was insured in 2002 and had his year of birth changed by a court from 1973 to 1964 in 2003 (E. 2025/17116, K. 2026/6088, 30.04.2026). This rule applies only within the Turkish social security system.

### Military service (Military Service Law No. 7179, Art. 14)

Military service age begins, according to the civil register, on 1 January of the year in which a man turns 20 and ends on 1 January of the year in which he turns 41 (Law No. 7179, Art. 3(1)(a)). On changes of age the law says (unofficial translation):

> (3) Those whose age changed before they reached military service age are examined according to their changed age.
>
> (4) Changes of age made after reaching military service age are not taken into account in military service matters. However, changes of age made ex officio on the basis of a court decision not obtained at the person's own request are accepted.

> (3) Askerlik çağına girmeden önce yaşları değişenlerin yoklamaları, değişen yaşlarına göre yapılır.
>
> (4) Askerlik çağına girdikten sonra yapılan yaş değişiklikleri, askerlik işlemlerinde dikkate alınmaz. Ancak kendi isteği dışındaki mahkeme kararına istinaden resen yapılan yaş değişiklikleri kabul edilir.

If you change your age through your own case after reaching military service age, your military service matters continue under the old date. For paid exemption and deferral while living abroad, see [Turkish military service: paid exemption](/en/publications/turkish-military-service-paid-exemption).

## If you live in Germany or the Netherlands

The Turkish judgment corrects the Turkish register. Whether German or Dutch registers, documents and social security records follow is a matter for the law of those countries.

In Germany, for age-dependent social security rights, the date of birth that counts is the one first given to a social security body or, in certain cases, to the employer; the body may depart from it only for a clerical error or where an original document issued before that first statement shows a different date (§ 33a SGB I). How that rule applies to your Turkish judgment is a question of German law. In the Netherlands, the municipality (gemeente) where you live is responsible for your entry in the Basisregistratie Personen (BRP). For these questions, contact the Deutsche Rentenversicherung, your municipality, or a lawyer qualified in that country.

A lawyer in Turkey can bring the case for you under a power of attorney. The court may order witnesses to be heard by video and audio link from where they are (HMK, Art. 149(2)). The same court and procedure apply to a change of name: [How to change your name in Turkey](/en/publications/how-to-change-your-name-in-turkey).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which court handles a change of date of birth in Turkey?

The civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) for the place of the residential address (Law No. 5490, Art. 36(1)(a)). The law names no special court for people whose only residence is abroad.

### My German birth certificate is correct but my Turkish record is wrong. Do I need a court case?

If the correct date already appeared in the source document, it may be a clerical error that the registry office corrects itself (Law No. 5490, Arts. 35(1) and 38). Otherwise a court case is needed.

### How many times can I change my date of birth in Turkey?

The law sets no numerical limit. A further correction on the same subject requires justified reasons (Law No. 5490, Art. 36(1)(b)).

### How long does it take and what does it cost?

The law sets no time frame; it depends on the evidence, any medical board report and whether there is an appeal. I do not give court fee amounts because I could not verify current figures from an official source for this article; the court where the case is filed can tell you.

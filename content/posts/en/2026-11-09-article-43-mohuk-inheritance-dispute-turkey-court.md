Article 43 of Turkish Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) decides which Turkish court hears inheritance disputes with a foreign element. If the deceased’s last domicile was in Turkey, the court there has jurisdiction; if it was abroad, the court where assets of the estate are located in Turkey. When a Turkish citizen dies in Germany or the Netherlands and leaves a flat, a plot of land or a bank account in Turkey, this is usually the provision that tells you where to sue.

## Article 43 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Inheritance actions**
>
> Article 43 – (1) Actions relating to inheritance are heard by the court of the deceased’s last domicile in Turkey or, if the last domicile is not in Turkey, by the court of the place where assets belonging to the estate are located.

Turkish original:

> **Miras davaları**
>
> MADDE 43 – (1) Mirasa ilişkin davalar ölenin Türkiye'deki son yerleşim yeri mahkemesinde, son yerleşim yerinin Türkiye'de olmaması hâlinde terekeye dâhil malların bulunduğu yer mahkemesinde görülür.

The article has not changed since the Law entered into force on 12 December 2007; as of November 2026 the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system carries no amendment note.

## Legislative rationale

According to the rationale of the government bill, the article partly corresponds to Article 30 of the former Law No. 2675. The rule itself was not changed; to align with the Civil Code, only the word “ikametgâh” was replaced by “yerleşim yeri” (domicile). (Source: article rationale of the bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 42 of the bill.)

## Explanation

### Which actions “relate to inheritance”?

Article 43 does not list them. In domestic law, Article 11 of the Turkish Code of Civil Procedure (HMK, Law No. 6100) lists actions arising from succession: division of the estate, invalidity of a division agreement, annulment (iptal) and abatement (tenkis) of testamentary dispositions, actions for recovery of the inheritance (miras sebebiyle istihkak), disputes among heirs about administering the estate, and all actions against the heirs until the estate is finally divided. Since the international jurisdiction of Turkish courts generally follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Article 40 MÖHUK), in my view this list can also guide the scope of Article 43; the law does not say so expressly.

### Two connecting factors

The first is the last domicile (yerleşim yeri). Domicile is the place where a person lives with the intention of staying permanently, and no one can have two domiciles at the same time (Article 19 of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK). Succession opens at the deceased’s domicile (Article 576 TMK). The second half of the sentence (“if the last domicile is not in Turkey”) shows that the domicile at the time of death is what counts. In my assessment, someone who lived in Turkey decades ago, then settled in Germany and died there, had no “last domicile in Turkey” within the meaning of the article.

If the last domicile was abroad, the second factor applies: the place where estate assets are located. Article 11 HMK gives exclusive jurisdiction to the court of the deceased’s last domicile; when that domicile is abroad, the domestic rule points to no Turkish court. Article 43 fills that gap.

If there are assets in several places in Turkey, the law does not say expressly which court has jurisdiction. That any of those courts may hear the case, given the wording (“the place where assets … are located”), is a view; there is no express provision and no verified binding court decision on the point, so how a court would decide is uncertain.

### Applicable law and property actions

Article 43 only allocates jurisdiction. Which law governs the succession is set out in Article 20 MÖHUK: the national law of the deceased applies, and Turkish law applies to immovable property in Turkey (see [Article 20 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-20-mohuk-inheritance-turkey)). Provisions of international treaties to which Turkey is a party are reserved (Article 1(2) MÖHUK).

Ending co-ownership (ortaklığın giderilmesi) of an inherited property is a separate action. It is heard by the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi, Article 4(1)(b) HMK), with exclusive jurisdiction at the place of the property (Article 12(1) HMK). Since 31 July 2026 a new rule applies to the auction: if all owners acquired the property by inheritance and no one else holds ownership, the first auction is held among the heirs only (Article 114 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, İİK, added by Law No. 7589). Details in [Inherited property in Turkey: partition and sale](/en/publications/inherited-property-turkey-partition-lawsuit).

### Is the jurisdiction of Turkish courts exclusive?

This is disputed. The law does not state whether Article 43 gives Turkish courts exclusive jurisdiction that excludes foreign courts. The question arises when a foreign inheritance judgment is to be enforced (tenfiz) in Turkey: foreign judgments on matters within the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts are not enforced (Article 54(1)(b) MÖHUK). In the decision of the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu) of 6 November 2024, E. 2023/1038, K. 2024/538, the view that inheritance matters fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts was raised during deliberations; the majority did not adopt it and based the decision on the fact that the German document submitted for recognition was not a court judgment within the meaning of Article 50 MÖHUK. I have not found a binding decision that settles the question.

## What matters if you live abroad

### The deceased died in Germany: where is the case filed?

An example: the deceased lived and died in Cologne and owned a flat in Kayseri and a plot in Izmir. Because the last domicile was not in Turkey, an action in Turkey to annul a will or to abate a disposition that infringes a reserved share (saklı pay) is filed in Kayseri or Izmir, where the assets are (on several locations, see the view above). Unless a special rule applies, the competent court is the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi, Article 2(1) HMK). If the heirs want the Kayseri flat sold, the case goes to the civil court of peace in Kayseri (Articles 4(1)(b) and 12(1) HMK). For an overview, see the [inheritance law](/en/practice-areas/inheritance-law) page.

### Assets in more than one country

Article 43 only governs the jurisdiction of Turkish courts. Whether German or Dutch courts decide on the same estate depends on their own law, and so does the effect of a Turkish judgment on assets located there. For that part, please consult a lawyer admitted in that country.

### Certificate of inheritance: notary or court?

A Turkish certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi, veraset ilamı) is issued by the civil court of peace or a notary (Article 598(1) TMK). It is not a lawsuit but non-contentious proceedings (Article 382(2)(c)(6) HMK). If issuing it requires court proceedings, if the civil registry records are insufficient, or if foreigners apply, the notary may not issue it (Article 71/B of Notaries Law No. 1512); the civil court of peace then decides. The 14th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation applied Article 43 to a certificate application for a German deceased with no domicile in Turkey and held the court of peace where the land was located competent (see below). In actions to annul a certificate and issue a new one, the court where any of the heirs lives also has jurisdiction (Article 11(3) HMK). Practical details: [Certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey).

### Renunciation and protective measures

A renunciation is entered in a special register by the court of peace of the place where succession opened (Article 609 TMK); protective measures for the estate are taken by the judge of the peace at the deceased’s domicile (Article 589 TMK). These articles do not say which Turkish court of peace is competent when the domicile was abroad. In the 2022 decision below, the Court of Cassation chose the court of the place where the deceased was entered in the civil registry.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 14. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2015/1997, K. 2015/6013, T. 01.06.2015**
A certificate of inheritance was sought for a German national with no domicile in Turkey, and two courts of peace declined jurisdiction. The Chamber held that jurisdiction in inheritance matters with a foreign element is governed by Article 43 MÖHUK, found the Balıkesir court of peace, where the property was located, competent and reversed the decision declining jurisdiction.

**Yargıtay 5. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2020/9261, K. 2020/10291, T. 23.11.2020**
The case concerned handing over the estate of a Dutch national. The Chamber noted that the civil registry showed his domicile in the Netherlands and that a hotel address in Turkey did not show an intention to stay permanently (Article 19 TMK); referring to the place of death and the bank branch holding the estate’s deposits, it designated the court of peace there.

**Yargıtay 5. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2022/1041, K. 2022/2636, T. 21.02.2022**
In a renunciation file, the deceased’s registered domicile and MERNİS address were in the United States, and no last domicile before death could be established. The Chamber cited Article 43 and then designated the court of peace in Çivril, where the deceased was entered in the civil registry. The place of registry is not a connecting factor under Article 43, and a single decision does not make settled case law.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which court hears an inheritance dispute in Turkey?

For the actions listed in Article 11 HMK, the court of the deceased’s last domicile has exclusive jurisdiction. If that domicile was abroad, the court where estate assets are located in Turkey has jurisdiction (Article 43 MÖHUK). Abatement and will annulment cases go to the civil court of first instance, co-ownership cases to the civil court of peace.

### My father died in Germany. Where do I sue over his house in Turkey?

If his last domicile was in Germany, before the Turkish court where the house is located. An action to end co-ownership is always filed where the property is (Article 12(1) HMK).

Article 60 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK, Official Gazette of 12.12.2007, No. 26728) governs the enforcement (tenfiz) of foreign arbitral awards (yabancı hakem kararı) in Turkey. It answers two questions: which foreign awards can be declared enforceable, and in which court the application is filed. If you obtained an arbitral award in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere and the debtor has assets in Turkey, this is where enforcement of the arbitral award in Turkey starts.

## Article 60 MÖHUK text

Unofficial translation:

> **Enforcement of foreign arbitral awards**
>
> Article 60 – (1) Foreign arbitral awards that have become final and enforceable or that are binding on the parties may be enforced.
>
> (2) Enforcement of foreign arbitral awards is requested by petition from the court of first instance (asliye mahkemesi) of the place agreed in writing by the parties. Where there is no such agreement, the court of the place of domicile in Turkey of the party against whom the award was made, failing that the court of the place where that party resides, and failing that the court of the place where assets subject to enforcement are located, has jurisdiction.

Original Turkish text:

> **Yabancı hakem kararlarının tenfizi**
>
> MADDE 60 – (1) Kesinleşmiş ve icra kabiliyeti kazanmış veya taraflar için bağlayıcı olan yabancı hakem kararları tenfiz edilebilir.
>
> (2) Yabancı hakem kararlarının tenfizi, tarafların yazılı olarak kararlaştırdıkları yer asliye mahkemesinden dilekçeyle istenir. Taraflar arasında böyle bir anlaşma olmadığı takdirde, aleyhine karar verilen tarafın Türkiye'deki yerleşim yeri, yoksa sâkin olduğu, bu da yoksa icraya konu teşkil edebilecek malların bulunduğu yer mahkemesi yetkili sayılır.

The article has not been amended since the law entered into force on 12.12.2007. As of November 2026, the amendment table of the current text in the UYAP Mevzuat system lists only Article 27 (amended by Law No. 7550).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article partly corresponds to Article 43 of the former Law No. 2675. Because in international arbitration an award being binding on the parties is generally considered enough on its own for recognition and enforcement, this criterion was added to paragraph 1. In paragraph 2 the term “ikametgâh” was replaced by “yerleşim yeri” (domicile) to align with the Turkish Civil Code (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 59 of the bill).

## Explanation

### When is an award “foreign”?

MÖHUK does not define a foreign arbitral award. The line is drawn by the Turkish arbitration statutes. Law No. 4686 on International Arbitration (MTK) applies to disputes with a foreign element where the seat of arbitration is in Turkey, or where the parties or the arbitrators chose that law (Art. 1(2) MTK). Disputes without a foreign element seated in Turkey fall under Articles 407 et seq. of the Code of Civil Procedure (HMK). Awards under these two laws are Turkish awards and are not subject to enforcement proceedings. For an MTK award, the court of first instance issues a certificate of enforceability once an annulment action has been finally dismissed, the time limit for it has expired, or the parties have waived it (Art. 15(B) and Additional Article 1 MTK).

An award seated abroad is, as a rule, foreign and can only be enforced in Turkey under Articles 60 et seq. MÖHUK or the New York Convention. Borderline cases are disputed. The law has no express rule making the seat the sole test, and I have not found a binding court decision on the point. A case in which an award with a stated seat in The Hague was treated as domestic is summarised below (6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1895, K. 2026/1542); the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) did not discuss the point separately, and a single affirmance is not settled case law.

### Paragraph 1: final or binding

The law gives two alternatives: the award is final and enforceable, or it is binding on the parties. Either is enough. According to the reasoning, a binding award need not also be final and enforceable under the law of the state where it was made.

The Yargıtay treats this requirement as a procedural precondition that the court examines of its own motion. It did not accept as sufficient that the institution’s rules describe the award as “final”. If necessary with an expert report on those rules, it must be established beyond doubt that the award is final or binding (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/5986, K. 2024/2257).

### Paragraph 2: competent court

The application goes to the “asliye mahkemesi”, the court of first instance. In a commercial dispute, the commercial court of first instance (asliye ticaret mahkemesi) has subject-matter jurisdiction (Art. 5(1) Turkish Commercial Code; 19th Civil Chamber, E. 2014/208, K. 2014/4501). In that court, cases on recognition and enforcement of foreign arbitral awards are heard by a panel of a presiding judge and two members, regardless of the amount in dispute (Art. 5(3)(4) of Law No. 5235). The jurisdiction of the regional court of appeal under Art. 410 HMK concerns domestic arbitration only, not the enforcement of foreign awards (20th Civil Chamber, E. 2017/7998, K. 2017/7167).

Territorial jurisdiction follows this order:

1. the court of the place agreed in writing by the parties;
2. without such an agreement, the court of the losing party’s domicile in Turkey;
3. without a domicile, the court of the place where that party resides, and otherwise the court where assets subject to enforcement are located.

For foreign court judgments there is a fallback to the courts of Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir (Art. 51(2) MÖHUK). For arbitral awards there is none. If the debtor has no domicile, residence or assets in Turkey, the law names no court. In my view, enforcement proceedings in Turkey would then serve no practical purpose either; the law has no express rule for this situation.

### Relationship with the New York Convention

International conventions to which Turkey is a party are reserved (Art. 1(2) MÖHUK), and international agreements duly put into effect have the force of law (Art. 90(5) of the Turkish Constitution). Ratification of the New York Convention of 10 June 1958 on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards was approved by Law No. 3731 of 8.5.1991 (Official Gazette of 21.5.1991, No. 20877); the Convention was ratified and published by Council of Ministers Decision No. 91/2151 of 15.8.1991 (Official Gazette of 25.9.1991, No. 21002). According to the UNCITRAL status list, it entered into force for Turkey on 30.9.1992.

Under Article 2 of Law No. 3731, Turkey made two declarations. On the basis of reciprocity, it applies the Convention only to awards made in the territory of another contracting state. It also applies it only to disputes arising from legal relationships, contractual or not, that are considered commercial under Turkish law. Germany and the Netherlands are contracting states, so the Convention applies to a commercial award made there. For non-commercial disputes, or awards from non-contracting states, Articles 60 et seq. MÖHUK apply.

The Convention does not say which court hears the case; awards are enforced in accordance with the rules of procedure of the territory where the award is relied upon (Art. III). In my view, the competent court is therefore determined under Art. 60(2) MÖHUK even when the Convention applies. The documents to be filed and the grounds for refusal belong to Articles 61 and 62 and are covered in separate articles.

## Practical points for readers living abroad

**Debtor in Germany, assets in Turkey.** A debtor living abroad usually has no “domicile in Turkey”. If he or she does not reside in Turkey either, the case is filed where enforceable assets are located, such as a flat or a bank account.

**Choosing the enforcement court in the contract.** The law looks first at the parties’ written agreement. If you do business with Turkey, you can agree in writing, when drafting the arbitration clause, which Turkish court will hear a later enforcement case.

**Proof that the award is binding.** Documents showing that the time limit for setting the award aside at the seat has expired, or that the award is final under the arbitration rules, make the court’s own examination easier. Article 61 lists the documents to attach to the petition.

You do not have to travel to Turkey; the case can be handled through an authorised representative. For the framework on foreign court judgments, see [Article 50 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-50-mohuk-enforcement-turkey) and [Article 51 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-51-mohuk-competent-court-turkey); general information is on the [private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law) page.

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2022/5986, K. 2024/2257, T. 20.03.2024**

For an award seated in Atlanta (USA), the first-instance court had relied on the institution’s rules describing the award as final. The Chamber held that the requirement in Art. 60(1) is a procedural precondition examined of the court’s own motion and reversed: the award may not be enforced until it is established beyond doubt, if necessary through an expert report, that it is final and enforceable or binding.

**Yargıtay 20. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2017/7998, K. 2017/7167, T. 02.10.2017**

A commercial court had declined jurisdiction under Art. 410 HMK in favour of the regional court of appeal. The Chamber held that the HMK arbitration provisions apply only to disputes without a foreign element seated in Turkey, and that the commercial court has jurisdiction to enforce a foreign award arising from a commercial sale.

**Yargıtay 11. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/6475, K. 2024/7846, T. 07.11.2024**

For an award made in England, the regional court of appeal cited the declarations in Law No. 3731 and held that, England being a contracting state, the New York Convention and not MÖHUK governed the case. The Yargıtay upheld the judgment with general reasoning.

**Yargıtay 6. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/1895, K. 2026/1542, T. 16.04.2026**

The applicant sought recognition of an ICC award with a stated seat in The Hague and of a related judgment of the Dutch Supreme Court (Hoge Raad). The first-instance court treated the award as a domestic MTK award because both parties were Turkish companies, Turkish was the language of the arbitration and Turkish arbitrators had conducted the proceedings in Ankara; such an award needs no enforcement proceedings in Turkey. The appeal was dismissed and the Yargıtay upheld the judgment with general reasoning.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an arbitral award be enforced in Turkey before it becomes final?

Besides an award that is final and enforceable, an award that is binding on the parties can also be enforced (Art. 60(1)). The binding effect must be clearly shown in the file; the Yargıtay examines it of its own motion.

### Does the New York Convention apply to an award made in Germany?

Germany is a contracting state. If the dispute is commercial under Turkish law, the Convention applies; otherwise Articles 60 et seq. MÖHUK apply.

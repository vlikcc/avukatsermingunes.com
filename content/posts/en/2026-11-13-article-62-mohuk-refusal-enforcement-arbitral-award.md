Article 62 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) lists the grounds for refusal of enforcement (tenfiz) of a foreign arbitral award in Turkey and places the burden of proof for some of them on the party resisting enforcement. The Turkish court does not ask whether the award is right; it only checks whether one of the listed obstacles exists. Recognition of an award is governed by the same rules (Article 63 MÖHUK). Whether you want to enforce an award in Turkey or resist one, the answer usually turns on this provision.

## Article 62 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Grounds for refusal**
>
> Article 62 – (1) The court shall reject the request for enforcement of the foreign arbitral award if:
>
> a) no arbitration agreement was concluded and no arbitration clause was included in the main contract,
>
> b) the arbitral award is contrary to morality or public order,
>
> c) the dispute that is the subject of the award cannot be resolved by arbitration under Turkish law,
>
> ç) one of the parties was not duly represented before the arbitrators and did not later expressly approve the acts performed,
>
> d) the party against whom enforcement is sought was not duly notified of the appointment of the arbitrators or was deprived of the opportunity to present claims and defences,
>
> e) the arbitration agreement or clause is invalid under the law to which the parties subjected it or, failing any such agreement, under the law of the country where the award was made,
>
> f) the appointment of the arbitrators or the procedure they applied is contrary to the agreement of the parties or, failing such agreement, to the law of the country where the award was made,
>
> g) the award concerns a matter not contained in the arbitration agreement or clause, or exceeds the limits of the agreement or clause, as regards that part,
>
> h) the award has not become final or has not acquired enforceability or binding force under the law to which it is subject or of the country where it was made, or under the procedure to which it is subject, or has been set aside by the competent authority of the place where it was made.
>
> (2) The burden of proof for the matters listed in subparagraphs (ç), (d), (e), (f), (g) and (h) of the first paragraph lies with the party against whom enforcement is sought.

> (2) Birinci fıkranın (ç), (d), (e), (f), (g) ve (h) bentlerinde yazılı hususların ispat yükü, hakkında tenfiz istenen tarafa aittir.

The provision has not changed since the law entered into force on 12.12.2007. As of November 2026, the amendment list of the current text in UYAP Mevzuat shows only the change to Article 27 by Law No. 7550.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the reasoning, Article 62 partly corresponds to Article 45 of the former Law No. 2675. “Binding force” was added to subparagraph (h) to match the standard in Article 60. To remove uncertainty in practice about which law governs whether the award is final, enforceable or binding, the “procedure to which the award is subject” is also mentioned (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 61 of the bill).

## Explanation

### No review of the merits

Enforcement proceedings are not an appeal against the award. According to the Grand Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu, HGK), the enforcement court may not review a foreign award on the merits or check whether it is legally correct; otherwise it would act as an appellate court (HGK, E. 2022/660, K. 2023/1066). Objections such as “there was no debt” or “the amount is excessive” therefore belong in the arbitration.

### Who has to prove what?

Paragraph 2 places the burden of proving the procedural obstacles in (ç) to (h) on the party resisting enforcement. Defects of representation, notice or the right to be heard, invalidity of the arbitration agreement, irregular appointment of arbitrators, excess of authority, lack of finality and annulment must be proved by the debtor. An allegation that is not proved does not lead to refusal. In a case upheld by the Court of Cassation, the first-instance court granted enforcement because the respondent had offered no evidence on these subparagraphs (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5304, K. 2026/2520).

Subparagraphs (a), (b) and (c) are outside this burden-of-proof rule. Attaching the arbitration agreement is the applicant’s task anyway (Article 61(1)(a)). The court examines public policy and arbitrability of its own motion; in a case upheld by the Court of Cassation, the regional court of appeal (Bölge Adliye Mahkemesi) based this distinction on Article V of the New York Convention (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5304, K. 2026/2520). Turkish law decides which disputes are not arbitrable: disputes over rights in rem in immovable property and matters not subject to the free will of the parties (Article 408 of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK).

### Relationship with New York Convention Article V

If the award falls under the 1958 New York Convention, the court examines the grounds for refusal first under Article V of the Convention. Relying on Article 1(2) MÖHUK, which reserves international treaties, and Article 90 of the Constitution, the Grand Civil Chamber held that the Convention applies first, as a special rule, to awards within its scope, and that Articles 60 et seq. MÖHUK apply to other awards (HGK, E. 2022/660, K. 2023/1066). Which awards the Convention covers is explained in the post on [Article 60 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-60-mohuk-foreign-arbitral-award-turkey).

Comparing the Turkish text of the Convention published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete of 25.9.1991, No. 21002) with Article 62, the grounds largely overlap. The differences:

- Under Article V(1), recognition and enforcement may be refused only at the request of the party against whom the award is invoked and only if that party proves the listed grounds; for non-arbitrability and public policy (Article V(2)) recognition and enforcement “may” be refused. Article 62 MÖHUK says the court “shall reject”.
- Article V(1)(a) also covers incapacity of the parties to the arbitration agreement. MÖHUK has no separate subparagraph for this; subparagraph (ç) deals with lack of representation.
- Article V(1)(e) mentions, besides annulment, suspension of the award by a competent authority. Under Article VI, if annulment or suspension has been applied for, the court may, if it considers it proper, adjourn its decision on enforcement and, on the application of the party seeking enforcement, order the other party to give suitable security. MÖHUK has no equivalent of these two rules.

Whether the word “may” in the Convention gives the court discretion to enforce an award even though a ground for refusal exists is disputed. The law does not address this expressly, and the Court of Cassation decisions I have read contain no binding ruling on it. How a court will decide in a specific case is uncertain.

### Public policy is read narrowly

For court judgments the law requires that the judgment is not “manifestly” contrary to public policy (Article 54(1)(c)). More on this: [Article 54 MÖHUK – Conditions for Enforcement](/en/publications/legislation/article-54-mohuk-enforcement-conditions-turkey). Article 62(1)(b) does not contain the word “manifestly”. Even so, in a case concerning an award covered by the New York Convention, the Grand Civil Chamber applied a “manifest” violation test and stressed that what matters is not the content of the award but the consequences of enforcing it in Turkey. In the same decision it held that a conflict with a final Turkish judgment may violate public policy, whereas a criminal acquittal for lack of evidence does not have this effect (HGK, E. 2022/660, K. 2023/1066). In a case upheld by the Court of Cassation, the regional court of appeal found that compound interest awarded by the arbitral tribunal did not violate public policy (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/5437, K. 2024/7837).

## Practical points if you live abroad

**If enforcement is sought against you.** You have to prove that you were not informed of the arbitration or could not present your case; notice given under the rules at the seat of arbitration may be enough (see 11th Civil Chamber, K. 2026/2829 below). Keep the service records and, if you applied for annulment at the seat, the application and decisions ready with certified translations.

**If you are seeking enforcement.** Filing documents that show the award is binding and that the other side was notified from the outset prepares you for objections under (ç) to (h). The documents to attach are covered in the post on [Article 61 MÖHUK](/en/publications/legislation/article-61-mohuk-enforcement-arbitral-award-turkey). General information is available under [Private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay, Grand Civil Chamber (HGK), E. 2022/660, K. 2023/1066, 08.11.2023**

Enforcement of two awards of an ICC tribunal was sought. The Chamber had upheld the refusal for the award on the amount of damages, because a report the tribunal largely relied on had been withheld from the other side (Convention Article V(1)(b) and V(2)(b); Article 62(1)(b) and (d) MÖHUK). As to the award on liability, the Grand Civil Chamber held that a criminal acquittal for lack of evidence contains no definitive finding on the unlawfulness of the facts, so recognition and enforcement of the award could not be regarded as contrary to public policy; it reversed the lower court’s decision by majority.

**Yargıtay, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5304, K. 2026/2520, 30.04.2026**

Against an award made in the Czech Republic, the respondent argued that the contract had not been signed by the company’s former authorised representative. The first-instance court granted enforcement because no evidence had been offered on subparagraphs (ç) to (h); the regional court of appeal dismissed the appeal, finding that the objections went to the merits and that a review of the merits is prohibited. The Chamber upheld the decision.

**Yargıtay, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5706, K. 2026/2829, 11.05.2026**

In a case concerning an award made in Bulgaria, the respondents claimed they had not been properly notified of the arbitration. The regional court of appeal held that notice under the rules of the seat of arbitration was necessary and sufficient, found that one respondent had submitted a defence by email and the other had been summoned under those rules, and dismissed the appeal. The Chamber upheld the decision.

**Yargıtay, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/271, K. 2025/6277, 16.10.2025**

Enforcement of certain operative paragraphs of an ICC award made in Switzerland was sought. The regional court of appeal held that the respondent bears the burden of proving that the award was set aside or suspended, noted that the Swiss Federal Supreme Court had rejected a request for a stay concerning the paragraph in question, and dismissed the appeal. The Chamber upheld the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the grounds for refusal of enforcement of an arbitral award in Turkey?

The nine grounds in Article 62(1) MÖHUK; if the award falls under the New York Convention, those in Article V of the Convention.

### Can I argue in enforcement proceedings that the award is wrong?

No. The court does not review the award on the merits. You can rely on the procedural obstacles in Article 62 and on public policy.

### Who bears the burden of proof?

For (ç) to (h), the party resisting enforcement (Article 62(2)). Public policy and arbitrability are examined by the court of its own motion.

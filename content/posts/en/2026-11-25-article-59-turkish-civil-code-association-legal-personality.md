An association in Türkiye acquires legal personality the moment it hands the founding notice (kuruluş bildirimi), its statute and the required documents to the chief local administrator of the place where it has its domicile. This is Article 59 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK), which leaves the list of documents to a regulation. It concerns founders and anyone acting for a newly formed association.

## Article 59 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **IV. Acquisition of legal personality**
>
> **1. Moment of acquisition**
>
> Article 59 – Associations acquire legal personality at the moment they hand the founding notice, the statute of the association and the required documents to the chief local administrator of the place where their domicile is located.
>
> The content of the founding notice and the documents required are set out in the regulation.

Original Turkish text:

> **IV. Tüzel kişiliğin kazanılması**
>
> **1. Kazanma anı**
>
> Madde 59- Dernekler, kuruluş bildirimini, dernek tüzüğünü ve gerekli belgeleri yerleşim yerinin bulunduğu yerin en büyük mülkî amirine verdikleri anda tüzel kişilik kazanırlar.
>
> Kuruluş bildiriminin içeriği ve gerekli belgelerin nelerden ibaret olduğu, yönetmelikte gösterilir.

As of November 2026, Article 59 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, legal personality arises when the founding notice, statute and documents are handed to the chief local administrator of the domicile. The reasoning calls this a “normative notification system” (normatif bildirim sistemi) that replaces the “free formation system” of the old Civil Code (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### Where to file

Article 59 refers to the “chief local administrator of the place where the domicile is located”. The Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu) makes this concrete: the domicile is where the association is managed, the seat (merkez) is the province or district of that place, and the competent official is the governor (vali) or district governor (kaymakam) of the seat (Article 2(b), (c), (d)).

### Required documents

The regulation referred to in Article 59(2) is the Associations Regulation (Dernekler Yönetmeliği). Under its Article 5, the founding notice signed by the founders is accompanied by:

- one copy of the statute, each page signed by the founders;
- where a founder is a legal person: its name, domicile, founding document and a copy of the decision of its organ naming the natural person who will represent it;
- where a founder is a foreign association or non-profit organisation: a written declaration signed by the founders that the Ministry of the Interior has given permission;
- where a founder is a foreign national: copies of documents showing a right to settle in Türkiye.

For children's associations, the consent of the founders' legal representatives is added.

### How the moment is recorded

The authority forwards the founding notice to its civil society relations unit with the date and time noted, gives the applicant a receipt (Alındı Belgesi, Annex 1) and enters the association in the register (Regulation, Article 5). Legal personality starts with this filing; there is no approval to wait for. A district office sends the notice and statute to the provincial directorate within seven days.

### After legal personality

From that moment the association has legal capacity (Article 48 TMK). It gains capacity to act once it has the organs required by law and its founding document (Article 49 TMK). Until the first general meeting there is the provisional board named in the statute (Article 58(2) TMK), and the authority also sends the result of its review to the chair of that board (Regulation, Article 6). More in the post on [Article 49 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-49-tmk-capacity-to-act-legal-persons).

The authority then reviews the documents and statute within sixty days (Article 60 TMK); the steps are described in the post on [Article 57 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-57-turkish-civil-code-right-to-form-association). If a defect is not fixed, the association does not simply cease to exist: the authority informs the public prosecutor so that an action for dissolution can be brought, and the prosecutor may also ask the court to suspend the association's activities (Article 60(2) TMK).

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

The notice goes to the authority of the seat in Türkiye. The Regulation requires the founders to sign it but does not say who must hand the documents in. Additional Article 2 of Law No. 5253 allows procedures concerning associations to be carried out electronically, while Article 5 of the Regulation describes the founding notice in terms of filing and a receipt. Whether the founding notice can be filed electronically in practice is something to ask the provincial directorate for civil society relations (il sivil toplumla ilişkiler müdürlüğü) at the future seat.

An association founded in Germany or the Netherlands acquires legal personality under that country's law, not under Article 59 TMK; for that, consult a lawyer authorised to practise there.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does an association in Türkiye acquire legal personality?

When the founding notice, statute and documents are handed to the governor or district governor of the seat (Article 59(1) TMK; Article 2(d) of Law No. 5253).

### Do you have to wait for approval?

No. Legal personality arises on filing; the sixty-day review follows (Articles 59 and 60 TMK).

### Which documents are needed to found an association?

The founding notice signed by the founders and the statute signed on every page by the founders; where founders include legal persons, foreign organisations or foreign nationals, the extra documents listed in Article 5 of the Associations Regulation.

From 1 January 2027, the limit for household connections under Turkey’s last resort supply tariff (son kaynak tedarik tarifesi) is 3,000 kWh a year. In 2026 it is 4,000 kWh. The Board of the Energy Market Regulatory Authority (EPDK Kurulu) took the decision on 30 October 2026, and it was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) of 31 October 2026.

“Elektrik” is currently one of the most searched terms in Turkey, and the headlines are about this limit. This article uses the official texts to explain what the limit means, from which date a household moves to the high-consumption tariff, and the 14-day right to withdraw from a contract with a supplier. The last section is for readers in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe who manage the electricity connection of their home in Turkey from abroad.

## Turkey electricity tariff 2027: what the decision says

EPDK Board Decision No. 15005 of 30.10.2026 (Official Gazette of 31.10.2026, No. 33416) sets the consumption amount under Article 5(3) of the Communiqué on the Last Resort Supply Tariff (Son Kaynak Tedarik Tarifesinin Düzenlenmesi Hakkında Tebliğ, “the Communiqué”) for 2027 as follows:

- 3,000 kWh a year for the residential group (mesken). The decision includes detached houses, flats in apartment buildings, housing cooperatives and housing estates, and their separately metered shared facilities such as central heating, lifts, water pumps, stairwell lighting and the caretaker’s flat.
- 150 million kWh a year for agriculture and certain facilities named in the decision (such as AFAD temporary shelters and village drinking water systems).
- 15,000 kWh a year for all other groups.

Under Article 2 of the decision, the coefficient (KBK) for households stays at 1.05 from 1.1.2027. The decision enters into force on 1.1.2027 (Article 3).

For comparison: Board Decision No. 13912 of 30.10.2025 (Official Gazette of 31.10.2025, No. 33063) set the 2026 household limit at 4,000 kWh a year, also with a KBK of 1.05.

## What is the last resort supply tariff and who pays it?

The last resort tariff applies to consumers who qualify as “eligible consumers” (serbest tüketici), and could therefore choose their supplier, but do not buy electricity under a contract with a supplier (ikili anlaşma, bilateral agreement) (Electricity Market Law No. 6446, Articles 3, 10(5) and 17(6)(f); Communiqué Articles 1 and 5(1)). The regional incumbent supplier (görevli tedarik şirketi) must supply these customers (Law No. 6446, Article 10(5)).

An eligible consumer is someone who uses more than the amount set by the Board and may therefore choose a supplier (Law No. 6446, Article 3(1)(cc)). For 2026 that threshold is 500 kWh (Board Decision No. 14039 of 18.12.2025, Official Gazette of 23.12.2025, No. 33116). A household using 3,000 kWh a year is therefore already an eligible consumer. As of November 2026 I could not find a decision in the Official Gazette setting the eligibility threshold for 2027.

The Communiqué splits last resort customers into two groups (Article 5(2)):

- **Low-consumption customers** below the limit pay the approved retail tariff that also applies to non-eligible consumers (Communiqué Article 7).
- **High-consumption customers** at or above the limit pay a tariff calculated separately for each billing period: the market clearing price (PTF) plus the YEKDEM unit charge, multiplied by the coefficient KBK (Communiqué Article 6(5)). It may not be lower than the approved single-rate retail tariff for non-eligible consumers (Communiqué Article 6(3)).

So a household above the limit moves to a market-linked tariff. I have not seen an official price per kWh for 2027 and give no figure here.

## When does the high-consumption tariff start?

Article 8(3) of the Communiqué governs this. For a customer entering the high-consumption group for the first time, the tariff applies from the first day of the third calendar month following the month in which the limit was reached “in the previous calendar year or the current year”. If a household reaches 3,000 kWh in September 2027, for example, the high-consumption tariff applies from 1 December 2027.

The distribution company (dağıtım şirketi) must inform the customer in writing or on a durable medium (such as email or text message) within 15 days of establishing that the limit has been reached. A customer who was in the group before and enters it again pays the higher tariff immediately; notice is again given within 15 days.

What counts is the energy the customer uses at one point of consumption under a contract in their own name (Communiqué Article 8(5)). If you have two homes in Turkey, each is assessed separately; their consumption is not added up.

One question remains open. The Communiqué also counts the previous calendar year. Neither Decision No. 15005 nor the Communiqué says expressly from when a household that used more than 3,000 kWh but less than 4,000 kWh in 2026 moves to the high-consumption tariff in 2027. I cannot give a definite answer. If this is your situation, watch for the distribution company’s notice under Article 8(3) and go by the date it gives.

## Supplier contracts and the 14-day withdrawal right

The way out of the high-consumption tariff is a contract with a supplier. The low-consumption tariff also runs only until such a contract is made (Communiqué Article 8(2)). Eligible consumers can sign directly with a supplier or through the comparison platform run by the market operator EPİAŞ (Regulation on Consumer Services in the Electricity Market, Elektrik Piyasası Tüketici Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği, “the Regulation”, Article 6(3)). If the contract ends for any reason, the regional incumbent supplier takes over again under last resort supply (Regulation Articles 6(4) and 8).

For contracts with eligible consumers using less than 100,000 kWh a year, the Regulation lays down these rules:

- **Withdrawal:** You may withdraw within **14 days** of the contract being made or renewed, without giving reasons and without a withdrawal fee, by telephone, email or in writing (Article 13). Article 13 makes no distinction between contracts made in writing and at a distance; both are permitted (Article 12(2)).
- **Silence is not acceptance:** Not answering an offer or a proposed change does not count as accepting it (Article 10(4)).
- **Term:** A fixed-term contract may run for no more than three years (Article 10(2)) and ends automatically. Automatic renewal clauses are not allowed, and the supplier must notify you at least 60 days before the end (Article 14).
- **Changes:** No changes to your detriment during a fixed term (Article 15(1)). A proposed change must reach you at least 15 days before it takes effect and only applies if you accept it (Article 15(2) and (3)).
- **Termination:** You can end an open-ended contract at any time without a fee or penalty (Article 16(3)). A fixed-term contract can be ended in the same way on the grounds listed in Article 16(1), including moving to another address or vacating the property, or the supplier failing to give required information.
- **Penalty clause:** It may only be agreed for termination without good cause (Article 20(1)). The 10 percent cap in Article 20(2) expressly excludes consumers covered by Consumer Protection Law No. 6502. I have not checked for this article which cap applies to a household using its home privately. Read the withdrawal and termination fee clause before signing and, if in doubt, ask the supplier in writing.

Article 48(4) of Law No. 6502 also gives a general 14-day withdrawal right for distance contracts. For electricity supply contracts, though, Article 13 of the Regulation is a specific and express rule.

The “14 days” concern withdrawal from a contract you have made or renewed. The last resort tariff is not a contract. It applies automatically once the limit is exceeded, so there is nothing to “cancel”. You leave it either by staying below the limit or by signing with a supplier.

## A home in Turkey while living in Europe

A holiday home used only a few weeks a year may well have low consumption. What matters, though, is the meter reading, not the number of weeks you spend there. Electric heating, long hours of air conditioning or a relative living there all year can push a home over the limit. The annual consumption shown on your bills tells you where you stand against the new threshold.

If the home is let, the question is whose name the electricity contract is in. Article 8(5) of the Communiqué looks at consumption under the customer’s own contract. If the connection is still in your name and a tenant lives there, the tenant’s consumption builds up on your connection. If the limit is exceeded, the high-consumption tariff applies to that connection, and it is the contract holder who signs with a supplier. How costs are shared between you and the tenant depends on the tenancy agreement. On the legal cap for rent increases, see [rent increase in Turkey](/en/publications/rent-increase-turkey-legal-limit).

Applying for a supply contract with the incumbent supplier and ending one can both be done through the company’s official channels or through the e-Devlet portal (Regulation Articles 21(1) and 24(2)). You do not need to travel to Turkey for your tenant to open a connection in their own name or for you to close yours.

The distribution company’s 15-day notice comes in writing or on a durable medium. A letter to your Turkish address or a text to a number you no longer use may never reach you, so it helps to keep your phone number and email up to date with the supplier.

For other obligations as a property owner in Turkey, see [property tax in Turkey](/en/publications/property-tax-turkey-payment-november); for consumer contracts in general, see [Consumer law](/en/practice-areas/consumer-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the electricity limit in Turkey for 2027?

3,000 kWh a year for households, from 1 January 2027 (EPDK Board Decision No. 15005 of 30.10.2026, Official Gazette of 31.10.2026). In 2026 the limit is 4,000 kWh.

### Does the higher tariff start as soon as I exceed the limit?

Not the first time. It applies from the first day of the third calendar month after the month in which you reached the limit, and the distribution company must notify you within 15 days (Communiqué Article 8(3)). If you were a high-consumption customer before, it applies immediately.

### How do I get off the last resort tariff in Turkey?

By signing a contract with a supplier, either directly or through EPİAŞ’s comparison platform (Regulation Article 6(3)). You can withdraw free of charge within 14 days of the contract being made or renewed (Regulation Article 13).

### Is my holiday home’s consumption added to my other home?

No. The Communiqué looks at consumption at one point of consumption under the contract in your name (Article 8(5)). Each point of consumption is assessed separately.

Article 67 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medenî Kanunu, TMK) governs expulsion from an association (dernekten çıkarılma), where membership ends by decision of the association. Whether an expelled member can challenge the decision depends on whether the statute lists grounds for expulsion. The article concerns association boards and members who have been, or may be, expelled.

## Article 67 TMK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **II. Termination**
>
> **3. By expulsion**
>
> Article 67 – The statute may set out the grounds for expelling members.
>
> Where the statute sets out the grounds for expulsion, the expulsion decision may not be challenged on the claim that those grounds cannot be regarded as justified.
>
> Where the statute does not regulate expulsion, a member may be expelled only for just cause. This expulsion decision may be challenged on the ground that there was no just cause.

Original Turkish text:

> **II. Sona ermesi**
>
> **3. Çıkarılma ile**
>
> Madde 67- Tüzükte üyelerin çıkarılma sebepleri gösterilebilir.
>
> Tüzükte çıkarma sebepleri gösterilmişse, çıkarma kararına bu sebeplerin haklı sayılamayacağı iddiasıyla itiraz edilemez.
>
> Tüzükte çıkarma düzenlenmemişse üye, ancak haklı sebeple çıkarılabilir. Bu çıkarma kararına, haklı sebep bulunmadığı ileri sürülerek itiraz edilebilir.

As of November 2026, Article 67 on mevzuat.gov.tr is in its original wording; the amendment table has no entry for it (latest law: No. 7589, in force since 31 October 2026).

## Legislative reasoning

The reasoning notes that associations commonly list the grounds for expulsion in their statute. Where the statute says nothing, a member can be expelled only for just cause, and paragraph 3 gives the expelled person a right to challenge the decision (Source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, summarised).

## Explanation

### With and without grounds in the statute

The Code leaves the grounds to the statute but does not require them: the statute "may" set them out (Article 67(1)). The Associations Law No. 5253 (Dernekler Kanunu), Article 4(1)(c), requires the statute to state the conditions and form of joining and of leaving the association; it does not mention expulsion.

If the statute lists grounds, the member cannot argue that those grounds are unjustified (Article 67(2)); by joining, the member accepted the statute. If the statute is silent, the association may expel only for just cause (haklı sebep), and the member may argue that there was none (Article 67(3)). The Code does not define just cause. The members' duties to respect the association's rules, to be loyal to it and to act in line with its purpose (Article 71) offer a yardstick.

### Who decides

The general assembly (genel kurul) has the final say on expulsion (Article 80(1)). The statute may give the first decision to the board. A decision of another organ cannot be challenged in court until the association's internal review has been used up (Article 83(2)). Decisions of the general assembly that breach the law or the statute can be challenged within one month, and in any case within three months of the decision (Article 83(1)).

### What the law leaves open

The Code does not say expressly to whom, within what time and by what procedure the challenge (itiraz) in Article 67 is to be made. Neither the TMK nor the Associations Law contains a special time limit, subject-matter rule or venue rule for a lawsuit against an expulsion.

> This question is disputed. What follows are views put forward in legal writing; there is no express provision in the law and no binding court decision on the point. Which view a court will adopt in a given case is uncertain.

The main open questions are: whether the one-month limit in Article 83(1) also applies to a challenge to an expulsion; whether, in the Article 67(2) situation, a court may check that the ground in the statute actually occurred and that the decision was taken properly; and whether the simplified procedure under Article 18 of the Associations Law applies to such cases. In the author's assessment, with no special rule, the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) has jurisdiction as the general rule (Article 2, Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100, HMK). The general venue is the domicile of the defendant association, which, unless the statute says otherwise, is where it is run (Article 6 HMK, Article 51 TMK). Because the time limit is unclear, the safer course is not to let the one-month period in Article 83(1) pass.

### Consequences of expulsion

An expelled member has no claim to the association's assets (Article 68(3)) and owes the dues for the period of membership (Article 70(1)). The association reports the end of the membership to the associations authority at its seat within 45 days (Article 23, Associations Law).

## What Turkish citizens living abroad should know

If you learn that a hometown or village association in Türkiye has expelled you, start with its statute: what grounds and what procedure it provides decides what you can object to. The Code does not say how the decision must be notified to the member; the statute may. For leaving voluntarily, see the post on [Article 63 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-63-turkish-civil-code-association-membership); for what a statute contains, see [Article 58 TMK](/en/publications/legislation/article-58-turkish-civil-code-association-statute).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I challenge my expulsion from an association in Türkiye?

If the statute does not regulate expulsion, yes, on the ground that there was no just cause (Article 67(3) TMK). If the statute lists grounds, the argument that they are unjustified is not heard (Article 67(2)).

### What is the time limit for suing against an expulsion?

The Code sets no express limit. Challenges to general assembly decisions are subject to one month and three months (Article 83(1)); whether that applies here is disputed.

### Do I get my dues back after being expelled?

The Code gives no such right. An expelled member has no claim to the association's assets (Article 68(3)) and owes the dues for the time of membership (Article 70(1)).

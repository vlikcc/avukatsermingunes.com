If a creditor starts enforcement against you in Turkey without a court judgment (ilamsız takip), you have seven days from service of the payment order (ödeme emri) to object (itiraz), and the objection goes to the enforcement office (icra dairesi) (Article 62(1) of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, İİK). Living in Germany or the Netherlands does not extend this period. Turkish law has no separate objection period for debtors abroad; living abroad only affects how the order is served and which day counts as the date of service.

Different periods apply to cheques and bills of exchange (kambiyo senedi). I checked the provisions below in November 2026 against the current texts in UYAP Mevzuat, the Ministry of Justice database. The İİK text there was recorded on 31 July 2026. The last amending law in its list is Law No. 7589, which changed only Article 114; the provisions on payment orders and objections (Articles 58–72 and 168–172) have not changed in the past two years.

## Objection deadline for a payment order

The period depends on the type of enforcement:

- **Enforcement without judgment (general attachment procedure):** seven days to pay or object. The objection is made to the enforcement office in writing or orally (İİK Article 60, item 2; Article 62(1)).
- **Special attachment procedure for cheques and bills of exchange:** ten days to pay; five days to complain that the document is not a negotiable instrument, to deny the signature, or to dispute the debt. These are filed in writing with the **enforcement court** (icra mahkemesi), not the enforcement office (İİK Article 168(1), items 2–5; Articles 169 and 170). They do not, by themselves, stop enforcement steps other than the sale.
- **Bankruptcy route based on cheques and bills:** five days, in writing to the enforcement office (İİK Article 172).
- **Enforcement of a judgment (ilamlı takip):** you receive an enforcement order (icra emri) rather than a payment order, and objections go to the enforcement court within seven days (İİK Articles 32 and 33).

The day of service is not counted. If the last day is an official holiday, the period ends on the next working day at the close of office hours (İİK Article 19). An objection to the enforcement office's territorial jurisdiction must be raised together with the objection on the merits (İİK Article 50(2)).

### Where does the “15 days” come from?

Turkish searches often mention a 15-day period. It comes from Law No. 6183 on the Collection of Public Receivables, not from the İİK. Against a payment order from a public creditor such as a tax office, the debtor may claim within 15 days of service that there is no debt, that it was partly paid or that it is time-barred (Law No. 6183, Article 58(1)). Law No. 7061 extended this period from 7 to 15 days with effect from 1 January 2018. The text still refers to the former tax objection commissions; Law No. 2576 ended their role, and disputes under Law No. 6183 concerning taxes and similar charges go to the tax courts (vergi mahkemesi) (Law No. 2576, Articles 6(1)(b) and 15). For a payment order from an enforcement office, the period is not 15 days.

## How is a payment order served abroad?

Service abroad is in principle made through the competent authority of the country concerned. The documents are sent via the relevant ministry and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to the Turkish embassy or consulate (Notification Law No. 7201, Article 25; Regulation on the Implementation of the Notification Law, Article 38). They are drawn up in line with the applicable treaties and translated into the language of the receiving country or the language set by treaty (Regulation, Articles 39 and 40).

Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are parties to the 1965 Hague Service Convention (in force for Turkey since 28 April 1972). Under Germany's declarations, formal service is permitted only if the document is in German or translated into German. Germany objects to service by post (Article 10 of the Convention) and allows service through diplomatic or consular agents (Article 8) only on nationals of the sending state. The Netherlands has made no declaration on Articles 8 and 10.

### Service through the consulate and the 30-day notice

If you are a Turkish citizen, service may also be made through the Turkish embassy or consulate in your country (Notification Law, Article 25/a). You first receive a notice stating the subject, the issuing authority and that service will be deemed made if you do not come forward within 30 days. Once delivery of this notice is documented, if you do not apply to the consulate within 30 days, service is deemed made at the end of the thirtieth day. If you go to the consulate and refuse the documents, the date of the record of refusal is the date of service (Notification Law, Article 25/a; Regulation, Article 43(3)–(4)).

The seven-day objection period runs from that date. If you were previously served at a foreign address and did not report a new one, service is deemed made 30 days after documented delivery of the notice to the old address (Notification Law, Article 35, last paragraph).

### Service at your address in Turkey

Being abroad does not prevent service at your address in Turkey. The 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) held that a debtor's temporary stay abroad, with no known foreign address, does not require service under Article 25 of the Notification Law and Article 43 of the Regulation. It found service at the known address, completed under Article 21(2) of the Notification Law, to be valid (**E. 2024/2854, K. 2024/7878, 30.09.2024**).

If the address is unknown, service is made by public announcement (Notification Law, Article 28). It is deemed made seven days after the last announcement, and the authority may set a longer period of up to 15 days (Article 31). Those 15 days concern the date of service, not the objection period.

## Where and how to object

In enforcement without judgment, you object in writing or orally to the enforcement office handling the case. You may also file with any other enforcement office, which forwards the objection after collecting the cost (İİK Article 62(1)). If you are in Turkey, the enforcement office where you are staying will do.

The objection comes with some requirements:

- **Give an address in Turkey.** The debtor or their representative must give a domestic address with the objection. If you do not and no new address can be found, service at the address in the enforcement request counts as service on you (İİK Article 62).
- If you object to only part of the debt, state the part and amount clearly; otherwise you are treated as not having objected (İİK Article 62).
- If the signature on the document is not yours, say so separately and expressly (İİK Article 62).
- State all your grounds at the outset. You cannot change or add to them in the hearing on the creditor's request to lift the objection (İİK Article 63).

If you cannot travel to Turkey, a lawyer in Turkey can file the objection under a power of attorney from you.

### e-Devlet, UYAP and electronic service

According to a Ministry of Justice guide, you can view your enforcement file on e-Devlet: open the “UYAP Vatandaş Portal” service, go to “Sorgulama İşlemleri” and “Dosya Sorgulama”, and choose “İcra” as the type of proceedings. The file shows the account of the claim, the parties and the documents. I could not confirm from an official source whether an objection can be filed through this portal; check with the enforcement office.

Individuals receive an electronic service address on request, after which service on them must be electronic (Notification Law, Article 7/a). The application can be made through e-Devlet (Electronic Notification Regulation, Article 7(3)). Electronic service is deemed made at the end of the fifth day after the document reaches your electronic address (Article 7/a). If you have such an address, the payment order may arrive there, and the period runs even if you never open it.

## Missed the deadline: late objection and defective service

If an obstacle that was not your fault prevented you from objecting in time, you can still object until the sale of the attached assets is completed. You must file with the enforcement court within three days after the obstacle ends, with evidence of it and your grounds (late objection, gecikmiş itiraz; İİK Article 65).

The 12th Civil Chamber keeps this route separate from a complaint about defective service. A late objection requires valid service; if service was defective, the date of service is fixed under Article 32 of the Notification Law. Where the payment order was handed to the debtor's spouse living in the same home while the debtor was out, service was valid under Article 16, and the claim of having been abroad had to be examined as a late objection (**E. 2023/2273, K. 2023/8969, 19.12.2023**). For a debtor who had left Turkey before service and not returned, the Chamber held that being abroad did not make service that was formally valid under Article 21(1) defective (**E. 2024/137, K. 2024/5196, 22.05.2024**).

If service really was defective, you can complain to the enforcement court within seven days of learning of it (İİK Article 16(1)). If you learned of the document despite defective service, service is valid and the date you state counts as the date of service (Notification Law, Article 32).

## What happens if you do not object?

A timely objection stops the enforcement. Without one, the enforcement becomes final (kesinleşme), and at the creditor's request the office continues for the whole claim (İİK Article 66(1)). Once the period in the payment order has expired, the creditor can request attachment without waiting for your declaration of assets, and can search and attach your assets through the UYAP system (İİK Article 78(1)). The payment order also warns that if you neither object nor pay you must declare your assets (mal beyanı), and states the sanctions (İİK Article 60, item 4).

If you object, the creditor can bring an action in the civil courts within one year of being served with your objection to have it set aside (itirazın iptali, İİK Article 67), or, if it holds a document of the kind described in İİK Article 68, apply to the enforcement court within six months to remove the objection. While the enforcement is pending, you can also bring a negative declaratory action (menfi tespit davası) to establish that you owe nothing (İİK Article 72).

A Turkish address matters at later stages too. A debtor seeking to annul an auction must give an address in Turkey (İİK Article 134(2)). The 12th Civil Chamber held that the request of a debtor who gave only a foreign address in the complaint had to be dismissed for lack of a procedural requirement (**E. 2025/5706, K. 2025/6703, 23.10.2025**).

## Your assets in Germany or the Netherlands

Enforcement that has become final in Turkey is not automatically enforced by German or Dutch authorities. In Germany, a foreign court judgment can be enforced only once a German enforcement judgment (Vollstreckungsurteil) has declared it enforceable (sections 722 and 723 ZPO). Whether Turkish enforcement that became final without a court judgment can be enforced in Germany or the Netherlands is a question of that country's law, and you should consult a lawyer admitted there. For the Turkish side, see [enforcement and insolvency](/en/practice-areas/enforcement-insolvency) and [Türkiye–Germany](/en/turkiye-germany).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I have to object to a Turkish payment order?

Seven days from service in enforcement without judgment (İİK Article 62(1)) and five days in the special procedure for cheques and bills (İİK Article 168(1)). The 15-day period applies to payment orders from public creditors under Law No. 6183 (Article 58).

### Where do I file the objection?

With the enforcement office; an objection filed with another enforcement office is forwarded (İİK Article 62(1)). For cheques and bills, objections to the debt or signature go to the enforcement court (İİK Articles 168–170).

### Does living abroad extend the deadline?

No, there is no separate period. With service through the consulate, the date of service can move to the end of the 30-day notice period (Notification Law, Article 25/a); the seven days run from then.

### What happens if I do not object to the payment order?

The enforcement becomes final and the creditor can have your assets attached (İİK Articles 66 and 78). If you were prevented without fault, a late objection under İİK Article 65 remains possible.

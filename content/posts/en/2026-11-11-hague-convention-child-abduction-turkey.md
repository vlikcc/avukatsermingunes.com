If one parent takes a child under 16 from Germany or the Netherlands to Turkey in breach of the other parent's rights of custody, or keeps the child there after a holiday, the parent left behind can ask for the child's return to the country of habitual residence under the 1980 Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction. In Turkey the return case (iade davası) is not brought by the parent. The public prosecutor's office (Cumhuriyet başsavcılığı) files it before the family court (aile mahkemesi) on behalf of the Central Authority, the Turkish Ministry of Justice (Turkish Law No. 5717, Articles 3, 6 and 7).

Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are all parties. According to the status table of the Hague Conference (HCCH), the Convention entered into force for Turkey on 1 August 2000, for Germany on 1 December 1990 and for the Netherlands on 1 September 1990. In Turkey it is implemented by Law No. 5717 on the Legal Aspects and Scope of International Child Abduction (Uluslararası Çocuk Kaçırmanın Hukuki Yön ve Kapsamına Dair Kanun). According to the current text on UYAP Mevzuat (November 2026), the law was last amended by Law No. 7343, in force since 30 November 2021.

## When does the Hague Convention apply?

The Turkish text of the Convention was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) on 15 February 2000, No. 23965. Removal or retention of a child is wrongful where two conditions are met (Article 3 of the Convention):

- it breaches rights of custody held by a person under the law of the State where the child was habitually resident (mutat mesken) immediately before the removal or retention;
- those rights were actually being exercised at the time, or would have been but for the removal or retention.

Rights of custody include in particular the right to determine the child's place of residence (Article 5). Who holds that right is decided not by Turkish law but by the law of the State of the child's habitual residence; for a child living in Germany, German law. Turkish law also provides that married parents exercise parental custody (velayet) jointly while the marriage lasts (Article 336(1) of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK).

The Convention applies to children under 16 (Article 4; Law No. 5717, Article 3(1)(c)). The age limit also applies while the case is pending. The 2nd Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) set aside a judgment because the child, whose return to Germany had been requested, turned 16 during the appeal and so fell outside the Convention; the court was to rule that no decision on the merits was needed (Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5180, K. 2024/6199, 19 September 2024).

## Where to apply and who brings the case in Turkey

A parent claiming that their rights of custody have been breached may apply to the Central Authority of the child's habitual residence, to the Turkish Central Authority or to the Central Authority of any other Contracting State (Law No. 5717, Article 7(1); Convention, Article 8). In Turkey the Central Authority is the Ministry of Justice (Law No. 5717, Article 3(1)(a)); in Germany it is the Federal Office of Justice, Bundesamt für Justiz (§ 3 IntFamRVG). In the Netherlands the role is carried out by the Centrale autoriteit Internationale Kinderaangelegenheden (Ca IKA).

The application sets out the identity of the parties and the child, the grounds for the claim and all available information on where the child is; any custody decision is attached (Article 8). Documents sent by a Central Authority need no authentication (Law No. 5717, Article 7(3)).

Through the local public prosecutor, the Turkish Central Authority has the child located and first tries to obtain a voluntary return or an amicable solution. If that fails, a case is filed (Law No. 5717, Articles 4 and 5). **The return case is filed by the local public prosecutor's office on behalf of the Central Authority** (Article 7(2)). The Court of Cassation applies this rule. It upheld the dismissal, for lack of standing, of a return case that a parent had brought directly concerning a child taken from Germany to Turkey (Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/6386, K. 2024/6508, 26 September 2024). On the same day it set aside a judgment in which the first-instance court had ruled on the merits of a case brought directly by a parent (Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/6919, K. 2024/6516, 26 September 2024).

The parent is not left out of the proceedings. According to a decision of the 2nd Civil Chamber quoted in a judgment of the Court of Cassation's Assembly of Civil Chambers (Hukuk Genel Kurulu) of 15 May 2024, mother and father are parties to the case brought by the Central Authority, and the statement of claim and hearing date must be served on them as well (Yargıtay HGK, E. 2024/255, K. 2024/245; Law No. 5717, Article 9(1)).

## Competent court and procedure in Turkey

The family court has subject-matter jurisdiction; where there is none, Article 2(2) of Law No. 4787 applies. The competent court is the court of the place where the child is living, or has been placed in care, when the application is made (Law No. 5717, Article 6).

The main procedural rules:

- Before deciding on the merits, the court encourages a settlement, with help from experts (Article 8).
- The case is heard under the simplified procedure, with priority and urgency (Article 9(2)), including during the judicial recess (Article 16).
- Until the end of the case the court may order a temporary ban on the child leaving the country, stop passport applications or have passports held (Article 24).
- Temporary contact between the applicant and the child may be ordered while the case is pending (Article 11(2)).
- No court fees are charged. The state does not cover the cost of a lawyer for the applicant or the costs of returning the child (Article 27); Turkey ratified the Convention with a reservation to this effect under Article 26(3). The applicant may receive legal aid (adli yardım) (Article 28).

If the court has not decided within six weeks, the applicant or the Central Authority may ask for a statement of the reasons for the delay (Convention, Article 11).

## Time limits: the one-year rule

Article 12 of the Convention distinguishes two situations:

- If **less than one year** has passed between the wrongful removal or retention and the start of proceedings before the court or authority of the State where the child is, that authority orders the return of the child forthwith.
- If proceedings start **after one year**, return is still ordered unless it is shown that the child is now settled in its new environment.

One year is therefore not a cut-off for return. Once it has passed, however, the other side can argue that the child has settled, so a delay in applying affects the prospects of return.

## When can return be refused?

Return is the rule. The court is not bound to order it only in these cases (Article 13), and the first two must be established by the party opposing return:

- The parent left behind was not actually exercising custody rights at the time, or consented to or later acquiesced in the removal or retention (Article 13(1)(a)).
- There is a grave risk that return would expose the child to physical or psychological harm or otherwise place the child in an intolerable situation (Article 13(1)(b)).
- The child objects to being returned and has reached an age and degree of maturity at which its views should be taken into account (Article 13(2)).

In addition, return may be refused where the child has settled after more than a year (Article 12(2)) or where return would not be permitted by the fundamental principles of the requested State on human rights (Article 20). A custody decision given after the return application is not a ground for refusal (Law No. 5717, Article 13), and a custody decision in the requested State is not in itself a reason to refuse return (Convention, Article 17).

On "grave risk", the outcome usually depends on the expert reports. The chamber decision quoted in the Assembly's judgment of 15 May 2024 states that the child's adjustment to life with the mother after being brought from Germany, and the child's age, are not grounds for refusal under the Convention, and that an expert report based on assumptions is not enough. In another case the chamber upheld a refusal to return a four-and-a-half-year-old child brought from the Netherlands, who had been cared for by the mother since birth, because social inquiry reports found that return would expose the child to psychological harm (Yargıtay 2nd Civil Chamber, E. 2024/4977, K. 2024/5357, 4 July 2024). In my view these two decisions do not yield a general rule; what matters is how concretely the expert report in the individual case shows the risk of return.

## Return case and custody case

A return decision is not a decision on the merits of custody (Convention, Article 19). If the Turkish court orders return, it does not rule on custody in the same judgment; if it refuses return, it may rule on custody (Law No. 5717, Article 12). A custody case filed while the return case is pending is stayed; if the two have been joined, they are separated and the return case is decided first (Articles 14 and 15). How Turkish courts decide custody is explained in [child custody in a Turkish divorce](/en/publications/child-custody-divorce-turkey).

## Enforcement and the criminal law side

The return order is enforced once it is final (Law No. 5717, Article 17(1)). The Central Authority, the applicant and the respondent may appeal (Article 17(2)). Enforcement is carried out under Part Four of the Child Protection Law No. 5395 by the Directorate of Judicial Support and Victim Services (adli destek ve mağdur hizmetleri müdürlüğü), without prior service of a handover order (Law No. 5717, Article 18(1)). If an expert finds that handing over the child would put the child's development in serious danger, the handover is postponed (Article 22). Anyone who hides the child during enforcement or abducts the child again is subject to Article 41/F of Law No. 5395 (Law No. 5717, Article 25); its first paragraph provides for disciplinary detention of up to three months, on a complaint made within one month, in cases concerning the handover of a child.

The return case is a civil case and runs independently of any criminal proceedings. Article 234 of the Turkish Criminal Code (TCK) punishes a parent who has been deprived of custody, or a blood relative up to the third degree, who without force or threat takes or keeps a child under 16 away from the parent, guardian or person caring for the child, with imprisonment of three months to one year; the penalty is doubled if force or threat is used or the child is under 12 (TCK Article 234(1)–(2)).

## If the child was taken from Turkey to Germany or the Netherlands

The same Convention applies in the other direction. If the child's habitual residence was in Turkey, the parent there may apply to the Turkish Ministry of Justice, which forwards the application to the Central Authority of the other State (Articles 8 and 9). In Germany such cases are, as a rule, heard by the family court at the seat of the Higher Regional Court (Oberlandesgericht) for that court's whole district (§§ 11, 12 IntFamRVG); in the Netherlands the Rechtbank Den Haag has exclusive jurisdiction. Those proceedings are governed by German or Dutch law, so please consult a lawyer admitted there or an organisation such as Centrum IKO. ## Frequently asked questions

### My spouse took our child to Turkey without my consent. What can I do?

You can file a return application under the Hague Convention with the Central Authority of the country where the child lived (in Germany the Bundesamt für Justiz, in the Netherlands the Ca IKA) or directly with the Turkish Ministry of Justice (Law No. 5717, Article 7(1)). In Turkey the case is brought by the public prosecutor's office.

### Can I bring the return case in Turkey myself?

No. Under Article 7(2) of Law No. 5717 the public prosecutor's office files the case on behalf of the Central Authority, and the Court of Cassation dismisses cases brought directly by a parent for lack of standing. As a parent you are, however, a party to the proceedings.

### Is there a deadline for a Hague Convention application?

There is no cut-off date. But if proceedings start more than one year after the abduction, return can be refused if the child has settled in the new environment (Article 12). The Convention stops applying when the child turns 16 (Article 4).

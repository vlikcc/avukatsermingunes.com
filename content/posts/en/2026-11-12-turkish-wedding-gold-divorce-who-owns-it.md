No Turkish statute says who owns the gold pinned on the couple at a Turkish wedding (ziynet eşyası); the rule comes from the case law of the 2nd Civil Chamber of the Turkish Court of Cassation (Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi). The chamber now looks first at any agreement between the spouses, then at a proven local custom, and otherwise treats each item as belonging to the person it was given to, with jewellery typical for women deemed a gift to the wife and jewellery typical for men a gift to the husband. A spouse who proves that the other took the gold can sue for its return in kind or, if that is impossible, for its value (ziynet eşyası davası).

I checked the provisions below against the current text in UYAP Mevzuat, the Turkish Ministry of Justice legal database, in November 2026 (Civil Code TMK, Code of Obligations TBK and Code of Civil Procedure HMK: recorded 31 July 2026, latest act in the amendment list No. 7589; Private International Law Act MÖHUK: recorded 5 June 2025, latest amendment No. 7550). I read the Court of Cassation judgments in full in UYAP Emsal.

## Who owns Turkish wedding gold? What the law says and does not say

The Turkish Civil Code (TMK) does not use the word "ziynet". Courts rely on these provisions:

- Items used solely for the personal use of one spouse are that spouse's personal property (kişisel mal) by law (Art. 220(1) TMK). The official explanatory memorandum gives jewellery as an example.
- When the marital property regime ends, each spouse takes back their assets held by the other (Art. 226(1) TMK).
- Where the law has no applicable provision, the judge decides according to customary law (Art. 1 TMK).
- Whoever claims that an asset belongs to one of the spouses must prove it (Art. 222(1) TMK).

The claim for the wedding gold is therefore separate from the participation claim (katılma alacağı) in the division of marital property. The owner asks for the gold itself; the rule of sharing acquired property half and half does not apply. For the participation claim, see [division of property on divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/division-of-property-divorce-turkey).

## The Court of Cassation's rule: agreement, custom, recipient

What follows is not a statutory rule but settled case law.

The 2nd Civil Chamber used to hold that, absent a contrary agreement or custom, everything given at the wedding (gold, foreign currency, cash) belonged to the wife, no matter who gave it to whom. The chamber states in its judgments that it has abandoned this line because customs have changed and couples are now often given other things of value to help them set up home. The new rule, the oldest example of which I found in UYAP dates from 2024 and which has since been repeated word for word, reads:

1. If the spouses agreed how to divide the jewellery, the agreement applies.
2. If there is no agreement and a local custom is asserted and proven, the custom applies.
3. Otherwise, anything of economic value pinned on or given to the wife or the husband belongs, as a rule, to that person.
4. An item typical for the other sex is deemed a gift to that sex; if this is disputed, the court obtains an expert report. Items suitable for both sexes belong to the spouse they were given to.
5. Items put into the jewellery box or gift bag are deemed a gift to the sex for which they are typical; if they suit both, they are treated as joint.

The rule appears in **Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/5704, K. 2024/2402, T. 04.04.2024** and in later judgments, among the latest **Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2026/136, K. 2026/6720, T. 16.06.2026**.

## Proof in a claim for the return of wedding gold

According to the Court of Cassation, the normal situation is that such jewellery is worn by the wife or kept at home. Because gold is easy to carry and hide, the court accepts that the wife may have taken it with her when she left. The wife who sues therefore has to prove that the jewellery existed and that the husband took it from her.

- **Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/8901, K. 2026/4068, T. 14.04.2026.** The wife's witnesses only repeated what they had heard from others, and she had not relied on the oath as evidence. The chamber reversed the judgment in her favour.
- **Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/10292, K. 2026/4873, T. 29.04.2026.** Once it is proven that the husband received the gold, he must prove that he was entitled to keep it. Here the husband's partial admission, witnesses and the seizure record for the gold in his mother's bank safe deposit box were enough. A judgment ordering return in kind must not also state a lira value, because that makes enforcement unclear.
- **Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2025/9439, K. 2026/6627, T. 16.06.2026.** The husband argued that the gold had been sold and spent on renovating the house in Germany owned by the spouses in equal shares. Unless a gift is proven, he must return the full amount; the wife's half share in the house does not halve her claim.

In these judgments it mattered whether the witnesses had seen events themselves. In my assessment, wedding photos and videos, a list of the jewellery and jeweller or bank records can also be submitted; the judgments do not discuss these separately. If there is no other evidence, a party can rely on the oath (in E. 2026/136 the chamber required the court to remind the wife of her right to offer it).

## Together with the divorce or as a separate claim?

The claim can be raised in the divorce petition or counterclaim, or brought separately after the divorce. In the cases I read, family courts (aile mahkemesi) decided. According to the Court of Cassation, the claim is not an ancillary matter of the divorce and is subject to a court fee based on its value; until that fee is paid, the court may not decide on the merits, even if the gold was already claimed in the divorce petition (**Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/5574, K. 2024/4095, T. 30.05.2024**).

The petition usually lists each item and asks for return in kind or, failing that, its value. The "claim for an unquantified amount" (belirsiz alacak davası, Art. 107 HMK) was repealed by Act No. 7589 (in force since 31 July 2026 according to the amendment list). In a partial claim, the amount can now be increased once until the end of the evidence stage, and the limitation period is treated as interrupted from the filing date for the increased part as well (Art. 109(4) HMK). Which rule applies to cases filed earlier depends on the transitional provision of Act No. 7589, which I could not check against the Official Gazette.

If the gold is held by the spouse's family, the claim is brought against them. One such case I read was heard by a general civil court (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) and reviewed by the 3rd Civil Chamber (see below).

## Limitation period for wedding gold claims

There is no special time limit for this claim. Claims generally become time-barred after ten years (Art. 146 Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, TBK), and limitation does not run between spouses while the marriage lasts (Art. 153 No. 3 TBK). In practice the period therefore starts when the divorce becomes final.

- **Yargıtay 2. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2023/9642, K. 2024/3687, T. 21.05.2024.** The spouses were divorced in Germany in 2009; the German judgment was recognised in Turkey (tanıma) in 2022 and the gold claim was filed in 2022. The family court counted the ten years from the date the German judgment became final, not from the recognition, and dismissed the claim. The appeal failed and the Court of Cassation upheld the result. The ten-year period was applied even though the wife had asked for return in kind.
- **Yargıtay 3. Hukuk Dairesi, E. 2026/211, K. 2026/3415, T. 02.06.2026.** A widow claimed the value of her gold from her parents-in-law more than ten years after her husband's death. The first-instance court held that the claim was not based on ownership because she had not asked for return in kind, and dismissed it as time-barred; the Court of Cassation upheld this.

This point is disputed. The law does not say expressly whether a claim for return based on ownership is subject to limitation at all. The 3rd Chamber's reasoning suggests that a claim for return in kind might be treated differently, but the 2nd Chamber upheld the ten-year period in exactly such a case. In my assessment, the safest course is to count ten years from the date the divorce became final; this is an assessment, not a settled rule.

Engagement gifts follow a different rule: unusual gifts can be claimed back when an engagement ends, and the claim is time-barred one year after the engagement ended (Art. 122, 123 TMK).

## Living in Germany or the Netherlands: jurisdiction and applicable law

### Which court?

The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on territorial jurisdiction (Art. 40 MÖHUK, Act No. 5718). The court of general jurisdiction is the court of the defendant's domicile at the time of filing (Art. 6(1) HMK). If divorce proceedings are pending in Turkey, the court of either spouse's domicile or of the place where they last lived together for six months has jurisdiction (Art. 168 TMK), and for Turkish citizens living abroad the court under Art. 41 MÖHUK. In my assessment, claiming the gold within those divorce proceedings is the route least open to dispute. On the divorce itself: [contested divorce in Turkey](/en/publications/contested-divorce-turkey-grounds).

If the divorce took place in Germany or the Netherlands and the defendant spouse has no domicile in Turkey, the law does not say expressly which Turkish court can hear a stand-alone claim for the gold. Art. 41 MÖHUK covers matters of personal status; whether this claim falls within it is disputed, and I found no binding court decision on the point.

### Which law?

The Turkish judge applies Turkish conflict-of-laws rules, and any foreign law they point to, of their own motion (Art. 2(1) MÖHUK). There is no specific conflict rule for wedding jewellery. The law does not say whether the claim falls under matrimonial property (Art. 15 MÖHUK), ownership of movable property (Art. 21 MÖHUK) or gifts as contracts (Art. 24 MÖHUK), and I found no binding court decision on this. Which view a court will adopt is uncertain.

If both spouses were Turkish citizens when they married, Turkish law applies under Art. 15(1) MÖHUK; for dual nationals who are also Turkish, Turkish nationality prevails (Art. 4(1)(b) MÖHUK). If one spouse had only a foreign nationality at the time of the marriage, or the wedding took place abroad, the outcome may differ, because Art. 21 refers ownership to the law of the country where the item was located at the time of the transaction. A German or Dutch court applies its own rules; for that, please consult a lawyer qualified in that country. More topics: [family law](/en/practice-areas/family-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who gets the wedding gold in a Turkish divorce?

The law does not say expressly. Under the 2nd Civil Chamber's case law, an agreement between the spouses comes first, then a proven local custom; otherwise each item belongs to the person it was given to, and jewellery typical for one sex is deemed a gift to that sex.

### When can I sue for the return of my wedding gold?

Together with the divorce or afterwards. Limitation does not run between spouses while the marriage lasts (Art. 153 No. 3 TBK).

### Is there a time limit for wedding gold claims in Turkey?

There is no special time limit. In judgments upheld by the Court of Cassation, the general ten-year period (Art. 146 TBK) ran from the date the divorce became final, including a German divorce.

### Who has to prove what?

The claimant proves that the jewellery existed and that the other spouse took it. Once that is proven, the other spouse must prove that they do not have to return it.

Article 61 of Turkey’s Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (MÖHUK) governs the enforcement (tenfiz) of foreign arbitral awards in Turkey: which documents go with the petition and how the court examines it. You attach the arbitration agreement, the award and certified translations of both, and the court applies Articles 55, 56 and 57 on the enforcement of court judgments by analogy. Which awards can be enforced at all (Article 60) and the grounds for refusal (Article 62) are covered in separate posts.

## Article 61 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Petition and examination procedure**
>
> Article 61 – (1) A party seeking enforcement of a foreign arbitral award shall attach to its petition the following documents, with as many copies as there are opposing parties:
>
> a) The original or a duly certified copy of the arbitration agreement or arbitration clause.
>
> b) The original or a duly certified copy of the arbitral award that has become final and enforceable, or binding on the parties, in accordance with the applicable procedure.
>
> c) Translated and duly certified copies of the documents listed in (a) and (b).
>
> (2) In the enforcement of arbitral awards, the court shall apply Articles 55, 56 and 57 by analogy.

Original Turkish text:

> **Dilekçe ve inceleme usulü**
>
> MADDE 61 – (1) Yabancı bir hakem kararının tenfizini isteyen taraf, dilekçesine aşağıda yazılı belgeleri, karşı tarafın sayısı kadar örnekleriyle birlikte ekler:
>
> a) Tahkim sözleşmesi veya şartının, aslı yahut usulüne göre onanmış örneği.
>
> b) Hakem kararının usulen kesinleşmiş ve icra kabiliyeti kazanmış veya taraflar için bağlayıcılık kazanmış aslı veya usulüne göre onanmış örneği.
>
> c) (a) ve (b) bentlerinde sayılan belgelerin tercüme edilmiş ve usulen onanmış örnekleri.
>
> (2) Mahkemece hakem kararlarının tenfizinde 55 inci, 56 ncı ve 57 nci madde hükümleri kıyas yoluyla uygulanır.

The article has not changed since the Law came into force on 12 December 2007. As of November 2026, the amendment list of the current text in UYAP Mevzuat shows only the change to Article 27 by Law No. 7550.

## Legislative reasoning

According to the official reasoning, the article partly takes over Article 44 of the former Law No. 2675. It adds the award that has become “binding on the parties”, in line with Article 60. The former cross-reference to the reciprocity requirement was dropped because reciprocity was considered incompatible with arbitration (source: reasoning of the government bill submitted to the Grand National Assembly, Article 60 of the bill).

## Explanation

### Documents to attach

The petition needs three documents: the arbitration agreement or the arbitration clause in the main contract, the award, and a certified translation of both. For the first two you file the original or a duly certified copy. The award must be final and enforceable, or binding on the parties; Article 60(1) uses the same test. You file as many copies as there are opposing parties.

Photocopies are not enough. In a case upheld by the Yargıtay (Court of Cassation), the claimant had filed only photocopies of the award and its translation within the deadline set by the court, and no arbitration clause at all. The case was dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a procedural requirement (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/809, K. 2024/8873). If documents are missing, the court must first give a final deadline to complete them; deciding without doing so was one of the grounds on which the Yargıtay reversed a judgment (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/13151, K. 2016/2168).

### The New York Convention

International treaties to which Turkey is a party take precedence (Article 1(2) MÖHUK). Turkey acceded to the 1958 New York Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards in 1992. According to the UNCITRAL status table, Turkey made two declarations: it applies the Convention only to awards made in the territory of another contracting state, and only to disputes arising from relationships considered commercial under Turkish law. For such awards, Article IV of the Convention also lists the documents: the duly authenticated original award or a duly certified copy, the original arbitration agreement or a duly certified copy, and a translation certified by an official or sworn translator or by a diplomatic or consular agent.

In practice, Turkish courts check the documents against Article IV as well. The 17th Commercial Court of Istanbul found that the Article IV documents had been filed and enforced an award on a freight claim; the decision was upheld on appeal and in cassation (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5174, K. 2026/1864).

Article IV of the Convention does not list separate proof that the award is final, while Article 61(1)(b) MÖHUK requires a final or binding award. This point is contested. The Law does not expressly say how the two rules relate, and the Yargıtay decisions I have read contain no binding ruling on it. In my assessment, it is safer to attach information or documents showing that the award is final or binding.

### Procedure applied by analogy

Paragraph 2 applies three articles on the enforcement of court judgments to arbitral awards “by analogy”, meaning as far as they fit:

- The petition is served on the other side together with the hearing date, and the court decides under the simple procedure (Article 55(1)). More: [Article 55 MÖHUK – Service and Objections](/en/publications/legislation/article-55-mohuk-service-objections-turkey).
- The court enforces the award in full or in part, or rejects the petition (Article 56). More: [Article 56 MÖHUK – Decision on Enforcement in Turkey](/en/publications/legislation/article-56-mohuk-partial-enforcement-turkey).
- An award declared enforceable is executed like a Turkish judgment; in the words of the Law, “an appeal to the Court of Cassation suspends execution” (Article 57). In the recent decisions I have read, the first-instance judgment went first to the regional court of appeal (istinaf) and then to the Court of Cassation.

There is no reference to Article 54, which sets the conditions for court judgments. Reciprocity under Article 54(1)(a) is therefore not required for arbitral awards; the grounds for refusal are listed separately in Article 62.

### Which court?

Under Article 60(2) MÖHUK, the petition goes to the court of first instance (asliye mahkemesi). Where the dispute is commercial, the Yargıtay treats the commercial court (asliye ticaret mahkemesi) as competent (19th Civil Chamber, E. 2014/208, K. 2014/4501). At the commercial court, cases on the recognition and enforcement of foreign arbitral awards are heard by a panel of a presiding judge and two members, whatever the amount (Article 5(3) of Law No. 5235). Jurisdiction is covered in more detail in the post on Article 60.

## Practical points if you live abroad

Enforcement of foreign arbitral awards mostly comes up in commercial disputes, for example where a company operating in Germany or the Netherlands has obtained an award against a Turkish counterparty. Germany and the Netherlands are also parties to the New York Convention. You can run the case through a representative in Turkey without travelling there yourself. Gathering the signed contract with the arbitration clause, a certified copy of the award and information on its finality or binding effect before filing helps avoid a dismissal for missing documents. For general information, see [Private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law).

## Court of Cassation decisions

**Yargıtay, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/809, K. 2024/8873, 10.12.2024**

The case concerned an award made in London. The claimant was given a final deadline to file the Article 61 documents but filed only photocopies of the award and its translation. The arbitration clause and certified proof that the award was final or binding were missing. The first-instance court dismissed the case for lack of a procedural requirement, the regional court of appeal rejected the appeal on the merits, and the Chamber upheld that decision.

**Yargıtay, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/13151, K. 2016/2168, 29.02.2016**

The Chamber held that even where the decision is treated as an arbitral award, the court must first check the documents required by Article 61 and, if any are missing, give the claimant a final deadline. Deciding without this check was one of the grounds for reversal.

**Yargıtay, 19th Civil Chamber, E. 2014/208, K. 2014/4501, 06.03.2014**

A commercial court had dismissed the case, holding that the general civil court (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) was competent for foreign arbitral awards. The Chamber reversed: the dispute arose from a commercial sale, so the commercial court was competent, and it is itself a court of first instance (asliye mahkemesi).

## Frequently asked questions

### What documents are needed to enforce an arbitral award in Turkey?

The arbitration agreement or clause and the final or binding award, each as an original or certified copy, plus certified translations (Article 61(1) MÖHUK). If the award falls under the New York Convention, Article IV of the Convention lists similar documents.

### Is the case dismissed at once if a document is missing?

According to the Yargıtay, the court must first set a final deadline to complete the documents. If they are not filed in time, the case is dismissed on procedural grounds.

### Is reciprocity required for arbitral awards?

No. Article 61(2) does not refer to Article 54, which contains the reciprocity requirement. Under the New York Convention, Turkey’s declaration means the award must have been made in another contracting state.

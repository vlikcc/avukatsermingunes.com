If you inherit anything from an estate in Turkey, you have to file a Turkish inheritance tax return (veraset ve intikal vergisi beyannamesi), even if you live in Germany or the Netherlands and even if your share stays below the tax-free amount. Under the law, heirs who are abroad file the return with a Turkish consulate. The deadline is four, six or eight months, depending on where the deceased died and where you are.

This article reflects the law as of November 2026. The most recent change to the current text of the Inheritance and Gift Tax Law No. 7338 (Veraset ve İntikal Vergisi Kanunu, VİVK) on mevzuat.gov.tr is a paragraph added to Article 16 by Law No. 7582 (in force since 4 June 2026). The rules on the return discussed here (Articles 5 to 9) were not affected.

## Who has to pay inheritance tax in Turkey?

The tax applies to property of Turkish citizens and to property located in Turkey (Article 1(1) VİVK). If a flat, a bank account or a car in Turkey passes to you, your nationality and your place of residence make no difference. The taxpayer is the individual heir (Article 5). Tax is charged on each heir’s share, not on the estate as a whole.

For Turkish citizens living abroad there is one more rule: the tax also covers property they inherit in other countries (Article 1(2)). The September 2026 brochure of the Turkish Revenue Administration (Gelir İdaresi Başkanlığı, GİB) also says that Turkish citizens must file a return in that case. A foreigner who has no residence in Turkey and inherits a Turkish citizen’s property abroad is not liable (Article 1(3)). This article does not cover how property abroad is treated for former Turkish citizens holding the Blue Card (Mavi Kart).

Property abroad is valued as determined under the rules of the country where it is located (Article 291(2) of the Tax Procedure Law No. 213, Vergi Usul Kanunu, VUK). Debts relating to that property, and inheritance tax paid abroad on it, are deducted if documented (Article 12(c) VİVK), up to the declared value of that property (Article 12(ç)).

## Deadline for the inheritance tax return

The deadlines run from the day after the date of death (Article 9(1) VİVK):

- Death in Turkey, heir in Turkey: four months.
- Death in Turkey, heir abroad: six months.
- Death abroad, heir in Turkey: six months.
- Death abroad, heir in the same country as the deceased: four months.
- Death abroad, heir in a different foreign country: eight months.

If your father died in Germany and you also live in Germany, you have **four months**. Living abroad does not always give you more time. For the same death, your sister in the Netherlands would have eight months and your brother in Turkey six. Because the law ties the deadline to where each taxpayer is, in the author’s reading the heirs’ deadlines run separately; this follows from the wording. Where a person is declared missing, the deadline is one month from the entry of the decision in the death register (Article 9(1)(c)).

## Where and how to file the return

The competent tax office (vergi dairesi) is the one for the deceased’s place of residence. If that was abroad, the last residence in Turkey counts. If the deceased never lived in Turkey or the last residence cannot be established, the Ministry designates the tax office (Article 6). The return is filed there (Article 8(1)).

Taxpayers abroad file their returns with Turkish consulates (Article 8(2)). Heirs may file separately or jointly (Article 8(3)). According to the GİB brochure, a joint return must be signed by every heir.

The brochure describes a paper return handed in at the tax office or sent by post, signed by the taxpayers or their attorneys. If the deceased lived in Ankara, Istanbul or İzmir, you first book an appointment for “veraset işlemleri” on the website of the relevant provincial finance directorate (defterdarlık).

Whether an heir abroad may file through an attorney in Turkey, or by post directly with the tax office, instead of at the consulate, is not expressly regulated. This point is disputed. What follows is an opinion; there is no express statutory rule and no binding court decision on it, and it is not certain which view would be followed in a dispute. In the author’s view, Article 8(2) is meant to make filing easier for taxpayers abroad, and since the brochure accepts an attorney’s signature, filing at the tax office through an attorney is not ruled out. If you go this way, have the power to file the return stated expressly in the power of attorney and check with the tax office first.

## Documents to attach

The GİB brochure lists mainly:

- the Turkish certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi, veraset ilamı),
- a civil registry record showing the deceased’s last residence,
- any will or contract of inheritance,
- documents on debts and funeral costs,
- for real estate, a certificate from the municipality stating the property tax value (emlak vergisi değeri),
- for vehicles, a copy of the registration document,
- statements from banks and other institutions where the deceased had funds or claims,
- for a business, the balance sheet or a summary of the business accounts.

How to obtain the Turkish certificate of inheritance from abroad is covered in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey). According to the brochure, if no property passed to the heirs at all, you do not need to submit a petition saying that no return will be filed.

## How much is inheritance tax in Turkey? 2026 exemption and calculation

The tax base is the value of the inherited property under the Tax Procedure Law (Article 10 VİVK). Real estate is valued at its property tax value, movable property at market value and foreign currency at the exchange rate (Article 10(b), (c), (f)). The valuation date is the date of death (Article 11). Documented debts and tax debts of the deceased and funeral costs are deducted if they are shown in the return (Article 12).

Under General Communiqué No. 57 on Inheritance and Gift Tax (Official Gazette of 31 December 2025, No. 33124, 5th repeat issue), from 1 January 2026 the following are tax-free:

- **TRY 2,907,136** of the share of each child (adopted children included) and of the spouse,
- **TRY 5,817,845** of the spouse’s share if there are no children

(Article 4(b)). The 2026 rates apply to the rest: 1 percent on the first TRY 3,000,000, 3 percent on the next TRY 7,000,000, 5 percent on the next TRY 15,000,000, 7 percent on the next TRY 30,000,000 and 10 percent on the part above TRY 55,000,000 (Article 16). These amounts are also shown in the law text on mevzuat.gov.tr and in the GİB brochure. Since the estate passes on death, the 2026 amounts do not apply if the deceased died in 2025.

A worked example (the figures are an example only): the declared value of a child’s share is TRY 4,000,000. After deducting the exemption, TRY 1,092,864 remains. That falls in the first band, so the tax is 1 percent, or TRY 10,928.64. If your share does not exceed TRY 2,907,136, no tax is due, but you still have to file.

Law No. 7582 added a paragraph to Article 16: for people who benefit from the income tax exemption in Article 20/D (mükerrer) of the Turkish Income Tax Law, the rate on inheritances during the period of that exemption is 1 percent (in force since 4 June 2026). The conditions of that exemption are not covered here; ask the tax office whether your case falls under it.

## Payment and consequences of filing late

The tax assessed is paid over three years in two equal instalments each year, in May and November (Article 19(1) VİVK); the GİB brochure describes this as six instalments in total. According to the brochure, you can also pay through the online tax office (Dijital Vergi Dairesi) with cards issued by foreign banks. After the return is filed and the tax paid, the tax office issues a clearance letter (ilişik kesme yazısı). Banks ask for it before paying heirs (Article 17); see [deceased person’s bank account in Turkey](/en/publications/deceased-bank-account-turkey-heirs). Registering a property in the heirs’ names at the land registry does not wait for the tax, but the property cannot be sold until the tax attributable to it has been paid in full (Article 19(2)); see [inheriting property in Turkey](/en/publications/inheriting-property-in-turkey-title-deed).

If you miss the deadline, Article 342 VUK provides two further periods. After the deadline the tax office waits 15 days before assessing; a return filed within that time is treated as causing no tax loss. The taxpayer is then given another 15 days, on condition that this is formally notified. A return filed in this second period is a first-degree procedural irregularity (usulsüzlük; Article 352, section I, no. 11 VUK). If the return is still not filed and tax is due, a tax loss penalty (vergi ziyaı cezası) may follow (Articles 341, 344 VUK). If a late return is filed before a tax audit begins and before referral to the assessment commission, that penalty is applied at 50 percent (Article 344(3)). Which penalty applies and in what amount depends on the case; this article does not give penalty amounts, so ask the tax office.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do I file the Turkish inheritance tax return?

With the tax office for the deceased’s residence in Turkey, or the last Turkish residence if the deceased lived abroad (Articles 6 and 8(1) VİVK). Heirs abroad file with a Turkish consulate under the law (Article 8(2)).

### Do I have to file if my share is below the exemption?

Yes. According to the GİB brochure, a return is required even if the inherited property stays below the exemption. If no property passed at all, no return is needed.

### What is the deadline for an heir living in Germany or the Netherlands?

Six months if the death occurred in Turkey. Four months if the deceased died in the country where you are; eight months if the deceased died in another foreign country (Article 9(1) VİVK).

### How much is the inheritance tax exemption in Turkey in 2026?

TRY 2,907,136 for each child and the spouse, and TRY 5,817,845 for the spouse if there are no children. The rest is taxed in bands from 1 to 10 percent (Articles 4(b) and 16 VİVK; Communiqué No. 57).

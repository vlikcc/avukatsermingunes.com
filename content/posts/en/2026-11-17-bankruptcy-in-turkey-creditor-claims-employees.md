When a company is declared bankrupt in Turkey, its creditors can no longer enforce individually. They file their claims with the bankruptcy office (iflas dairesi) within one month of the public notice and are paid from the bankruptcy estate in the order set by law (Articles 219 and 206 of the Turkish Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, İİK). Employee claims that arose in the year before bankruptcy, including notice pay (ihbar tazminatı) and severance pay (kıdem tazminatı), rank first. Up to three months of unpaid wages can also be claimed from the Wage Guarantee Fund (Ücret Garanti Fonu) run by the Turkish employment agency İŞKUR (Additional Article 1 of the Unemployment Insurance Law No. 4447).

This article explains the general legal framework and does not concern any particular company. It reflects the law as of November 2026. The İİK was last amended by Law No. 7589 (Article 114, in force since 31 July 2026); the bankruptcy provisions discussed here were not affected.

## What happens when bankruptcy is opened?

Bankruptcy is opened by a court decision that states the exact time of opening (Article 165 İİK). From that moment:

- All of the company's attachable assets, wherever they are, form the bankruptcy estate (iflas masası) and are used to pay creditors (Article 184).
- Enforcement by attachment stops, and those proceedings lapse once the bankruptcy decision becomes final. Proceedings to realise a pledge continue against the estate (Article 193).
- Civil lawsuits to which the company is a party are stayed, except in urgent cases and a few listed exceptions, and resume ten days after the second creditors' meeting (Article 194).
- Apart from claims secured by a mortgage on real property, all of the company's debts fall due, and interest accrued up to the opening is added to the principal (Article 195). Later interest is paid only after all principal amounts have been paid (Article 196).

The decision is notified to the land registry, the trade registry and other bodies, and published in a newspaper or on a news website and in the Trade Registry Gazette (Article 166).

## Filing a claim: time limit and documents

In ordinary liquidation the bankruptcy office publishes a notice asking creditors to register their claims **within one month of the notice** and to submit their evidence (bills, invoices, extracts from accounts) in original or certified copy. The period runs from the last publication (Article 219 İİK).

If the assets will not even cover the costs of the proceedings, summary liquidation applies, and the office sets a filing period of no less than twenty days and no more than two months (Article 218).

Missing the deadline does not mean losing the claim altogether. Late claims are accepted until the bankruptcy is closed, but the creditor bears the costs caused by the delay and does not share in distributions decided before the filing (Article 236).

A creditor who gives an address for service and pays an advance for service costs can ask to be served with the decisions of the bankruptcy administration (iflas idaresi); time limits for challenging those decisions then run from service (Article 223(3)).

## The ranking schedule: who gets paid first?

Once the filing period ends, the bankruptcy administration examines the claims and prepares the ranking schedule (sıra cetveli) within two months of its election; this can be extended once by up to two months (Articles 230 and 232 İİK). The schedule is published, and creditors whose claims were rejected in whole or in part are also notified directly (Article 234).

Distribution follows this order:

1. The costs of opening and administering the bankruptcy are deducted first (Article 248).
2. Secured creditors have priority over the proceeds of their collateral (Article 206(1)).
3. Claims not covered by collateral and unsecured claims are divided into four ranks (Article 206(4)): first, employee claims, debts to employee welfare funds and maintenance claims for the last year; second, claims of persons whose property the debtor managed as parent or guardian; third, claims given priority by special laws; fourth, all other claims.

Creditors in the same rank share equally. A lower rank receives nothing until the rank above it has been paid in full (Article 207). Suppliers and buyers without collateral or statutory priority are in the fourth rank.

A creditor who disputes the schedule must sue **within fifteen days** of its publication before the commercial court where bankruptcy was declared. If the objection concerns only the rank, it goes to the enforcement court as a complaint (Article 235). A creditor not paid in full receives a certificate of loss (aciz vesikası) for the balance (Article 251).

## Employee claims in the first rank

Article 206(4)(A) İİK reads:

> A) İşçilerin, iş ilişkisine dayanan ve iflâsın açılmasından önceki bir yıl içinde tahakkuk etmiş ihbar ve kıdem tazminatları dahil alacakları ile iflâs nedeniyle iş ilişkisinin sona ermesi üzerine hak etmiş oldukları ihbar ve kıdem tazminatları,

Unofficial translation: “A) Claims of employees arising from the employment relationship that accrued within one year before the opening of bankruptcy, including notice and severance pay, and the notice and severance pay they became entitled to when the employment relationship ended because of the bankruptcy,”

In calculating the one-year period, the time spent in a prior concordat (including the moratorium) and the time a lawsuit about the claim was pending are, among others, not counted (Article 206). On the wording of the law, employee claims that accrued more than a year before bankruptcy do not fall in the first rank; they rank fourth as ordinary claims.

Does bankruptcy end the employment contract automatically? The İİK does not deal with this directly; Article 206 only refers to the employment relationship ending “because of the bankruptcy”. Whether severance and notice pay are owed depends on who ended the contract and on what ground, which is a question of Turkish employment law. If the business is transferred to someone else as part of the bankruptcy liquidation, the rules in Article 6 of the Labour Law No. 4857 on contracts passing to the transferee do not apply (Article 6, last paragraph).

## Wage Guarantee Fund: how many months are paid?

If the employer enters a concordat, a certificate of loss is issued against it, or it goes bankrupt, the Wage Guarantee Fund pays unpaid wages from the employment relationship for **up to three months**. The employee must have worked at the same workplace in the year before the employer became unable to pay, and payment is based on the basic wage (Additional Article 1, Law No. 4447). The Wage Guarantee Fund Regulation treats one day of work as sufficient for that condition (Article 9(2)).

Other rules in the Regulation:

- The basic wage is the net wage calculated from earnings subject to contributions (Article 4(g)). The daily amount cannot exceed the earnings ceiling under Article 82 of Law No. 5510 (Article 9(3)).
- Pay for public holidays, weekly rest days, paid leave and overtime in the month claimed is included if contributions were reported for it (Article 9(8)). Severance and notice pay are not part of the defined “wage claim” (Article 4(e)); they are filed with the bankruptcy estate.
- In bankruptcy, the application must include the bankruptcy decision or proof of its publication and a statement of the employee's claim (işçi alacak belgesi) approved by the bankruptcy office or administration. It can be made whether or not the employment contract is still in force and, except in cases of force majeure, must be made **in person** at an İŞKUR office (Article 8).
- Payment is made by the end of the month following the application, limited by the Fund's resources and in order of application (Article 9(4) and (5)).

## Concordat and bankruptcy: the difference

A concordat (konkordato) lets a debtor who cannot pay its debts, or is at risk of not paying them, avoid bankruptcy through deferral or reduction of its debts (Article 285 İİK). In bankruptcy the assets are sold and distributed. In a concordat, contracts that matter for the debtor's business are, as a rule, kept in force (Article 296).

During the moratorium no enforcement may be brought against the debtor. Claims in the first rank of Article 206, which include employee claims, can still be enforced by attachment (Article 294). The court approves a concordat only if full payment of those claims is adequately secured (Article 305(1)(d)).

The Wage Guarantee Fund also applies in a concordat: the application needs the decision granting the final moratorium and a claim statement approved by the concordat commissioner (Regulation Article 8). If the concordat is approved, the Fund pays nothing; if it ends in bankruptcy, the same person receives at most 90 days' pay in total (Article 9(12)).

## Creditors and former employees living abroad

A buyer or supplier in Germany, or a former employee who worked in Turkey and now lives abroad, files the claim with the bankruptcy office in Turkey in the same way. The law allows the filing period to be extended for creditors living very far away or abroad (Article 219 İİK). It only says the period “may” be extended. In the author's assessment the period does not extend by itself, so treat the one-month period in the notice as the deadline.

If a lawyer in Turkey is to file the claim for you, you can grant a power of attorney at a Turkish consulate; one made before a foreign notary needs an apostille and a Turkish translation. More on this: [Article 30 MÖHUK – power of attorney](/en/publications/legislation/article-30-mohuk-power-of-attorney-agency). Give an address for service so that decisions reach you (Article 223(3)).

For former employees abroad, the rule that applications to the Wage Guarantee Fund must be made in person matters (Regulation Article 8). Apart from “force majeure”, the Regulation does not expressly provide for an application through a representative; ask the competent İŞKUR office in advance what counts as force majeure.

Whether anything also needs to be done in the country where you live depends on that country's law; for this, consult a lawyer admitted there. General information: [Enforcement and insolvency](/en/practice-areas/enforcement-insolvency) and [Employment law](/en/practice-areas/employment-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens to employee claims when a company goes bankrupt in Turkey?

Claims that accrued in the year before bankruptcy, and notice and severance pay earned when employment ended because of the bankruptcy, rank first (Article 206 İİK). Up to three months of unpaid wages can be claimed from the Wage Guarantee Fund.

### What is the deadline for filing a claim in a Turkish bankruptcy?

One month from the public notice in ordinary liquidation (Article 219 İİK); in summary liquidation the office sets a period between twenty days and two months (Article 218). Late filing is possible until the bankruptcy is closed (Article 236).

### How much does the Turkish Wage Guarantee Fund pay?

Up to three months of unpaid basic wages (Additional Article 1, Law No. 4447). It does not pay severance or notice pay.

### How long do I have to challenge the ranking schedule?

Fifteen days from its publication, by a lawsuit before the commercial court where bankruptcy was declared (Article 235 İİK).

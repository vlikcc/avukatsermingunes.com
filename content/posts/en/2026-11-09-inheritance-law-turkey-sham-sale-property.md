When a person registers a property in Turkey as a sale at the land registry (tapu) but in reality gives it away, Turkish law calls this *muris muvazaası*: a sham transaction by the deceased. After that person dies, every heir whose inheritance right was harmed can sue to have the title cancelled and registered in their name in proportion to their share (tapu iptali ve tescil davası). The Turkish Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) has held that no limitation period or time bar applies to this claim.

There is no specific statutory provision for it. The basis is a unifying decision of the Court of Cassation (İçtihadı Birleştirme Kararı), cited in the court’s judgments as “decision of 01.04.1974, No. 1/2”, and the case law built on it. Below I explain the requirements, how the claim differs from a reduction claim, when courts reject it, and what heirs living in Germany or the Netherlands should keep in mind. The provisions cited were checked against the current text in the official UYAP Mevzuat system in November 2026.

## What is muris muvazaası under Turkish inheritance law?

In a simulated transaction (muvazaa) the parties sign a contract that hides what they really intend. Under Article 19(1) of the Turkish Code of Obligations (TBK), the type and content of a contract are determined by the parties’ real and common intention, regardless of the words they used to hide their true purpose.

In muris muvazaası the land registry shows a sale, while the real intention was a gift. The 1st Civil Chamber of the Yargıtay describes this as a relative (disguised) simulation: the deceased does want to transfer the property, but hides the real purpose in order to deprive an heir of their inheritance, and declares a sale or a life-care contract (ölünceye kadar bakma sözleşmesi) in the official deed (E. 2025/6738, K. 2026/4545, judgment of 03.06.2026).

Two form rules lead to the result:

- The visible sale is void because it does not reflect what the parties wanted.
- The hidden gift is void as well, because contracts transferring ownership of land must be made in official form (Article 706(1) of the Turkish Civil Code, TMK; for sales, Article 237(1) TBK; the official deed is drawn up by the land registry, Article 26 of the Land Registry Law). No deed of gift was ever made.

A registration based on a transaction that is not binding is unlawful (Article 1024(2) TMK), and a person whose property right is harmed by it can sue to have the register corrected (Article 1025(1) TMK).

## Who can sue, from when, and is there a time limit?

According to the Yargıtay, every heir whose inheritance right was harmed may sue separately, whether or not they have a forced share (1st Civil Chamber, E. 2025/6738, K. 2026/4545). Each heir asks for cancellation and registration in proportion to their own share. Siblings, who have no forced share under Turkish law, can bring this claim too; that is the main difference from a reduction claim.

The right to sue arises when the deceased dies. The heir does not sue as the deceased’s successor but on the basis of their own inheritance right. A claim filed while the owner is still alive is dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of standing (1st Civil Chamber, E. 2022/6485, K. 2023/54, judgment of 10.01.2023).

On time limits, the 1st Civil Chamber has held that claims for cancellation and registration based on muris muvazaası are subject to no limitation period or time bar; a simulated transaction has no effect and does not become valid with time (E. 2010/2775, K. 2010/5274, judgment of 05.05.2010). In my own assessment, the absence of a deadline does not make waiting harmless: witnesses and documents are lost over the years.

The figure of “10 years” that appears in many online answers comes from two other rules:

1. For the **reduction claim**, Article 571 TMK sets time limits of one year and, in any case, ten years. They do not apply to a muris muvazaası claim.
2. In **cadastral registration**, a finalised cadastral record can no longer be challenged on grounds dating from before the cadastre once ten years have passed since it became final (Article 12(3) of Cadastre Law No. 3402). Where the deceased died after the cadastral record became final, the Yargıtay held that this ten-year period had not run for the muris muvazaası claim (E. 2010/2775, K. 2010/5274). Where the deceased died before the cadastral survey, the chamber examined that period (E. 2026/3574, K. 2026/4584, judgment of 03.06.2026).

## How it differs from a reduction claim and forced shares

The two claims are often filed together, with reduction as an alternative request.

A **reduction claim (tenkis davası)** belongs to heirs who have not received their forced share (saklı pay); it targets dispositions exceeding the freely disposable portion (Article 560 TMK). Forced heirs are descendants, parents and the surviving spouse (Article 506 TMK); siblings are not. Lifetime gifts subject to reduction are listed in Article 565 TMK, including obvious gifts made to defeat the forced-share rules (Article 565, item 4). In a reduction claim the transaction stays valid; only the part that infringes the forced share is reduced.

A **muris muvazaası claim** argues instead that the transaction was void from the start. A forced share is not required, the time limits of Article 571 TMK do not apply, and the result is cancellation and registration in proportion to the heir’s share.

If the deceased openly gave the property as a gift at the land registry, nothing was hidden. The 1974 decision then does not apply, and only a reduction claim remains if its conditions are met. The 1st Civil Chamber upheld an appeal judgment that had rejected a claim on this ground (E. 2026/812, K. 2026/6185, judgment of 17.09.2026). Forced-share fractions are covered in the article on [making a will in Turkey](/en/publications/making-a-will-in-turkey), statutory shares in [inheritance shares in Turkey](/en/publications/inheritance-shares-turkey-spouse-children).

## What the court looks at, and when the claim fails

The case turns on proving the deceased’s real purpose. The Yargıtay requires the court to take into account local customs, the ordinary course of events, whether the deceased had a reasonable need to sell, whether the buyer could afford the price, the gap between the price and the real value, and the relationship between the parties and the deceased (1st Civil Chamber, E. 2025/6738, K. 2026/4545). A low price in the deed is not enough on its own; the evidence is assessed as a whole, and in the files reviewed by the Yargıtay this included witness statements.

According to the judgments checked for this article, the claim fails in these situations:

- **Fair distribution.** If the deceased made a balanced distribution during their lifetime that included all heirs, there is no intent to divert assets (E. 2025/6738, K. 2026/4545).
- **Gratitude for care.** In the same judgment the Yargıtay found that a transfer to a son who had cared for the deceased for years, well beyond ordinary family duty, was made out of gratitude; since other land had passed to the heirs, it overturned the appeal judgment that had granted the claim.
- **Unregistered land.** Transferring mere possession of land not entered in the land register requires no form; the 1974 decision does not apply and only a reduction claim is possible (E. 2010/2775, K. 2010/5274; E. 2026/3574, K. 2026/4584).
- **Open gift.** As above: reduction, not cancellation.
- **Third party in good faith.** A person who acquired in good faith, relying on the land register, is protected (Articles 1023 and 1025(2) TMK). A person who knew or should have known of the sham cannot rely on the registration (Article 1024(1) TMK).

## Which court, and how the case runs

The case belongs to the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi, Article 2(1) of the Code of Civil Procedure, HMK). For claims concerning property rights in land, the court where the property is located has exclusive jurisdiction (Article 12(1) HMK).

Even if the deceased died in Germany or the Netherlands, Turkish law governs real property in Turkey (Article 20(1) of the Turkish Act on Private International Law, MÖHUK), and property rights are governed by the law of the place where the property is (Article 21(1) MÖHUK). The international jurisdiction of Turkish courts follows the domestic rules on venue (Article 40 MÖHUK). For inheritance disputes, Article 43 MÖHUK points to the deceased’s last domicile in Turkey or, failing that, to where estate assets are located. My own assessment is that, because the claim seeks cancellation of a title, the exclusive venue of Article 12 HMK is decisive.

Article 18/B of the Mediation in Civil Disputes Law (HUAK) lists the disputes in which mediation is a precondition for filing suit: tenancy, partition and termination of co-ownership, condominium matters and neighbour law. As of November 2026, a muris muvazaası claim is not on that list.

The law sets no duration for this kind of case. How long it takes depends on the number of witnesses, site inspection and valuation, service of documents abroad and appeals.

If the claim succeeds, the property is registered to you in proportion to your share, which often creates co-ownership. If the co-owners cannot agree, the next step is a partition action, which requires prior mediation. For property owned entirely by heirs, since 31.07.2026 the first round of a court auction is open only to the heirs (Article 114(2) of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, İİK). Whether shares registered through a muris muvazaası judgment count as “acquired by inheritance” for this rule is not expressly settled in the law, and no checked judgment on it is reported here. See [partition of inherited property in Turkey](/en/publications/inherited-property-turkey-partition-lawsuit).

## If you live in Germany or the Netherlands

You can file and pursue the claim through a lawyer in Turkey to whom you give a power of attorney (vekâletname). Without express authority, the representative cannot, for example, settle, withdraw the claim or accept the other side’s claim (Article 74 HMK), so the scope of the power of attorney should fit the case.

Because each heir sues for their own share, you can bring the claim alone even if your siblings do not want to join.

For the tax or inheritance consequences of a Turkish property under German or Dutch law, please consult a lawyer or tax adviser admitted in that country. General information is on the [inheritance law](/en/practice-areas/inheritance-law) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a time limit for a muris muvazaası claim in Turkey?

According to the 1st Civil Chamber of the Yargıtay, there is no limitation period or time bar for this claim (E. 2010/2775, K. 2010/5274). The one-year and ten-year limits belong to the reduction claim (Article 571 TMK). For land registered through the cadastre, the ten-year limit in Article 12(3) of the Cadastre Law is examined separately.

### Who can challenge a sham sale of inherited property in Turkey?

Every heir whose inheritance right was harmed, even without a forced share, each for their own share. The claim can only be brought after the owner’s death.

### My father sold the house to my brother on paper. What can I do?

If you can prove that the sale was really a gift meant to take the property out of the estate, the title is cancelled and registered to you in proportion to your share. If the land registry shows an open gift, only a reduction claim is possible.

Article 50 MÖHUK opens the part of Turkish Law No. 5718 on Private International Law and International Civil Procedure (Milletlerarası Özel Hukuk ve Usul Hukuku Hakkında Kanun, MÖHUK; Official Gazette of 12 December 2007, No. 26728) that governs the enforcement of foreign judgments. It provides that a foreign court judgment can only be enforced in Turkey once a Turkish court has issued an enforcement decision (tenfiz kararı). It concerns anyone who wants to enforce a German, Dutch or other foreign judgment in Turkey, or against whom such a judgment is to be enforced.

## Article 50 MÖHUK – text

Unofficial translation:

> **Enforcement decision**
>
> Article 50 – (1) The enforcement in Turkey of judgments rendered by foreign courts in civil cases that have become final under the laws of that state is subject to an enforcement decision being issued by the competent Turkish court.
>
> (2) An enforcement decision may also be requested for provisions concerning personal rights contained in criminal judgments of foreign courts.

Original Turkish text:

> **Tenfiz kararı**
>
> MADDE 50 – (1) Yabancı mahkemelerden hukuk davalarına ilişkin olarak verilmiş ve o devlet kanunlarına göre kesinleşmiş bulunan ilâmların Türkiye'de icra olunabilmesi yetkili Türk mahkemesi tarafından tenfiz kararı verilmesine bağlıdır.
>
> (2) Yabancı mahkemelerin ceza ilâmlarında yer alan kişisel haklarla ilgili hükümler hakkında da tenfiz kararı istenebilir.

The current text on mevzuat.gov.tr shows no amendment note for this article (as of October 2026).

## Explanation

A foreign judgment is not automatically enforceable in Turkey. Before a German judgment for payment, damages or maintenance can be enforced through a Turkish enforcement office (icra dairesi), a Turkish court must first grant enforcement (tenfiz). The foreign judgment is then enforced in the same way as a judgment of a Turkish court (Article 57(1) MÖHUK).

The elements of paragraph 1:

- **A judgment of a foreign court:** Foreign arbitral awards are governed separately (Article 60 et seq. MÖHUK).
- **A civil case:** As a rule, judgments on private-law disputes are declared enforceable.
- **Final under the law of the state of origin:** Whether the judgment is final is determined by the law of the state where it was given, not by Turkish law.
- **Enforcement in Turkey:** The enforcement decision is needed where the judgment is to be enforced by compulsory means in Turkey.

Under paragraph 2, enforcement may also be sought for parts of a foreign criminal judgment that concern personal rights (for example, compensation awarded to the victim). The criminal penalty itself is not the subject of this paragraph.

### Relationship with other provisions

- **Article 51 MÖHUK:** The court of first instance (asliye mahkemesi) has subject-matter jurisdiction. Venue lies with the court of the Turkish domicile of the person against whom enforcement is sought, failing that the place of residence; if there is neither, one of the courts in Ankara, İstanbul or İzmir.
- **Articles 52 and 53 MÖHUK:** The application is made in writing. It must be accompanied by the duly certified original or a certified copy of the judgment, a certified document showing that it is final, and certified translations.
- **Article 54 MÖHUK:** Conditions for enforcement: reciprocity, no exclusive jurisdiction of Turkish courts, no manifest violation of public policy, and respect for the right to be heard.
- **Article 58 MÖHUK:** If the judgment is not to be enforced but only accepted as res judicata or conclusive evidence, the procedure is **recognition** (tanıma). The reciprocity requirement in Article 54(1)(a) does not apply to recognition.
- **Article 1(2) MÖHUK:** Provisions of international conventions to which Turkey is a party are reserved. Where such a convention covers the matter, it should be examined first.

## Practical points for Turks living abroad

**Recognition or enforcement?** If the only aim is to update civil status in the Turkish population register (for example after a divorce), recognition is generally sufficient. If the judgment contains an obligation to be enforced in Turkey (for example maintenance, damages or a payment), enforcement is required for that part. General information is available on our [Turkey–Germany](/en/turkiye-germany) page.

**Certificate of finality.** For German judgments, the certificate of finality (Rechtskraftvermerk) should be obtained separately. A judgment that is not final and only provisionally enforceable does not meet the requirement of finality in Article 50.

**Certification and translation.** Article 53 MÖHUK requires "duly certified" documents. Turkey, Germany and the Netherlands are parties to the 1961 Hague Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents (Apostille Convention); documents from these states are therefore generally certified with an apostille. Certified Turkish translations must be attached.

**Acting from abroad.** The proceedings can be conducted through an authorised representative in Turkey without travelling. See also our page on [private international law](/en/practice-areas/private-international-law).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a German judgment be enforced directly in Turkey?

No. Under Article 50(1) MÖHUK, enforcement of a foreign judgment in Turkey requires an enforcement decision of the competent Turkish court.

### Does the Turkish court re-examine the merits of the judgment?

The court examines whether the conditions in Article 54 MÖHUK are met. The other party may object only on the grounds that these conditions are not met, or that the judgment has been fully or partly satisfied or that an obstacle to its performance has arisen (Article 55(2) MÖHUK).

### Can enforcement be granted for a judgment that is not yet final?

Article 50(1) MÖHUK requires the judgment to be final under the law of the state of origin. A judgment that is not final therefore cannot be declared enforceable.

Heirs collect the money in a deceased person’s Turkish bank account by presenting the Turkish certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi) and, as a rule, a clearance letter from the tax office confirming that no inheritance tax is outstanding (ilişik kesme yazısı). Where there are several heirs, the money belongs to all of them jointly, so as a rule all heirs or their attorneys have to act together (Article 640(2) TMK). If you live in Germany, the Netherlands or elsewhere abroad, all of this can be done through a power of attorney issued at a Turkish consulate, without travelling to Turkey.

This article reflects the law as of November 2026. The most recent amendment to the Inheritance and Gift Tax Law No. 7338 (Veraset ve İntikal Vergisi Kanunu, VİVK) is a paragraph added to Article 16 by Law No. 7582 (in force since 4 June 2026); it does not affect Articles 6, 8, 9 and 17 discussed below. The Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Türk Medeni Kanunu, TMK) was last amended by Law No. 7589 in Articles 440 and 444 (in force since 31 October 2026), which do not concern inheritance.

## Who owns the money in a deceased person’s bank account?

Heirs acquire the estate as a whole, by operation of law, at the moment of death, and that includes claims (Article 599 TMK). A bank deposit is the deceased’s claim against the bank, so it passes to the heirs on death. Who the heirs are, and their shares, is shown by the certificate of inheritance (Article 598 TMK).

With more than one heir, a community of heirs exists until the estate is divided. The heirs hold the estate in joint ownership (elbirliği mülkiyeti) and dispose of all its rights together (Article 640(1)–(2) TMK). Joint ownership has no defined shares; each heir’s right extends to the whole (Article 701(2) TMK). Unless the law or a contract says otherwise, management and disposal require unanimity (Article 702(2) TMK). Even if your inheritance share is one quarter, a quarter of the account is not yet “yours” before the estate is divided.

Leaving the money untouched for years creates a different problem. Under Article 62 of the Banking Law No. 5411 (Bankacılık Kanunu), deposits not claimed within ten years of the holder’s last request, transaction or written instruction become time-barred. If the bank cannot reach the holder, the money is transferred as revenue to the Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (Tasarruf Mevduatı Sigorta Fonu) after a public notice.

## How to find a deceased person’s bank account in Turkey

If you do not know which bank held an account, the e-Devlet service “Mevduat/Katılım Fonu Hesabı Bulunan Banka Sorgulama” can help. According to the FAQ of the banking regulator BDDK, it shows Turkish citizens who have obtained a certificate of inheritance which banks held deposit or participation accounts of the person they inherit from. Certificates issued by civil courts of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) after 16 December 2016 and by notaries after 2 June 2021 are covered.

The service names the bank only; for the balance and branch you have to ask the bank. Accounts at foreign branches and accounts of people who were not Turkish citizens do not appear. If the deceased held a Blue Card (Mavi Kart), this route may not help.

## What the bank asks for: certificate of inheritance and tax clearance letter

The first document is the certificate of inheritance, issued by a notary or the civil court of peace (Article 598(1) TMK). When a notary cannot issue it for a death abroad, and why the death must first be entered in the Turkish civil register, is explained in [certificate of inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/certificate-of-inheritance-turkey) and [registering a death abroad in Turkey](/en/publications/death-certificate-turkey-register-death-abroad).

The second document concerns tax, and it comes from the statute, not from bank policy. Under Article 17(1) VİVK, before handing over money or securities to entitled persons in a transaction subject to the tax, banks must first ask for a certificate from the tax office showing that the tax has been paid (“verginin ödenmiş olduğuna dair vergi dairesinden verilmiş bir tasdikname”). The Revenue Administration (Gelir İdaresi Başkanlığı, GİB) calls this an “ilişik kesme yazısı (borcu yoktur)” in its September 2026 brochure and says the tax office issues it once the return has been filed and the tax paid.

So the order is: certificate of inheritance, inheritance tax return, payment if tax is due, clearance letter, then the bank. A statement from the bank must be attached to the return, so you may need to request a balance confirmation from the bank first. According to the brochure, the return must be filed even if your share stays below the exemption.

The return goes to the tax office where the deceased lived, or, if they lived abroad, where their last Turkish residence was (Articles 6 and 8(1) VİVK). The deadline is four, six or eight months depending on where the death occurred and where the heir is (Article 9(1) VİVK); the full table is in [inheriting property in Turkey](/en/publications/inheriting-property-in-turkey-title-deed). If the deceased died in Germany and you also live in Germany, the deadline is four months, so living abroad does not always extend it.

## Is tax deducted from the deceased’s bank balance?

The bank may pay out even without the clearance letter, but with a deduction. Under Article 17(2) VİVK, where an entitled person does not produce the certificate, the bank may withhold **5 percent** as security for the tax on an inheritance and pay out the rest. The bank must pay the withheld amount to the treasury within one week and notify the tax office in writing.

This gives the bank an option, not the heir a right: the law says banks “verebilirler” (they may pay). Ask your bank beforehand whether it does this. The withholding is security for the tax; nothing in the law releases you from filing the return. Ask the tax office how the amount will be credited against the tax assessed later.

Whether any tax is due depends on your share. Under General Communiqué No. 57 on Inheritance and Gift Tax, from 1 January 2026, 2,907,136 TL of the share of each child (including adopted children) and of the spouse is exempt, and 5,817,845 TL of the spouse’s share where there are no children (Article 4(b) VİVK). Funeral expenses and documented debts of the deceased are deducted in the return (Article 12 VİVK).

## Can one heir claim their share alone?

The legal rule is joint action (Articles 640(2) and 702(2) TMK). If one heir lives in Turkey, one in Germany and a third cannot be reached, things stall. The Civil Code offers two routes:

- Any heir can ask the civil court of peace to appoint a representative for the community of heirs until division (Article 640(3) TMK).
- Any heir can demand division of the estate at any time and apply to the civil court of peace for it (Article 642 TMK).

Whether one heir can claim their part of the deposit directly from the bank, without the others, is disputed. What follows is a view; there is no express statutory rule on this and no binding court decision verified for this article. How a court would decide in a given case is uncertain. Two readings are possible. Under the first, a money claim is divisible, so each heir can demand payment in proportion to their share. Under the second, while the joint ownership lasts, a claim of the estate can only be asserted by all heirs or by the appointed representative; the wording of Articles 640(2) and 702(2) TMK sits closer to this reading. Banks may handle this situation differently. In the author’s assessment, where the other heirs’ written consent cannot be obtained, appointing a representative under Article 640(3) TMK is the most predictable route.

## Does withdrawing money count as accepting the inheritance?

If you are thinking of renouncing the inheritance, be careful with the account. The renunciation period is three months and for statutory heirs usually starts when they learn of the death (Article 606 TMK). Under Article 610(2) TMK, an heir who, before the period ends, meddles in the affairs of the estate, does acts beyond ordinary administration, or hides or appropriates estate assets can no longer renounce.

Withdrawing and spending money with the deceased’s card and PIN after the death can therefore cost you the right to renounce. What counts as ordinary administration depends on the facts. Paying the funeral from the account may be treated that way, but no provision says this always preserves the right to renounce (author’s assessment). If you suspect the estate has debts, read [renouncing an inheritance in Turkey](/en/publications/renouncing-inheritance-in-turkey) first.

A withdrawal by one heir alone also causes trouble with the others. The money belongs to all of them jointly (Article 640(2) TMK), and the other heirs can argue that the amount taken belongs to the estate.

## Deceased person’s bank account in Turkey: acting from abroad

Without travelling to Turkey, the process runs through a power of attorney for a representative in Turkey and filing the tax return at the consulate.

**Power of attorney.** You can have the power of attorney (vekâletname) drawn up at a Turkish consulate, or by a notary in Germany or the Netherlands with an apostille and a Turkish translation. In the author’s assessment, it should expressly cover: obtaining the certificate of inheritance, filing the inheritance tax return and obtaining the clearance letter, and collecting the money in the deceased’s bank accounts. Name the bank if you know it. Banks may have different requirements for the form and wording, so check with the bank first.

**Tax return.** Taxpayers abroad file their returns with Turkish consulates (Article 8(2) VİVK). Each heir may file separately, or the heirs may file jointly (Article 8(3) VİVK); a joint return must be signed by each heir (GİB brochure). According to the brochure, the tax can also be paid through GİB’s Digital Tax Office (Dijital Vergi Dairesi) with cards issued by banks operating abroad.

Any tax or reporting duties in Germany or the Netherlands arising from the transfer of the money are governed by the law of that country; ask a tax adviser qualified there.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do heirs get money from a deceased person’s bank account in Turkey?

With the certificate of inheritance, the inheritance tax return, payment of any tax due and the tax office’s clearance letter (Article 598 TMK, Article 17(1) VİVK). With several heirs, all of them normally act together (Article 640(2) TMK).

### Does a Turkish bank deduct tax from the deceased’s balance?

Without the clearance letter, the bank may withhold 5 percent as security for the tax and pay out the rest (Article 17(2) VİVK). The actual tax is assessed through the return; in 2026, 2,907,136 TL of each child’s and the spouse’s share is exempt.

### Can the surviving spouse withdraw the money alone?

Not unless the spouse is the sole heir; the money belongs to all heirs jointly (Article 640(2) TMK). Whether one heir can claim only their own share is disputed. The routes provided by law are appointing a representative (Article 640(3) TMK) and an action for division (Article 642 TMK).

### How can I find out which Turkish bank the deceased used?

Turkish citizens holding a certificate of inheritance can check this through the e-Devlet service “Mevduat/Katılım Fonu Hesabı Bulunan Banka Sorgulama”. The balance is obtained from the bank.
